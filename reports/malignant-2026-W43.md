## Summary

This cycle is dominated by *Leukemia*, *Haematologica*, *Blood Advances*, and *British Journal of Haematology* output spanning CML translation biology, AML mitochondrial dependencies, a new ASH ITP guideline update, real-world marginal zone lymphoma data with zanubrutinib, and a CAR-HEMATOTOX risk model validated in bispecific-treated lymphoma. **(1)** ASH released a focused 2026 update of its immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) guidelines, newly recommending combination initial therapy — rituximab plus corticosteroids or a thrombopoietic agent plus corticosteroids — over corticosteroid monotherapy (conditional recommendation), alongside a strong recommendation for thrombopoietic agents as second-line therapy after corticosteroid failure[^1]. **(2)** A CML mechanistic study demonstrates that targeting the eIF4F translation-initiation complex (via eIF4G1 inhibition with SBI-756) suppresses BCR::ABL1 protein translation independent of kinase mutation status, resensitizing T315I-mutant and imatinib-resistant stem/progenitor cells to TKIs in a PDX model[^2]. **(3)** The CAR-HEMATOTOX score, originally developed for CAR-T toxicity prediction, was shown to stratify outcomes in odronextamab-treated relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma and DLBCL patients from the ELM-1/ELM-2 trials, with high-score DLBCL patients showing markedly inferior PFS (2.6 vs. 9.6 months, p<0.0001) and OS (4.8 vs. 17.2 months, p<0.0001)[^3]. **(4)** Real-world Spanish data on zanubrutinib in 118 relapsed/refractory marginal zone lymphoma patients (26.3% MAGNOLIA-ineligible) show 76% overall response and 22.2% complete response, with 1-year PFS of 79.4% and OS of 86.8%[^4]. **(5)** A subtype-dependent analysis of 2,500 AML patients defines CSF3R mutations (3.5% prevalence) as having context-restricted prognostic weight, with the strongest adverse signal confined to CEBPA-bZIP AML (EFS HR 2.76, p=0.001; RFS HR 2.66, p=0.002)[^5].

## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

Several new mechanistic and clinical-correlate papers refine AML biology this cycle without overlapping prior NPM1/PETHEMA data. A metabolism-focused CRISPR screen identifies the mitochondrial complex I subunit NDUFS2 as a driver of gilteritinib tolerance in FLT3-ITD AML; NDUFS2 knockdown or pharmacologic complex I inhibition with IACS-010759 impaired oxidative phosphorylation, activated AMPK-PERK-ATF4-CHOP stress signaling, and resensitized resistant cells and PDX models to gilteritinib, with a preliminary murine safety assessment showing no overt short-term toxicity[^6]. A complementary mitochondrial-quality-control study shows that PINK1 and its stabilizer Prohibitin 2 (PHB2) govern mitophagy in AML, and that genetic or pharmacological PHB2/PINK1 inhibition significantly increases venetoclax sensitivity both in vitro and in vivo, nominating this axis as a combination strategy to overcome venetoclax resistance[^7]. In the measurable residual disease space, a new assay-development study in *British Journal of Haematology* reports successful construction of patient-specific genomic PCR-MRD assays in 97.6% of myeloid malignancy patients using NGS/WGS-derived targets, with 50% achieving a quantitative sensitivity range of ≤5×10⁻⁵ (versus 29% in historical ALL cohorts) and serial MRD correlating with clinical outcome, extending EuroMRD-guideline methodology beyond ALL into AML/MDS[^8]. A large subtype-stratified cohort of 2,500 intensively treated de novo non-APL AML patients clarifies that CSF3R mutations (3.5% prevalence, frequently co-occurring with CEBPA-bZIP and RUNX1::RUNX1T1) carry prognostic weight that is highly context-dependent: in the whole cohort CSF3R-mutant disease showed inferior relapse-free survival only, but within CEBPA-bZIP AML specifically, both membrane-proximal activating and cytoplasmic-tail truncating mutation classes were associated with adverse EFS (HR 2.76, p=0.001) and RFS (HR 2.66, p=0.002) after multivariable adjustment including time-dependent transplantation modeling[^5]. Mechanistically interesting but early-stage findings include a pediatric AML immunology study showing that leukemic blasts transfer mitochondria to macrophages via tunneling nanotubes, reprogramming them toward an immunosuppressive, oxidative-phosphorylation-dependent phenotype that impairs phagocytosis and blunts T-cell/bispecific antibody-mediated killing — a mechanism specific to RUNX1::RUNX1T1 and KMT2A-rearranged subtypes[^9]. A novel FLT3 inhibitor, PHI-101, is reported with potent activity against the F691L gatekeeper resistance mutation, addressing an unmet resistance mechanism not covered by currently available agents[^10]. Separately, a case report describes hyperinflammatory differentiation syndrome with hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis-like features following menin inhibitor therapy in AML, flagging an emerging toxicity signature for this drug class[^11], while another case series shows menin inhibition with revumenib clearing measurable residual disease in UBTF-tandem-duplication AML after allogeneic transplant[^12]. A Blood Advances report on AZA-VEN induction describes a bimodal pattern of neutropenia duration that correlates with infection risk, offering a practical risk-stratification signal for supportive care during induction[^13], and a companion Blood Advances study shows long-acting pegylated interferon-gamma restores MHC-II expression on post-HSCT relapsed AML cells and enhances donor T-cell-mediated clearance in xenograft models, a potential strategy to counter immune escape after transplant[^14].

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

A multi-institutional real-world analysis of inotuzumab ozogamicin in 72 pediatric/AYA relapsed/refractory B-ALL patients found standard fractionated dosing (1.8 mg/m²) achieved higher composite complete response rates than alternative lower-dose regimens, though no difference emerged in the ability to bridge to consolidative cellular therapy or in 2-year event-free/overall survival; sinusoidal obstruction syndrome occurred in 10% of patients, predominantly post-transplant and in the standard-dosing arm[^15]. A Pediatric Real-World CAR Consortium analysis of 162 children and young adults with R/R B-ALL receiving tisagenlecleucel found that peripheral blasts at apheresis were associated with inferior unadjusted EFS (27% vs. 55% at 12 months) and OS (55% vs. 78% at 12 months), but this association lost significance after adjustment for disease burden at infusion (multivariable HR 1.11, 95% CI 0.93–1.33, p=0.25), indicating the apheresis blast signal is a surrogate for overall tumor burden rather than an independent risk factor[^16]. A developmental biology study using single-cell ATAC-seq on first-trimester human HSPCs found that fetal cells lack the lineage-restrictive transcription factor motif enrichment seen in adult HSCs and show reduced accessibility at PAX5 regulatory elements, supporting a model in which heightened fetal lymphomyeloid chromatin plasticity underlies prenatal susceptibility to ALL-initiating mutations[^17].

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

New mechanistic work this week converges on translational and post-transcriptional control of BCR::ABL1 as resistance nodes distinct from kinase-domain mutation biology covered previously. Using antibody microarray proteomics, a *Leukemia* study identifies hyperactivation of the eIF4F translation-initiation complex — particularly eIF4G1 — in imatinib-resistant CML stem/progenitor cells; genetic or pharmacologic (SBI-756) inhibition of eIF4G1 reduced BCR::ABL1 protein expression and downstream signaling, impaired survival of nonresponder progenitors including T315I-mutant cells, and produced strong TKI synergy in vitro and in a PDX model[^2]. A separate study in blast-phase CML shows that RUNX1 aberrations (loss-of-function mutations or RUNX1::MECOM/EVI1 fusion) induce the RNA-binding protein SPATS2L, promoting stress-granule assembly that supports blast growth and survival; homoharringtonine selectively sensitized RUNX1-depleted blast-phase CML cells by ablating stress-granule formation, nominating SPATS2L/stress-granule biology as a mutationally-targeted vulnerability in RUNX1-aberrant CML[^18].

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

A systematic review and meta-analysis in *Leukemia* examines concordance between bone marrow and peripheral blood measurable residual disease after first-line novel-agent treatment in CLL, addressing a long-standing methodological question for MRD-adapted trial design, though granular concordance statistics were not available in the abstract provided[^19]. The SF3b spliceosome complex review highlights SF3B1 as a recurrent mutational target in CLL and MDS, detailing how mutant SF3B1 reshapes splice-site selection and genome stability and surveying early-phase clinical experience with SF3b-targeting inhibitors as an emerging therapeutic class relevant to CLL biology[^20].

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

The prospective longitudinal BoHemE study provides the first systematic assessment of bone health in MDS, showing that 111 MDS patients had significantly lower baseline T-scores than 84 age-matched controls (−0.2, p<0.05), with the deficit most pronounced in women with MDS (−0.3, p<0.05) and in DNMT3A-mutated patients (−0.7, p<0.05); T-scores declined further by −0.3 (p<0.05) over 3 years despite stable quality-of-life scores, and osteoporosis was independently associated with inferior overall survival (p<0.01), supporting incorporation of bone health assessment into routine MDS diagnostic work-up[^21].

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

A long-term outcomes study in primary myelofibrosis (217 patients diagnosed before 2001) identifies clinical predictors of exceptional long-term survival: 41 patients (19%) survived more than 25 years, and in multivariable Cox regression with landmark analysis, younger age (<50 years), platelet count >450×10⁹/L, and grade-0 bone marrow fibrosis at diagnosis independently predicted long-term survival, while absence of a JAK2V617F variant and lower CD34+ cell counts were also enriched among long-term survivors[^22]. A separate essential thrombocythemia study reports that marrow hypercellularity is a common finding at diagnosis but carries no independent clinical or prognostic significance, a reassuring finding for pathological interpretation in ET work-up[^23]. In systemic mastocytosis — a myeloid neoplasm relevant to this section — a retrospective analysis of 79 midostaurin- and 176 avapritinib-treated advanced systemic mastocytosis patients found that baseline mutation burden in SRSF2/ASXL1/EZH2 (midostaurin) or SRSF2/RUNX1/SETBP1 (avapritinib) stratified patients into predictive 3-tier risk groups, and that an early (day 15) ≥10% reduction in peripheral KIT D816V VAF with avapritinib predicted improved overall survival, while a ≥25% rise in alkaline phosphatase within 3 months ("AP flare," seen in 46–47% of patients) was paradoxically associated with longer OS[^24]. ASH separately issued new recommendations to improve myelofibrosis care, reflecting a broader guideline push in this disease area this cycle[^25].

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

The CAR-HEMATOTOX score — originally validated for CAR-T toxicity prediction — was retrospectively applied to patients with R/R follicular lymphoma and DLBCL treated with the CD20×CD3 bispecific antibody odronextamab in the ELM-1 (NCT02290951) and ELM-2 (NCT03888105) studies. Patients with high baseline scores (≥2) showed numerically higher rates of grade ≥2 cytokine release syndrome, grade ≥3 infection, and delayed neutrophil recovery, and significantly worse outcomes: in DLBCL, PFS was 2.6 vs. 9.6 months (p<0.0001) and OS was 4.8 vs. 17.2 months (p<0.0001) for high- versus low-score patients, while in follicular lymphoma OS was 26.5 months vs. not reached (p=0.030)[^3]. A narrative review of antibody-based regimens for large B-cell lymphoma summarizing the 2026 ASCO Annual Meeting highlights the phase 3 frontMIND trial (tafasitamab plus lenalidomide and R-CHOP in newly diagnosed high-risk disease), OLYMPIA-3 (CD20×CD3 bispecific intensification in the same setting), and the phase 3 SUNMO trial establishing a PFS advantage for mosunetuzumab plus polatuzumab vedotin in ASCT-ineligible relapsed/refractory disease, alongside EPCORE NHL-2 supporting epcoritamab-based salvage in transplant-eligible patients[^26]. A separate real-world REALI-T study evaluated ibrutinib plus lenalidomide in large B-cell lymphoma patients who were refractory to or relapsed after CAR-T therapy and were ineligible for or had failed bispecific antibodies, addressing an important post-CAR-T, post-bispecific treatment gap, though outcome figures were not detailed in the available abstract[^27]. A comprehensive review catalogs the rare but clinically significant phenomenon of secondary T-cell lymphoma after CAR T-cell immunotherapy, identifying 18 reported cases across all commercial CAR-T products and underlying malignancy types; the infused CAR transgene was detected in lymphoma cells in 63% (10/16) of tested cases, with CAR transgene integration into a known tumor suppressor or oncogene in 73% (8/11), and frequent co-occurring TET2/DNMT3A and JAK-STAT pathway mutations (notably JAK3), implicating preexisting clonal hematopoiesis and malignant CAR-T transformation as contributory mechanisms[^28].

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

Real-world Spanish data on zanubrutinib in 118 patients with relapsed/refractory marginal zone lymphoma (55.3% splenic, 18.9% MALT, 19.8% nodal subtype) after at least one prior anti-CD20-based regimen show an overall response rate of 76% and complete response rate of 22.2%, with no significant differences by MZL subtype, age, POD24 status, or MAGNOLIA trial eligibility; at median follow-up of 14 months, 1-year PFS was 79.4% and OS was 86.8%, and fewer prior lines of therapy were associated with deeper responses — notably, 26.3% of this real-world cohort would not have met MAGNOLIA eligibility criteria yet derived comparable benefit[^4]. A mechanistic study on mantle cell lymphoma biology identifies the neoantigenic receptor ROR1 as supporting MCL cell growth through pyrimidine/purine synthesis induction and DEK phosphorylation, with ROR1 inhibition driving cell-cycle arrest and apoptosis, reinforcing ROR1 as a therapeutic target in MCL[^29]. An editorial highlights emerging combination strategies pairing BTK degraders with BCL2 blockade as a new therapeutic path specifically in marginal zone lymphoma[^30].

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

A longitudinal post-hoc analysis of mogamulizumab in cutaneous T-cell lymphoma (mycosis fungoides/Sézary syndrome) found that while overall mSWAT skin-score trajectories were not well captured by a standard linear mixed model, clinically meaningful responder-associated reductions were seen in blood CD7− (−690.5/μL, 95% CI −1195 to −186.2, p=0.0076) and CD26− (−1029/μL, 95% CI −1705 to −353.7, p=0.0030) malignant cell counts, with a rapid drop in blood CD7− cells by week 4 preceding later skin-score improvement at weeks 12–24, underscoring the importance of dual blood-and-skin monitoring in advanced-stage disease[^31]. A short commentary piece reviews immune alterations characterizing classic Hodgkin lymphoma biology, though without new primary data this cycle[^32].

## X. NK/T-cell Lymphoma (Asia focus)

A single-center retrospective study of 34 patients with relapsed/refractory extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma (ENKTCL) undergoing allogeneic HSCT reports that neither pre-transplant PD-1 inhibitor use, number of prior treatment lines, nor pre-transplant plasma EBV status significantly affected overall or progression-free survival, but achieving complete or partial response before transplant was associated with more favorable prognosis. Chronic active EBV infection pre-transplant and post-transplant EBV reactivation were both associated with inferior survival; genetic analysis revealed frequent hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH)-associated gene variants, with LYST variants most common and HLH development linked to significantly higher non-relapse mortality, while TP53 variants were associated with post-transplant relapse — findings the authors note are hypothesis-generating pending multicenter validation[^33]. This dataset reinforces allo-HSCT as a viable option for selected high-risk, advanced-stage R/R ENKTCL patients, a disease with well-recognized Asia-Pacific predominance directly relevant to Taiwan practice.

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

A Dutch population-based registry analysis of 35-year incidence and survival trends in multiple myeloma provides long-term epidemiological context, though specific incidence/survival figures were not detailed in the available abstract[^34]. Proteogenomic immunopeptidomics profiling of primary myeloma samples and cell lines identified personalized immunoglobulin-derived neoantigens generated by VDJ recombination and somatic hypermutation in all tested samples, along with shared cancer-testis antigen-derived HLA ligands; while approximately 30% of cell-line-derived neoantigen peptides elicited CD8+ T-cell responses in allogeneic healthy-donor PBMCs, none of the patient-derived neoantigens were immunogenic in autologous PBMCs or tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, highlighting severe immune dysfunction as a barrier to TCR-based immunotherapy development in myeloma[^35]. A nationwide Danish observational study of 681 patients with newly or relapsed/refractory myeloma found that frailty, present in 22% by IMWG frailty score, was associated with clinically relevant lower baseline global quality of life and physical functioning; frail newly-diagnosed patients showed QoL improvement over follow-up while frail relapsed/refractory patients experienced deterioration in physical functioning and worsening pain, demonstrating that trial-derived QoL data do not generalize to frail real-world populations[^36]. A complementary geriatric assessment study of 90 older myeloma patients (mean age 72) found frailty present in 22% and associated with worse baseline health-related quality of life and increased high-grade nonhematologic toxicity, with patient-reported CTCAE toxicity rates exceeding physician-reported rates, underscoring discordance between clinician and patient symptom capture[^37]. A real-world claims analysis of 13,427 myeloma patients examined utilization patterns of zoledronic acid versus denosumab, though comparative outcome data were not detailed in the available abstract[^38].

## XII. Transplant & Cellular Therapy (CAR-T / HSCT / GVHD)

A phase I study of nivolumab given post-allogeneic HSCT to promote graft-versus-tumor effect in 14 patients with relapsed/refractory hematologic malignancies found 6 complete remissions, all occurring in patients with chronic lymphoid malignancies; patients with myeloid malignancies or ALL had markedly inferior progression-free survival (4.3 vs. 11.7 months, p<0.05) and overall survival (43.6 months vs. undefined, p<0.05) compared with chronic lymphoid disease, and nine serious adverse events occurred including four dose-limiting toxicities from GVHD or pneumonitis — baseline effector-memory T-cell phenotype and HLA-DR expression were proposed as biomarkers to guide patient selection for this salvage approach[^39]. In non-malignant but clinically adjacent territory, a comparative study of 227 classical PNH patients treated without access to complement inhibitors found that allogeneic HSCT (n=51) achieved 96.08% normal complete blood count at 6 months versus 3.41% with non-transplant treatment (p<0.001), and 5-year failure-free survival of 92.16% versus 0.57% (p<0.001), though 5-year overall survival did not differ significantly (92.16% vs. 98.83%, p=0.097) — relevant historical context as complement inhibitor access expands globally[^40]. A biomarker study in haploidentical HSCT with post-transplant cyclophosphamide found that serum ST2 and REG3α surge significantly from pre-conditioning to day 0 (p=0.031 and p<0.05 respectively), with early post-infusion measurement carrying prognostic value for later GVHD-related outcomes[^41]. A novel cord-blood-derived CAR-NK platform targeting CLL-1 with a newly developed high-affinity monoclonal antibody (clone 2-23) demonstrated elimination of CLL-1-dim AML cells both in vitro and in AML patient-derived xenograft models, addressing a key antigen-density escape mechanism that limits conventional CLL-1-directed cellular therapies[^42]. Separately, a CD24-directed bispecific antibody and CXCR3-armed CD24 CAR-T platform was shown to overcome CD19 CAR-T resistance driven by antigen loss in B-cell malignancy models, with an engineered dual-specific CD19 CAR-T secreting a CD24 bispecific T-cell engager achieving superior efficacy against heterogeneous tumor populations through combined innate (macrophage M1 reprogramming) and adaptive immune mechanisms[^43]. A Blood Advances report on shortened-manufacturing axicabtagene ciloleucel in relapsed/refractory large B-cell lymphoma describes faster in vivo CAR-T expansion and a shifted acute toxicity profile relative to standard manufacturing, though detailed comparative toxicity figures were not available in the abstract[^44].

## XIV. Taiwan Clinical Context

The ASH 2026 ITP guideline update recommending combination initial therapy (rituximab plus corticosteroids, or thrombopoietic agent plus corticosteroids) rather than corticosteroid monotherapy is directly actionable in Taiwan, where rituximab and both eltrombopag and avatrombopag carry NHI reimbursement for ITP under defined second-line criteria; the new guidance may prompt earlier NHI pre-authorization discussions for combination approaches in steroid-refractory-prone patients[^1]. The extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma allo-HSCT data are especially relevant given the markedly higher incidence of ENKTCL in Taiwan and East Asia compared with Western populations; the finding that pre-transplant complete/partial response — rather than EBV status alone — drives post-transplant outcome, and that LYST-associated HLH substantially worsens non-relapse mortality, should inform risk counseling at NTUH, CGMH, and VGH transplant programs managing this endemic disease[^33]. Zanubrutinib is NHI-reimbursed in Taiwan for relapsed/refractory MZL following prior anti-CD20 therapy, and the real-world Spanish effectiveness data (ORR 76%, 1-year OS 86.8%) support continued confidence in this regimen for comorbid, older local patients who often resemble the MAGNOLIA-ineligible subgroup represented in this cohort[^4]. Bone health surveillance in MDS, highlighted by the BoHemE study, is a readily implementable addition to routine Taiwan MDS clinic workflow (DXA screening, calcium/phosphate monitoring) given minimal additional NHI burden and the demonstrated survival association with osteoporosis[^21].

## XV. Key Takeaways

The ASH 2026 ITP guideline update marks the most immediately practice-changing development this cycle, shifting initial therapy toward upfront combination regimens and solidifying thrombopoietic agents as the preferred second-line option[^1]. CML and AML mechanistic studies converge on translational/post-transcriptional control (eIF4F, SPATS2L/stress granules) and mitochondrial quality control (NDUFS2, PHB2/PINK1) as next-generation combination targets to overcome TKI and venetoclax resistance, respectively[^2][^18][^6][^7]. The CAR-HEMATOTOX score's extension beyond CAR-T into bispecific antibody risk stratification for odronextamab-treated lymphoma represents a meaningful cross-modality biomarker validation with clear prognostic separation (PFS HR implied by 2.6 vs. 9.6 months, p<0.0001)[^3]. Real-world marginal zone lymphoma data with zanubrutinib reinforce trial-concordant effectiveness even in MAGNOLIA-ineligible populations[^4], while the ENKTCL allo-HSCT dataset offers Asia-relevant, hypothesis-generating guidance on timing and HLH risk stratification pending larger validation[^33].
