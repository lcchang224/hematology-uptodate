## Summary

This week's data cycle is dominated by a large *Blood* and *Haematologica* batch, including the LACEWING final analysis, MDS/MPN risk biology, and a first disclosure of a GPRC5D CAR-T in multiple myeloma. **(1)** The phase III LACEWING final (6.5-year) analysis of gilteritinib plus azacitidine in newly diagnosed FLT3-mutated AML unfit for intensive chemotherapy shows no significant OS advantage over azacitidine alone (median OS 9.82 vs 9.23 months; p=0.182), despite a markedly higher overall response rate (70.2% vs 36.8%), tempering enthusiasm for FLT3-inhibitor doublets in this population and reframing the case for triplet regimens[^1]. **(2)** Arlocabtagene autoleucel (arlo-cel), a GPRC5D-targeted CAR-T for heavily pretreated relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma, reports an 87% ORR (53% CR) with median PFS of 18.3 months (95% CI, 11.8–21.9) and 1-year OS of 90% in a phase 1 study of 84 patients with a median of 5 prior lines[^2]. **(3)** The phase III IMerge trial publishes overall survival and long-term outcomes for imetelstat in lower-risk MDS, extending the transfusion-independence data previously reported[^3]. **(4)** A COMMAND registry study of 652 patients with newly diagnosed TP53-mutant AML finds no significant survival difference between decitabine- and azacitidine-based induction, with or without venetoclax (12-month OS 31%, 23%, 29%, 32% for DEC+VEN, AZA+VEN, DEC, and AZA respectively; all pairwise comparisons non-significant), underscoring that HMA choice does not meaningfully alter outcomes in this high-risk genomic subgroup[^4]. **(5)** The phase 2/3 ENERGY study establishes nipocalimab as the first agent to show a statistically significant durable hemoglobin response in warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia (23.7% vs 7.7% placebo; 1-sided p=0.015), a landmark result for a historically treatment-refractory hemolytic disease with direct relevance to hematology-oncology co-management of AIHA[^5].

## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

The final, 6.5-year analysis of the phase III LACEWING trial, published in *Haematologica*, delivers a sobering counterpoint to last week's optimism around FLT3-triplet regimens[^1]. Among 168 randomized patients with newly diagnosed FLT3-mutated AML ineligible for intensive induction chemotherapy, gilteritinib plus azacitidine (GIL+AZA) failed to demonstrate a statistically significant OS benefit over azacitidine alone (median OS 9.82 vs 9.23 months; p=0.182), with gilteritinib monotherapy performing worst (median OS 5.24 months). Two-year OS rates were 18.8% (GIL+AZA), 12.9% (AZA), and 4.5% (GIL), and although overall response rate favored the combination (70.2% vs 36.8%), this did not translate into durable survival gains. This result, contextualized by an accompanying editorial on lessons from LACEWING[^6], strengthens the rationale — reinforced by last week's azacitidine-venetoclax-gilteritinib triplet data[^7] — for moving beyond FLT3-inhibitor doublets toward triplet or venetoclax-containing backbones in this molecular subgroup.

The COMMAND registry study in *Leukemia* provides the largest retrospective comparison to date of hypomethylating agent choice in TP53-mutant AML, evaluating 652 patients (321 receiving HMA-based induction; decitabine n=183, azacitidine n=138)[^4]. In propensity-score-matched analysis, no significant differences emerged in EFS (p=0.920) or OS (p=0.927) between decitabine and azacitidine, with or without venetoclax; median EFS ranged narrowly from 3.4 to 5.9 months and median OS from 5.7 to 9.2 months across the four regimen combinations. Multi-hit TP53 status, rather than mutation subtype or HMA choice, was the dominant independent predictor of inferior outcomes, reinforcing that novel non-HMA strategies remain the priority for this genomically defined high-risk group.

Mechanistic advances in venetoclax resistance biology continue to accumulate. A *Blood* study identifies PRMT9 as a driver of venetoclax resistance in AML, showing that PRMT9 ablation disrupts RNA splicing of ALG13, downregulates the venetoclax efflux transporter ABCC1, suppresses MCL1 translation, and resensitizes venetoclax-resistant patient-derived xenograft models to therapy — nominating PRMT9 inhibition as a combination strategy[^8]. Separately, chromosome 5q deletion is shown to be a key evolutionary driver of complex karyotype in AML: an iPSC model demonstrates that TP53-mutant HSPCs with del(5q), but not TP53 mutation alone, undergo stepwise acquisition of aneuploidy, converging on a BCL2-dependent gene expression state that is eradicated by venetoclax, with resistant clones undergoing lineage switching to alternative BCL2-family dependencies[^9]. A companion *Leukemia* paper further dissects CSNK1A1 E98 hotspot mutations in del(5q) MDS/AML, showing these represent a change-of-function (not loss-of-function) event that induces a stress-tolerant, ferroptosis-vulnerable cellular state with impaired megakaryopoiesis, distinguishing the two-step model of del(5q) clonal evolution from simple haploinsufficiency[^10]. In pediatric AML1-ETO–positive AML, the DHX15R222G mutation described last week is further elaborated mechanistically, with confirmation that OXPHOS inhibition via S-Gboxin overcomes chemoresistance in AML1-ETO+/DHX15R222G leukemia stem cells[^11]. A KLF4/MLL3 complex axis is newly identified as a suppressor of AML growth via transcriptional activation of NRBP2 and downstream restraint of the pro-leukemic kinase TNIK, with the small-molecule TNIK-IN-1 showing selective anti-AML activity with limited toxicity to normal hematopoiesis[^12].

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

A single-center retrospective analysis of 140 adult patients with Philadelphia-like ALL demonstrates that pediatric-inspired regimens (received by 75% of patients) are associated with significantly higher complete remission rates (p=0.034) and reduced relapse risk compared with adult-style regimens, with CRLF2 rearrangements present in 85% of the cohort[^13]. In the transplant setting, a phase 1 study of memory-enriched CD19 CAR-T cells as definitive consolidation in MRD-negative first complete remission in older adults (≥55 years, median age 64) with B-ALL reports 18-month event-free and overall survival of 84% and 100% respectively, with no dose-limiting toxicities or grade ≥2 CRS/ICANS, supporting further investigation of CAR-T as an alternative to allogeneic transplant consolidation in this population[^14]. A large European real-world study of tisagenlecleucel for post-transplant relapse in 220 pediatric/young adult B-ALL patients across 31 centers reports 2-year EFS of 43.6% and OS of 67.2%, with relapse from a matched sibling donor associated with significantly worse 2-year OS (59.1%) compared with mismatched (80.2%) or matched unrelated donors (68.3%; p=0.046), and early relapse (<6 months post-HSCT) predicting markedly inferior outcomes (2-year EFS 23.7% vs 49.8%, p=0.001; OS 47.2% vs 73.9%, p<0.001)[^15].

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

A SEER database analysis of 63,242 US MPN patients (2000–2021) reports a median overall survival of 202 months for CML, the longest among the MPN subtypes analyzed (compared with 152 months for ET, 150 months for PV, and 48 months for PMF), reflecting the durable efficacy of modern TKI therapy at a population level[^16]. However, a complementary quality-of-life study finds that long-term TKI-treated CML survivors (median follow-up 12.5 years) experience substantially worse health status than non-cancer controls, including higher adjusted odds of poor general health (aOR 4.43; 95% CI, 1.8–10.7), functional impairment (aOR 2.84; 95% CI, 1.4–6.0), and frailty/pre-frailty (aOR 3.71; 95% CI, 1.7–8.1) — a burden comparable to that of BMT survivors without TKI exposure, highlighting the need for structured long-term survivorship monitoring in chronic TKI-treated patients[^17].

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

A mechanistic *Blood* study identifies IL-16 as a novel, non-genetic driver of BTK inhibitor and chemoimmunotherapy resistance across B-cell malignancies including CLL, mantle cell lymphoma, marginal zone lymphoma, and ABC-DLBCL[^18]. IL-16 is transcriptionally upregulated and actively secreted in ibrutinib-resistant models lacking BTK/PLCG2 mutations, engaging CD9-enriched membrane microdomains to activate PI3Kδ/AKT/ERK signaling, stabilize MYC, and upregulate the antiapoptotic protein BFL1; serum IL-16 levels were elevated in ibrutinib-refractory CLL patients without canonical resistance mutations, and pharmacological disruption of the IL-16/CD9/PI3K axis restored BTK inhibitor and R-CHOP sensitivity in primary CLL samples, nominating this axis as a therapeutic target for resistant disease[^18], with an accompanying editorial contextualizing the finding as a new resistance paradigm[^19]. Separately, a MAP3K14 (NIK) synonymous splice-altering variant is characterized as a novel activator of non-canonical NF-κB signaling across mature B-cell malignancies including CLL, Waldenström macroglobulinemia, multiple myeloma, and mantle cell lymphoma, expanding the genetic landscape of NF-κB dysregulation beyond classical TRAF3 loss[^20].

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

The phase III IMerge trial reports final overall survival and long-term outcomes for imetelstat in lower-risk MDS in *Haematologica*, extending the previously established transfusion-independence benefit with mature survival follow-up[^3]. A companion *Blood* study identifies MBD1-L, an aberrantly spliced isoform of methyl-binding-domain 1 driven by reduced WTAP expression (rather than a canonical splicing-factor mutation), as a convergent driver of myeloerythroid defects across MDS regardless of mutational status; overexpression of MBD1-L in healthy HSPCs recapitulated core MDS phenotypes including suppressed erythroid differentiation and impaired xenotransplantation reconstitution, and antisense oligonucleotide-mediated correction of the splicing defect enhanced erythroid differentiation in primary MDS samples, suggesting an RNA-therapeutic avenue applicable across MDS genotypes[^21]. Ivosidenib monotherapy in IDH1-mutated MDS is reported from a phase 2 trial (IDIOME) across three cohorts, with ORR of 63.6% (95% CI, 40.7–82.8) in relapsed/refractory post-azacitidine patients and 78.3% (95% CI, 56.3–92.5) in treatment-naïve high-risk patients; 12-month OS was 18.2% and 91.3% in these respective cohorts, with higher baseline IDH1 mutant clone size predicting response and TP53/IDH2 co-mutations predicting resistance — positioning ivosidenib as a potential frontline option in treatment-naïve IDH1-mutated high-risk MDS[^22]. VEXAS syndrome-associated MDS is reviewed in *Blood Advances*, noting that MDS is diagnosed in approximately 30–50% of VEXAS patients (driven by UBA1 somatic mutation), while only ~1% of unselected MDS patients harbor UBA1 mutations, underscoring VEXAS-MDS as a distinct clinical, morphologic, and prognostic entity warranting dedicated diagnostic vigilance[^23].

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

The FDA approval of rusfertide for polycythemia vera is reported this week via OncLive, marking a new hepcidin-mimetic therapeutic option for PV, though detailed trial efficacy data were not included in this week's cache and will be tracked in subsequent reports as full prescribing information and pivotal trial publications emerge[^24]. The SEER-based epidemiological analysis noted above also provides updated US incidence rates for MPN subtypes: 1.8, 1.7, 0.5, and 0.7 per 100,000 person-years for ET, PV, PMF, and CML respectively, with 5-year relative survival for PMF improving from 50.9% (2009 cohort) to 60.6% (2016 cohort), reflecting incremental gains from JAK inhibitor-era management[^16]. Final results of a phase II trial combining azacitidine and ruxolitinib in myelofibrosis (61 patients treated 2013–2021, median follow-up 28 months at interim) confirm a 72% response rate by IWG-MRT criteria in the interim analysis, with final study outcomes now reported across the full cohort inclusive of chronic- and advanced-phase disease[^25]. A proposed biomarker-driven framework for defining and predicting MPN disease progression is published in *Leukemia*, aiming to standardize risk assessment for transformation to myelofibrosis or acute leukemia across PV, ET, and early MF[^26].

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

A conceptual *Blood* review proposes a formal framework for functional cure in follicular lymphoma, noting that with rituximab-based therapy, median overall survival now approaches 20 years and only 35–40% of patients require more than one treatment line[^28]. The authors propose early clinical milestones — event-free survival at 24 months (EFS24) and complete remission at 30 months (CR30) — alongside durable remission after treatment discontinuation, MRD/PET-CT-defined deep remission, and normalized quality of life relative to the general population, as criteria for identifying patients on a favorable, potentially curable disease trajectory, drawing parallels to functional cure paradigms established in CML and multiple myeloma[^28].

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

_No new signal this week._ A case report describes an unusual interfollicular and intrasinusoidal pattern of involvement in classic Hodgkin lymphoma, of diagnostic rather than therapeutic significance[^29].

## X. NK/T-cell Lymphoma (Asia focus)

A multimodal spatial profiling study of extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma (ENKTL) identifies two macrophage-defined tumor microenvironment subgroups with distinct prognostic implications[^30]. Subgroup 1 is characterized by inflammatory, IFN-α/γ-responsive macrophages co-localizing with an NF-κB-activated, EBV-associated tumor subset within a defined "inflammation niche," and higher abundance of this niche correlates with improved survival across independent cohorts, whereas Subgroup 2 is immune-quiescent with scavenging STAB1+ macrophages. This spatially resolved framework offers a novel prognostic and immunotherapeutic stratification tool for ENKTL, a lymphoma subtype of particular relevance to Taiwan and East Asian populations[^30]. Complementing this, a chromatin biology study demonstrates that EBV drives three-dimensional chromatin reorganization and enhancer activation in T/NK-cell lymphoproliferative disorders, identifying CACNA2D1 and RGS1 as EBV-interacting target genes whose knockdown impairs growth of EBV-positive lymphoma cell lines, providing mechanistic insight into non-genetic oncogenic drivers in chronic active EBV disease and ENKTL[^31].

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

Arlocabtagene autoleucel (arlo-cel, BMS-986393), a GPRC5D-targeted autologous CAR-T therapy, is reported in a phase 1 dose-escalation/expansion study of 84 heavily pretreated relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma patients (median 5 prior regimens, 49% with prior BCMA-directed therapy)[^2]. With median follow-up of 16.1 months, ORR was 87% (53% complete response), median duration of response 18.0 months, median PFS 18.3 months (95% CI, 11.8–21.9), and 1-year OS 90%. CRS occurred in 82% (mostly grade 1/2, one fatal case at the highest dose), with transient grade 1/2 on-target/off-tumor skin (30%), nail (19%), and oral (32%) toxicities characteristic of GPRC5D targeting; MTD was not reached, and this dataset — alongside an accompanying editorial framing GPRC5D CAR-T as a path beyond BCMA[^32] — reinforces GPRC5D as a validated, non-BCMA immunotherapeutic target in RRMM.

A first-in-human study of CT0590, a triple-knockout allogeneic BCMA/NKG2A dual-targeting CAR-T designed to resist host-versus-graft NK-cell rejection, enrolled 5 patients (4 RRMM, 1 primary plasma cell leukemia)[^33]. No dose-limiting toxicities, treatment-related deaths, or GVHD occurred; 3 of 5 patients achieved confirmed responses including 2 stringent complete responses, one ongoing beyond 23 months and one lasting 20 months in the pPCL patient, with robust CAR-T expansion (>280,000 copies/µg genomic DNA) correlating with higher baseline NK-cell NKG2A expression — an early but notable proof-of-concept for allogeneic "off-the-shelf" BCMA CAR-T overcoming host immune rejection.

Real-world data on BCMA CAR-T bridging therapy in 399 RRMM patients (87% receiving bridging) show that bridging regimen choice significantly affects efficacy, with patients requiring bridging therapy generally presenting with more advanced, high-risk, and penta-refractory disease at the time of infusion[^34]. Exposure-response modeling of belantamab mafodotin from the DREAMM-6/7/8 program supports a 2.5 mg/kg starting dose over 1.9 mg/kg, given a substantially higher probability of achieving ≥VGPR (70.4% vs 54.3%) that outweighs modestly increased ocular toxicity risk, with step-down dosing and schedule extensions proposed to manage tolerability while preserving efficacy[^35]. Mechanistic work also identifies CXXC1, a COMPASS complex component, as a novel regulator sustaining the IKZF1/3-IRF4-MYC lenalidomide-resistance loop in myeloma, with CXXC1 depletion disrupting IRF4/IKZF3 chromatin binding in both lenalidomide-sensitive and -resistant cells, nominating COMPASS/CXXC1 as a therapeutic target for IMiD-resistant disease[^36]. A companion mechanistic study shows that mezigdomide, via IKZF1/3 degradation, reverses ex vivo T-cell exhaustion and enhances killing by the BCMA T-cell engager alnuctamab, supporting combination strategies to overcome TCE resistance in MM[^37]. Real-world flow cytometry-based MRD assessment using EuroFlow-standardized panels in 74 MM patients demonstrates 45% achieving undetectable MRD (10⁻⁵ sensitivity), with only 1 relapse among uMRD patients versus 15 relapses/3 deaths among persistent-MRD patients over 19 months median follow-up, reinforcing MRD negativity as a robust real-world prognostic marker[^38].

## XII. Transplant & Cellular Therapy (CAR-T / HSCT / GVHD)

Two complementary mechanistic papers in *Blood* dissect steroid-resistant gastrointestinal GVHD pathophysiology. Single-cell RNA sequencing identifies a CD16hiCD177+ neutrophil subset markedly expanded in steroid-resistant GI-GVHD, which transmigrates into the intestine via CD177-CD31 binding and drives epithelial damage through MyD88-dependent NET formation; retinoic acid drives expansion of this pathogenic subset via a RARA-SPI1 transcriptional axis, and pharmacological RARA inhibition with AGN193109 ameliorated GI-GVHD, improved survival, and restored corticosteroid sensitivity in murine models — nominating RARA inhibition as a novel therapeutic strategy for this lethal transplant complication[^39], with an accompanying editorial highlighting the previously underappreciated role of neutrophils in this setting[^40]. Separately, allogeneic CD19-directed CAR-T therapy CTX110 in 63 heavily pretreated relapsed/refractory B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma patients achieves 65% objective and 39% complete response rates at dose level ≥3, with a two-infusion regimen prolonging response duration versus single infusion (HR 0.65), and CRS/ICANS rates of 54%/13% respectively, supporting further development of allogeneic "off-the-shelf" CD19 CAR-T platforms[^41].

Genomic safety monitoring after CAR-T continues to mature: a review defines practical diagnostic criteria for CAR-transgenic T-cell lymphoproliferative neoplasm (CTTLN), a rare but serious genomic safety signal following CAR-T therapy, proposing a proportionate surveillance framework[^42]. A related study shows that CAR construct architecture materially influences resistance biology, with CD19-4-1BB-based CARs (tisagenlecleucel-type) driving greater CD19 epitope loss and mutation compared with CD19-CD28-based constructs (axicabtagene ciloleucel-type), attributable to differential elimination efficiency against CD19-low tumor populations — a mechanistic explanation for previously observed clinical differences in relapse patterns between these two CAR-T products[^43]. TP53-mutant clonal hematopoiesis (CHIP) is identified as a high-risk genotype in CAR-T recipients, associated with lower baseline platelet/hemoglobin counts, higher CAR-HEMATOTOX scores, inferior clinical outcomes, and a distinct inflammatory proteomic signature, supporting risk-adapted longitudinal CHIP monitoring in CAR-T candidates with cytopenias[^44].

## XIV. Taiwan Clinical Context

The LACEWING final results carry direct relevance for Taiwan NHI reimbursement policy, since gilteritinib is currently covered for relapsed/refractory FLT3-mutated AML but not universally for frontline non-intensive combination use; the lack of OS benefit for GIL+AZA over AZA alone in the final analysis[^1] argues against expanding frontline doublet reimbursement and strengthens the case for prioritizing triplet regimens (as emerging from azacitidine-venetoclax-gilteritinib data) in future NHI protocol discussions at centers such as NTUH and CGMH. The GPRC5D CAR-T data (arlo-cel)[^2] and allogeneic BCMA/NKG2A CAR-T (CT0590)[^33] are of particular interest for Taiwan's growing cellular therapy programs at NTUH, VGH, and CGMH, which currently offer BCMA-directed CAR-T (cilta-cel/ide-cel) under NHI-negotiated access pathways; GPRC5D-directed and allogeneic platforms represent the next wave of clinical trial interest as domestic manufacturing and import pathways develop. The ENKTL spatial biology findings[^30] are especially pertinent given the substantially higher incidence of extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma in Taiwan and East Asia compared with Western populations, and the proposed macrophage-based prognostic niche framework may inform future biomarker studies at CMUH and other Taiwanese lymphoma referral centers. Rusfertide's FDA approval for PV[^24] should prompt monitoring of Taiwan Center for Drug Evaluation review timelines, as hepcidin-mimetic therapy would address an unmet need for phlebotomy-dependent PV patients currently managed with hydroxyurea or ruxolitinib under NHI criteria.

## XV. Key Takeaways

The LACEWING final analysis represents a cautionary, mature dataset showing that FLT3-inhibitor doublets with azacitidine do not confer a statistically significant survival advantage over azacitidine alone in unfit newly diagnosed FLT3-mutated AML, reinforcing the shift toward triplet regimens incorporating venetoclax. GPRC5D CAR-T therapy (arlo-cel) delivers robust, durable phase 1 efficacy (87% ORR, 18.3-month PFS) that positions this target as a genuine complement to BCMA-directed cellular therapies in relapsed myeloma, while an early allogeneic BCMA/NKG2A dual-targeting platform (CT0590) offers proof-of-concept for overcoming host-versus-graft rejection in off-the-shelf CAR-T. Mechanistic biology this week substantially deepens understanding of resistance across disease states — IL-16/CD9/PI3K signaling in BTK inhibitor resistance, PRMT9-mediated splicing in venetoclax resistance, and CAR construct architecture (4-1BB vs CD28) in CD19 antigen loss — collectively nominating multiple new combination and re-sensitization strategies for future clinical testing. Finally, nipocalimab's statistically significant durable hemoglobin response in warm AIHA marks a milestone for a historically undertreated hemolytic disease with meaningful overlap into hematology-malignancy practice via shared FcRn-targeting and immunotherapy expertise.

## Journal Literature — CrossRef

Beyond the studies discussed above, this week's CrossRef pull included several additional hematology-relevant basic/translational papers of note: GLUL-mediated ammonia detoxification is shown to be essential for megakaryocyte polyploidization and platelet production, with the small molecule fulvotomentoside A identified as a candidate GLUL agonist promoting thrombocytopoiesis after radiation/chemotherapy injury[^45]. YAP/TAZ activation in bone marrow niche endothelial and mesenchymal stromal cells is shown to accelerate hematopoietic regeneration after myeloablative stress, with pharmacological YAP/TAZ activation proposed as a niche-targeted regenerative strategy[^46]. CD38 biology in pediatric T-ALL is comprehensively mapped across 1,335 primary tumors, identifying RUNX1/RUNX3/TP53 as positive regulators of CD38 expression and nominating DFMO (polyamine pathway inhibition) plus daratumumab, or daratumumab plus dasatinib (via LCK/SRC signaling), as rational combination strategies to enhance CD38-directed immunotherapy in relapsed T-ALL[^47]. Notch1 PEST domain deletion is engineered to enhance CD19-BBζ CAR-T proliferation and antitumor activity in B-ALL xenograft models, offering a genetic engineering strategy to address CAR-T exhaustion and relapse[^48]. IFN-γ from CD8+ T cells is identified as a mediator of long-term bone marrow niche dysfunction following doxorubicin-based chemotherapy, with combined IFN-γ blockade and deferoxamine mesylate attenuating chemotherapy-associated skeletal damage in murine models, with parallel findings confirmed in paired human leukemia patient bone marrow samples[^49].
