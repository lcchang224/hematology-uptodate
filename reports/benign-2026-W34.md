## Summary

This week's five most clinically actionable developments span platelet biology, aplastic anemia, myeloproliferative neoplasms, hemostasis, and red cell genetics. First, a mechanistic *Blood* study extends last week's histidine-rich glycoprotein (HRG) findings by demonstrating that HRG directly binds GPIbα on resting and activated platelets and GPIIb/IIIa on activated platelets, competing with von Willebrand factor (VWF) and fibrinogen respectively, and that plasma HRG is roughly halved in sepsis and COVID-19, promoting a hyperreactive platelet phenotype — establishing HRG as a dual regulator of coagulation and primary hemostasis with therapeutic implications for both thrombotic and consumptive states[^1]. Second, the phase III EMAA trial (*Blood*) provides the first randomized, placebo-controlled evidence that adding eltrombopag to cyclosporine A (CSA) as first-line therapy for moderate aplastic anemia significantly improves week-24 overall response rate (71.4% vs 42.5%, p=0.011), with crossover patients achieving 73.5% ORR by week 48 — filling a major evidence gap for a population historically undertreated relative to severe aplastic anemia[^2]. Third, the IMPRSSION phase 2a trial of sapablursen, a TMPRSS6-targeting antisense oligonucleotide, demonstrates clinically meaningful phlebotomy-frequency reduction (−0.10 phlebotomies/week, p<0.0001) and symptom improvement (MPN-SAF-TSS change −6.21 points, p=0.0052) in polycythemia vera, positioning a novel iron-restriction mechanism as an alternative to interferon and JAK inhibition for erythrocytosis control[^3]. Fourth, a large UK Biobank study of 467,779 adults definitively refutes an association between sickle cell trait (SCT) genotype and excess all-cause mortality (HR 1.04, 95% CI 0.84–1.29) while confirming modestly increased risks of diabetes (HR 1.30), chronic kidney disease (HR 1.46), and hypertension (OR 1.24), and importantly demonstrates that prior hospital-registry-based studies showing markedly elevated mortality (HR 2.24) likely reflect selection bias rather than a true SCT effect[^4]. Fifth, a *Blood Advances* study characterizes previously underappreciated skeletal fragility in bleeding disorders, showing that persons with von Willebrand disease (PwVWD) have significantly lower bone mineral density z-scores than healthy controls, with thrombin-plasmin generation dynamics — rather than clinical bleeding severity — accounting for the greatest proportion of variance in bone density[^5].

## I. Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP)

Following last week's landmark McMaster emergency management guideline, this week's *Blood Journal* literature adds mechanistic insight into chronic ITP pathobiology rather than new clinical management data. A study identifies reduced SRSF1 expression in CD4+ naive T cells from ITP patients as a driver of LCK exon 8 skipping, which impairs T-cell receptor signaling and in vitro regulatory T-cell (Treg) induction[^6]. Using an LCK minigene system combined with RNA pull-down mass spectrometry, the authors show that SRSF1 directly regulates this splicing event, and that SRSF1 overexpression restores Treg induction capacity in patient-derived cells. Adoptive transfer of CD4+ naive T cells expressing an exon 8-skipped Lck variant into CD61-knockout mice reduced both Treg proportions and platelet counts in an active ITP model, and mechanistic studies showed reduced binding of the skipped LCK variant to ZAP70 and CD3ζ, attenuating downstream FOXP3 induction. This SRSF1-LCK splicing axis represents a novel candidate pathway for Treg dysfunction in ITP and may offer future therapeutic targets distinct from the antibody-mediated destruction pathway that dominates current ITP treatment paradigms. No new clinical trial or guideline data were identified this week beyond the McMaster emergency bleed guideline already covered.

## III. Aplastic Anemia

The EMAA trial represents this week's most significant aplastic anemia development. This investigator-initiated, prospective, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind, multicenter phase III trial addressed a long-standing evidence gap: while eltrombopag plus horse antithymocyte globulin (hATG) and CSA is established standard of care for severe aplastic anemia (SAA) ineligible for transplant, no consensus existed for moderate aplastic anemia (MAA)[^2]. Investigators randomized 41 patients to eltrombopag plus CSA and 44 to placebo plus CSA as first-line therapy for MAA with clinically significant cytopenia. Among 75 patients with evaluable response at week 24, the primary endpoint — overall response rate combining partial and complete response — was significantly higher with eltrombopag+CSA (71.4%; 95% CI, 54.8–83.8%) than placebo+CSA (42.5%; 95% CI, 28.5–57.8%; p=0.011, Fisher's exact test). Following unblinding, placebo-arm patients without complete response were crossed over to add eltrombopag to continued CSA, achieving an ORR of 73.5% by week 48 (p=0.009, McNemar test comparing week 24 vs 48 response). Relapse rate and failure-free survival did not differ significantly between arms, and eltrombopag was well tolerated without new safety signals. This trial establishes eltrombopag+CSA as an evidence-based first-line option for MAA, a population previously managed largely by extrapolation from SAA data or observation alone, and is directly relevant to NCKUH practice given eltrombopag's established NHI reimbursement pathway for SAA in Taiwan.

## V. Hemophilia A & B (including gene therapy)

A *Blood Advances* cross-sectional study addresses an underexplored comorbidity axis in bleeding disorders: skeletal health. Investigators compared total bone mineral density (BMD) among persons with hemophilia (PwH, n=34), persons with von Willebrand disease (PwVWD, n=45), and healthy controls (n=30) aged 5–45 years, using dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry and peripheral quantitative computed tomography, alongside bone turnover biomarkers and simultaneous thrombin-plasmin generation (STP) assays[^5]. PwVWD demonstrated significantly lower BMD z-scores compared with controls, while PwH showed a similar but statistically nonsignificant trend; both groups had significantly lower bone mineral content than controls. In multivariable modeling, higher thrombin generation was associated with higher BMD z-scores while higher plasmin generation was associated with lower BMD z-scores, and STP parameters accounted for a greater proportion of variance in BMD than models incorporating clinical bleeding severity or bone turnover markers alone. These hypothesis-generating findings suggest a mechanistic hemostasis-bone crosstalk that merits longitudinal follow-up and may inform future bone health surveillance recommendations for patients with inherited bleeding disorders, an area not currently addressed in most hemophilia comprehensive care protocols at NCKUH.

## IX. Red Cell Disorders (G6PD, PK deficiency, hereditary spherocytosis)

A large UK Biobank analysis provides the most robust epidemiological data to date on sickle cell trait (SCT) outcomes, directly relevant to counseling of carrier populations, including in Taiwan where hemoglobinopathy carrier screening programs are established for thalassemia and could extend conceptually to other structural hemoglobin variants[^4]. Among 467,779 general-population adults with whole-exome sequencing followed prospectively for a median of 15 years, the 1,253 identified SCT carriers showed no increased risk of all-cause death (HR 1.04; 95% CI, 0.84–1.29), cardiovascular death (HR 1.34; 95% CI, 0.90–2.00), myocardial infarction (HR 0.72; 95% CI, 0.42–1.24), ischemic stroke (HR 1.30; 95% CI, 0.88–1.94), pulmonary embolism (HR 1.29; 95% CI, 0.84–1.99), or heart failure (HR 0.79; 95% CI, 0.55–1.14) compared with noncarriers. However, SCT carriers did show increased risk of diabetes (HR 1.30; 95% CI, 1.12–1.50), chronic kidney disease (HR 1.46; 95% CI, 1.18–1.80), and hypertension (OR 1.24; 95% CI, 1.08–1.42), and notably, cardiovascular death risk was markedly increased among SCT carriers who also had diabetes compared with non-carrier diabetics (HR 2.17; 95% CI, 1.14–4.12), suggesting a gene-comorbidity interaction. Critically, when the same investigators repeated analyses using hospital-registry-diagnosed SCT rather than genotype-confirmed SCT, they reproduced the previously reported marked mortality excess (HR 2.24; 95% CI, 1.42–3.52) and stroke risk (HR 2.92; 95% CI, 1.30–6.60), strongly suggesting that earlier literature associating SCT with adverse outcomes suffered from ascertainment/selection bias inherent to clinically-triggered diagnosis. This distinction has direct implications for pretest counseling frameworks and should temper alarm in genotype-identified, asymptomatic SCT carriers.

## X. Iron Deficiency & Nutritional Anemias

A *Blood Advances* cost-effectiveness analysis addresses screening policy for maternal iron deficiency (ID), a condition affecting an estimated 85% of pregnancies globally by the third trimester[^8]. Using a modified US societal perspective incorporating wage loss from infusion time and long-term hematology follow-up costs, the analysis compared ferritin thresholds of 30 μg/L versus 15 μg/L versus no screening. Screening at 30 μg/L was the preferred, cost-effective strategy across all base-case, threshold, and sensitivity analyses, with an incremental cost-effectiveness ratio of $34,000 per quality-adjusted life-year (95% credible interval, $32,000–$36,200) compared with no screening, and was favored in 100% of 10,000 Monte Carlo iterations. While a US-specific economic model, this reinforces the rationale for routine antenatal ferritin screening — a practice with variable adoption in Taiwan obstetric-hematology shared care pathways — and supports advocacy for higher, more physiologically informed ferritin thresholds rather than reliance on the WHO's diagnostic (nonscreening) cutoff of 15 μg/L.

## XI. Thrombosis & Coagulation (VTE, APS, inherited thrombophilia)

Two complementary *Blood* papers this week substantially advance understanding of platelet-coagulation crosstalk. The first mechanistic study on HRG demonstrates that this 75-kDa hepatically-produced plasma protein, circulating at approximately 2 μM with an additional platelet-releasable pool, binds GPIbα on both resting and activated platelets and GPIIb/IIIa selectively on activated platelets, competing with VWF and fibrinogen respectively[^1]. Functionally, HRG attenuated platelet agglutination, aggregation, and platelet-mediated thrombus growth in vitro, and reduced platelet deposition under high-shear conditions in endothelial-platelet flow and collagen-coated microperfusion systems. Clinically, plasma HRG levels in patients with sepsis or COVID-19 were approximately half those of healthy controls, and experimentally reducing HRG to these levels in vitro induced a hyperreactive platelet phenotype — suggesting that HRG depletion may contribute to the thrombotic diathesis observed in severe infection and raising the possibility of HRG repletion as a novel antithrombotic strategy in consumptive/inflammatory states. A second mechanistic paper identifies endothelial PIEZO1 as a mechanosensitive trigger of Weibel-Palade body exocytosis and VWF secretion, demonstrating in vivo that PIEZO1 agonism (Yoda1) dramatically increases leukocyte and platelet adhesion to postcapillary venular walls, coupling mechanical inflammation signals to acute hemostatic activation[^9]. Separately, a translational study using rapid cycling compression devices (RCDs) in a brain-dead organ donor model of critical illness showed that restoring oscillatory shear stress at venous valve sinuses upregulates antithrombotic endothelial genes (FoxC2, Prox1), suppresses adhesion molecules (P-selectin, CCL2), and reduces DVT and microthrombus formation compared with standard anticoagulation alone, offering mechanistic support for enhanced mechanical VTE prophylaxis strategies in immobilized critically ill patients[^10]. None of these three studies had been previously reported in this report series.

## XIII. Taiwan Clinical Context

The EMAA trial results carry direct relevance for NCKUH hematology practice, as eltrombopag is already NHI-reimbursed for severe aplastic anemia under existing criteria; extension of this evidence base to moderate aplastic anemia may prompt future reimbursement policy discussion, particularly for patients with clinically significant cytopenias who do not meet formal severe criteria but experience meaningful transfusion burden or bleeding risk. The sapablursen data in polycythemia vera are of interest given that ruxolitinib is currently the primary NHI-reimbursed cytoreductive option for hydroxyurea-resistant/intolerant PV in Taiwan; a TMPRSS6-targeting ASO acting via iron restriction represents a mechanistically distinct alternative that, if it proceeds to phase III, would need separate NHI evaluation given Taiwan's distinct approval pathway for antisense therapeutics. The sickle cell trait mortality data, while derived from a UK Biobank population with negligible East Asian representation of hemoglobin S carriers, remain conceptually important for genetic counseling frameworks as Taiwan's national thalassemia carrier screening program (affecting an estimated 4–5% of the population for alpha-thalassemia and 1–2% for beta-thalassemia carrier status) faces analogous questions about how registry-based versus genotype-based ascertainment can bias perceived phenotype-outcome associations. Finally, the maternal iron deficiency screening cost-effectiveness data support ongoing efforts to standardize antenatal ferritin testing thresholds in Taiwan's National Health Insurance-covered prenatal care pathway, an area with historically inconsistent screening practice across obstetric providers.

## XIV. Key Takeaways

The EMAA trial fills a genuine unmet need by providing level-1 evidence for eltrombopag use in moderate, not just severe, aplastic anemia, and should prompt reconsideration of first-line treatment algorithms for MAA at NCKUH. The HRG mechanistic work substantially deepens understanding of an emerging endogenous hemostatic regulator with dual anti-thrombotic and anti-inflammatory properties, and its depletion in sepsis/COVID-19 offers a plausible mechanistic link to infection-associated thrombosis meriting further clinical correlation studies. The UK Biobank sickle cell trait study provides reassuring, methodologically rigorous data refuting excess mortality in genotype-confirmed SCT carriers while highlighting a critical methodological lesson — that registry-based ascertainment of rare genetic traits is prone to substantial selection bias — applicable well beyond hemoglobinopathies. The sapablursen PV data offer a promising non-cytoreductive mechanism for erythrocytosis control that could diversify the PV treatment armamentarium pending phase III confirmation. Finally, emerging data on skeletal fragility in hemophilia and VWD populations suggest that comprehensive bleeding disorder care programs should consider incorporating bone health surveillance, an area not currently standardized in most comprehensive care center protocols.

Beyond the studies detailed above, the following non-malignant hematology-relevant articles appeared in this week's CrossRef cache: a *Blood* study on tRNA m1A modification safeguarding fetal liver hematopoietic stem/progenitor cells via iron homeostasis regulation, of translational relevance to iron-restriction therapeutic strategies[^12]; a *Blood* study demonstrating durable BCL11A-targeted shmiR gene therapy in sickle cell disease with 58-month median follow-up showing stable HbF induction (71% F cells, 11.9 pg HbF/F cell) and sustained vaso-occlusive episode mitigation in 9 of 10 treated patients[^13]; and a *Blood* cost-effectiveness analysis favoring nonmyeloablative haploidentical transplant over gene therapy for sickle cell disease across 100% of Monte Carlo iterations, with gene therapy's maximum cost-effective threshold estimated at $1.4 million in the US and substantially lower ($4,200–$22,000) in India, Nigeria, and Tanzania[^14].
