## Summary

This week's literature yields the final analysis of a landmark phase 3 trial in congenital TTP, a practice-informing prospective study of sirolimus in refractory warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia and Evans syndrome, and several notable additions across sickle cell disease, hemophilia joint pathophysiology, thalassemia, and thrombosis. First, the final analysis of the phase 3 crossover trial of recombinant ADAMTS13 (rADAMTS13) in congenital TTP confirms durable clinical benefit over plasma-based therapy (PBT) across a full 18-month observation period: no participants experienced an acute TTP event during rADAMTS13 prophylaxis versus 1 during PBT (mean annualized event rate 0.04), the model-based mean annualized rate of subacute TTP events was 0.04 with rADAMTS13 versus 0.26 with PBT, and treatment-related adverse events occurred in only 4.3% of participants on rADAMTS13 compared with 45.8% on PBT[^1]. Second, a *Blood Advances* prospective single-center trial reports that sirolimus produced a 6-month overall response rate of 80.7% (81.0% in warm AIHA, 80.0% in Evans syndrome) and a complete response rate of 51.3% in 78 patients with relapsed/refractory disease, with responses sustained at 12 months (ORR 78.2%, CRR 50.0%) and a favorable safety profile, positioning sirolimus as a viable steroid-sparing option[^2]. Third, a *Blood* mechanistic study identifies a neutrophil-dependent, type-I interferon-driven pathway underlying hemophilic arthropathy, showing that therapeutic blockade of the type-I interferon receptor attenuates neutrophil extracellular trap shedding and reduces joint damage severity in F8-knockout mice, opening a new non-factor-replacement target for prevention of joint destruction in hemophilia A[^3]. Fourth, a *Blood Advances* review addresses conditioning strategy trade-offs in hemoglobinopathy gene therapy, contrasting busulfan's established efficacy against emerging reduced-toxicity alternatives including reduced-intensity melphalan and treosulfan, and highlighting investigational nongenotoxic antibody-based conditioning as a future direction[^4]. Fifth, a large BJH population study of over 2.6 million pregnancies in Quebec quantifies markedly elevated risk of severe maternal morbidity in sickle cell crises during pregnancy (RR 8.50, 95% CI 6.93–10.41), with especially pronounced risks for peripartum sepsis (RR 12.64) and acute renal failure (RR 32.37)[^5].

## I. Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP)

New data this week are limited to real-world dosing patterns rather than mechanistic advances. A *BJH* report on real-world romiplostim dosing, response, and home administration in pediatric ITP was published, though granular efficacy figures were not available in the abstract[^6]. Separately, a *Haematologica* case report describes avatrombopag as salvage therapy for refractory thrombocytopenia in THPO-mutated congenital amegakaryocytic thrombocytopenia following allogeneic HSCT, an off-label application illustrating expanding use of thrombopoietin receptor agonists beyond classic ITP[^7]. A relevant complementary contribution comes from a *Blood* "How I Treat" article on thrombopoietin receptor agonists (romiplostim, avatrombopag) in chemotherapy-induced thrombocytopenia, which — while centered on the oncology setting — offers practical guidance on dosing, titration, and monitoring directly transferable to ITP management given the shared drug class[^8]. No new mechanistic or trial data on daratumumab, BAFF-directed therapy, or B-cell resistance biology were identified this week, following last week's substantive updates in these areas.

## II. TTP / HUS / Thrombotic Microangiopathy

The final analysis of the pivotal phase 3 crossover trial of rADAMTS13 in congenital TTP represents this week's most significant coagulation-disorder development[^1]. In this 48-participant study (age range 3–68 years) randomized to two 6-month periods of rADAMTS13 (40 IU/kg) versus PBT prophylaxis followed by 6 months of open-label rADAMTS13, longer-term follow-up confirms the durability of findings from the earlier interim analysis: a roughly sixfold increase in ADAMTS13 activity and prolonged time with activity ≥10% were sustained with rADAMTS13, no ADAMTS13-neutralizing antibodies were detected throughout the extended observation, and thrombocytopenia — the most frequent TTP manifestation — occurred at a lower model-based mean annualized event rate with rADAMTS13 (0.91) than with PBT (1.62). Only one serious adverse event (pyrexia) was considered PBT-related, and none were attributed to rADAMTS13; patient-reported treatment satisfaction scores also favored rADAMTS13. These final data solidify the evidence base supporting rADAMTS13 as the preferred prophylactic strategy in congenital TTP over legacy plasma-based approaches.

## V. Hemophilia A & B (including gene therapy)

A *Blood* study provides the first mechanistic evidence directly linking innate immune activation to structural joint damage in hemophilia. Using Factor 8 total-knockout (F8TKO) mice alongside blood samples from hemophilia A patients with and without active joint bleeding, the authors demonstrate that acute joint bleeding provokes a type-I interferon response in neutrophils, driving systemic shedding of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs); therapeutic inhibition of the type-I interferon receptor attenuated NET shedding and reduced the severity of joint damage in the F8TKO model[^3]. This is the first report to implicate NETs in hemophilic arthropathy pathogenesis and suggests that adjunctive immunomodulatory therapy — independent of factor replacement — could help prevent progressive joint destruction, an unmet need even with modern factor VIII replacement or mimetic (emicizumab-class) therapy. On the gene therapy front, a *Blood Advances* review by Sharma and Corbacioglu examines conditioning regimen strategy for hemoglobinopathy gene therapy broadly, but its discussion of the narrow therapeutic window between niche clearance and toxicity, busulfan's persistent role as standard-of-care despite infertility and genotoxicity risk, and emerging reduced-intensity melphalan and treosulfan alternatives is directly relevant to hemophilia gene therapy vector delivery paradigms as the field matures toward broader clinical adoption[^4].

## VII. Thalassemia (alpha & beta; Taiwan-relevant)

Two substantive contributions advance thalassemia natural history and complication surveillance this week. A large multicenter BJH study of 994 adult transfusion-dependent thalassemia (TDT) patients from the Extension-Myocardial Iron Overload in Thalassemia network establishes pancreatic R2* on MRI as a robust biomarker for endocrine complications, finding that 60.4% of patients had at least one endocrine disorder and identifying a pancreatic R2* cutoff of >80.86 Hz as the optimal threshold for predicting endocrinopathy, with age, splenectomy, pancreatic R2*, and cardiac R2* emerging as independent predictors and a significant interaction between pancreatic and cardiac iron burden[^10]. Separately, a *Blood Advances* study of 82 adults with non-transfusion-dependent thalassemia (NTDT) first presenting to specialized care in adulthood documents substantial diagnostic and management delay: 71% of available liver MRIs showed hepatic iron >5 mg Fe/g dry weight, yet only 24.4% of those with elevated liver iron were on chelation at referral, and 49% of patients were recommended to start regular transfusions at their initial comprehensive consultation, predominantly for symptomatic anemia or ineffective-erythropoiesis complications[^11]. An accompanying *Blood Advances* editorial underscores the clinical burden imposed by delayed NTDT care recognition[^12]. Together these findings reinforce the case for systematic pancreatic iron surveillance and earlier specialist referral pathways — points of direct relevance to Taiwan's thalassemia registry and transfusion program given the high regional carrier burden.

## VIII. Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia (AIHA)

The prospective sirolimus trial in relapsed/refractory warm AIHA and Evans syndrome is this week's principal AIHA development[^2]. Among 78 consecutive adult patients (median age 52 years; 80.8% wAIHA, 19.2% Evans syndrome) treated with oral sirolimus (1–3 mg/day, target trough 4–15 ng/mL) for a median follow-up of 13.9 months, the 6-month overall response rate was 80.7% (81.0% wAIHA, 80.0% Evans syndrome) with a complete response rate of 51.3% (52.4% and 46.7%, respectively); median times to overall and complete response were 2.4 and 3.6 months. Responses were durable, with 12-month ORR of 78.2% and CRR of 50.0%, relapse rate of 12.7%, and treatment-emergent adverse events in 28.2% of patients, predominantly mild and reversible. No baseline clinical variable predicted 6-month response. These data support sirolimus as a mechanistically distinct, steroid-sparing therapeutic option, complementing the BAFF/BAFF-R pathway rationale for wAIHA articulated in last week's *AJH* review[^13]. Separately, a *Haematologica* commentary by Coscia and Arcaini discusses the overlapping biology of AIHA occurring in the context of chronic lymphocytic leukemia, framing the autoimmune-malignancy interface as clinically important though outside the primary non-malignant scope of this report[^14].

## XI. Thrombosis & Coagulation (VTE, APS, inherited thrombophilia)

Several papers this week refine risk phenotyping in thrombosis and antiphospholipid syndrome. A BJH cytokine-profiling study of 37 patients with primary antiphospholipid syndrome (PAPS) on stable anticoagulation identifies significant elevation of 14 of 20 measured inflammatory analytes compared with healthy controls (all p<0.05), including GM-CSF, IL-6, IL-10, IL-12p70, IL-13, and TNF-α; unsupervised clustering revealed three distinct inflammatory APS subgroups, with one cluster enriched for arterial thrombosis showing a more pronounced inflammatory signature including elevated IFN-α, and significantly higher concentrations of GM-CSF, IFN-α, IL-1α, IL-4, IL-6, IL-10, IL-17a, and TNF-α distinguishing arterial from venous thrombotic phenotypes[^15]. This supports a divergent thrombo-inflammatory mechanism for arterial versus venous events in PAPS and raises the prospect of precision immunomodulatory targeting. In the venous thromboembolism space, analysis of the RIETE registry (2011–2024) characterizes estrogen-associated VTE in 2786 women, finding these patients to be substantially younger (mean age 32±9 years) than men (39±8, p<0.001) or women with non-estrogen-associated VTE (38±8, p<0.001); no additional transient risk factor was present in 74% of cases, and recurrence after anticoagulation discontinuation was lowest in the estrogen-associated group (2.07 per 100 patient-years; 95% CI 1.57–2.68) compared with men (8.70; p<0.001) and other women (5.05; p<0.001), though bleeding during anticoagulation was more frequent in women overall (8.70 per 100 patient-years, 95% CI 7.09–10.6) than in men (5.46; 95% CI 4.64–6.39; p<0.05)[^16]. A *Haematologica* review provides a comprehensive mechanistic synthesis of procoagulant, phosphatidylserine-exposing platelets across 136 implicated genes and proteins, cataloguing their role in arterial thrombosis, stroke, venous thromboembolism, trauma, COVID-19, and immune-mediated thrombotic disorders, and proposing this platelet subpopulation as a tractable therapeutic target[^17]. Complementing this, a *Blood Advances* preclinical study demonstrates that the selective BTK degrader NX-5948 achieves potent, hook-effect-free degradation of platelet BTK, markedly reducing arterial thrombosis in mice without prolonging bleeding time, outperforming the comparator degrader NX-2127 in potency and kinetics — a promising selective antithrombotic strategy that spares hemostasis[^18].

## XIII. Taiwan Clinical Context

Taiwan's thalassemia carrier prevalence (approximately 4–5% for alpha-thalassemia and 1–2% for beta-thalassemia) makes this week's pancreatic iron overload findings from the Extension-Myocardial Iron Overload in Thalassemia network directly actionable[^10]; incorporation of pancreatic R2* thresholds (>80.86 Hz) into existing NHI-reimbursed cardiac/hepatic MRI surveillance protocols at major thalassemia centers (e.g., NTUH, CGMH, KMUH) could improve early detection of endocrinopathy in the transfusion-dependent population. The NTDT under-recognition data are similarly relevant, as Taiwan's aging NTDT cohort may harbor a comparable burden of undiagnosed iron overload and endocrine complications warranting proactive referral pathways[^11]. On the hemophilia front, the NET/type-I interferon arthropathy mechanism, while preclinical, raises a future consideration for joint-protective adjunctive strategies in Taiwanese hemophilia A patients maintained on NHI-reimbursed factor VIII prophylaxis or emicizumab, particularly those with breakthrough joint bleeds. The sirolimus data in wAIHA/Evans syndrome offer a low-cost, orally administered steroid-sparing alternative worth considering within Taiwan's NHI formulary framework for refractory autoimmune cytopenias, an area where access to newer biologic agents (e.g., BAFF-directed therapies) remains limited domestically. Finally, the rADAMTS13 final trial data support continued advocacy for expanding NHI coverage discussions for recombinant ADAMTS13 in the small but clinically vulnerable congenital TTP population in Taiwan, historically managed with plasma-based therapy alone.

## XIV. Key Takeaways

The maturation of the rADAMTS13 phase 3 program to final analysis cements a durable, well-tolerated alternative to plasma-based prophylaxis in congenital TTP, with essentially eliminated acute events and markedly reduced treatment-related toxicity. The sirolimus data in warm AIHA/Evans syndrome provide the most robust prospective efficacy signal yet reported for an mTOR-inhibitor strategy in these historically difficult-to-treat autoimmune cytopenias, complementing last week's BAFF/BAFF-R mechanistic rationale. The discovery of a neutrophil/type-I interferon/NET axis in hemophilic arthropathy opens a genuinely novel, non-factor-based therapeutic avenue that could eventually be layered onto standard factor replacement or emicizumab-based prophylaxis. In thalassemia, two independent cohorts converge on a consistent message: iron-related endocrine and hepatic complications remain under-recognized and under-treated, whether due to insufficiently granular biomarkers in transfusion-dependent disease or outright delayed diagnosis in non-transfusion-dependent disease, reinforcing the need for systematic, imaging-based surveillance programs. Finally, this week's thrombosis literature — spanning APS cytokine phenotyping, estrogen-associated VTE natural history, and a novel BTK-degrader antithrombotic strategy — collectively advances a precision-medicine framework for risk stratification and therapy selection in benign thrombotic disorders.
