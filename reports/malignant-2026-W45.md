## Summary

This week's *Blood*, *Haematologica*, *Blood Advances*, and *British Journal of Haematology* output is anchored by a mature UK NCRI AML18 report on quizartinib in older AML, a mechanistic resistance map for asciminib in CML, a large real-world comparison of CD19 CAR-T versus platinum-based salvage in primary mediastinal B-cell lymphoma, a nationwide KIT-TKI dataset in systemic mastocytosis with associated hematologic neoplasm, and a new conditioning platform (Evomela) for autologous transplant in myeloma. **(1)** In the UK NCRI AML18 trial, adding quizartinib to intensive chemotherapy in 463 older AML/high-risk MDS patients (median age 68) showed no overall survival benefit in the unselected population and increased non-relapse mortality, but in the pre-planned FLT3-mutated subgroup (n=116) quizartinib significantly improved OS (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.37–0.93, p=0.024), with greater benefit from the shorter maintenance schedule[^1]. **(2)** Mechanistic structural studies of BCR::ABL1 resistance to asciminib identify two distinct mutation classes — myristate-pocket "class I" mutations that reduce drug affinity up to 90-fold, and distal "class II" mutations (e.g., F359I, M244V) that retain wild-type binding affinity but increase intrinsic kinase autophosphorylation via disruption of a long-range allosteric network[^2]. **(3)** A 252-patient, 14-center US real-world comparison of second-line therapy for relapsed/refractory primary mediastinal B-cell lymphoma found CD19 CAR-T superior to platinum-based salvage plus autologous transplant across overall response (91.3% vs. 44.2%, p<0.001), complete response (65.2% vs. 14.7%, p<0.001), and PFS (median not reached vs. 3.5 months, HR 0.28, 95% CI 0.15–0.54, p=0.001), without a significant overall survival difference[^3]. **(4)** An international registry analysis of 201 patients with systemic mastocytosis with associated hematologic neoplasm (SM-AHN) found that KIT D816V-selective TKI exposure was associated with longer median overall survival (37.9 vs. 17.8 months, p<0.001) and a markedly lower 36-month cumulative incidence of AML transformation (5.9% vs. 27.1%, p<0.001)[^4]. **(5)** A phase 1/2 trial of Evomela (propylene glycol–free melphalan) for autologous transplant conditioning in 60 newly diagnosed myeloma patients found MRD-negative sCR/CR rates of 45% at day 90, with higher melphalan AUC strongly associated with MRD-negative response (posterior probability of benefit 0.99), and propensity-matched comparisons suggesting longer PFS versus standard melphalan 200 mg/m² (PBE 0.90)[^5].

## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

The AML18 report from the UK NCRI group provides long-awaited mature data (median follow-up 76 months) on FLT3-targeted maintenance in older patients, clarifying that quizartinib's benefit is confined to FLT3-mutated disease and that unselected use carries a non-relapse mortality penalty without survival gain[^1]. Complementing this, a companion Haematologica analysis proposes "salvage-free, transplantation-free survival" (STFS) as a new composite endpoint for AML trials: in the 1,966-patient DATAML registry cohort, five-year STFS was only 14.6% (95% CI 13.0–16.2, median 5.5 months) versus OS of 40.4% (95% CI 38.2–42.6, median 30.6 months), demonstrating how conventional endpoints can mask the burden of relapse and salvage therapy; in the APL validation cohort, STFS (83.6%) closely tracked OS (85.6%), and in the randomized ALFA-0701 trial gemtuzumab ozogamicin significantly improved STFS, supporting its adoption alongside OS/EFS in future trial design[^6]. Mechanistically, a genome-wide CRISPR screen in KMT2A-rearranged acute leukemia identifies the deubiquitinase USP22 as a novel regulator of MEIS1 protein stability, with USP22 depletion driving MEIS1 polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation, impairing leukemic growth and nominating USP22 as a therapeutic target complementary to menin inhibitors in this poor-prognosis subgroup[^7]. A large Swedish AML Registry study characterizing IDH1-, IDH2-, and TET2-mutant AML as distinct clinicobiological entities found TET2-mutant disease occurring in older patients with more pronounced thrombocytopenia, while IDH1/IDH2-mutant AML showed deeper neutropenia but more favorable overall survival after adjustment for clinical covariates, with 5-hydroxymethylcytosine profiling providing clearer subgroup separation than transcriptomics[^8]. In a translational immunology study, combined MEK/HDAC inhibition (trametinib plus quisinostat) potently suppressed NRAS/ASXL1-driven, MLL-rearranged, and NPM1-mutated AML in vitro and prolonged survival in NRAS/ASXL1-driven AML mice through T-cell-dependent mechanisms, with the combination reprogramming AML-associated T cells from an exhausted to an activated state and improving MHC-dependent leukemia killing in coculture assays[^9]. Separately, a British Journal of Haematology analysis of the EORTC-GIMEMA-German MDS Study Group AML21 randomized phase III trial (606 fit, older AML patients) found that fetal hemoglobin (HbF) increase from baseline to day 10 on decitabine was associated with higher odds of CR/CRi (OR 2.40, 95% CI 1.15–5.02, p=0.020), supporting HbF kinetics as an early, practical biomarker of hypomethylating-agent response[^10].

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

A Children's Oncology Group genomic analysis of the AALL1331 trial (blinatumomab vs. chemotherapy alone in relapsed B-ALL), using whole-genome and transcriptome sequencing on 288 patients, identified subtype-specific resistance alterations accounting for 30% of first relapses, including ETV6::RUNX1 fusion-allele gain or exon 5–exon 4 fusion transcripts conferring higher relapse risk than exon 5–exon 3 variants, low modal chromosome number (51–52) in hyperdiploid ALL combined with CREBBP mutations, IKZF1 alterations enriched in Philadelphia-like ALL, and NSD2 E1099K associated with relapse in TCF3::PBX1 disease, with TCF3::PBX1/NT5C2 co-occurrence linked to worse outcomes regardless of randomized arm[^11]. A companion Haematologica Spotlight Review addressed an important toxicity signal of blinatumomab consolidation: CD19+ B-cell depletion combined with dexamethasone premedication may compound immunosuppression and hypogammaglobulinemia, prompting the authors to call for immunoglobulin monitoring guidelines and dedicated studies to define optimal corticosteroid dosing during blinatumomab therapy[^12]. A PETHEMA-LAL-19 study applying combined optical genome mapping and targeted NGS to 100 adult BCR::ABL1-negative B-ALL patients improved subtype classification from 50% to 16% unclassified cases, with OGM-identified BCR::ABL1-like subtype (26% of cases) correlating with postinduction MRD positivity (72% vs. 34%, p=0.0011) and NGS-defined PAX5-altered subtypes correlating with MRD negativity (91% vs. 51%, p=0.012)[^13].

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

A detailed biophysical and structural study dissected the molecular basis of asciminib resistance, establishing that BCR::ABL1 mutations fall into two mechanistically distinct classes: class I mutations within the myristate pocket that sterically reduce asciminib affinity up to 90-fold, and class II mutations (including F359I and M244V) located up to 40 Å away in the kinase N-lobe, SH2-kinase linker, or SH3 domain that retain wild-type drug-binding affinity but instead increase intrinsic kinase activity and BCR::ABL1 autophosphorylation by disrupting an allosteric network linking the myristate and ATP-binding pockets[^2]. This dual-mechanism framework has direct implications for resistance monitoring and rational combination or sequencing strategies with ATP-competitive TKIs in asciminib-exposed patients.

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

A multicenter retrospective study of 511 patients with chronic myelomonocytic leukemia evaluated the Molecular International Prognostic Scoring System (IPSS-M) adapted to CMML, demonstrating effective risk stratification for both overall survival and cumulative incidence of AML progression, with a 4-tier model achieving a C-index of 0.678 for OS and 0.628 for AML progression; this adapted IPSS-M performed comparably to the CMML-specific international CPSS (iCPSS) and outperformed CPSS-Mol, with consistent discrimination across both myelodysplastic and myeloproliferative CMML subsets[^14]. The AML21 trial's decitabine-arm HbF biomarker findings (above) are also directly relevant to higher-risk MDS patients receiving hypomethylating agents, given the shared mechanistic rationale[^10].

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

An international registry study of 201 patients with systemic mastocytosis and associated hematologic neoplasm found that exposure to KIT D816V-selective TKIs was independently associated with longer overall survival (37.9 vs. 17.8 months, p<0.001) and a substantially reduced 36-month cumulative incidence of AML transformation (5.9% vs. 27.1%, p<0.001), providing the strongest evidence to date that targeted KIT inhibition alters the natural history of this high-risk SM subtype rather than merely palliating symptoms[^4]. A Haematologica commentary addressed causal inference challenges in a reported association between thrombopoietin receptor agonist overuse and myelofibrosis development in pediatric ITP, cautioning that observational correlation in this setting does not establish a causal mechanism and underscoring the need for prospective confirmation before altering TPO-RA prescribing practice in children[^15].

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

The 252-patient, 14-institution US real-world analysis of second-line therapy for relapsed/refractory primary mediastinal B-cell lymphoma represents the largest such comparison to date, confirming that CD19 CAR-T (n=26) outperforms platinum-based salvage with intent to proceed to autologous transplant (n=157) across every efficacy endpoint except overall survival: ORR 91.3% vs. 44.2% (p<0.001), CR 65.2% vs. 14.7% (p<0.001), and median PFS not reached vs. 3.5 months (HR 0.28, 95% CI 0.15–0.54, p=0.001)[^3]. This fills an important evidence gap, as PMBCL patients were underrepresented in the pivotal second-line CAR-T phase III trials. A pilot feasibility study of comprehensive whole-exome and transcriptome sequencing in 100 large B-cell lymphoma patients (71 analyzable) demonstrated a median turnaround time of 8 days (range 6–22 days, 73% delivered within 10 days), with molecular risk classification (dark-zone signature, lymphoma microenvironment classification) associated with high-risk early event-free survival status independent of cell-of-origin or LymphGen subtype, supporting real-world feasibility of integrated genomic profiling to guide subtype-driven therapy selection[^16].

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

A mechanistic study in mantle cell lymphoma identified loss of miR-146a-5p as contributing to ibrutinib resistance, adding to the growing catalogue of non-genetic resistance mechanisms relevant to BTK-inhibitor sequencing decisions in MCL[^17]. In follicular lymphoma, a retrospective analysis of high-tumor-burden patients treated with first-line immunochemotherapy found that baseline maximum SUV on PET did not predict survival outcomes, but did identify patients at elevated risk of histological transformation, with no transformation events occurring below the cohort median SUV — suggesting a role for baseline PET intensity in guiding biopsy and surveillance strategy rather than upfront therapeutic selection[^18].

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

A syngeneic mouse model study of ALK-positive anaplastic large cell lymphoma demonstrated that immune checkpoint inhibitor monotherapy (PD-L1 blockade) induced complete remissions in approximately 50% of treated animals, with mechanism-of-action studies showing reversal of regulatory T-cell-mediated immunosuppression and expansion of circulating effector CD8+ T lymphocytes; critically, CD4+ T cells were shown to be indispensable for both spontaneous immune surveillance and sustained immunotherapy-induced antitumor responses, while nonresponder animals exhibited CD4+ T-cell exhaustion with a T-helper-22-like transcriptomic signature, offering preclinical rationale for ICI use in ALK+ ALCL where clinical data remain limited to case reports[^19].

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

Building on its MRD theme, a large GEM2012MENOS65/GEM2014MAIN and GEM2017FIT dataset analyzed longitudinal MRD dynamics (3,610 assessments) across 539 newly diagnosed myeloma patients with three or more flow cytometry MRD assessments, identifying five distinct subgroups with markedly different survival: patients with late-sustained or early-sustained MRD response had excellent outcomes, whereas those with volatile, primary-resistant, or resurgent MRD resistance patterns had dismal survival; MRD dynamics outperformed both transplant eligibility and the Revised ISS, and multiomic characterization of MRD-resistant mouse models showed that anti-BCMA CAR-T infusion at the point of MRD resistance prolonged survival compared with treatment delayed until frank relapse, directly supporting an MRD-triggered interception strategy[^20]. The Evomela phase 1/2 trial provides a practical advance in ASCT conditioning: among 60 patients randomized to short (30–60 min) versus long (8–9 hour) infusion schedules at 200 or 225 mg/m², no grade ≥4 nonhematologic toxicities or day-100 nonrelapse mortality occurred, MRD-negative sCR/CR at day 90 was achieved in 45% overall (43% short-infusion, 47% long-infusion), and higher melphalan AUC was strongly linked to MRD-negative response (PBE 0.99) independent of toxicity, with propensity-matched analysis suggesting a PFS advantage over standard melphalan 200 mg/m² (PBE 0.90)[^5]. A Chinese cohort study of 260 newly diagnosed myeloma patients constructed an immune-risk nomogram integrating CD3+CD8+ T-cell absolute count and IL-10 with high-risk cytogenetics, serum calcium, and LDH, achieving a cross-validated 3-year time-dependent AUC of 0.825 for overall survival and further stratifying outcomes among patients on anti-CD38 antibody-containing regimens, pending external multicenter validation[^21]. On bone disease biology, a pilot longitudinal imaging and biopsy study found that myeloma bone disease shows partial repair potential after first-line therapy, with increased trabecular bone volume (p=0.003) and osteoblast number (p<0.05) at 3 months post-induction/autograft, though osteocyte canalicular connectivity was significantly reduced (p<0.01), suggesting that while osteolytic lesions can partially heal, impaired lacunar network connectivity may limit full bone restoration and underscoring the need for bone-anabolic strategies beyond current anti-resorptive agents[^22]. Two Haematologica editorials this week address evolving myeloma CAR-T sequencing and safety themes: one discusses re-treatment with BCMA-directed CAR-T following prior GPRC5D CAR-T exposure as salvage strategy, and another highlights the low but clinically significant incidence of parkinsonism as a delayed BCMA CAR-T toxicity requiring long-term neurological surveillance[^23],[^24].

## XII. Transplant & Cellular Therapy (CAR-T / HSCT / GVHD)

A rare but serious genomic safety signal continues to be characterized: a *Blood* review of CAR transgenic T-cell lymphoproliferative neoplasms proposes a multistep pathogenic model involving pre-existing clonal fitness, vector integration site, construct biology, and postinfusion selective pressure, and offers practical diagnostic criteria together with a proportionate framework for genomic surveillance after CAR-T therapy, rather than blanket screening of all recipients[^25]. On GVHD biology, a murine and clinical translational study of haploidentical PTCy-based transplantation found that early calcineurin inhibitor administration (cyclosporine or tacrolimus before PTCy) preserved less-exhausted, pathogenic effector-like CD8+ Tex-int and CD4+ cytotoxic T-cell populations that predicted subsequent chronic GVHD, whereas delaying tacrolimus until day 5 post-PTCy significantly reduced expansion of these effector-like subsets — providing a mechanistic rationale for CNI-timing optimization to preserve PTCy's tolerogenic effect[^26]. A pediatric phase 1/2 study of the human milk oligosaccharide 2′-fucosyllactose (2′-FL) given peritransplant in 70 allo-HSCT recipients found the agent safe and associated with preserved Shannon diversity of the gut microbiome (versus significant decline in 29 contemporary controls, p=0.04), higher abundance of Bifidobacteria and Blautia, and clinically meaningful reductions in grade II–IV acute GI GVHD (0% vs. 17%, p=0.009) and adenovirus reactivation (0% vs. 17%, p=0.009)[^27]. In poor graft function after allo-HSCT, a two-center retrospective study of 116 patients (87% haploidentical) receiving CD34+-selected stem cell boost reported an overall response rate of 67.24% (CR 62.93%, PR 4.31%), with active infection at the time of boost as the sole independent predictor of lower response (OR 3.79, 95% CI 1.58–9.05, p=0.003), and responders showing markedly better overall survival than nonresponders (81.65% vs. 11.67%, p<0.001)[^28]. A nationwide/European DDHN theme from last week is extended by new data this cycle: a propylene glycol-free, low-cost matched sibling BMT protocol for transfusion-dependent thalassemia across 176 pediatric patients in Asia and the Middle East achieved 97.3% engraftment, 93.4% thalassemia-free survival, and 96% overall survival at median 11.3-month follow-up, with low rates of moderate-to-severe acute GVHD (3.9%), demonstrating that a simplified, WHO Essential Medicines-based regimen (fludarabine, dexamethasone, busulfan, cyclophosphamide) can achieve excellent outcomes even in newly established transplant centers[^29].

## XIV. Taiwan Clinical Context

The AML18 FLT3-subgroup finding (HR 0.59 for OS with quizartinib maintenance) is directly relevant to Taiwan practice, where quizartinib is NHI-reimbursed for FLT3-ITD-positive relapsed/refractory AML but maintenance use after intensive chemotherapy in older patients is not yet uniformly funded; centers such as NTUH and CGMH should note that benefit appears confined to FLT3-mutated disease and that unselected use carries excess non-relapse mortality, reinforcing the importance of FLT3 testing at diagnosis and at relapse before considering maintenance strategies[^1]. The asciminib resistance-mechanism data are pertinent given asciminib's growing NHI-approved role in CML patients who are intolerant of or resistant to at least two prior TKIs in Taiwan; the class I/class II mutation framework may inform BCR::ABL1 kinase domain sequencing panels increasingly used at VGH and CMUH to guide TKI sequencing after asciminib failure[^2]. The CD19 CAR-T data in primary mediastinal B-cell lymphoma are notable for Taiwan, where PMBCL is relatively uncommon but axicabtagene ciloleucel and other CD19 CAR-T products are NHI-conditionally reimbursed for relapsed/refractory LBCL after two prior lines; this new real-world comparator dataset supports earlier CAR-T consideration over platinum-salvage/ASCT pathways in PMBCL specifically, pending local reimbursement criteria review[^3]. Finally, the low-cost matched sibling BMT protocol for transfusion-dependent thalassemia, achieving 96% OS using WHO Essential Medicines-listed agents, is of direct relevance to Taiwan's thalassemia minor/major populations and may offer a reference protocol for centers managing patients who face cost barriers to standard busulfan/cyclophosphamide-based conditioning regimens[^29].

## XV. Key Takeaways

The FLT3-selective benefit of quizartinib maintenance in AML18 reinforces that molecularly unselected use of targeted maintenance therapy in older AML can introduce toxicity without survival gain, strengthening the case for routine FLT3 testing before maintenance decisions[^1]. The two-class mechanistic model of asciminib resistance clarifies why some BCR::ABL1 mutations evade detection by binding-affinity assays alone, with implications for how resistance surveillance panels should be designed[^2]. In lymphoma, the PMBCL CAR-T data and the LBCL molecular-profiling feasibility study both point toward earlier and more systematic incorporation of CAR-T and genomic stratification into frontline relapse management algorithms[^3],[^16]. In myeloma, convergent MRD-dynamics and Evomela-dosing data this week strengthen the rationale for AUC-guided conditioning and MRD-triggered immunotherapeutic interception as two complementary strategies to improve depth and durability of response[^5],[^20]. Finally, the SM-AHN and CMML prognostic model advances underscore the continued value of molecularly refined risk stratification across myeloid neoplasms outside core AML/MDS categories[^4],[^14].
