## Summary

This cycle brings a dense *Blood* output spanning NPM1-mutated AML clonal architecture, a landmark FDA approval editorial on regulatory T-cell (Treg) therapy for chronic GVHD prevention, mature CLL/CLL13 biology on telomere length, long-term GLOW follow-up in frontline CLL, and new DLBCL combination data pairing bispecific antibodies with pola-R-CHP. **(1)** A PETHEMA analysis of 688 ## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

This week's AML literature centers heavily on NPM1-mutated disease biology and refines last cycle's BCR::ABL1+ AML intensification data with complementary clonal-architecture findings rather than overlapping endpoints. The PETHEMA group's analysis of 688 intensively treated NPM1-mutated AML patients found co-mutations in 97% of cases (median 3 per patient), dominated by DNMT3A (49%), FLT3-ITD (46%), TET2 (22%), and IDH2 (20%); beyond the VAF-based NPM1high/NPM1low stratification (HR 1.46 for OS, p=0.037; HR 1.40 for RFS, p=0.036), gene-specific VAF analysis showed that high DNMT3A, FLT3-OTHER (non-ITD/TKD), KRAS, and PTPN11 burdens were adverse while high IDH2 VAF was protective, and clonal reconstruction revealed predominantly linear evolutionary trajectories (84.3%) with mutation ordering placing NPM1 after epigenetic/splicing lesions but before signaling/transcription-factor mutations[^1]. Two accompanying *Blood* editorials frame these findings: one asks whether NPM1 VAF size matters for risk stratification[^6], and a second emphasizes that combinatorial co-mutation profiling—rather than NPM1 status alone—is "better together" for prognostication[^7]. A related mechanistic study identifies FGF18 as a stromal fibroblast-derived cytokine that accelerates AML progression via FGFR3-AKT-mTOR signaling and IL-6-driven feed-forward loops suppressing CD8+ T-cell function; an FGF18-neutralizing antibody restored antileukemic immunity and synergized with anti-PD-1 therapy in vivo, with elevated FGF18 correlating with poor AML prognosis clinically[^8]. In the IDH1/DNMT3A space, a Hi-C-based 3D genome study found IDH1-mutant AML cells exhibit loop-mediated IGF1R upregulation conferring sensitivity to the IGF1R inhibitor BMS-754807 (alone or combined with ivosidenib), while DNMT3A-mutant AML showed selective sensitivity to the p38/MAPK inhibitor ralimetinib[^9]. A multinational PETHEMA study of 2,623 intensively treated AML patients establishes measurable residual disease (MRD) as an early endpoint surrogate for treatment efficacy, reinforcing the field's shift toward MRD-anchored trial design[^10]. Separately, a real-world PETHEMA registry comparison of venetoclax-based therapy versus hypomethylating agents alone in unfit newly diagnosed AML adds practice-relevant comparative data, though granular outcome figures were not available in this abstract[^11]. Mechanistically, a phenotypic drug screen identified the CDK inhibitors dinaciclib and flavopiridol as synergistic partners for the RNA Pol I/TOP2A inhibitor CX-5461 in AML, with dinaciclib improving survival in murine AML models and reducing colony formation in primary human samples via combinatorial activation of the nucleolar surveillance pathway[^12].

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

New genomic data from the ECOG-ACRIN E1910 study provide the most granular analysis yet of blinatumomab response determinants in adult BCR::ABL1-negative B-ALL, building on but not duplicating last cycle's venetoclax-pediatric-inspired-regimen report. Among 569 adults, investigators identified 23 molecular subtypes and 267 putative driver genes, including a novel subtype defined by CEBPA overexpression or CEBPB elevation via enhancer hijacking. MRD-negativity after induction/intensification was more common in PAX5alt, TCF3::PBX1, and ZNF384-R subtypes and less common in BCR::ABL1-like and KMT2A-rearranged disease; the addition of blinatumomab to chemotherapy was associated with improved relapse-free and overall survival in several subtypes including hyperdiploid, PAX5alt, PAX5 P80R, BCR::ABL1-like JAK-STAT, and KMT2A-R B-ALL, though the authors caution that small subtype sample sizes require confirmation. TP53 alterations and CHIP-associated mutations were found in 15.3% and 9.8% of patients respectively, both associated with older age and inferior chemotherapy outcomes[^13]. A new modified Delphi consensus from Muffly, Frey, Roloff and colleagues addresses CAR T-cell administration logistics in adult ALL, covering patient selection, bridging strategy, and toxicity management algorithms[^14]. Mechanistic work in T-ALL demonstrates that mutant IL7R and RASGRP1 overexpression cooperate to drive aggressive, polyclonal T-ALL in murine models, with the resulting leukemia susceptible to combined ruxolitinib (JAK inhibition) and trametinib (MEK inhibition), achieving near-elimination of leukemic burden in combination[^15]. A complementary study shows leukemia-associated myeloid cells support both ETP-like and non-ETP-like T-ALL subtypes via distinct signaling (IL6ST/STAT3 for ETP-like; growth factor receptor signaling for non-ETP-like) that converge on AKT activation and oxidative phosphorylation, and combining myeloid depletion with OxPhos inhibition reduced leukemic survival beyond either approach alone in mouse and patient samples[^16].

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

A Japanese cohort study offers confirmatory and novel findings on CML in a global context, though specific comparative outcome metrics were not detailed in the available abstract[^17]. Two case reports describe an imatinib-sensitive PRKG2::PDGFRA-positive chronic myeloid neoplasm with basophilia, expanding the differential for rare fusion-driven myeloid neoplasms responsive to tyrosine kinase inhibition[^18]. _No major phase III CML trial readouts this week._

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

CLL biology and long-term trial follow-up dominate this week's Blood/Leukemia output. The GLOW study's 67-month update confirms durability of fixed-duration ibrutinib-venetoclax in older/comorbid treatment-naïve CLL: 66-month OS was 79.0% vs. 60.8% (HR 0.46) and PFS 51.7% vs. 18.1% (HR 0.27) favoring the combination over chlorambucil-obinutuzumab, with a 77.4% reduction in need for second-line therapy and grade 3/4 TEAE-free PFS extended by 21.4 months (51.6 vs. 30.2 months)[^3]. From the GAIA/CLL13 and CLL2-GIVe trials, telomere length analysis in 917 and 41 samples respectively identified a prognostic cutoff of 4.17 kb; short telomeres correlated with adverse features (advanced Binet stage, unmutated IGHV, del(11q), NOTCH1/SF3B1/XPO1/RPS15/EGR2/NFKBIE mutations, karyotypic complexity, all p<0.01) and independently predicted shorter PFS across treatment arms within GAIA/CLL13 (all p<0.01) and in CLL2-GIVe (p=0.04)[^19]. A "How I Treat"-style review on TP53 loss-of-function CLL by Tam, Seymour, and Blombery reaffirms continuous covalent BTK inhibitor therapy as the de-facto frontline standard in this subgroup, citing approximately 7-year median PFS, while noting that fixed-duration venetoclax-based regimens show slower time to undetectable MRD and reduced PFS specifically in TP53-LoF patients, with retreatment data in this subset still lacking granularity[^20]. A phase 1b/2 study of the ROR1-targeting antibody zilovertamab plus ibrutinib in relapsed/refractory CLL (randomized phase 2b: n=18 combination vs. n=10 ibrutinib alone) showed similar CR rates, overall response, and PFS between arms overall, but a post hoc subset of 10 patients with del(17p) showed prolonged disease control relative to historical BTK inhibitor experience—a hypothesis-generating signal requiring prospective confirmation[^21]. Mechanistically, TLR9-dependent migratory phenotyping in 102 patient samples identified distinct "Responder" and "Reverse-Responder" signaling states under BTK inhibition, with TLR9-induced migration persisting despite BTKi in Responders via a BTK-independent pathway, and BTKi-resistant patients showing higher TLR9 expression at relapse[^22]. Notably, a case report documents CLL development from shared hematopoietic progenitor cells in two siblings following allogeneic transplantation between them, with shared MED12 and NOTCH1 mutations implicating NOTCH pathway involvement in disease predisposition despite distinct IG gene rearrangements in the resulting clones[^23].

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

A validation study spanning the International Consortium for MDS (icMDS) VALIDATE registry (n=1,047) and the randomized phase III PANTHER trial (azacitidine ± pevonedistat, n=221) confirms that composite CR per the 2023 IWG response criteria independently predicts improved overall survival in higher-risk MDS across both cohorts (VALIDATE multivariable HR 0.65, 95% CI 0.55–0.76)[^5]. On the transplant front, a large EBMT analysis of 313 adults undergoing second allogeneic stem cell transplant (alloSCT2) for MDS relapse (2012–2023) reports 2-year OS/PFS/relapse incidence/NRM of 45%/33%/37%/30%, with outcomes improving over time (2018–2023 2-year OS/PFS 51%/39%); critically, donor change (DoC) at alloSCT2—used in 71% of cases—was independently associated with reduced relapse risk (HR 0.56, 95% CI 0.36–0.89), a finding not previously observed after second transplant for acute leukemia, while adverse cytogenetics increased relapse risk (HR 1.93, 95% CI 1.18–3.17)[^24]. A spatial transcriptomics correction/update further characterizes bone marrow architecture signatures of disease activity and therapeutic response in myelodysplastic neoplasms[^25].

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

Long-term natural history data for CALR-mutated MPN provide important risk-stratification refinement this week. Among 149 CALR-mutated MPN patients, CALR-mutated ET (n=106) progressed to myelofibrosis at a median of 20.4 years with 20-year OS of 81%; among 83 patients with myelofibrosis, median leukemia-free survival was 12.9 years and OS 13.9 years, while median survival after leukemic transformation was only 6.8 months. High molecular risk (HMR) mutations (ASXL1, EZH2, SRSF2, U2AF1 Q157, IDH1/2, TP53, SETBP1, KRAS, NRAS, RUNX1) were associated with significantly higher leukemic transformation and inferior LFS/OS (HR 3.82, 95% CI 1.30–11.18), with TP53, KRAS, and RUNX1 independently adverse beyond MIPSS70-plus v2.0 (HR 2.87, 3.80, and 5.61 respectively)[^26]. An accompanying commentary underscores that CALR-mutated MPN outcomes are driven by acquired somatic mutations rather than the CALR driver itself, supporting serial molecular profiling for risk stratification and early transplant consideration in HMR-positive patients[^27]. A review of PIM1 kinase biology in MPN describes its role in JAK2-inhibitor persistence, noting that PIM1 inhibition restores ruxolitinib sensitivity in JAK2-mutant cells and attenuates myelofibrosis phenotype in preclinical models, with PIM kinase inhibitors now in clinical evaluation for myelofibrosis[^28]. A novel knock-in mouse model expressing oncogenic Kit D814V in the hematopoietic stem cell compartment recapitulates systemic mastocytosis with associated hematologic neoplasm (SM-AHN), showing proliferative clonal advantage correctable with KIT-targeting tyrosine kinase inhibition in competitive transplantation experiments[^29]. Separately, the MDM2 degrader KT-253 is reported to eliminate malignant myelofibrosis stem/progenitor cells, though detailed efficacy figures were not available in the abstract[^30].

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

Two notable DLBCL combination studies expand the frontline bispecific-antibody landscape this week. A phase II trial combining glofitamab with polatuzumab-R-CHP (two cycles pola-R-CHP, glofitamab added cycles 3–6, then glofitamab monotherapy cycles 7–8) in 41 newly diagnosed DLBCL patients with IPI ≥2 achieved a best CR rate of 95% (39/41, 95% CI 83–99); with median follow-up of 23.9 months, median PFS was not reached and 24-month PFS was 95% (95% CI 89–100). CRS occurred in only 10% of patients (all grade 1) with no ICANS reported, supporting the delayed-incorporation schedule as a strategy to mitigate bispecific-related toxicity[^4]. Separately, a phase I/II trial of subcutaneous mosunetuzumab plus polatuzumab vedotin as a chemotherapy-free frontline option for older unfit/frail DLBCL patients (n=101 full-dose, median age 81, 58% unfit/41% frail) reported ORR of 61.4% (95% CI 51.1–70.9) and CR rate of 56.4% at end of treatment (best ORR/CR 80.2%/66.3% on therapy); with 36.5-month median follow-up, estimated 2-year PFS and OS were 52.2% and 62.4% respectively, and CRS occurred in 30.6% (predominantly grade 1), though infection-related mortality was noted, concentrated during the COVID-19 pandemic and among frail patients[^31]. A Nordic phase II trial establishes circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) duplex sequencing as a powerful molecular surveillance tool in high-risk large B-cell lymphoma: early ctDNA clearance after two immunochemotherapy cycles had 91% negative predictive value for progression, and end-of-therapy MRD status was strongly prognostic (2-year PFS 94.3% vs. 18.2% for MRD-negative vs. positive, HR 18.7), with ctDNA detectable a median of 6 months before systemic clinical relapse[^32]. A SENIOR-IPI prognostic index derived from 438 LBCL patients over age 80 treated with anti-CD20-miniCHOP outperformed IPI, aaIPI, and NCCN-IPI for both PFS (C-index 0.66 vs. 0.61/0.64/0.61) and OS (C-index 0.67 vs. 0.59/0.62/0.61) in a 195-patient validation cohort, incorporating aaIPI predictor count, LDH >3× ULN, albumin <35 g/L, and years over 80[^33].

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

Real-world data on nivolumab-AVD in older adults (≥60 years) with advanced classic Hodgkin lymphoma, drawn from a 20-center retrospective cohort of 327 patients (81 aged ≥60), show high efficacy translating from trial to practice: interim ORR/CRR were 100%/81.6% and end-of-treatment rates 88%/82.6%, with 1-year PFS and OS of 91.5% and 98.6% respectively at 12.6-month median follow-up, despite grade ≥3 AEs in 67% and grade ≥3 neutropenia in 52% of older patients[^34]. A companion real-world comparison across 17 US centers (646 patients, 1:1 propensity-matched) found BV-AVD and N-AVD produced comparable 2-year OS (97% vs. 100%, p=0.08) and PFS (84% vs. 88%, p=0.06), though N-AVD carried higher rates of neutropenia (any grade 77.2% vs. 43.9%, p<0.0001) and infections (31.9% vs. 19.7%, p=0.004), while BV-AVD caused significantly more neuropathy (57.2% vs. 20.6%, p<0.0001)[^35]. In T-cell lymphoma biology, whole-exome sequencing of ALK-positive anaplastic large cell lymphoma identified a high frequency of cohesin complex mutations, predominantly STAG2 variants (6/16 patients) predicted to impair function; functional modeling showed STAG2 loss accelerates NPM1::ALK-driven oncogenesis by enhancing early proliferation, reducing oncogene-induced senescence, and promoting cell adhesion/migration via YAP1 activation and PD-L1 upregulation[^36].

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

This week's myeloma literature is dominated by single-cell immune microenvironment characterization rather than new trial readouts, complementing but not overlapping last cycle's PKMYT1/TP53 and SeaLAND maintenance findings. A large single-cell RNA/TCR/BCR-seq study of 235 patients spanning the MM disease spectrum identified five reproducible tumor microenvironment "ecotypes" with distinct cell-cell communication networks and immunotherapy-response associations; an ecotype marked by bone marrow-resident populations and limited immune infiltration was linked to tumor expansion and inferior outcomes, while T-cell functional-state ecotypes tracked with immunotherapy response and survival[^37]. Two companion papers examine ancestry-associated immune remodeling in myeloma: one analysis of 320 patients (64 with ≥50% African genetic similarity) found AFR-High status associated with enrichment of late T-effector cells, expansion of cytotoxic adaptive NK cells, and a shift from innate inflammatory toward adaptive cytotoxic/immunoregulatory monocyte programs[^38], while a second mass cytometry/scRNA-seq study of 128 bone marrow aspirates (validated in an independent 286-patient MMRF cohort) found self-identified Black patients had higher terminally differentiated T-cell proportions and lower monocytes, with overexpressed genes enriched for E2F targets and G2-M checkpoints and reduced genes enriched for TNF-α/NF-κB and interferon pathways[^39]. On the maintenance-therapy front, updated MAIA frailty subgroup analysis (64.5-month median follow-up) confirms sustained OS benefit of daratumumab-lenalidomide-dexamethasone (D-Rd) over Rd in non-frail transplant-ineligible NDMM (median NR vs. 69.8 months, HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.37–0.76, p=0.0004), with a favorable but not statistically significant HR point estimate in frail patients (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.58–1.06, p=0.1128)[^40]. An MRD-guided, fixed-duration therapy approach for standard-risk newly diagnosed myeloma is also reported, though detailed efficacy data were not available in the abstract[^41]. Separately, a registry study identifies a high incidence of venous thromboembolism following autologous stem cell transplantation in multiple myeloma, flagging an underappreciated post-transplant complication warranting closer surveillance[^42].

## XII. Transplant & Cellular Therapy (CAR-T / HSCT / GVHD)

The standout development this cycle is the FDA approval of a centrally manufactured regulatory T-cell (Treg) therapy for prevention of chronic GVHD, grounded in the phase 3 Precision-T trial. A *Blood* editorial characterizes this as the first randomized, multicenter demonstration that a defined allograft markedly reduces moderate-to-severe chronic GVHD and non-relapse mortality without loss of graft-versus-leukemia effect or increased graft failure/infection risk, with the engineered product apparently steering immune reconstitution toward a regulated, tolerance-associated state[^43]. In parallel, a *Blood* 80th-anniversary editorial from Deputy Editor Helen Heslop traces the evolution of commercial CAR T-cell therapy from 2017 approvals to 2026 community-center administration, highlighting future priorities of relapse reduction and access expansion via shorter manufacturing times or in vivo delivery[^44]. Mechanistically, a study identifies KPNA2-mediated MYC nuclear import as a driver of CAR-T cell memory formation and antitumor potency; KPNA2 overexpression in CAR-T cells augmented memory-associated transcriptional programs and cytotoxicity without inducing transformation risk, and a small-molecule (TMS) that potentiates the KPNA2-MYC interaction enhanced antitumor potency in preclinical models—offering a pharmacological strategy to improve CAR-T persistence[^45]. In mantle cell lymphoma, ROR1 upregulation was identified following CD19-CAR T-cell relapse, nominating ROR1-directed bispecific modalities as a targetable vulnerability in this relapse setting[^46]. A retrospective analysis of 113 CAR-T-treated patients found that clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP, detected in 30.8% of myeloma, 13.7% of LBCL, and 28.6% of follicular/mantle cell lymphoma patients) did not influence OS, PFS, or CRS/ICANS incidence overall, but was associated with increased IEC-HS and higher ferritin in myeloma patients, while pre-treatment neutropenia was associated with severe ICAHT[^47]. On the haploidentical HSCT front, a prospective nonrandomized study of 276 patients comparing higher-dose anti-CD25 GVHD prophylaxis (methotrexate-free) versus conventional lower-dose anti-CD25 plus methotrexate found the higher-dose strategy reduced 100-day grade III–IV acute GVHD (7.9% vs. 17.5%, adjusted sHR 0.39, 95% CI 0.18–0.88, p=0.022) and improved GVHD-free, relapse-free survival (adjusted HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.43–0.83, p=0.002), without significant differences in chronic GVHD, OS, or relapse[^48]. Finally, a Mayo Clinic experience in blastic plasmacytoid dendritic cell neoplasm reports that allogeneic HSCT—rather than choice of front-line induction therapy—is the key driver of long-term survival[^49].

## XIV. Taiwan Clinical Context

The FDA approval of centrally manufactured Treg cell therapy for chronic GVHD prevention is of direct relevance to Taiwan's transplant centers (NTUH, CGMH, VGH, CMUH), which perform substantial haploidentical and matched-unrelated-donor HSCT volumes; NHI reimbursement pathways for advanced cellular therapies in the GVHD-prophylaxis space will need evaluation given the current absence of local Treg-manufacturing infrastructure, and post-transplant VTE surveillance data from this week's myeloma registry study[^42] should prompt review of local thromboprophylaxis protocols after autologous transplant. The CD25-based haploidentical GVHD prophylaxis comparison[^48], conducted in a Chinese cohort, is methodologically relevant given Taiwan's similarly high reliance on haploidentical donors when matched siblings or unrelated donors are unavailable. In lymphoma, the glofitamab/pola-R-CHP and mosunetuzumab/polatuzumab-vedotin frontline DLBCL regimens represent next-generation chemotherapy-light approaches that may eventually extend options for elderly Taiwanese patients currently managed with R-CHOP or R-mini-CHOP, pending bispecific antibody reimbursement expansion under NHI (currently glofitamab and mosunetuzumab retain restricted access in relapsed/refractory settings only). For CLL, the continued dominance of continuous BTK inhibitor therapy in TP53-aberrant disease[^20] aligns with current Taiwan practice given ibrutinib/acalabrutinib NHI coverage, though fixed-duration venetoclax-obinutuzumab remains an alternative for standard-risk patients. NK/T-cell lymphoma, disproportionately prevalent in Taiwan and East Asia generally, had no new data this cycle but remains a standing priority area for local trial development.

## XV. Key Takeaways

The week's strongest translational signal is the FDA approval of Treg cell therapy for chronic GVHD prevention, representing a genuine paradigm shift in post-transplant immune modulation backed by randomized phase 3 data showing benefit without loss of graft-versus-leukemia effect. In CLL, GLOW's 67-month data solidify fixed-duration ibrutinib-venetoclax as a durable frontline option with a now-quantified toxicity-free survival advantage, while the TP53-LoF review reinforces that continuous BTK inhibition remains preferred in this high-risk subgroup pending more mature fixed-duration retreatment data. In DLBCL, the glofitamab-pola-R-CHP combination's 95% CR rate with minimal CRS/no ICANS suggests bispecific antibodies can be safely integrated into frontline high-risk regimens with careful scheduling. NPM1-mutated AML prognostication is being meaningfully refined beyond binary mutation status toward quantitative VAF and clonal-order models, which may eventually inform risk-adapted consolidation decisions. Finally, the consistent finding across three independent myeloma single-cell studies that ancestry-associated and ecotype-based immune remodeling shapes outcomes underscores the need for biology-informed, rather than purely genomic, risk stratification in myeloma going forward.
