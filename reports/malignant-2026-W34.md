## Summary

This week's literature is dominated by *Blood* and *Blood Advances* end-of-summer releases with several practice-relevant updates. **(1)** The 3-year follow-up of the pivotal MonumenTAL-1 study of talquetamab in relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma, published in *Blood*, confirms durable responses across three treatment cohorts, with overall response rates of 67–74%, median overall survival reaching 34.0 months, not reached, and 28.3 months across the three cohorts, and a long-term safety profile with continued low rates of high-grade infection relative to BCMA bispecifics[^1]. **(2)** A phase 2 trial of anti-BCMA/GPRC5D bispecific CAR T cells for relapsed/refractory myeloma with extraosseous extramedullary disease demonstrates a 97% overall response rate with measurable residual disease negativity and a 43% stringent complete response rate in a historically treatment-refractory population, though median PFS remains modest at 5.8 months[^2]. **(3)** The expanded MRD analysis of the phase 3 CEPHEUS trial in transplant-ineligible/deferred newly diagnosed myeloma, published in *Blood Advances*, confirms that daratumumab-VRd achieves significantly higher sustained MRD-negativity rates than VRd alone (10⁻⁵: 49.2% vs 27.3%; 10⁻⁶: 34.0% vs 16.2%; both p<0.0001) at nearly 5-year follow-up, reinforcing D-VRd as standard of care in this population[^3]. **(4)** BMT CTN 1702, a large multicenter prospective biologic assignment trial comparing donor sources for allogeneic HCT, finds that haploidentical and mismatched unrelated donor transplants achieve survival outcomes statistically comparable to matched unrelated donor transplant (HR 1.08 and 1.18 respectively, not significant), while umbilical cord blood is associated with markedly inferior survival (HR 2.65; p<0.001), providing practical guidance for donor selection when a matched unrelated donor is not readily available[^4]. **(5)** A phase 2 study of mosunetuzumab with response-adapted polatuzumab vedotin/obinutuzumab in untreated indolent B-cell lymphoma, published in the *Journal of Clinical Oncology*, achieves a 100% overall response rate and 86% complete response rate with a chemotherapy-free approach, and 2-year PFS of 89% (95% CI, 80–100), signaling a potential paradigm shift for frontline follicular and marginal zone lymphoma therapy[^5].

## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

Several complementary papers this week refine molecular MRD methodology and disease biology in AML. The ELN-DAVID consortium published formal recommendations in *Blood* for NGS-based *FLT3*-ITD MRD testing in patients in AML remission, formalizing laboratory and clinical standards now incorporated into updated standard-of-care guidelines[^6]. This technical guidance is complemented by a companion editorial emphasizing that FLT3-ITD MRD assessment is now an established fixture of post-remission management[^7]. A related retrospective multicenter study of 219 patients with *FLT3*-ITD-mutated AML in *Blood Advances* demonstrates that pretransplant MRD positivity by PCR-NGS (≥0.001%) is associated with markedly inferior 2-year relapse-free survival (56.8% vs 86.5%), higher cumulative relapse incidence (30.0% vs 6.9%), and lower overall survival (65.9% vs 88.4%, all p<0.001) compared with MRD negativity, and that post-transplant FLT3 inhibitor maintenance independently improves relapse-free survival (p=0.003), with the greatest benefit in patients who remain MRD-positive[^8]. Mechanistically, two companion papers in *Blood* dissect how mutant IDH1 impairs neutrophil differentiation through repression of myeloid progenitor transcriptional programs, including CEBPE, with reversibility demonstrated upon hypomethylating agent treatment or CEBPA overexpression, offering biological rationale for combination strategies in *IDH1*-mutated myeloid neoplasms[^9]. A systematic review and reconstructed individual-patient-data meta-analysis in *Blood Advances*, encompassing 44 cohorts (n=7,781) for overall survival at diagnosis and 19 cohorts (n=2,006) for MRD assessment in remission, confirms that leukemic stem cell positivity is independently associated with inferior outcomes at both diagnosis (HR 1.96, 95% CI 1.85–2.08) and in remission (HR 2.59, 95% CI 2.23–3.03), supporting incorporation of LSC frequency into risk stratification algorithms[^10]. Separately, the French ALFA-PPP cohort analysis of real-world treatment intensity and prognostic model performance in older AML patients was published in *Leukemia* this week, extending last week's preliminary reporting[^11].

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

A multicenter AIEOP-BFM analysis of 159 pediatric cases of PAX5-rearranged B-ALL, published in *Blood*, establishes this as a high-risk molecular subtype characterized by higher rates of hyperleukocytosis at diagnosis (22.9% vs 8.0%, p<0.001) and enrichment for IKZF1plus profile (14.7% vs 7.8%, p=0.0015) compared with non-PAX5-rearranged disease[^12]. Notably, the adverse prognostic impact was confined to patients with positive end-of-induction MRD, with 4-year event-free survival of 100% in standard-risk versus 63.4% and 63.3% in medium- and high-risk groups, respectively. High-throughput drug screening of patient-derived xenografts revealed marked FLT3 overexpression and sensitivity to FLT3 inhibitors, with gilteritinib showing potent ex vivo cytotoxicity and synergism with dexamethasone, nominating a novel targeted strategy for this MRD-defined high-risk population. Separately, a molecular characterization of 57 patients with lenalidomide-associated B-ALL arising after multiple myeloma maintenance therapy identifies three distinct mutational subgroups (TP53-mutant 30%, IDH2 R140Q-mutant 23%, and NRAS/KRAS-mutant others), with IDH2 mutations shown by single-cell sequencing to arise from preleukemic clonal hematopoiesis and cooperate with IKAROS (IKZF1) downregulation to drive B-cell precursor maturation arrest[^13].

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

A secondary analysis of the phase 3 Japan Adult Leukemia Study Group CML212 trial, published in *Blood Advances*, identifies early predictors of MR4.5 attainment and TKI discontinuation eligibility among 431 patients treated with nilotinib or dasatinib as first-line second-generation TKI therapy[^14]. With median follow-up of 3.0 years, cumulative incidence of MR4.5 was 46.6%, and 36.3% of MR4.5 achievers met discontinuation eligibility criteria; the BCR::ABL1 halving time between 0–3 months (subdistribution HR 2.23, 95% CI 1.09–4.55) and 6-month International Scale value (HR 0.38, 95% CI 0.31–0.47) independently predicted MR4.5 attainment, while only the 6-month IS value predicted discontinuation eligibility (odds ratio 0.37, 95% CI 0.24–0.56). This provides a practical, early decision point for clinicians counseling patients on long-term treatment-free remission prospects. A related editorial in *Leukemia* explores whether FLT3 and KIR3DL1 converge on a common immune surveillance axis relevant to treatment-free remission biology in CML, though this represents commentary rather than new primary data[^15].

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

A propensity score-matched analysis published in *Leukemia* reports that BTK inhibitor therapy is associated with a lower risk of secondary myeloid neoplasms and non-melanoma skin cancer compared with chemoimmunotherapy in chronic lymphocytic leukemia, adding to the long-term safety comparative dataset supporting BTK inhibitor-based frontline approaches over historical chemoimmunotherapy regimens[^16]. No effect sizes or confidence intervals were available in the abstract-level data captured this week; readers should await full-text publication for precise risk estimates.

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

Mechanistic work in *Leukemia* identifies a FOLR2-IRF2BP2 axis mediating erythropoiesis impairment from excess folic acid in myelodysplastic neoplasms, demonstrating in NUP98-HOXD13 transgenic mouse models that excess dietary folic acid exacerbates anemia while restriction alleviates disease phenotypes, with high IRF2BP2 expression correlating with poor prognosis in MDS patients[^17]. This raises a testable dietary-modification hypothesis for MDS-associated anemia management. Separately, a new inherited bone marrow failure syndrome is defined in *Leukemia*: biallelic SLF2 and SMC5 dysfunction, previously linked to Atelis Syndrome, is shown to drive premature hematopoietic stem cell aging, genomic instability via p53/p21 activation, and predisposition to MDS through impaired erythroid differentiation and myeloid-biased chromatin remodeling[^18]. A companion *Leukemia* commentary on "MDS without clonal marker" underscores that deep sequencing outperforms broad panel breadth in cases lacking an identifiable clonal marker, though full quantitative data were not available this week[^19]. A *Blood* review on hypocellular bone marrow failure provides a practical framework distinguishing immune aplastic anemia, hypoplastic MDS, and inherited bone marrow failure syndromes using clonal hematopoiesis signatures, with direct implications for risk stratification and surveillance strategies[^20]. Finally, a novel antibody-drug conjugate targeting CCRL2 with a pyrrolobenzodiazepine payload demonstrates strong selective cytotoxicity in TP53-mutated MDS/AML with erythroid features, surpassing gemtuzumab and CD33/CD123-targeted PBD-ADCs in preclinical xenograft models, nominating CCRL2 as a novel therapeutic target for this poor-prognosis population lacking effective options[^21].

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

A prospective non-invasive multiparametric study of 128 patients (44 essential thrombocythemia, 53 prefibrotic myelofibrosis, 21 overt primary myelofibrosis) published in *Leukemia* evaluates inflammatory and immune biomarkers to discriminate across the MPN disease spectrum[^22]. A two-step Lasso regression model incorporating IL-1RA, IL-6, S100A8/S100A9, CD25, CXCL10, YKL-40, TIMP1, EGF, and CXCL4 reliably identified overt myelofibrosis (AUC 0.909, 95% CI 0.804–0.994) with reasonable discrimination between essential thrombocythemia and prefibrotic myelofibrosis (AUC 0.762, 95% CI 0.656–0.847), though the authors note the modest discriminatory power between ET and preMF supports a pathophysiological continuum rather than discrete entities.

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

A real-world prognostic factor analysis of 157 patients with relapsed/refractory aggressive B-cell lymphoma treated with epcoritamab, published in *Blood Advances*, extends last week's reported outcomes with granular multivariable analysis: ECOG performance status ≥2, elevated LDH, disease refractory to prior line of therapy, and clinical trial ineligibility were independently associated with inferior PFS and OS, while 1-year PFS and OS across the cohort were 29% (95% CI, 22–37) and 38% (95% CI, 31–47) respectively[^23]. This confirms and refines last week's headline real-world epcoritamab findings by identifying the specific clinical subgroups driving inferior outcomes relative to pivotal trial populations. A meta-analysis of survival by phased-variant ctDNA and PET response in large B-cell lymphoma was also indexed in *Blood Advances* this week, though abstract-level efficacy data were not available for citation of specific hazard ratios[^24].

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

Frontline treatment paradigms for indolent lymphoma advanced meaningfully this week. The phase 2 study of mosunetuzumab followed by response-adapted polatuzumab vedotin/obinutuzumab in 42 previously untreated patients with follicular lymphoma (88%) and marginal zone lymphoma achieved a 100% overall response rate and 86% complete response rate by PET-CT, with single-agent mosunetuzumab alone achieving 71% CR; at median follow-up of 34 months, 2-year PFS was 89% (95% CI, 80–100) and 2-year OS was 100%, with cytokine release syndrome limited to grade 1 in 64% of patients and no tocilizumab required[^5]. In mantle cell lymphoma, a phase 2 study of mosunetuzumab plus polatuzumab vedotin in 42 patients with relapsed/refractory disease after BTK inhibitor therapy (26% with prior CAR-T) achieved an 88.1% objective response rate (95% CI, 74.4–96.0) and 78.6% complete response rate (95% CI, 63.2–89.7), with median PFS of 18.6 months (95% CI, 13.9–not estimable) at 15.9 months follow-up, including consistent efficacy in TP53-aberrant (48% of cohort) and blastoid/pleomorphic (38%) high-risk subgroups[^25]. This is the first reported bispecific-ADC combination in MCL and represents a chemotherapy-free option for a heavily pretreated, high-risk population. Separately, an erratum was issued this week for the previously reported sonrotoclax phase 1/2 MCL data covered last week; the corrected original manuscript confirms the ORR-IRC of 52.4% (95% CI, 42.4–62.4) versus historic control of 30% (p<0.0001), median DOR-IRC of 15.8 months, and median PFS-IRC of 6.5 months, with no material change to the previously reported efficacy figures[^26].

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

This week brought substantial new myeloma data across several modalities. The 3-year follow-up of MonumenTAL-1 confirms talquetamab's durability across TCR-naïve weekly (n=143) and biweekly (n=154) dosing cohorts and a prior-TCR cohort (n=78): overall response rates ranged 67–74% with complete response or better in 33–42%, median PFS of 7.5, 11.2, and 7.7 months respectively, and 36-month OS rates of 49.3%, 60.8%, and 44.6%; taste changes (72–76%) and infections (61–78%, grade 3/4 in 21–26%) remained the dominant toxicities, with ataxia/balance disorders in only 5.3% and no talquetamab-related deaths[^1]. This dataset provides important context for last week's MonumenTAL-3 report of talquetamab combinations in earlier relapse, confirming a consistent long-term safety signature across the development program. In extramedullary disease, the anti-BCMA/GPRC5D bispecific CAR T-cell phase 2 trial in 37 patients with extraosseous extramedullary myeloma achieved a 97% overall response and MRD-negativity rate with 43% stringent complete response at median follow-up of 10.1 months, though median PFS of 5.8 months (95% CI, 2.2–9.4) underscores that even dual-antigen CAR-T does not fully overcome the aggressive biology of extramedullary disease; cytokine release syndrome occurred in 73% (all grade 1/2) and ICANS in only 5%[^2]. The expanded CEPHEUS MRD analysis in transplant-ineligible/deferred NDMM (n=395) demonstrates that D-VRd achieves significantly higher overall MRD-negativity at the 10⁻⁵ threshold (60.9% vs 39.4%; OR 2.37, 95% CI 1.58–3.55, p<0.0001) and 10⁻⁶ threshold (46.2% vs 27.3%; OR 2.24, 95% CI 1.48–3.40, p=0.0001) versus VRd, with sustained MRD negativity ≥12 months also significantly favoring D-VRd (49.2% vs 27.3% at 10⁻⁵), reinforcing daratumumab-based quadruplet therapy as standard of care in this population[^3]. In the preclinical space, a TACI × CD3 bispecific antibody was engineered to redirect T cells against myeloma cells with downregulated BCMA expression, demonstrating effective cytotoxicity against BCMA-low primary patient samples unresponsive to BCMA × CD3 bispecifics, offering a potential salvage strategy after BCMA-targeted therapy failure[^28]. Finally, a subgroup analysis of the phase 3 CASSIOPEIA trial examining SLIM-criteria myeloma patients was published in *Leukemia*, though granular outcome data were not available at abstract level this week[^29].

## XII. Transplant & Cellular Therapy (CAR-T / HSCT / GVHD)

BMT CTN 1702, a large multicenter prospective biologic assignment trial (n=1,179, 93% adults), provides the most rigorous prospective comparison to date of donor sources for allogeneic HCT using a donor search prognosis score to assign patients to 8/8 matched unrelated donor versus center-preferred haploidentical, mismatched unrelated donor, or umbilical cord blood transplantation[^4]. Compared with MUD, survival was significantly inferior with umbilical cord blood (HR 2.65, p<0.001) but not significantly different with haploidentical (HR 1.08) or mismatched unrelated donor transplant (HR 1.18); treatment-related mortality (HR 3.31, p<0.001) and disease-free survival (HR 1.99, p=0.002) were also inferior only for cord blood. Among patients receiving post-transplant cyclophosphamide, haploidentical and mismatched unrelated donor transplants carried higher rates of grade 3/4 acute GVHD (HR 2.39 and 2.53, p=0.017 and 0.038) and chronic GVHD (HR 1.71 for both, p=0.028 and 0.080) relative to MUD, though other outcomes were comparable — supporting expedited use of alternative donors when a matched unrelated donor is not promptly available. A landmark analysis of donor selection and HLA loss relapse mechanisms after allo-HCT, published in the *Journal of Clinical Oncology*, examined 533 post-transplant relapses across 27 centers worldwide and found HLA loss in 15.6% overall, with marked variation by donor type (28.7% haploidentical family vs 7.2% unrelated adult vs 2.7% cord blood, p<0.0001)[^30]. A novel phasing tool predicting HLA incompatibility distribution across patient haplotypes found that mismatches concentrated on the same haplotype were associated with markedly higher HLA loss risk (27.6% vs 5.4% when mismatches were on different haplotypes, p<0.0001), providing an actionable pretransplant donor-selection tool to minimize immune escape risk. In pediatric cellular immunotherapy, a Japanese phase 2 study of WT1 peptide vaccination as post-allo-HSCT maintenance in 17 children with refractory acute leukemias reported a 3-year overall survival of 70.6% (95% CI, 43.1–86.6), with immune responders (≥1.455-fold WT1-specific CTL increase) achieving 90.9% 3-year OS versus 40.0% in nonresponders (p=0.027)[^31]. Separately, a single-arm feasibility trial of plant-based whole-food diets during autologous HCT for myeloma (n=22) demonstrated dose-dependent modulation of gut microbiome composition and increased stool short-chain fatty acid concentrations, establishing feasibility for future outcome-focused dietary intervention trials in the peritransplant setting[^32]. Preclinically, single-day nonactivated IL-18-armed CAR T cells were shown in *Blood* to establish a durable stem-like cellular state with enhanced persistence, improved mitochondrial bioenergetics, and reduced expression of exhaustion markers (PDCD1, TOX, HAVCR2) across xenograft models of lymphoma, leukemia, and pancreatic cancer, offering a manufacturing platform that could shorten vein-to-vein time while improving CAR-T potency[^33].

## XIV. Taiwan Clinical Context

Talquetamab is not yet reimbursed under Taiwan's National Health Insurance for relapsed/refractory myeloma, though NTUH and CGMH continue to serve as investigational sites for GPRC5D-directed bispecific and CAR-T protocols; the 3-year MonumenTAL-1 durability data strengthen the case for expedited NHI review given the substantial unmet need in post-BCMA relapse[^1]. The CEPHEUS MRD findings are directly applicable to Taiwan practice, where daratumumab-based quadruplet therapy is increasingly used in transplant-deferred elderly myeloma patients under NHI's expanded daratumumab coverage criteria updated in recent years[^3]. For allogeneic transplant programs at CGMH, VGH, and NTUH — where haploidentical transplantation has grown substantially given limited unrelated donor registry depth for Taiwanese HLA haplotypes — the BMT CTN 1702 findings supporting haploidentical transplant as non-inferior to matched unrelated donor transplant provide reassuring prospective validation for a donor strategy already widely practiced locally[^4]. The HLA-loss phasing tool for predicting post-transplant relapse risk may be particularly relevant in Taiwan's haploidentical-heavy transplant population, where the reported 28.7% HLA loss rate in haploidentical family donor transplants warrants incorporation into post-relapse salvage planning discussions[^30]. FLT3-ITD NGS-MRD testing per the new ELN-DAVID recommendations should be prioritized for standardization across Taiwan's major AML referral centers as this becomes an increasingly expected standard-of-care assessment internationally[^6].

## XV. Key Takeaways

Three-year MonumenTAL-1 follow-up cements talquetamab's durable efficacy and manageable long-term safety profile in relapsed/refractory myeloma, directly complementing last week's earlier-line MonumenTAL-3 data to outline talquetamab's evolving role across the treatment continuum. The CEPHEUS MRD update, now with nearly 5-year follow-up, provides some of the most mature MRD data supporting daratumumab-based quadruplet therapy in transplant-ineligible myeloma. BMT CTN 1702 offers rigorously prospective, multicenter evidence that haploidentical and mismatched unrelated donor transplantation achieve outcomes statistically indistinguishable from matched unrelated donor transplant, while cord blood remains inferior — a practice-informing result for donor selection algorithms globally and particularly in populations with limited unrelated donor registry representation. In lymphoma, the frontline chemotherapy-free mosunetuzumab-based approach in follicular and marginal zone lymphoma, alongside the first bispecific-ADC combination data in relapsed/refractory MCL, signal continued momentum toward chemotherapy-sparing regimens across the B-cell lymphoma spectrum. Finally, formalization of NGS-based FLT3-ITD MRD testing recommendations by ELN-DAVID represents an important standardization milestone that AML treatment centers, including those in Taiwan, should proactively adopt.
