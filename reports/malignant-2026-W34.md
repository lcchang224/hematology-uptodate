## Summary

This week's literature is dominated by *Blood*, *Leukemia*, *Haematologica*, and *Blood Advances* output, with five signals of practical relevance. **(1)** The phase 3 CEPHEUS trial reports its expanded minimal residual disease analysis in transplant-ineligible/deferred newly diagnosed multiple myeloma, showing daratumumab-VRd achieves overall MRD negativity of 60.9% versus 39.4% with VRd at the 10⁻⁵ threshold (OR 2.37; 95% CI, 1.58–3.55; p<0.0001), with sustained MRD negativity at ≥12 months of 49.2% versus 27.3% (p<0.0001), at a median follow-up of 58.7 months[^1]. **(2)** A German multicenter real-world analysis of venetoclax added to high-dose cytarabine/mitoxantrone (HAM) in 128 patients with relapsed/refractory AML reports a CR/CRi rate of 69% and 1-year OS of 58%, with a validated prognostic classifier identifying TP53 mutation, complex karyotype, ≥2 prior lines, and ECOG PS ≥2 as adverse factors, isolating a favorable subgroup with 73% 1-year OS[^2]. **(3)** BMT CTN 1702, a large prospective biologic-assignment donor-selection trial (n=1,179), demonstrates that haploidentical and mismatched unrelated donor transplantation yield survival statistically comparable to matched unrelated donor transplant (HR 1.08 and 1.18, respectively, not significant), while umbilical cord blood transplantation is inferior (HR for survival 2.65; p<0.001), providing definitive guidance for donor selection algorithms[^3]. **(4)** A multicenter retrospective study of FLT3-ITD–mutated AML (n=219) establishes that peritransplant molecular MRD by PCR-NGS strongly stratifies outcome, with pretransplant MRD positivity associated with 2-year relapse-free survival of 56.8% versus 86.5% for MRD-negative patients (p<0.001), and post-HCT FLT3 inhibitor maintenance independently improving RFS (p=0.003)[^4]. **(5)** The phase 3 EMAA trial, a placebo-controlled study of eltrombopag added to cyclosporine A for moderate aplastic anemia, shows a significantly higher overall response rate at week 24 with eltrombopag (71.4% vs 42.5%; p=0.011), establishing a new evidence-based first-line option for this previously understudied population[^5].

## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

Real-world validation of venetoclax-based salvage regimens in relapsed/refractory AML is strengthened this week by a German multicenter analysis of 128 patients treated with venetoclax plus high-dose cytarabine and mitoxantrone (HAM), building on the phase 1/2 RELAX trial that originally reported a 75% CR/CRi rate[^2]. In this real-world cohort (median age 60 years, 38% with prior allogeneic HCT, 25% venetoclax-exposed), the composite CR/CRi rate was 69%, rising to 78% among patients with only one prior therapy line and 76% among venetoclax-naïve patients; 68% proceeded to HCT, and 1-year RFS and OS were 59% and 58% respectively. A pooled prognostic classifier combining this cohort with RELAX trial data (n=183 total) identified TP53 mutation, complex karyotype, ≥2 prior therapy lines, and ECOG PS ≥2 as independent predictors of inferior outcome; patients lacking these features achieved a 1-year OS of 73%, supporting continued use of HAM-VEN as an effective bridge-to-transplant strategy in appropriately selected patients.

Molecular MRD monitoring in FLT3-ITD–mutated AML receives further validation from a 219-patient multicenter retrospective study using PCR-NGS at peritransplant timepoints[^4]. Pretransplant MRD positivity (≥0.001%) was associated with markedly inferior 2-year relapse-free survival (56.8% vs 86.5%), higher cumulative incidence of relapse (30.0% vs 6.9%), and lower overall survival (65.9% vs 88.4%; all p<0.001) compared with MRD negativity. Notably, peritransplant MRD dynamics — including post-HCT MRD conversion — outperformed single-timepoint measurements for risk stratification, and post-transplant FLT3 inhibitor maintenance independently improved RFS (p=0.003), with the greatest benefit concentrated in MRD-positive patients, reinforcing a risk-adapted maintenance strategy.

Mechanistic work in *Leukemia* identifies transcription factor NFE2 as a redox-regulatory node in AML, demonstrating that NFE2 constitutes a stronger gene dependency than NRF2 in MLL-AF9-transformed leukemic cells under oxidative stress, with NFE2 knockdown sensitizing cells to glutathione depletion, ferroptosis, and cytarabine, and an NFE2 RedOx transcriptional score independently predicting inferior outcome at diagnosis[^6]. Complementary biology in *Blood* reports a dual MYC/GSPT1 degrader, GT19630, disrupting the MYC-GSPT1 feedforward loop; the compound shows nanomolar cytotoxicity across leukemia, lymphoma, and myeloma models, restores venetoclax sensitivity in resistant AML in vivo, and is well tolerated in humanized Crbn mice, providing a preclinical rationale for MYC-directed degrader therapy[^7]. Two companion CAR-T papers in *Blood* describe a high-avidity cathepsin-G (CTSG)–specific CAR T-cell platform engineered with LCK coexpression and duplicated CD3ζ, achieving antileukemic activity against patient-derived AML xenografts without hematopoietic toxicity in colony assays, expanding the target antigen repertoire beyond CD33/CD123 for myeloid-directed cellular therapy[^8][^9].

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

A Spanish PETHEMA-based analysis in *Blood* refines genetics- and MRD-guided therapy allocation in 436 adults with Philadelphia chromosome-negative ALL[^10]. Patients were stratified by high genetic risk (HGR — including KMT2A rearrangements, low hypodiploidy, TP53 biallelic alterations, or combined IKZF1/CDKN2A-B deletions in B-ALL, and absence of NOTCH1/FBXW7 mutations with RAS/PTEN alterations in T-ALL) combined with end-of-induction MRD. By intention-to-treat, 61% were assigned to allogeneic HSCT and 39% to chemotherapy consolidation; 3-year OS was 81% (95% CI, 70–89) for MRD-negative patients without HGR versus 50% (95% CI, 34–63) for MRD-negative patients with HGR, and 61% (95% CI, 37–79) for early T-cell precursor ALL. This confirms that genetic risk retains prognostic weight even after MRD clearance, supporting continued transplant allocation for HGR patients regardless of MRD status.

A novel B-ALL molecular subtype is described in *Blood*, defined by an IGH::FENDRR rearrangement co-occurring with recurrent KRAS p.A146T/V/P mutations across 20 of 4,857 sequenced patients, with distinct FOXF1/FENDRR overexpression and JAK/STAT and RAS/MAPK signaling signatures[^11]. This FOXF1/FENDRR subtype showed very poor conventional chemotherapy response (induction failure or MRD ≥10⁻³ in 8/13 patients), but intensification with blinatumomab and/or allogeneic HSCT achieved ongoing molecular remission in 13 of 16 patients, suggesting early immunotherapy incorporation may be warranted for this genetically defined high-risk group. Separately, *Leukemia* reports that mTORC1 inhibition upregulates CD20 in B-cell precursor ALL via the AKT-FOXO1 axis, including in IKZF1-deleted high-risk disease, and potentiates anti-CD20 antibody efficacy in preclinical models, offering a rational combination strategy to sensitize CD20-low BCP-ALL to immunotherapy[^12]. A related *Blood* paper characterizes IDH2 R140Q-mutated clonal hematopoiesis cooperating with IKAROS (IKZF1) loss to drive a distinct B-ALL subtype following lenalidomide maintenance for multiple myeloma, with IDH2 mutations persisting in MRD-negative remission and preceding leukemic transformation, defining a preleukemic origin for this iatrogenic secondary leukemia[^13].

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

An analysis of the phase 3 Japan Adult Leukemia Study Group CML212 trial (n=431) in *Blood Advances* identifies early predictors of deep molecular response and treatment-free remission eligibility among patients receiving second-generation TKIs (nilotinib 300 mg twice daily vs dasatinib 100 mg once daily)[^14]. With median follow-up of 3.0 years, cumulative incidence of MR4.5 was 46.6%, and 36.3% of MR4.5 achievers met TKI discontinuation eligibility criteria (≥3 years therapy with ≥2 years sustained MR4.5). The 0–3-month BCR::ABL1 IS halving time (subdistribution HR 2.23; 95% CI, 1.09–4.55) and 6-month IS value (HR 0.38; 95% CI, 0.31–0.47) independently predicted MR4.5 attainment, while only the 6-month IS value predicted discontinuation eligibility (OR 0.37; 95% CI, 0.24–0.56), providing a practical early-landmark tool for treatment planning toward TKI-free remission.

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

A German multicenter study in *Blood Advances* examines outcomes in high-risk single-refractory (SRHR, refractory to either cBTKi or BCL2i with TP53 abnormality/complex karyotype) versus double-refractory (DR, refractory to both classes) CLL[^15]. Among 47 patients, median event-free survival for the SRHR cohort was 22 months, and median OS was 51 months in SRHR versus 21 months in DR disease, underscoring the steep prognostic cliff associated with double-class refractoriness. Among 22 patients undergoing allogeneic HCT, 2-year non-relapse mortality, relapse incidence, PFS, and OS were 19%, 19%, 62%, and 67% respectively; although long-term survivors were observed post-transplant, exploratory comparison did not demonstrate a statistically significant OS benefit for alloHCT versus no transplant, leaving its precise role and timing in double-refractory CLL an open question. Separately, a large propensity-matched analysis in *Leukemia* reports that BTK inhibitors are associated with lower rates of therapy-related myeloid neoplasms and non-melanoma skin cancer compared with chemoimmunotherapy in CLL, though the abstract detail was not available for numerical extraction this week[^16].

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

A mechanistic *Leukemia* study identifies a folic acid–FOLR2–IRF2BP2 axis driving erythropoiesis impairment in myelodysplastic neoplasms, showing that excess dietary folic acid exacerbates anemia in NUP98-HOXD13 transgenic MDS mice via FOLR2 nuclear translocation and IRF2BP2-mediated repression of GATA1/KLF1, while restricted folic acid intake alleviates the phenotype; elevated IRF2BP2 expression correlated with poor prognosis in MDS patients, raising dietary folate restriction as a plausible adjunctive strategy pending clinical validation[^17]. A separate report describes SLF2 and SMC5 dysfunction as the molecular basis of Atelis Syndrome, a newly characterized inherited bone marrow failure syndrome predisposing to MDS through premature HSC aging, genomic instability, and p53/p21-driven senescence, expanding the differential for young patients presenting with MDS and neurodevelopmental features[^18]. A novel antibody-drug conjugate targeting CCRL2, conjugated to a pyrrolobenzodiazepine payload, is reported in *Blood Advances* to show selective cytotoxicity against TP53-mutated MDS/AML with erythroid features, outperforming gemtuzumab and PBD-conjugated anti-CD33/anti-CD123 ADCs in preclinical xenograft models while sparing healthy HSPCs, representing a candidate targeted approach for this historically treatment-resistant high-risk subset[^19].

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

The IMPRSSION phase 2a trial of sapablursen, a TMPRSS6-targeting antisense oligonucleotide that raises hepcidin and restricts iron availability for erythropoiesis, reports results in polycythemia vera requiring frequent phlebotomy[^20]. Across 49 treated patients, mean weekly phlebotomy rate was reduced by 0.10 events/week in both the 120/80 mg cohort (95% CI, −0.13 to −0.07; p<0.0001) and the 40 mg cohort (95% CI, −0.14 to −0.06; p=0.0001), with median phlebotomy requirement falling from 5.0 at baseline to 0 during the efficacy window in the higher-dose cohort. MPN-SAF-TSS symptom scores improved by a mean of 6.21 points (95% CI, −10.56 to −1.85; p=0.0052) in the higher-dose cohort, positioning sapablursen as a promising non-cytoreductive option for phlebotomy-dependent PV. A comprehensive review in *Leukemia* summarizes the maturing landscape of CALR-targeted immunotherapy in JAK2/MPL-wild-type essential thrombocythemia and myelofibrosis, highlighting early-phase data on Fc-silent monoclonal antibodies blocking mutant-CALR–MPL signaling that achieve durable hematologic remissions with minimal toxicity, alongside preclinical antibody-drug conjugates, bispecific T-cell engagers, and CAR-T constructs under development[^21].

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

A multicenter real-world study in *Haematologica* evaluates a commercially available immunoglobulin-based ctDNA MRD assay (clonoSEQ) in large B-cell lymphoma, reporting that post-frontline treatment MRD detection (n=102) is associated with markedly inferior 12-month PFS of 15% versus 85% for MRD-undetectable patients (HR 14.5), reinforcing the prognostic power of molecular response assessment with a practical median turnaround of 9 days from sample collection to result, supporting feasibility for real-time clinical decision-making[^22]. Mechanistic work in *Blood* demonstrates that B-cell–specific expression of cFLIP, the principal inhibitor of extrinsic apoptosis, is required for MYD88/BCL2-driven DLBCL pathogenesis in a murine model; genetic deletion of Cflar sensitizes activated B-cell–like (ABC) but not germinal center B-cell–like DLBCL to TRAIL- and caspase-8–mediated apoptosis, providing rationale for cFLIP inhibitor development specifically in the ABC subtype[^23].

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

The GHSG BV-ALLO phase 2 trial evaluating brentuximab vedotin consolidation after allogeneic HCT for relapsed/refractory Hodgkin lymphoma is published in *Blood Advances*, alongside the final analysis of the BRAPP2 trial assessing brentuximab vedotin consolidation for stage I/II Hodgkin lymphoma with FDG-PET positivity after two cycles of ABVD in *Haematologica*; both abstracts were not accompanied by extractable efficacy figures in this week's data, but their availability signals accumulating evidence for brentuximab vedotin across both post-transplant and PET-adapted frontline consolidation settings, worth monitoring for detailed results in subsequent weeks[^24][^25].

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

The expanded MRD analysis of the phase 3 CEPHEUS trial provides the most substantive myeloma data this week, comparing daratumumab-VRd (D-VRd) against VRd alone in transplant-ineligible or transplant-deferred newly diagnosed multiple myeloma (n=395)[^1]. At a median follow-up of 58.7 months, overall MRD negativity at the 10⁻⁵ threshold was 60.9% with D-VRd versus 39.4% with VRd (OR 2.37; 95% CI, 1.58–3.55; p<0.0001), and at the stringent 10⁻⁶ threshold, 46.2% versus 27.3% (OR 2.24; 95% CI, 1.48–3.40; p=0.0001). Sustained MRD negativity for ≥12 months favored D-VRd at both thresholds (10⁻⁵: 49.2% vs 27.3%; 10⁻⁶: 34.0% vs 16.2%; both p<0.0001), with consistent benefit maintained at ≥24 and ≥36 months. Among patients achieving MRD negativity, PFS trended toward benefit with D-VRd (HR 0.61; 95% CI, 0.35–1.06; p=0.0755 at 10⁻⁵), reinforcing D-VRd as standard of care in this population. A companion frailty subgroup analysis of CEPHEUS is also referenced in *Leukemia*, though detailed efficacy figures were not available in this week's abstract cache[^26].

Complementing this, a post-hoc analysis of the CASSIOPEIA trial published in *Blood* compares blood-based MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry against bone marrow MRD by next-generation flow and sequencing in 237 patients[^27]. MRD negativity by any method was associated with longer PFS with comparable prognostic performance across platforms, and agreement between serum MS and bone marrow assessment improved during maintenance, with high positive and negative predictive value relative to a 10⁻⁵ marrow reference — though more limited rule-out performance against NGS at 10⁻⁶ — supporting a complementary, phase-adapted monitoring strategy that could reduce the frequency of invasive bone marrow sampling during maintenance. A related CASSIOPEIA subgroup analysis in *Leukemia* examines outcomes in patients meeting SLIM criteria for myeloma diagnosis, though quantitative results were not extractable from this week's abstract[^28].

## XII. Transplant & Cellular Therapy (CAR-T / HSCT / GVHD)

BMT CTN 1702, a large multicenter prospective biologic-assignment trial, provides the most consequential transplant data this week, comparing donor sources in 1,179 patients (93% adults) using a donor-search prognosis score to assign matched unrelated donor (MUD, n=772) versus center-preferred haploidentical (HAPLO, n=254), mismatched unrelated donor (MMUD, n=112), or umbilical cord blood (UCB, n=41) transplantation[^3]. In multivariate analysis, survival was statistically comparable between MUD and both HAPLO (HR 1.08) and MMUD (HR 1.18), while UCB was associated with significantly inferior survival (HR 2.65; p<0.001), higher treatment-related mortality (HR 3.31; p<0.001), and inferior disease-free survival (HR 1.99; p=0.002). Among patients receiving post-transplant cyclophosphamide, HAPLO and MMUD carried increased risk of grade 3–4 acute GVHD (HR 2.39, p=0.017 and HR 2.53, p=0.038, respectively) and chronic GVHD (HR 1.71 for both; p=0.028 and p=0.080) relative to MUD, though other outcomes were equivalent — supporting HAPLO or MMUD as reasonable alternatives to expedite transplantation when a timely MUD is unavailable.

A real-world analysis of the mesenchymal stromal cell product tomostrocel for steroid- or treatment-refractory pediatric acute GVHD (140 treatment episodes, 139 patients) reports an overall day-28 response rate of 62.9% (74.1%, 75.0%, and 53.2% for grade II, III, and IV disease respectively), with 6-month overall survival of 64.1% and favorable safety consistent with prior MSC data, including in the 76 patients previously exposed to ruxolitinib[^29]. In cellular therapy engineering, an off-the-shelf approach using irradiated CD19 CAR-engineered YTS NK cell lines is described in *Haematologica*, demonstrating that irradiation abolishes proliferative capacity without compromising cytotoxic function even after cryopreservation, with significant CD19+ tumor burden reduction in preclinical models — a scalable alternative to autologous CAR-T manufacturing[^30]. A "How I Treat" review in *Blood* provides a practical phase-based framework for infection prevention in patients receiving bispecific antibody therapy for B-cell malignancies and myeloma, emphasizing that infections represent the principal cause of non-relapse mortality in this setting due to cumulative B-cell/plasma-cell aplasia, hypogammaglobulinemia, and T-cell exhaustion[^31].

## XIV. Taiwan Clinical Context

Several findings this week carry direct relevance for NCKUH practice. The CEPHEUS MRD data further consolidate daratumumab-VRd as standard first-line therapy for transplant-ineligible/deferred myeloma; daratumumab combinations are NHI-reimbursed in Taiwan for defined myeloma indications, and centers such as NCKUH, NTUH, and CGMH should anticipate increasing use of MRD-adapted maintenance decisions as local MRD testing capacity (NGF and NGS) expands. The BMT CTN 1702 donor-selection data are particularly actionable given Taiwan's relatively limited unrelated donor registry depth compared with Western cohorts; the demonstrated equivalence of haploidentical and mismatched unrelated donor transplantation to matched unrelated donor transplant supports continued institutional reliance on haploidentical platforms with post-transplant cyclophosphamide, an approach already well established at CGMH and VGH. The CML212 trial's early landmark predictors for TKI discontinuation eligibility are directly applicable to Taiwan's growing treatment-free remission programs, given that both nilotinib and dasatinib are NHI-reimbursed first-line options. Finally, the EMAA trial's validation of eltrombopag plus cyclosporine A for moderate (not just severe) aplastic anemia may prompt reconsideration of NHI reimbursement criteria, which currently restrict eltrombopag access predominantly to severe aplastic anemia in Taiwan; hematologists managing moderate aplastic anemia patients with clinically significant cytopenia should track this evidence as a potential basis for expanded coverage discussions.

## XV. Key Takeaways

The week's strongest actionable signal is the CEPHEUS MRD update, which further cements daratumumab-quadruplet therapy as the standard for transplant-ineligible myeloma with a durable and deepening MRD benefit over five years of follow-up. The BMT CTN 1702 donor-selection trial offers practice-changing reassurance that haploidentical and mismatched unrelated donor transplants are appropriate first alternatives to matched unrelated donors, directly informing donor algorithms at Taiwanese transplant centers. Real-world confirmation of venetoclax-HAM efficacy in relapsed/refractory AML, alongside a validated prognostic classifier separating favorable from adverse-risk patients, provides granular guidance for salvage therapy selection. The EMAA trial fills a genuine evidence gap for moderate aplastic anemia management. Finally, sapablursen's phase 2a data in polycythemia vera introduce a promising non-cytoreductive erythrocytosis-control strategy that merits close tracking as it advances toward phase 3 development.
