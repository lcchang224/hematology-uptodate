## Summary

The five most clinically actionable signals this week are as follows. First, a *Blood* study by Ahamed et al. uses conditional knockout mice to formally refute the prevailing hypothesis that platelet-derived TGF-β1 drives regulatory T-cell (Treg) deficiency in ITP, demonstrating that previously reported supraphysiological platelet TGF-β1 levels are ex vivo artifacts and that remission maintenance in ITP must involve extra-platelet TGF-β1 sources — a finding that reframes mechanistic research and calls into question therapeutic strategies based on this axis[^1]. Second, a *BJH* plasma metabolomics study by Zou et al. (n = 138 paediatric patients plus 39 healthy controls) identifies 30 differential plasma metabolites in ITP, with altered tryptophan metabolism as the predominant enriched pathway; glycocholic acid achieves an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.882 (95% CI: 0.814–0.950) in distinguishing ITP from controls, and distinct phosphatidylcholine and sphingolipid signatures segregate newly diagnosed from chronic ITP — establishing a metabolomic disease fingerprint with potential biomarker utility[^2]. Third, a *Blood* study by Sharma et al. (n = 6 unrelated individuals) establishes germline heterozygous *MDM4* variants as a novel cause of bone marrow failure, demonstrating that MDM4 haploinsufficiency causes p53-mediated impairment of colony-forming capacity and engraftment through both p53-binding and RING-finger domains, with one patient acquiring somatic TP53 loss-of-function as a maladaptive rescue — defining a new TP53-activating inherited syndrome[^3]. Fourth, a *Blood* study by Rosato et al. presents an integrative PIEZO1 classification framework applied to 2,565 variants and 176 in-house dehydrated hereditary stomatocytosis (DHS1) cases, enabling reclassification of nearly 1,000 variants of uncertain significance and identifying three genotype-phenotype clusters ranging from severe hemolysis with iron overload to subclinical presentations — with immediate implications for genetic counselling of red cell disorder patients in Taiwan[^4]. Fifth, a *Haematologica* post-hoc analysis of the RIDTS trial by Potere et al. (n = 398 patients) quantifies recurrent VTE after isolated distal deep vein thrombosis (IDDVT), demonstrating a 15.3% recurrence rate (median time 6.2 months post-anticoagulation), with unprovoked IDDVT (HR 3.2; 95% CI 1.7–5.7) and shorter anticoagulation duration (HR 1.8; 95% CI 1.1–3.0) as independent predictors — directly informing IDDVT management decisions in outpatient anticoagulation clinics[^5].

## I. Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP)

Two distinct papers published this week substantially advance understanding of ITP pathobiology, approaching the disease from complementary angles of mechanistic immunology and metabolomics.

The first, by Ahamed et al. in *Blood*, directly challenges the widely cited model in which platelet-derived TGF-β1 maintains peripheral Treg populations and, when platelet counts are low in ITP, causes Treg deficiency that perpetuates autoimmune platelet destruction[^1]. Using conditional knockout mice in which TGF-β1 was selectively deleted from platelets, the authors were unable to confirm Treg deficiency or a failure of ITP remission maintenance. Critically, they demonstrate that the implausibly high platelet TGF-β1 concentrations reported in prior studies are likely ex vivo artifacts arising from platelet activation during sample processing, and that physiological platelet TGF-β1 secretion in vivo is insufficient to be the dominant regulator of Treg homeostasis. A companion *Blood* editorial by Kapur provides further context, emphasising that extra-platelet TGF-β1 sources — likely from stromal cells, regulatory macrophages, and Tregs themselves — are the true drivers of immune tolerance maintenance in remission[^6]. These findings have direct translational implications: any therapeutic strategy designed to augment platelet TGF-β1 signalling in ITP is unlikely to be effective, and mechanistic ITP research should be redirected toward non-platelet TGF-β1 compartments.

The second study, by Zou et al. in *BJH*, employs untargeted plasma metabolomics to profile 60 newly diagnosed ITP (nITP) and 39 chronic ITP (cITP) patients alongside 39 healthy controls at Beijing Children's Hospital, spanning October 2020 to August 2024[^2]. Using a random forest classifier trained on 30 differentially abundant metabolites, the model achieved an overall accuracy of 83.9% and a precision of 95.1% for distinguishing ITP patients from controls. Glycocholic acid demonstrated the strongest discriminatory performance, with an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.882 (95% CI: 0.814–0.950), suggesting bile acid metabolism is substantially perturbed in paediatric ITP. Beyond the ITP-versus-control distinction, phosphatidylcholine-related and sphingolipid-related metabolites segregated nITP from cITP, offering a potential metabolic signature of disease chronification. Altered tryptophan metabolism emerged as the most significantly enriched pathway overall, consistent with emerging evidence for gut microbiome–immune axis dysregulation in ITP. While these findings are hypothesis-generating and require prospective validation, they open a novel avenue for non-invasive ITP monitoring and potentially for identifying patients at highest risk of disease chronification at presentation.

## III. Aplastic Anemia

This week's key contribution is a landmark *Blood* mechanistic study by Sharma et al. establishing *MDM4* haploinsufficiency as a novel TP53-activating syndrome causing inherited bone marrow failure (BMF)[^3]. The authors report six unrelated individuals with variable BMF phenotypes and hypocellular myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS), presenting at a median age of 10 years (range 4 weeks to 53 years), carrying germline heterozygous *MDM4* variants including four null variants (frameshift, nonsense, and splice-site confirmed by RNA sequencing) and two missense variants, one of which had previously been associated with familial BMF. MDM4 encodes a critical negative regulator of p53; loss-of-function variants therefore result in constitutive p53 activation in hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs).

Using CRISPR/Cas9 to generate MDM4-haploinsufficient HSPCs from healthy donors, the authors demonstrated impaired colony-forming capacity and reduced engraftment in immunodeficient mice. Complementation studies established that both the p53-binding domain and RING-finger domain of MDM4 are required for normal hematopoietic regulation. Patient-specific variants introduced into induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) yielded significantly reduced erythroid and myeloid output with elevated p21 expression confirming p53 pathway activation, while transcriptome analysis revealed broad upregulation of the p53 gene signature. Importantly, one patient who progressed to MDS acquired somatic TP53 loss-of-function mutations, indicating that escape from p53-driven apoptosis via TP53 mutation represents a maladaptive clonal rescue mechanism — directly analogous to what is observed in Fanconi anemia and Diamond-Blackfan anemia. This study adds *MDM4* to the growing list of TP53-pathway genes implicated in inherited BMF and suggests that next-generation sequencing panels for BMF workup should now include *MDM4*. The age range at presentation (4 weeks to 53 years) underscores the variable penetrance of this syndrome and implies that adult haematologists should consider *MDM4* screening in unexplained hypocellular MDS.

## V. Hemophilia A & B (including gene therapy)

A *Blood Advances* study by Furukawa et al. this week addresses an important but understudied question in haemophilia A management: whether emicizumab — the bispecific antibody mimicking FVIII cofactor activity that has transformed prophylaxis — is transferred across the placenta in a murine model[^7]. Emicizumab's long half-life and IgG-class structure make placental transfer pharmacologically plausible, with potential implications for neonatal haemostasis in pregnancies of haemophilia A carriers who are receiving emicizumab prophylaxis. Although the full data from this study are not yet available as an open-access abstract, the methodological approach using a pregnant mouse model provides a tractable preclinical framework for evaluating fetal exposure, which is currently not characterisable from clinical registry data given that neonatal haemostasis assessment methods differ from those used in adults and that emicizumab interferes with standard APTT-based clotting assays. This study is directly relevant to obstetric haematology practice in Taiwan, where emicizumab is NHI-covered for severe haemophilia A without inhibitors and where the question of peri-partum management of haemophilia carriers is increasingly encountered.

The *Haematologica* report by La Mura et al. on biopsy-proven immune-mediated hepatitis after valoctocogene roxaparvovec, first documented in the W27 report, requires no further elaboration as no new data or corrections have been published this week[^8].

## VII. Thalassemia (alpha & beta; Taiwan-relevant)

A *BJH* observational cohort study by Sethuraman et al. reports growth and endocrine outcomes in 50 children and adolescents (aged 2–25 years) with transfusion-dependent thalassaemia (TDT) at a UK tertiary paediatric haemoglobinopathy centre, with retrospective and prospective data collected up to 18 years of age[^9]. Short stature (height standard deviation score [SDS] < −2) occurred in 24% (12 of 50) of patients, but only one patient was short relative to mid-parental height, indicating that the majority of height reduction is familial rather than disease-attributable. Disproportionate truncal shortening — defined as leg length SDS minus sitting height SDS > 1 — was strikingly prevalent at 79.3% (23 of 29 patients with available data), suggesting that vertebral growth impairment from iron deposition or prior radiation is common even in optimally managed TDT, and should be routinely assessed. Endocrinopathies were uncommon in this cohort: only one patient had growth hormone deficiency with central hypothyroidism, hypogonadism and thyroid dysfunction were rare, and impaired glucose tolerance was infrequent. The authors attribute this favourable endocrine profile to optimised transfusion and chelation practices in the contemporary era.

Notably, there was no difference in height SDS, sitting height SDS, or truncal shortening indices when comparing patients with the highest average liver iron concentration (24.03 mg/g dry weight) to those with the lowest (5.04 mg/g dry weight), suggesting that truncal shortening in modern TDT is either too advanced by the time of assessment to correlate with current iron burden, or that other factors such as hypogonadism history or prior steroid exposure contribute. These findings are relevant for NCKUH and the broader Taiwanese haemoglobinopathy service, where beta-thalassaemia major patients on chronic transfusion programmes should receive routine spinal growth surveillance even in the absence of classic endocrine complications.

A separate *Blood Advances* observational trial by Yee et al. provides new data on red blood cell (RBC) transfusion survival in sickle cell disease (SCD) that is also directly informative for thalassaemia transfusion practice[^10]. Using biotin-labelled RBC units in 20 recipients across 22 transfusion episodes (49 units), the authors determined median post-transfusion recovery of 97.3% (range 79.4–112.7%) at 24 hours, 80% (range 35–105%) at 28 days, and only 21% (range 2–48%) at 90 days, with a median time to 50% recovery of 60 days (range 23–85 days). In multivariate mixed-effects analysis, recipient splenic volume and donor G6PD deficiency or alpha-thalassaemia trait were independently associated with decreased transfused RBC survival. This finding is of particular relevance to Taiwan's transfusion medicine practice: given the high prevalence of both G6PD deficiency (approximately 3% in males in Taiwan) and alpha-thalassaemia trait (~4–5% carrier frequency), donated units from G6PD-deficient or alpha-thalassaemia trait donors may have sub-optimal survival in transfusion-dependent recipients, a consideration that could inform future donor screening strategies for chronic transfusion programmes.

## VIII. Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia (AIHA)

A *Haematologica* retrospective study by Yan et al. examines the prevalence, clinical correlates, and prognostic impact of AIHA complicating CLL in a single-centre cohort of 1,404 patients[^11]. The AIHA incidence was 10.4%, with 69.2% classified as warm-antibody AIHA (wAIHA). Patients with AIHA were characterised by male predominance, advanced disease stage, IGHV4-34 usage, and higher frequencies of DNMT3A mutations (enriched in AIHA overall) and MYD88 mutations (enriched specifically in cold-antibody AIHA). Although AIHA conferred a significantly adverse prognostic impact on CLL outcomes in the overall cohort, this effect was markedly attenuated in patients receiving targeted therapies (BTK inhibitors or venetoclax-based regimens), suggesting that modern CLL-directed therapy can partially overcome the adverse immunobiology associated with AIHA. An additional finding of note is that unmutated IGHV status — normally one of the strongest adverse prognostic markers in CLL — did not predict inferior outcomes in the AIHA subset, implying that disease biology in AIHA-complicated CLL may be partially distinct from that of the general CLL population. These findings support routine AIHA screening (DAT, reticulocyte count, LDH, haptoglobin) in high-risk CLL (IGHV4-34 usage, DNMT3A mutation, advanced stage) and strengthen the rationale for early initiation of targeted therapy in CLL patients who develop AIHA.

## IX. Red Cell Disorders (G6PD, PK deficiency, hereditary spherocytosis)

The most significant new contribution this week in the red cell disorder space is the PIEZO1 variant classification study by Rosato et al. in *Blood*[^4]. Applying an integrative framework combining ACMG/AMP guidelines, quantitative in silico predictions, structural domain annotation, and detailed phenotyping to 2,565 PIEZO1 variants, the authors generated a Bayesian scoring system that enabled reclassification of nearly 1,000 variants of uncertain significance. Pathogenic variants clustered non-randomly within functionally constrained structural domains, particularly the anchor, inner helix, and C-terminal domains, which represent gain-of-function hotspots consistent with the mechanosensitive channel biology of PIEZO1.

Genotype-phenotype correlation analysis across 176 in-house DHS1 (dehydrated hereditary stomatocytosis / hereditary xerocytosis) cases identified three distinct phenotypic clusters: a classical cluster characterised by severe chronic haemolysis, macrocytosis, and significant iron overload (often with antenatal presentations of hydrops or polyhydramnios); an intermediate cluster with mild-to-moderate compensated haemolysis; and an atypical/subclinical cluster where haemolysis may be clinically silent but iron overload can still accumulate. This three-cluster framework has immediate practical implications because patients in the subclinical cluster are frequently misclassified or remain undiagnosed until iron overload is detected incidentally, and the finding that iron overload occurs even in mild phenotypes means that ferritin surveillance is warranted across all DHS1 genotypes. For Taiwanese clinicians, PIEZO1-related DHS is a differential diagnosis to consider in patients with congenital haemolytic anaemia who are DAT-negative, stomatocytes are present on peripheral blood film, and standard haemoglobin electrophoresis is unremarkable — a presentation that can masquerade as non-immune haemolytic anaemia of uncertain aetiology.

## X. Iron Deficiency & Nutritional Anemias

A *BJH* cross-sectional study by Addo et al. in 907 apparently healthy adult blood donors compares serum hepcidin and serum ferritin as tools for detecting the onset of iron-deficient erythropoiesis, using five biomarkers of iron-deficient red cell production as reference standards: haemoglobin, soluble transferrin receptor (sTfR), erythrocyte zinc protoporphyrin (ZPP), reticulocyte haemoglobin content (Retic-Hb), and mean corpuscular volume (MCV)[^12]. Serum hepcidin and ferritin were moderately correlated (Pearson r = 0.69, p < 0.001 in females; r = 0.65, p < 0.001 in males). At the population level, lower hepcidin and ferritin concentrations were both associated with decreases in Hb, Retic-Hb, and MCV and increases in sTfR and ZPP, consistent with onset of iron-restricted erythropoiesis. Using restricted cubic spline models to derive physiologically anchored thresholds, hepcidin cutoffs for iron deficiency were 13.9 μg/L (females aged 18–49 years), 24.8 μg/L (females aged 50–75 years), and 28.8 μg/L (all males 18–75 years), with corresponding ferritin thresholds of 25.4, 30.7, and 32.5 μg/L respectively. A key finding is that both biomarkers performed similarly in identifying iron deficiency onset, particularly in premenopausal women where the hepcidin threshold (13.9 μg/L) was substantially lower than in men or older women, reflecting expected differences in iron stores by age and sex. For clinical practice in Taiwan — where blood donor iron status monitoring is evolving — this study suggests that hepcidin could serve as an equivalent or complementary tool to ferritin for donor iron screening, particularly because hepcidin integrates both iron stores and erythropoietic demand into a single measurement, although its higher assay cost remains a practical limitation.

## XI. Thrombosis & Coagulation (VTE, APS, inherited thrombophilia)

Two new studies this week address important questions in VTE management and thrombus biology.

The RIDTS trial post-hoc analysis by Potere et al. in *Haematologica* provides contemporary, rigorously characterised data on recurrent VTE after isolated distal deep vein thrombosis (IDDVT) in patients without cancer who completed 6–12 weeks of anticoagulation with rivaroxaban[^5]. Among 398 participants, 61 (15.3%) experienced recurrent VTE at a median of 6.2 months after anticoagulation cessation. Of these recurrences, 47 (77%) were recurrent IDDVT and 14 (23%) were proximal DVT or symptomatic pulmonary embolism; 39 (63.9%) were symptomatic and 22 (36.1%) asymptomatic. Annualised recurrence rates were substantially higher after unprovoked than provoked IDDVT: 24.1 (95% CI 16.1–36.4) versus 6.8 (95% CI 3.0–15.1) per 100 person-years at 6 months, and 15.7 (95% CI 11.8–20.9) versus 4.5 (95% CI 2.7–7.7) per 100 person-years at 24 months. In multivariable analysis, three independent predictors of recurrence emerged: unprovoked IDDVT (HR 3.2; 95% CI 1.7–5.7), diabetes (HR 2.5; 95% CI 1.1–5.3), and shorter duration of anticoagulation (HR 1.8; 95% CI 1.1–3.0). Recurrence rates were notably higher in the 6-week versus 12-week anticoagulation arm, particularly in the first 6 months after cessation (24.4 [95% CI 16.1–37.0] vs 7.5 [95% CI 3.6–15.7] per 100 person-years). These data suggest that 12 weeks of anticoagulation is preferable to 6 weeks for unprovoked IDDVT, and that diabetic patients warrant heightened post-treatment surveillance regardless of provocation status. For Taiwanese anticoagulation practice, where IDDVT is frequently encountered in elderly patients with immobility or minor injury and empirically managed with short-course anticoagulation, these risk-stratification data provide a quantitative framework for individualising treatment duration decisions.

The second study, from Yakusheva et al. in *Blood*, addresses the fundamental mechanism by which a mature thrombus maintains itself in a stable, quiescent state — which the authors term the "stillness phase"[^13]. Using intravital microscopy and flow-based assays, the authors demonstrate that fibrin in a mature thrombus remnant acts as a thrombin sink: fibrin binds and sequesters active thrombin, preventing further platelet recruitment and thrombus growth. When recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (rtPA) is administered to a thrombus already in the stillness phase, fibrin lysis releases sequestered active thrombin, causing paradoxical thrombus regrowth — a finding confirmed in FgaEK mice (which cannot form fibrin), where repeated cycles of thrombus growth and detachment without achieving the stillness phase were observed alongside continuous thrombin release. This mechanistic insight has important potential clinical implications: it provides a fibrinolysis-based explanation for rebound thrombosis sometimes observed after thrombolytic therapy, and may explain why low-dose rtPA used for catheter-directed thrombolysis in DVT can occasionally precipitate proximal propagation. While translational validation in human systems is required, these data provide a novel mechanistic framework for understanding the haemostatic equilibrium that maintains established venous thrombi.

## XIII. Taiwan Clinical Context

Several findings from this reporting period carry specific relevance for Taiwanese clinical practice. Regarding thalassaemia, Taiwan has carrier frequencies of approximately 4–5% for alpha-thalassaemia and 1–2% for beta-thalassaemia, with an active transfusion-dependent TDT programme at NCKUH, NTUH, and Mackay Memorial Hospital. The Sethuraman et al. data on high prevalence of truncal shortening (79%) even in optimally chelated TDT patients provide evidence that routine spinal growth surveillance — not captured by standard height SDS measurements alone — should be incorporated into Taiwanese TDT follow-up protocols[^9]. The Yee et al. findings on donor G6PD deficiency and alpha-thalassaemia trait reducing transfused RBC survival are particularly relevant in Taiwan given that G6PD deficiency affects approximately 2–3% of the general population (higher in certain indigenous groups), and alpha-thalassaemia trait is highly prevalent; NCKUH blood bank may consider whether donor G6PD phenotyping for chronic transfusion recipients warrants prospective evaluation[^10].

Regarding ITP, both new mechanistic and metabolomic papers this week reinforce the complexity of ITP pathobiology and highlight that TGF-β1 augmentation strategies should not be pursued clinically without re-examination of the underlying data[^1]. The paediatric metabolomics study from Beijing Children's Hospital is directly relevant to paediatric haematology practice at NCKUH, where distinguishing early-onset chronic ITP from newly diagnosed ITP carries therapeutic implications for when to escalate from first-line steroids to thrombopoietin receptor agonists (eltrombopag is NHI-covered in Taiwan for chronic ITP). The PIEZO1 framework from Rosato et al. is similarly actionable for the NCKUH red cell disorder clinic, where PIEZO1-related DHS may be underdiagnosed among DAT-negative haemolytic anaemia referrals given limited access to ektacytometry in routine practice[^4]. Finally, the IDDVT recurrence data from the RIDTS post-hoc analysis are directly applicable to Taiwanese anticoagulation clinic practice, where diabetic patients with unprovoked IDDVT now have quantitative recurrence risk estimates to guide extended anticoagulation discussions[^5].

## XIV. Key Takeaways

This week's literature definitively refutes the platelet TGF-β1 → Treg axis as the mechanistic basis of ITP remission maintenance, fundamentally reorienting mechanistic and translational ITP research toward extra-platelet TGF-β1 sources. In parallel, a paediatric plasma metabolomics study identifies glycocholic acid (AUC 0.882) and tryptophan pathway metabolites as candidate ITP biomarkers with utility for distinguishing newly diagnosed from chronic disease. The identification of *MDM4* haploinsufficiency as a new TP53-activating inherited BMF syndrome expands the differential diagnosis of hypocellular MDS in both paediatric and adult patients and mandates inclusion of *MDM4* in BMF gene panels. The comprehensive PIEZO1 variant classification framework, resolving ~1,000 variants of uncertain significance across three genotype-phenotype clusters, provides a practical diagnostic resource for DHS1 workup that is immediately usable in Taiwanese genetic counselling contexts. Finally, the RIDTS post-hoc analysis delivers the most granular VTE recurrence risk estimates published for IDDVT to date — with unprovoked status (HR 3.2), diabetes (HR 2.5), and 6-week versus 12-week anticoagulation duration (HR 1.8) as independent predictors — directly supporting preferential use of 12-week anticoagulation in high-risk IDDVT patients.
