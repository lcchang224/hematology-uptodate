## Summary

Five signals define this week's literature. **(1)** The phase 2 GRAALL-2014/B-QUEST substudy published in *Blood* demonstrates that blinatumomab consolidation in high-risk Philadelphia chromosome–negative (Ph−) B-ALL reduces 5-year cumulative incidence of relapse from 49% to 23% (p = 0.001), improving disease-free survival to 68% vs. 42% (p = 0.001) and overall survival to 79% vs. 60% (p = 0.03), establishing a new frontline standard for this population[^1]. **(2)** Final phase 1 results for asciminib 200 mg twice daily in T315I-mutated CML-CP published in *Leukemia* confirm durable remissions, with 53.3% of evaluable patients achieving major molecular response (MMR) at a median exposure of 3.5 years and 86% (95% CI: 71.9–100.0%) maintaining their first MMR for ≥144 weeks, without new safety signals[^2]. **(3)** A large real-world CIBMTR registry study in *Blood* covering 1,116 patients receiving lisocabtagene maraleucel (liso-cel) for relapsed/refractory large B-cell lymphoma reports a 12-month overall survival of 67.6% (95% CI, 64.5–70.6%), objective response rate of 81.2%, and complete response rate of 71.3% in a predominantly elderly real-world population (median age 71.1 years)[^3]. **(4)** Integrated epigenomic and methylation profiling in *Leukemia* delineates two epigenetically distinct ENKTL subgroups, with a high-methylation/TP53-loss subgroup carrying significantly poorer overall survival, offering a new framework for risk stratification and therapeutic guidance in this Asia-prevalent disease[^4]. **(5)** Two concurrent papers in *Blood* define the t(14;16)(q32;q24)/FOXF1-FENDRR translocation as a novel BCL11B-enhancer–hijacked subtype of high-risk lineage-ambiguous leukemia in adolescents and young adults, detectable in 0.15–4.0% of T-ALL/MPAL cases, with uniformly poor outcomes and potential sensitivity to combined BCL2 and JAK/STAT inhibition[^5][^6].

## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

### NMD as an Isoform-Specific Checkpoint in t(8;21) AML: EIF4A3-Dependent Buffering of AML1-ETO9a

A mechanistic study in *Leukemia* identifies nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD) as a previously unrecognized isoform-specific checkpoint governing oncogenic dosage in t(8;21) AML, one of the most common cytogenetically defined AML subtypes[^7]. Zhang et al. demonstrate that the AML1-ETO fusion undergoes alternative splicing to generate AML1-ETO9a (AE9a), a truncated, more leukemogenic isoform whose inclusion cassette introduces premature termination codons, rendering the resulting transcript NMD-sensitive. In primary t(8;21) AML CD34+ hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells, AE9a inclusion inversely correlated with NMD-factor expression, and high EIF4A3 expression was associated with improved overall survival specifically in t(8;21) AML but not in other AML subtypes, demonstrating subtype specificity. Overexpression of EIF4A3 in t(8;21) AML cell lines restrained leukemic growth, spared healthy CD34+ progenitor expansion, and enhanced idarubicin sensitivity, while genetic or pharmacological impairment of EIF4A3 or SMG1 increased AE9a protein abundance and promoted leukemic fitness. These findings define EIF4A3-dependent NMD as a translational biomarker of clinical heterogeneity in t(8;21) AML and propose a rationale for NMD-augmenting or AE9a-suppressive therapeutic strategies.

### CLAG-M vs. CPX-351 in Medically Unfit Adults: Randomized Phase 2 Data

A single-institution randomized phase 2 trial (NCT04195945) published in *Leukemia* provides the first prospective head-to-head comparison of CPX-351 versus CLAG-M (cladribine, cytarabine, G-CSF, and mitoxantrone) in 60 adults with newly diagnosed AML and medical unfitness as defined by a Treatment-Related Mortality score ≥13.1, 68% of whom had ECOG performance status 3–4[^8]. Only CLAG-M met the primary endpoint of ≥63% three-month overall survival (70% vs. 60%; p = 0.41, a non-significant difference that nonetheless crossed the predefined threshold for CLAG-M only). The complete remission plus CR with incomplete hematologic recovery rate trended higher with CLAG-M (73% vs. 47%; p = 0.064). Relapse-free survival following CLAG-M versus CPX-351 did not differ significantly (median 37.6 vs. 19.9 months; p = 0.80), nor did overall survival (median 10.5 vs. 5.8 months; p = 0.76) in the overall cohort. A clinically important exception emerged among patients with proliferative disease, in whom CLAG-M conferred significantly longer OS (median 18.5 vs. 3.9 months; p = 0.02). These data suggest that in medically unfit AML patients, standard CPX-351 may not be universally preferable over intensive CLAG-M, particularly in the proliferative subset, and motivate further prospective investigation.

### Co-mutation Architecture in NPM1-Mutated AML: PETHEMA Multicenter Study

A retrospective PETHEMA multicenter analysis encompassing 1,360 adults with NPM1-mutated AML published in *Haematologica* maps the age-resolved co-mutation landscape and its prognostic consequences[^9]. Co-mutations were identified in 97% of patients (median 3 mutations per patient), with DNMT3A (45%), FLT3-ITD (42%), TET2 (27%), and IDH2 (20%) being most prevalent. RTK/RAS pathway alterations predominated in younger adults, while myelodysplasia-related genes and epigenetic lesions accumulated in the elderly. Among 688 patients receiving upfront intensive therapy, competing-risk modeling identified SRSF2 (adverse impact on OS, RFS, and EFS) and DNMT3A (inferior RFS and EFS, but not OS) as independent adverse prognostic co-mutations, while KRAS adversely impacted OS and FLT3-OTHER was associated with improved OS. These age-structured co-mutation signatures refine risk stratification beyond FLT3-ITD and argue for individualized molecular profiling in NPM1-mutated AML at diagnosis.

### USP22 as a Novel MEIS1 Stability Regulator in KMT2Ar Acute Leukemia

A genome-wide CRISPR/Cas9 screen in a KMT2A-rearranged B-ALL cell line reported in *Blood* identifies the deubiquitinase USP22 — a component of the SAGA chromatin-remodeling complex — as a critical regulator of MEIS1 protein stability in KMT2Ar acute leukemia[^10]. Bröchtel et al. show that USP22 and MEIS1 co-occupy chromatin at key oncogenic target gene loci, and that genetic or pharmacological inhibition of USP22 leads to polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of MEIS1, with consequent downregulation of HOXA9 and other leukemogenic targets. These findings are particularly relevant given the emergence of menin inhibitor resistance in KMT2Ar leukemia and provide a mechanistic rationale for USP22 as a complementary or combinatorial therapeutic vulnerability.

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

### GRAALL-2014/B-QUEST: Blinatumomab Consolidation Transforms Outcomes in High-Risk Ph− B-ALL

The phase 2 GRAALL-2014/B-QUEST substudy, published in *Blood*, delivers practice-defining evidence for frontline blinatumomab integration in high-risk (HR) Ph− adult B-ALL[^1]. Between 2015 and 2020, 489 adults aged 18–59 years with newly diagnosed Ph− B-ALL were enrolled in the GRAALL-2014 study; 259 met HR criteria (KMT2A-rearrangement, IKZF1 deletion, or end-of-induction MRD ≥10⁻⁴). Of these, 94 HR patients enrolled in the QUEST substudy (2018–2020) and received up to five 28-day cycles of blinatumomab during consolidation and maintenance. Ninety HR patients treated prior to QUEST activation without blinatumomab served as a post hoc comparison group, with comparable baseline characteristics. At 5 years, cumulative incidence of relapse was 23% vs. 49% (p = 0.001), disease-free survival was 68% vs. 42% (p = 0.001), and overall survival was 79% vs. 60% (p = 0.03) in the blinatumomab versus control groups, respectively. Patients eligible for allogeneic HSCT derived overall benefit from blinatumomab; however, no clear additional DFS advantage was demonstrated among those who ultimately underwent transplantation, raising important questions about optimal transplant integration in MRD-negative HR B-ALL. This trial (NCT03709719) establishes blinatumomab consolidation as a new standard of care in HR Ph− adult B-ALL and invites prospective evaluation of MRD-guided transplant de-escalation strategies.

### Predictors of Survival and AlloHSCT Benefit in R/R B-ALL Treated with Immunotherapy

A multi-institutional retrospective analysis from MD Anderson published in *Leukemia* provides important guidance on risk stratification and allogeneic HSCT consolidation in 172 adults with relapsed/refractory B-ALL receiving blinatumomab and/or inotuzumab ozogamicin (InO)-based regimens in first salvage[^11]. On multivariable analysis, relapse within 12 months of initial diagnosis (HR 2.21; 95% CI, 1.28–4.16) and MRD positivity by multiparametric flow cytometry after cycle 1 of salvage (HR 3.06; 95% CI, 1.75–5.33) were the strongest predictors of inferior relapse-free survival. In a clinically significant finding, patients achieving early flow MRD negativity who had late relapse or were primary refractory to frontline therapy did not benefit from consolidative allo-HSCT (4-year RFS 60% without allo-HSCT vs. 63% with allo-HSCT; p = 0.82). Conversely, allo-HSCT significantly improved outcomes in patients with MRD positivity and/or early relapse within 12 months (4-year RFS 56% vs. 22% without allo-HSCT; p = 0.03). These data provide a pragmatic framework for personalizing transplant decisions in the immunotherapy era of adult B-ALL.

### t(14;16)(q32;q24) Translocation: BCL11B Enhancer Hijacking Defines a New High-Risk Leukemia Subtype

Two simultaneous landmark papers in *Blood* from international consortia (Di Giacomo et al. and Mimura et al.) converge on the t(14;16)(q32;q24) translocation as the defining genomic event of a novel, high-risk subtype of lineage-ambiguous leukemia[^5][^6]. Both groups demonstrate that this structural variant repositions the FOXF1 gene and its antisense lncRNA FENDRR under regulatory control of the BCL11B super-enhancer, driving their ectopic overexpression. The immunophenotype is strikingly mixed, with co-positivity for immature (CD38), myeloid (CD13), T-lymphoid (cCD3, CD7), and B-lymphoid (CD19, CD79a, CD10) markers, rendering these cases particularly diagnostically challenging. Universal accompanying lesions include GATA3 loss-of-function mutations and CDKN2A/B deletions, with frequent JAK/STAT and NOTCH1 pathway activating mutations. Clinically, the subtype was detected in 0.15–4.0% of T-ALL/MPAL cases across different cohorts, with a median age of 15 years and enrichment in adolescents and young adults. Outcomes were uniformly poor, comparing unfavorably even with established high-risk groups such as KMT2A-rearranged ETP-like, SPI1-rearranged, and LMO2 γδ-like T-ALLs. High-throughput drug screening in 176 compounds demonstrated efficacy of combined BCL2 family and JAK/STAT inhibition, and some patients treated with tyrosine kinase inhibitors showed clinical benefit. Single-cell RNA sequencing by Mimura et al. further revealed that FOXF1 overexpression promotes myeloid differentiation while suppressing T-cell differentiation, delineating a key lineage-specification role. These two studies together establish the t(14;16) subtype as an actionable genomic entity warranting routine clinical screening.

### PI3Kδ Inhibition Enhances MUC1-Tn CAR T-Cell Persistence in T-ALL

A preclinical study in *Leukemia* demonstrates that the MUC1-Tn antigen is broadly expressed in T-ALL and that MUC1-Tn–targeted CAR T cells can achieve efficient cytotoxicity without fratricide[^12]. Combination with the PI3Kδ inhibitor linperlisib suppressed CAR T-cell exhaustion through inhibition of the EGR1/DUSP2 axis, promoted mitochondrial fusion, and enhanced respiratory capacity, resulting in sustained anti-leukemic control upon rechallenge in xenograft models. This study provides a translatable combinatorial strategy for a disease where CAR T-cell development has been significantly impeded by shared antigen expression and T-cell fratricide.

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

### Asciminib in T315I CML-CP: Phase 1 Final Analysis with up to 6 Years of Follow-up

Final long-term results of the phase 1 trial (NCT02081378) of asciminib 200 mg twice daily in T315I-mutated CML-CP, now published in *Leukemia*, confirm sustained efficacy with an excellent long-term safety profile across up to approximately 6 years of exposure[^2]. Among 45 evaluable patients, 24 (53.3%) achieved MMR, and 20 of 24 responders maintained or deepened response by the data cutoff. The Kaplan-Meier estimated proportion of patients maintaining their first MMR for at least 144 weeks (approximately 2.8 years) was 86% (95% CI: 71.9–100.0%). After a median drug exposure of 3.5 years, 52.1% of patients continued to receive asciminib through post-trial access. The grade ≥3 adverse event rate (60.4%) did not increase with 1.4 additional years of exposure since the previous analysis, and the exposure-adjusted incidence rate of first all-grade adverse events of interest was 4.4 cases per 100 patient-years. Only four patients (8.3%) discontinued due to adverse events. These definitive data, derived from the longest follow-up reported for asciminib in T315I disease, solidify its role as the preferred myristoyl pocket–targeting option for this treatment-resistant population and are particularly relevant given Taiwan's NHI coverage for asciminib in T315I-positive CML.

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

### Acalabrutinib First-Line Real-World EPIC Study: 3-Year Outcomes

Three-year follow-up data from the multicenter UK EPIC real-world study of 282 previously untreated CLL patients receiving acalabrutinib monotherapy, published in *Blood Advances*, provide mature effectiveness evidence outside of clinical trials[^13]. Patients had a median age of 73.9 years (IQR, 68.6–79.4) and a median follow-up of 48.9 months. At the 3-year landmark, real-world progression-free survival was 82.5% (95% CI, 78.2–87.1%) and overall survival was 84.3% (95% CI, 80.2–88.7%), with 72.3% of patients remaining on therapy. Discontinuation was most commonly due to adverse events (36%), death (18%), or disease progression (16%). The toxicity profile was consistent with trial experience, with upper respiratory tract infection (8%), rash (5%), and neutropenia (4%) being the most common prespecified adverse events. These data confirm that acalabrutinib maintains its effectiveness and favorable tolerability in the elderly, comorbid real-world CLL population and complement existing trial evidence from ELEVATE-TN and ASCEND.

A separate *Blood* review by Wu et al. synthesizes the remaining unmet needs in CLL, framing dual BTK/BCL-2 inhibitor resistance, TP53 disruption, and Richter transformation as the three foremost clinical challenges requiring mechanistic resolution and therapeutic innovation[^14].

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

### MDM4 Haploinsufficiency: A TP53-Activating Germline Syndrome Causing Bone Marrow Failure

A *Blood* study by Sharma et al. characterizes MDM4 germline deficiency as a novel TP53-activating syndrome presenting as variable bone marrow failure (BMF) and hypocellular MDS[^15]. Six unrelated individuals were described (median age at presentation 10 years; range, 4 weeks to 53 years), harboring heterozygous MDM4 variants including frameshift, nonsense, splice-site, and missense mutations. CRISPR/Cas9-generated MDM4-haploinsufficient HSPCs exhibited increased p53 activity, impaired colony formation, and reduced engraftment in immunodeficient mice. iPSC complementation studies confirmed that both the p53-binding and RING-finger domains are necessary for hematopoietic regulation, and MDM4-mutant iPSC-derived hematopoietic cells showed elevated p21 and upregulated p53 pathway transcriptomic signatures. One patient acquired somatic TP53 loss-of-function mutations in the setting of MDS progression, representing maladaptive somatic rescue — an observation that parallels Diamond-Blackfan anemia and Shwachman-Diamond syndrome biology. This work establishes MDM4 deficiency as a new hereditary BMF entity and highlights the importance of germline testing in pediatric and young-adult BMF presentations.

### IKZF1N159S Mutation: Adverse Prognostic Impact in MDS

A *Leukemia* study identifies the IKZF1 missense variant N159S as an adverse prognostic marker in MDS, expanding the known somatic mutational architecture of this disease and its prognostic implications[^16]. Full abstract data were not available, but the finding is consistent with emerging evidence that lineage transcription factor mutations confer additional risk beyond standard IPSS-R classification in MDS.

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

### Triple-Negative ET: Molecular Prognostication in 241 Patients

A multicenter study of 241 triple-negative essential thrombocythemia (TN-ET) patients with myeloid panel sequencing published in *Leukemia* provides the first large-scale molecular prognostic data for this diagnostically challenging entity[^17]. Pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants were identified in 19.5% of patients. Mutation carriers were significantly older (median 66 vs. 53 years; p < 0.001) and had a higher frequency of prior thrombosis (19.6% vs. 6.5%; p = 0.013). The presence of pathogenic variants was associated with leukemic progression (HR 12.608; 95% CI: 2.616–60.775; p = 0.002) and inferior overall survival (HR 3.008; 95% CI: 1.43–6.327; p = 0.004) in multivariable models. ASXL1 (p = 0.004), CBL (p < 0.001), EZH2 (p < 0.001), and ZRSR2 (p < 0.001) mutations were each associated with inferior leukemia-free survival. The Revised IPSET-thrombosis score retained utility, with 10-year thrombosis probabilities of 30%, 15%, and 6% for high-, intermediate-, and very-low risk patients respectively (p < 0.001). Progression to myelofibrosis was rare (2.5%). These findings support incorporating myeloid gene panel sequencing into routine TN-ET workup.

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

### Liso-cel in R/R LBCL: CIBMTR Real-World Registry Data (N = 1,116)

A landmark real-world effectiveness analysis from the CIBMTR registry, published in *Blood*, provides the largest and most mature post-approval dataset for liso-cel in relapsed/refractory LBCL[^3]. The study enrolled 1,116 patients treated between February 2021 and February 2025, with a median age of 71.1 years (range, 21.5–91.2) — notably, 72.3% were aged ≥65 years, reflecting the real-world breadth of use. The cohort included 195 second-line patients, 71 with secondary CNS involvement, and 257 with transformed LBCL; 53.4% had ≥1 comorbidity and median prior lines of therapy was 3 (range, 1–16). Among 1,109 effectiveness-evaluable patients, the objective response rate was 81.2% and complete response rate was 71.3%. At a median follow-up of 12.6 months (95% CI, 12.5–12.8), 12-month duration of response was 60.2% (95% CI, 56.4–63.9%), progression-free survival 51.2% (95% CI, 48.0–54.4%), and overall survival 67.6% (95% CI, 64.5–70.6%). Grade ≥3 cytokine release syndrome occurred in 2.5% and grade ≥3 ICANS in 9.2%. The 12-month non-relapse mortality rate was 6.1% (95% CI, 4.6–7.8%). These data are highly consistent with the TRANSCEND pivotal trial yet generated in an older, more comorbid population, reinforcing liso-cel's effectiveness and safety profile across a broad real-world LBCL spectrum. A *Blood* editorial by Kamdar contextualizes these findings within the trial-to-practice translational gap for CAR-T in LBCL[^18].

### Radiomic Prognostication in Primary Mediastinal B-cell Lymphoma

A post hoc analysis of 180 treatment-naïve PMBL patients from the LYSA cohort (2007–2017) published in *Blood Advances* demonstrates that baseline FDG PET/CT radiomic parameters provide independent prognostic information beyond the IPI[^19]. A composite 3-parameter radiomic score capturing total metabolic tumor volume (TMTV), maximal interlesion distance (Dmax), and median peripheral centroid distance (medPCD) was independently associated with overall survival (HR 7.76; 95% CI, 1.59–37.74) in multivariable analysis. The composite score outperformed conventional clinical risk factors and identified a high-risk subgroup at diagnosis, although prospective validation is required before clinical implementation.

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

### MAIPI: AI Prognostic Index for MCL Using H&E Histology

A deep learning algorithm (MAIPI — MCL Artificial Intelligence Prognostic Index) trained on H&E images from 428 MCL patients enrolled in clinical trials, validated in an independent cohort of 140 patients treated with immunochemotherapy with or without ibrutinib, was published in *Leukemia*[^20]. MAIPI provides prognostic information independent of MIPI and Ki67 without requiring molecular testing or expert pathologist evaluation. The algorithm self-selects areas of interest within the biopsy and stratifies patients into clinically meaningful risk groups, representing a potential democratizing tool for MCL risk stratification, particularly in resource-limited settings.

### Dose-Reduced ISRT Plus Rituximab in Non-Gastric MALT Lymphoma

A prospective phase 2 trial published in *Leukemia* evaluates 24 Gy involved-site radiotherapy combined with rituximab in early-stage non-gastric MALT lymphoma, though full numerical results were not available in the provided abstract[^21].

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

### AVD Superior to ABVD for Older cHL Patients: Nordic Real-World Cohort

A population-based Nordic registry study of 1,569 older (≥60 years) classical Hodgkin lymphoma patients diagnosed in Sweden, Denmark, and Norway between 2000 and 2021, published in *Blood Advances*, provides compelling real-world evidence for AVD as the preferred frontline backbone over ABVD in this age group[^22]. On multivariable analysis of OS, AVD was associated with improved survival compared to ABVD (HR not provided in abstract but statistically significant), while CHOP trended inferior. For PFS, AVD likewise demonstrated significantly improved outcomes relative to ABVD. The authors conclude that by omitting bleomycin — with its well-established pulmonary toxicity risk in older patients — AVD achieves equivalent or superior disease control with reduced treatment-related morbidity, making it the preferred backbone for novel combinations in elderly cHL. An accompanying *Blood Advances* editorial by Marconi and Ratnasingam contextualizes these findings[^23].

### NLPHL: Combined LP-IPS and Modified GHSG Score Provides Best Risk Stratification

A GHSG analysis of 583 NLPHL patients published in *Haematologica* compares three prognostic scoring systems for nodular lymphocyte-predominant Hodgkin lymphoma[^24]. Cox regression demonstrated that the LP-IPS performed best as a standalone risk score for PFS (HR for high vs. low risk: 2.71; 95% CI, 1.76–4.18) and OS (HR 8.24; 95% CI, 3.70–18.34). When combined with the modified GHSG score, the high-risk/high-risk group showed the most striking outcome disparity (PFS HR 3.74; 95% CI, 2.09–6.70; OS HR 13.52; 95% CI, 5.03–36.35). The LP-IPS is established as the most reliable standalone scoring instrument.

### PLK1 Inhibition Synergizes with Brentuximab Vedotin in CD30+ PTCL

A preclinical study in *Leukemia* demonstrates that the PLK1 inhibitor volasertib synergizes with brentuximab vedotin (BV) in CD30-positive PTCL cell lines and patient-derived xenograft models through sustained mitotic arrest via spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) activation, followed by mitotic catastrophe, apoptosis, and DNA damage[^25]. The combination significantly suppressed tumor growth and prolonged survival in both CDX and PDX models. The mechanistic dependency on SAC activation was confirmed using a selective Mps1 inhibitor. These findings provide a rationale for clinical evaluation of BV-PLK1 inhibitor combinations in relapsed CD30+ PTCL, a population with few effective options.

### Gray Zone Lymphoma: Activity of Checkpoint Inhibitors with or without Brentuximab Vedotin

A case series from *Haematologica* reports notable clinical activity of immune checkpoint inhibitors with or without brentuximab vedotin in relapsed or refractory gray zone lymphoma, though detailed outcome data were not available in the provided abstract[^26].

### Enteropathy-Associated T-cell Lymphoma: Clonal Evolution from Refractory Celiac Disease

A *Haematologica* study integrating NGS-based mutation profiling, copy-number analysis, and T-cell receptor clonotype sequencing in EATL and paired RCD specimens demonstrates that EATL arises via parallel subclonal evolution and selection from locally residing jejunal T-cell populations with restricted TCR repertoires, rather than necessarily from RCD populations in the duodenum[^27]. Only 2 of 5 EATL/RCD pairs showed a clonally related precursor population, challenging the assumption that duodenal RCD universally serves as the direct EATL precursor. These findings have immediate implications for clinical monitoring strategies in RCD patients.

## X. NK/T-cell Lymphoma (Asia Focus)

### ENKTL Epigenomics: DNA Methylation Defines Prognostically Distinct Subgroups

A *Leukemia* methylated DNA immunoprecipitation sequencing (MeDIP-seq) study by Phyu et al. comparing formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded samples from ENKTL, primary EBV-positive nodal T/NK-cell lymphoma (nodal-TNKL), and control tissues defines fundamental epigenetic differences between these entities and identifies clinically actionable ENKTL subgroups[^4]. ENKTL displayed extensive promoter hypermethylation silencing tumor suppressor genes, lineage regulators, and lymphoid signaling genes (including LEF1 and BANK1), with focal hypomethylation of immune- and interferon-responsive genes (e.g., IFITM1). In contrast, nodal-TNKL showed global hypomethylation preferentially affecting cytotoxicity, immune-response, and antigen-presentation pathways, with TET2-mutated cases displaying locus-specific changes. Unsupervised clustering identified two epigenetically distinct ENKTL subgroups: one characterized by higher global methylation, TP53 loss, increased copy number alterations and loss of heterozygosity, and significantly poorer overall survival. This methylation-based classification framework provides a foundation for refined diagnostic separation of ENKTL from nodal-TNKL, novel risk stratification incorporating epigenetic subgroup assignment, and rational selection of epigenetic therapies in the high-methylation/TP53-loss subgroup — an important priority given that dual asparaginase/PD-1-resistant ENKTL (reported last week) carries a median post-resistance OS of only 4.9 months.

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

### Ex Vivo Functional Profiling as a Predictive Biomarker for Bispecific T-cell Engager Response in MM

A *Leukemia* study by Herzberg et al. develops a high-content, image-based ex vivo assay using patient-derived bone marrow mononuclear cells exposed to teclistamab or talquetamab to predict clinical response to bispecific T-cell engagers (TCE)[^28]. Single-cell multiplex immunofluorescence enabled quantification of plasma cell lysis, T-cell expansion, T-cell morphology, and spatial T-cell–plasma cell engagement across patient samples, identifying three functional response phenotypes: non-responder, cytotoxic, and cytotoxic-expansive. A morphologic T-cell activation trajectory from resting to polarized and effector states correlated with ex vivo plasma cell killing efficacy and distinguished productive from abortive immunological synapses. Critically, ex vivo response phenotypes stratified clinical response and time on therapy in patients receiving TCE monotherapy. Given that up to 40% of myeloma patients are primarily refractory to TCE, this functional ex vivo platform represents a meaningful advance toward precision patient selection for bispecific antibody therapy.
