## Summary

This cycle delivers substantive new data in ITP mechanistic biology, a first murine model for congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I, a negative Phase 2a readout in non-transfusion-dependent β-thalassemia, a large whole-genome study defining genetic modifiers of hemoglobin H disease severity, and clarified AIHA/thrombophilia assay performance data relevant to bleeding-disorder and thrombosis workups. First, a gut-microbiota-derived metabolite, indole-3-lactic acid (ILA), is shown to be deficient in both newly diagnosed and refractory ITP patients and to ameliorate thrombocytopenia in an ITP mouse model by suppressing Th17 differentiation and promoting regulatory T-cell expansion, with corresponding reduction in IL-1β, TNF-α, IFN-γ and IL-12/IL-23p40 and upregulation of IL-10, offering a novel microbiome-immune mechanistic axis and a candidate therapeutic strategy for refractory disease[^1]. Second, researchers report the first inducible, erythroid-specific murine model of congenital dyserythropoietic anemia type I using a tamoxifen-activated Gata1-CreERT2-driven Cdan1 deletion, successfully recapitulating anemia, impaired erythroid differentiation, erythroblast cell-cycle disturbances, and the pathognomonic "spongy" heterochromatin phenotype, overcoming the embryonic lethality that had stalled prior constitutive-knockout approaches and establishing a durable platform for CDA-I drug development[^2]. Third, a randomized Phase 2a study of sapablursen, a TMPRSS6-targeting antisense oligonucleotide, in 29 patients with non-transfusion-dependent β-thalassemia failed to meet its primary or secondary endpoints of increased hemoglobin or reduced liver iron content, despite being safe and well tolerated, with elevated baseline liver iron content and serum erythroferrone apparently blunting the expected TMPRSS6-hepcidin pathway response seen in earlier polycythemia vera studies[^3]. Fourth, the largest whole-genome sequencing study of hemoglobin H disease to date (591 patients, 1246 ancestry-matched controls from Southern China) identifies two independent α-globin cluster variants (rs199594877, rs34583256) enriched in patients versus controls, with rs199594877 particularly enriched in Hb Constant Spring carriers, and an HBS1L-MYB locus variant (rs11759553) associated with increased Hb Bart's levels (P = 3.66 × 10⁻¹⁴), paradoxically opposite to its known protective effect in β-hemoglobinopathies[^4]. Fifth, a comprehensive meta-analysis of 11,268 patients across randomized controlled trials finds ferric derisomaltose carries a markedly lower risk of serious or severe hypersensitivity reactions than ferric carboxymaltose or ferumoxytol (SUCRA probability of lowest incidence 91.4% vs 4.5% and 4.2% respectively), a practically important finding for iron deficiency anemia management relevant to NHI-covered IV iron formulary decisions[^5].

## I. Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP)

A *British Journal of Haematology* study provides new mechanistic insight into the gut-immune axis in primary ITP, identifying indole-3-lactic acid (ILA) deficiency in both newly diagnosed and refractory ITP patients alongside an imbalance between Th17 cells and regulatory T cells (Tregs)[^1]. Using liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry for plasma metabolite profiling, flow cytometry for T-cell subset analysis, and an ITP mouse model, the investigators demonstrate that ILA supplementation alleviates thrombocytopenia by inhibiting Th17 differentiation and suppressing IL-1β, TNF-α, IFN-γ and IL-12/IL-23p40 expression, while simultaneously promoting Treg differentiation. In refractory ITP patients, ex vivo ILA exposure reduced Th17 cell frequency, increased the Treg/Th17 ratio, downregulated IL17A and TNFα mRNA, and upregulated IL10 mRNA, directly linking a microbiome-derived tryptophan metabolite to the adaptive immune dysregulation underlying ITP. This adds a plausible, druggable metabolic target to the growing list of non-TPO-RA, non-rituximab strategies under exploration for refractory disease, complementing the BCMA CAR T-cell and SRSF1-LCK splicing findings carried over from the prior two cycles, which remain the most mechanistically advanced recent developments in refractory ITP biology and are not restated here[^6]. Separately, a large AJH genetic study reports that targeted sequencing identifies a genetically defined inborn error of immunity in 7.7% of children undergoing testing for immune cytopenias, spanning both single-lineage and multilineage phenotypes, reinforcing the clinical utility of systematic genetic testing in pediatric immune cytopenia referral pathways[^7].

## III. Aplastic Anemia

A large multicenter AJH analysis of 11,340 patients delineates divergent consequences of BCOR and BCORL1 mutations depending on whether they occur in myeloid neoplasms (MN) versus bone marrow failure syndromes (BMF)[^9]. Among 418 BCOR/L1-mutated patients, those with BMF were younger, more often had a normal karyotype, showed a greater proportion of missense mutations, and had significantly lower variant allele frequencies than MN cases. Single-cell DNA sequencing and longitudinal VAF tracking showed that BCOR/L1 mutations are typically secondary clonal events in MN but primary, ancestral events in BMF, and BMF-associated BCOR/L1 clones frequently contracted or disappeared over time even during disease progression, which instead correlated with increasing overall mutational burden, particularly in RUNX1. Critically for clinical management, BCOR/L1 mutations in BMF were not prognostically adverse and tended to correlate with favorable response to immunosuppressive therapy, in contrast to their association with inferior survival when occurring in MN; RNA sequencing additionally linked reduced BCOR/L1 expression in BMF to reduced HLA class I expression, suggesting an immune-privilege-related mechanism distinct from the oncogenic cooperation seen in MN. This distinction has direct implications for risk-stratifying BCOR/L1 variants detected on next-generation sequencing panels in patients being worked up for aplastic anemia versus overt myeloid neoplasia.

## IV. PNH — Paroxysmal Nocturnal Hemoglobinuria

No new primary PNH efficacy data were indexed this cycle beyond the NTQ5082 Phase 2 study reported last week[^10], which remains the newest clinical trial data in this space. One relevant mechanistic cross-link appeared in the BCOR/L1 paper above: BMF patients with BCOR/L1 mutations showed a higher prevalence of PNH clones than wild-type BMF cases[^9], consistent with the known biological relationship between clonal hematopoiesis, immune-mediated marrow failure, and PNH clonal expansion, though this was a secondary observation rather than the primary focus of that study.

## V. Hemophilia A & B (including gene therapy)

Two RPTH studies this cycle refine the mechanistic and immunogenicity landscape of modern hemophilia therapeutics without superseding last week's FVIII-Aurora and AAV gene therapy findings[^11]. First, an in vitro study evaluating the effect of fitusiran-induced antithrombin lowering on activated protein C (APC)-mediated thrombin suppression in hemophilic plasma models demonstrates that low antithrombin activity (10–25%) blunts APC's ability to suppress thrombin generation in FVIII- or FIX-deficient plasma, with 10–30% of initial peak thrombin persisting despite APC addition, and that FVIII/FIX replacement or bypassing agents further increased residual thrombin generation under low-AT conditions, while FX reduction markedly decreased it[^12]. This provides mechanistic grounding for why fitusiran achieves hemostatic rebalancing and underscores the interplay between AT-lowering strategies and concomitant factor replacement dosing in clinical practice. Second, the GTH-AHA-EMI study assessed anti-drug antibody (ADA) formation against emicizumab in 47 patients with acquired hemophilia A (AHA), finding non-neutralizing ADA in only one patient (2.1%), a lower rate than the approximately 5% reported in congenital hemophilia A, supporting a favorable immunogenicity profile for emicizumab when used as bleeding prophylaxis in AHA, including in settings where immunosuppressive therapy initiation is deliberately postponed[^13].

## VII. Thalassemia (alpha & beta; Taiwan-relevant)

Two major thalassemia studies appear this cycle. The Phase 2a trial of sapablursen, an antisense oligonucleotide targeting TMPRSS6 to raise hepcidin, enrolled 29 patients with non-transfusion-dependent β-thalassemia intermedia across doses from 30 to 160 mg every 4 weeks for up to 2 years, and while safe and well tolerated, failed to meet its primary or secondary endpoints of increased hemoglobin or reduced liver iron content[^3]. Unlike prior phase 1 healthy-volunteer and phase 2 polycythemia vera studies where sapablursen produced dose-dependent serum hepcidin increases, NTDT patients failed to show consistent dose-dependent hepcidin elevation, an effect the authors attribute to elevated baseline liver iron content and serum erythroferrone potentially saturating or bypassing the TMPRSS6-hepcidin regulatory axis — a cautionary signal for other hepcidin-mimetic or TMPRSS6-targeting agents in development for iron-loading anemias. Separately, the largest whole-genome sequencing study of hemoglobin H disease to date, comprising 591 Southern Chinese patients and 1246 ancestry-matched controls, identifies two α-globin cluster variants (rs199594877, rs34583256) associated with disease susceptibility, with rs199594877 enriched specifically among Hb Constant Spring carriers, and reveals six additional loci associated with quantitative hematological and iron-metabolism traits across the broader cohort[^4]. Notably, the HBS1L-MYB (HMIP) locus variant rs11759553 was associated with increased Hb Bart's (γ4) levels (P = 3.66 × 10⁻¹⁴) in Hb H disease, a finding that contrasts with HMIP's well-established beneficial, HbF-raising effect in β-hemoglobinopathies, suggesting divergent genetic modifier biology across different thalassemia genotypes that warrants further functional validation. A complementary AJH longitudinal cohort of 994 adults with β-thalassemia followed for up to 16 years in the Italian Webthal network found overall eGFR decline of −1.13 mL/min/1.73m² per year (95% CI, −1.20 to −1.07), steeper in transfusion-dependent than non-transfusion-dependent patients, with time-varying hemoglobin exposure differentially associated with renal trajectory depending on transfusion phenotype, though chelation regimen did not independently affect slope in the monotherapy subgroup[^14].

## IX. Red Cell Disorders (G6PD, PK deficiency, hereditary spherocytosis)

A prospective functional genomics study of RHD coding-region splicing variants screened all possible coding substitutions using SpliceAI and SPiP in silico tools, functionally validating the top 25 scoring single nucleotide variants and finding that 11 either partially (8) or completely (3) disrupted splicing[^15]. Most notably, the study redefines the mechanism underlying the RHD weak D type 3 allele, showing that the c.8C>G variant dramatically impairs splicing both in vitro and in vivo, rather than acting through the previously presumed p.(Ser3Cys) amino acid substitution directly affecting RhD membrane expression. This has practical implications for transfusion medicine genotyping algorithms that currently interpret weak D type 3 purely at the protein level, and illustrates the broader principle that a meaningful fraction of RHD coding variants exert their phenotypic effect through cryptic splice disruption rather than straightforward missense mechanisms, relevant to Rh genotyping discordance investigations.

## X. Iron Deficiency & Nutritional Anemias

A Bayesian network meta-analysis spanning 11,268 patients from randomized controlled trials provides the most comprehensive comparative hypersensitivity safety analysis yet for the three dominant high-dose IV iron formulations[^5]. Serious or severe hypersensitivity reactions under the anaphylactic reaction Standardized MedDRA Query occurred in 0.14% of patients receiving ferric derisomaltose (FDI), 0.59% with ferric carboxymaltose (FCM), and 0.50% with ferumoxytol (FER); the pooled relative risk favored FDI over FCM (0.21; 95% credible interval, 0.03–1.73) and over FER (0.07; 95% CrI, 0.00–1.93), and surface-under-cumulative-ranking-curve analysis assigned FDI a 91.4% probability of having the lowest hypersensitivity incidence among the three agents. A companion commentary piece further interrogates the evolving comparative safety narrative around ferric carboxymaltose specifically[^16]. Separately, a cross-sectional screening study of 201 pregnant individuals at first obstetric visit found a 59% prevalence of iron deficiency (54% non-anemic ID, 5% iron deficiency anemia), with a predictive model based on clinical risk factors (higher BMI, pagophagia, prior OTC iron use) achieving only modest discrimination (AUC 0.68), supporting a shift toward universal laboratory ferritin screening in early pregnancy rather than reliance on symptom-based selective testing[^17].

## XI. Thrombosis & Coagulation (VTE, APS, inherited thrombophilia)

A multicenter retrospective French cohort study characterized the performance of the STA-Stachrom-ATIII assay for detecting genetically confirmed inherited antithrombin deficiency across 539 patients (409 probands, 130 relatives), finding the assay reliably detected most heparin-binding-site (HBS) variants, including subtypes frequently missed by other commercial assays, but returned normal activity (≥80%) in 35 patients (6.5%) spanning 11 of 67 identified variants (16.4%), reinforcing that genetic testing remains warranted in patients with strong personal or family thrombosis history despite normal AT activity[^18]. In a related thrombophilia methodology commentary, authors discuss the interpretation challenges inherent in recurrence-risk estimates for thrombophilic disorders, cautioning against overreliance on point estimates from heterogeneous historical cohorts[^19]. On the antiphospholipid syndrome front, a prospective single-center translational study of 88 APS patients found soluble C5b-9 and factor Bb complement activation markers elevated in 79.5% and 58% of patients respectively at baseline, with persistent Bb elevation (68.6% of patients at follow-up) independently associated with triple antiphospholipid antibody positivity and, in univariable analysis, with prior catastrophic APS, relapse, and microvascular involvement; 5 of 7 patients who developed incident APS manifestations during follow-up were Bb-positive, suggesting a candidate biomarker for identifying high-risk APS patients warranting closer surveillance[^20]. In MPN-associated thrombosis, a large international GASTRO-MPN consortium retrospective cohort of 757 patients with myeloproliferative neoplasm-associated splanchnic vein thrombosis found that cytoreductive therapy, modeled as a time-dependent covariate, was associated with reduced recurrent thrombosis risk (HR 0.67; 95% CI, 0.50–0.89; P = 0.01), driven predominantly by reduced splanchnic vein extension/recurrence (HR 0.62; 95% CI, 0.42–0.90; P = 0.01) rather than extra-splanchnic recurrence (HR 0.97; 95% CI, 0.72–1.30; P = 0.82), over a median follow-up of 7.4 years[^21]. Finally, a systematic review and meta-analysis of progestin-only contraceptives and first venous thrombotic event found a pooled risk ratio of 3.14 (95% CI, 2.18–4.53) for injectable progestin-only contraceptives, in contrast to a reduced risk with intrauterine devices (0.77; 95% CI, 0.70–0.85) and null association with oral progestin-only pills (0.92; 95% CI, 0.70–1.27), while progestin-only implants showed a modestly increased risk (1.27; 95% CI, 1.08–1.49) warranting further study[^22].

## XIII. Taiwan Clinical Context

Taiwan's carrier prevalence of approximately 4–5% for alpha-thalassemia and 1–2% for beta-thalassemia means the new whole-genome modifier data from the Southern Chinese Hb H disease cohort are directly relevant to local practice, given close genetic ancestry overlap between Southern Chinese and Taiwanese Han populations[^4]; the identification of α-globin cluster variants enriched in Hb Constant Spring carriers may eventually inform genetic counseling nuance for Taiwanese families with this relatively common non-deletional variant. The negative sapablursen NTDT trial result is a useful cautionary reference for Taiwanese centers (NTUH, CGMH, VGH) considering enrollment in future TMPRSS6-targeting or hepcidin-mimetic trials, given that Taiwan has an active NTDT population managed largely with iron chelation and occasional transfusion rather than curative options[^3]. On iron deficiency anemia, the comparative hypersensitivity meta-analysis favoring ferric derisomaltose has direct NHI formulary relevance, as ferric carboxymaltose is the most widely reimbursed high-dose IV iron agent in Taiwan; centers should weigh this comparative safety signal when selecting formulations for patients with prior infusion reactions[^5]. For hemophilia care, emicizumab remains NHI-reimbursed for congenital hemophilia A with inhibitors in Taiwan, and the new low ADA incidence data in acquired hemophilia A may support broader local uptake of emicizumab as bridging prophylaxis in the rarer AHA population managed at CGMH and NTUH immunology-hematology joint clinics[^13].

## XIV. Key Takeaways

The ILA-Th17/Treg axis in ITP and the CDA-I inducible mouse model both represent genuine mechanistic advances that open new translational avenues rather than incremental trial updates, while the sapablursen failure in NTDT is an important negative result tempering enthusiasm for TMPRSS6-targeting strategies in iron-loading thalassemia syndromes. The BCOR/L1 mutation study meaningfully refines risk stratification at the aplastic anemia-MDS boundary by demonstrating divergent clonal dynamics and prognostic implications depending on disease context, directly actionable for NGS panel interpretation in bone marrow failure workups. On the coagulation side, the antithrombin assay performance data and APC-thrombin generation mechanistic work both carry direct implications for thrombophilia diagnostics and fitusiran-era hemophilia management, respectively, while the comparative IV iron hypersensitivity meta-analysis offers an immediately actionable formulary consideration.
