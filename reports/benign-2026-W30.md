## Summary

The five most clinically actionable signals this week are as follows. First, the final 2-year analysis of the phase III EBMT-SAAWP RACE trial (n = 197), published in *AJH* by Risitano et al., demonstrates that the addition of eltrombopag to horse antithymocyte globulin plus cyclosporine A as front-line therapy for severe aplastic anemia (SAA) yields a 2-year complete response rate of 62.4% versus 35.3% for standard immunosuppression alone (p < 0.001), with a 2-year disease-free survival of 56% versus 37% (adjusted HR 0.49; p < 0.001), without an increased risk of secondary myeloid malignancies — establishing this triplet as the evidence-based standard of care[^1]. Second, a *Blood* study by Al-Samkari et al. from the CHORUS registry (n = 600) characterises the real-world clinical spectrum of hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia (HHT), finding that 68% of patients developed iron deficiency and/or anemia, 41% required intravenous iron, and 25% required red cell transfusions, while serious thromboembolic and cardiopulmonary complications were substantially more prevalent than previously recognised — data that reframe HHT as a high-burden systemic disorder demanding multidisciplinary haematological management[^2]. Third, a *BJH* post-hoc analysis of the phase 3b AVATAR trial by Grace et al. (n = 54 paediatric ITP patients) demonstrates that avatrombopag produced a platelet response of ≥50 × 10⁹/L in 75.9% of patients by day 22 versus 0% for placebo, and that protocol-mandated dose holds triggered by platelet counts of 250–400 × 10⁹/L — a range not requiring dose modification in routine clinical practice — were the primary driver of non-durable responses in this refractory paediatric cohort[^3]. Fourth, a *BJH* case report by Subhan et al. addresses recurrent thrombocytopenia in immune thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (iTTP) in the caplacizumab era, providing contemporary clinical context for this management challenge[^4]. Fifth, a *Blood* study by Zeng et al. identifies biallelic loss-of-function mutations in *BPNT1* as the causal mechanism of a previously unexplained form of recurrent vitamin B12–dependent megaloblastic anemia, demonstrating that BPNT1 deficiency leads to accumulation of 3′-phosphoadenosine 5′-phosphate (PAP), impairs ribosome biogenesis, and reduces ileal cubilin/amnionless receptor complex expression in murine models — defining a new genetic entity in the diagnostic workup of unexplained megaloblastic anemia[^5].

## I. Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP)

A *BJH* post-hoc analysis of the phase 3b AVATAR trial by Grace et al. provides important nuance on the efficacy of avatrombopag in paediatric ITP[^3]. The study enrolled children with persistent or chronic ITP, a heavily pre-treated and refractory population, in whom avatrombopag was compared against placebo during the 12-week core phase (NCT04516967). Avatrombopag achieved a platelet response, defined as two consecutive platelet counts ≥50 × 10⁹/L, in 70.4% of patients by day 15 and in 75.9% by day 22, compared with 0% in the placebo group at both time points. Among the 92.6% of avatrombopag-treated patients (50/54) who achieved a clinically meaningful platelet response (≥30 × 10⁹/L), the mean percentage of time with response was 62.5%. Notably, 61.1% of patients experienced at least one protocol-specified dose hold triggered by a platelet count exceeding 250 × 10⁹/L, and 78.3% of non-durable responders had at least one such dose hold occurring at a count in the 250–400 × 10⁹/L range — a threshold that would not ordinarily prompt a dose hold in real-world clinical practice per current product labelling. The authors conclude that the formal trial protocol may have systematically underestimated real-world response durability by enforcing dose interruptions at platelet counts that are clinically safe, and suggest that future paediatric ITP trials align dose-hold thresholds with clinical practice standards. These findings are directly relevant to prescribers managing paediatric ITP at NCKUH, where avatrombopag is increasingly considered for children refractory to corticosteroids and intravenous immunoglobulin.

## II. TTP / HUS / Thrombotic Microangiopathy

A *BJH* report by Subhan et al. examines the clinical problem of recurrent thrombocytopenia in patients with immune thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura managed in the caplacizumab era[^4]. The study characterises contemporary patterns of relapse and recurrence against the backdrop of caplacizumab's widespread use as both an acute and relapse-prevention strategy in iTTP. While the complete quantitative dataset was not fully available in the current feed, this publication adds to an expanding body of real-world evidence describing residual disease recurrence risk even with optimised ADAMTS13 activity monitoring and early caplacizumab deployment. This signal should be read in conjunction with the W29 observation from Chaturvedi regarding the methodological constraints of research in ultra-rare diseases such as iTTP.

## III. Aplastic Anemia

The final 2-year analysis of the EBMT-SAAWP RACE phase III randomised superiority trial (NCT02009747), published in *AJH* by Risitano et al., represents the most mature randomised data yet available for eltrombopag-augmented immunosuppressive therapy in treatment-naïve SAA[^1]. One hundred ninety-seven patients were randomised to standard immunosuppression (hATG 40 mg/kg × 4 days plus cyclosporine A 5 mg/kg/day; arm A, n = 101) or standard immunosuppression plus eltrombopag 150 mg/day from day +14 for up to 6 months (arm B, n = 96). At a median follow-up of 23.2 months, the 2-year cumulative incidence of complete response was 62.4% in arm B versus 35.3% in arm A (p < 0.001). The 2-year overall survival was 91% in arm B and 86% in arm A (p = 0.081), with an adjusted HR of 0.54 (p = 0.064), indicating a non-statistically significant survival trend favouring eltrombopag addition at this follow-up duration. Both disease-free survival (56% vs. 37%; adjusted HR 0.49; p < 0.001) and event-free survival (48% vs. 32%; adjusted HR 0.54; p < 0.001) were significantly superior in arm B. The cumulative incidence of relapse was comparable between arms, and evolution to clinical paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH) was 8% in arm A versus 1% in arm B (p = 0.041), favouring the eltrombopag arm. Clonal evolution risk remained negligible across both arms, with only three total patients developing karyotypic abnormalities (one in arm A, two in arm B). These 2-year results comprehensively confirm the short-term hematological response advantage of the eltrombopag triplet while providing reassurance that the secondary myeloid malignancy signal previously hypothesised for thrombopoietin receptor agonists is not realised at this follow-up interval in SAA. For clinicians at NCKUH, these data reinforce eltrombopag addition to hATG/CsA as the front-line standard of care for treatment-naïve SAA in patients lacking a matched sibling donor.

A *Blood* case report by Zhang and Wang documents an unusual complication of HSCT performed for aplastic anemia: vitreoretinal post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder (PTLD), a rare but vision-threatening event warranting awareness among transplant clinicians managing patients with AA[^6].

## IV. PNH — Paroxysmal Nocturnal Hemoglobinuria

The RACE trial 2-year analysis reported above (Risitano et al.) provides new clinical data pertinent to PNH surveillance in SAA: the cumulative incidence of evolution to clinical PNH was significantly lower in the eltrombopag arm (1% vs. 8%; p = 0.041)[^1]. This suggests that superior haematological recovery driven by eltrombopag-augmented immunosuppression may reduce the selective pressure favouring outgrowth of PNH clones in the recovering marrow, though the mechanistic basis of this observation remains speculative at present.

## V. Hemophilia A & B (including gene therapy)

A *BJH* educational correspondence by Lau, Srivatsa, and Mackman provides a mechanistic framework explaining why joints and skeletal muscle are the predominant bleeding sites in hemophilia A and B[^7]. The authors present a two-component model: first, the formation of the platelet-fibrin haemostatic plug through vascular injury-driven exposure of von Willebrand factor and tissue factor (TF), and second, the anatomical correlation between TF expression density and bleeding phenotype. Vital organs such as the brain, heart, and lungs exhibit high TF expression that provides additional haemostatic protection through the extrinsic pathway, while the uniformly low TF expression in synovial joints and skeletal muscle renders these sites disproportionately reliant on FVIII- and FIX-dependent intrinsic pathway amplification. In patients with hemophilia A or B, this deficiency creates a site-specific haemostatic vulnerability that explains the characteristic musculoskeletal bleeding phenotype — a conceptual framework with direct educational and counselling value for hemophilia patients and their treating physicians.

The ISTH News feed this week featured the 2024 ISTH Clinical Practice Guideline for Treatment of Congenital Hemophilia A and B as a highlighted resource, and the ISTH-EAHAD partnership agreement to advance shared hemophilia and bleeding disorder goals (signed June 2025) was also referenced. These are archival items and do not constitute new primary data.

## VII. Thalassemia (alpha & beta; Taiwan-relevant)

A *BJH* review by Joshi, Natarajan, Dickerson, and Voit surveys the emerging landscape of gene therapy for inherited non-haemoglobinopathy erythroid disorders, including Diamond-Blackfan anemia, pyruvate kinase deficiency, X-linked sideroblastic anemia, congenital erythropoietic porphyria, and congenital dyserythropoietic anemia[^8]. The authors contextualise these efforts against the backdrop of recently FDA-approved gene therapies for sickle cell disease and beta-thalassaemia, noting that the translational pipeline for non-haemoglobinopathy erythroid disorders remains largely preclinical. Convergent biological themes — including shared conditioning challenges, erythroid-specific promoter requirements, and engraftment optimisation — are identified as potential accelerators of cross-disease translation. While this review does not report primary clinical trial data, it is directly relevant to the NCKUH thalassemia programme, where gene therapy eligibility counselling is becoming an increasingly frequent component of pre-transplant discussions for patients with transfusion-dependent beta-thalassemia.

## IX. Red Cell Disorders (G6PD, PK deficiency, hereditary spherocytosis)

A *BJH* molecular genetics study by Marin et al. reports deep intronic *ANK1* variants causing pseudo-exon inclusion in hereditary spherocytosis, with functional characterisation by whole-genome sequencing[^9]. Ankyrin-1 (ANK1) mutations are the most common cause of autosomal dominant hereditary spherocytosis, and this work extends the mutational spectrum to include cryptic splice-site variants in deep intronic regions that escape detection by standard exon-focused sequencing panels. The identification of pseudo-exon inclusion events as a pathogenic mechanism has immediate implications for the diagnostic workup of patients with clinical hereditary spherocytosis but no causative variant identified on standard next-generation sequencing: whole-genome sequencing with RNA-level validation should be considered in such cases. For the Taiwan context, where hereditary spherocytosis cases are managed primarily by the haematology and paediatric haematology services, this report argues for consideration of deeper sequencing strategies in diagnostically unresolved cases.

## X. Iron Deficiency & Nutritional Anemias

A *Blood* paper by Zeng et al. identifies biallelic loss-of-function mutations in *BPNT1* as the molecular basis of a previously uncharacterised form of recurrent vitamin B12–dependent megaloblastic anemia in three affected patients[^5]. Bisphosphate nucleotidase 1 (BPNT1) is a cytosolic enzyme that dephosphorylates 3′-phosphoadenosine 5′-phosphate (PAP), a byproduct of sulfonation reactions. The authors demonstrate mechanistically that BPNT1 deficiency leads to PAP accumulation, which impairs ribosome biogenesis, and that *Bpnt1*-null mice exhibit reduced ileal expression of the cubilin/amnionless (CUBN/AMN) receptor complex — the endocytic machinery responsible for cobalamin absorption in the terminal ileum. The clinical consequence is a functionally acquired defect in vitamin B12 uptake that manifests as megaloblastic anemia responsive to parenteral cobalamin supplementation. This genetic entity should now be considered in the differential diagnosis of patients with recurrent megaloblastic anemia, elevated methylmalonic acid and homocysteine, and a B12-responsive phenotype in whom conventional causes (dietary insufficiency, intrinsic factor antibodies, pernicious anemia, CUBN/AMN mutations) have been excluded. Separately, the CHORUS registry data by Al-Samkari et al. (detailed in the Bleeding Disorders section below) document a high prevalence of iron deficiency anemia in HHT, with 68% of 600 enrolled patients developing iron deficiency and/or anemia, 41% requiring intravenous iron supplementation, and 25% requiring red cell transfusions[^2].

## XI. Thrombosis & Coagulation (VTE, APS, inherited thrombophilia)

The CHORUS registry initial report, published in *Blood* by Al-Samkari et al., describes the clinical spectrum of HHT in the first 600 participants of this prospective, 15-centre, longitudinal US national registry (NCT06259292)[^2]. Participants had a median age of 53 years (range 0–88) and were 60% female. Despite the majority developing typical HHT manifestations by age 13 years, 63% were not diagnosed until mid-to-late adulthood, with a mean interval between first symptoms and diagnosis exceeding two decades — a gap during which substantial and preventable morbidity occurs, including early intracranial hemorrhage. Recurrent spontaneous epistaxis occurred in 95% of participants, chronic gastrointestinal bleeding in 30%, and heavy menstrual bleeding in 35% of post-menarche females, resulting in moderate-to-severe mucosal bleeding in 76% of participants overall. Iron deficiency and/or anemia were documented in 68%, with 41% requiring intravenous iron and 25% requiring red cell transfusions. Beyond bleeding, serious vascular complications of solid-organ arteriovenous malformations were more prevalent than previously appreciated in registry-based estimates: intracranial hemorrhage occurred in 3%, pulmonary hemorrhage in 2%, venous thromboembolism in 7%, arterial thromboembolism in 11%, heart failure in 7%, and pulmonary hypertension in 7%. The paradoxical thromboembolic burden — occurring in a condition defined by hemorrhagic vasculopathy — underscores the bidirectional haemostatic dysregulation characteristic of HHT. These CHORUS data establish reliable, real-world prevalence estimates for the full spectrum of HHT manifestations, directly informing multidisciplinary surveillance protocols and reinforcing the need for proactive iron replacement and early vascular screening in this population. Clinicians at NCKUH managing patients with HHT should note that the mean diagnostic delay of more than 20 years, observed in a well-resourced healthcare system, suggests that an active clinical screening programme for patients presenting with recurrent epistaxis and a family history is warranted.

## XIII. Taiwan Clinical Context

Several findings from this week's report have specific relevance to clinical practice at NCKUH and affiliated centres in Taiwan. The RACE 2-year final analysis provides definitive evidence supporting eltrombopag addition to hATG/CsA as first-line therapy for SAA[^1]; eltrombopag (Revolade) holds NHI coverage in Taiwan for SAA refractory to immunosuppressive therapy, but uptake of the front-line triplet regimen varies across centres. Centres managing newly diagnosed SAA should review NHI reimbursement criteria in light of this mature phase III evidence to ensure eligible patients receive the superior regimen at first line. The BPNT1 megaloblastic anemia discovery[^5] is relevant to the NCKUH genetics and haematology services: patients from Taiwan with recurrent unexplained B12-responsive megaloblastic anemia without intrinsic factor antibodies should now have *BPNT1* included in panel sequencing.

Regarding thalassemia, Taiwan's carrier prevalence (approximately 4–5% for alpha-thalassemia and 1–2% for beta-thalassemia) positions the NCKUH paediatric and adult haematology thalassemia programmes as important stakeholders in the gene therapy landscape reviewed by Joshi et al.[^8] The approved gene therapy products for beta-thalassemia (betibeglogene autoleucel/Zynteglo and lovotibeglogene autotemcel/Lyfgenia) are currently not NHI-reimbursed in Taiwan; the NCKUH programme continues to offer HSCT as the standard curative modality.

The HHT CHORUS data[^2] highlight the underdiagnosis problem that is also prevalent in Taiwan, where HHT is rarely considered in initial otolaryngological or gastroenterological consultations for recurrent epistaxis or GI bleeding. Haematologists at NCKUH should maintain a low threshold for HHT workup — including genetic testing for *ENG*, *ACVRL1*, and *SMAD4* mutations — in patients presenting with recurrent epistaxis and unexplained iron deficiency anemia, particularly those with a family history of vascular malformations.

## XIV. Key Takeaways

The four most practice-changing results from this week's literature are as follows. First, the RACE trial 2-year final data confirm the hATG + CsA + eltrombopag triplet as the front-line standard for treatment-naïve SAA, with a 27-percentage-point absolute improvement in 2-year complete response rate (62.4% vs. 35.3%) and no excess clonal evolution signal at this time point[^1]. Second, the CHORUS registry comprehensively quantifies the systemic burden of HHT across 600 US patients, revealing that iron deficiency anemia, thromboembolism, and cardiopulmonary complications are far more prevalent than guideline-based estimates suggested — demanding proactive multidisciplinary management[^2]. Third, the AVATAR post-hoc analysis demonstrates that the paediatric ITP trial protocol systematically underestimated avatrombopag's real-world efficacy by mandating dose holds at platelet counts (250–400 × 10⁹/L) that would not prompt interruption in clinical practice, with 78.3% of non-durable responders having experienced such artificially imposed holds[^3]. Fourth, biallelic *BPNT1* loss-of-function mutations represent a newly defined monogenic cause of recurrent vitamin B12–dependent megaloblastic anemia, expanding the differential diagnosis for haematologists evaluating unexplained megaloblastic phenotypes with no conventional aetiology[^5].
