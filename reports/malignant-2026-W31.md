## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

### OPTI-AML: Prospective Comparison of 28-day versus 14-day Venetoclax Induction with Azacitidine

The standard azacitidine plus venetoclax (Aza/Ven) regimen employs a 28-day venetoclax schedule, which is associated with prolonged cytopenias, frequent dose reductions, and cycle delays in older and unfit patients with AML. Retrospective data had suggested that abbreviated venetoclax dosing may preserve efficacy while reducing toxicity, but prospective evidence was lacking. The OPTI-AML trial (NCT03013998), a prospective randomized phase 2 study published in *Blood* this week, enrolled 169 patients aged ≥60 years with newly diagnosed AML to receive either the standard 28-day venetoclax schedule (AV28, n = 83) or a 14-day schedule (AV14, n = 86), both combined with azacitidine 75 mg/m² on days 1–7 for cycles 1–2[^2]. The primary endpoint was CR rate across two cycles of therapy. CR was achieved in 49.4% of patients receiving AV28 versus 43.0% in the AV14 arm, a difference of 6.4% (90% CI, −6.1% to 19.0%), which did not meet the prespecified non-inferiority margin. However, the confidence interval crossed zero, indicating the CR rates were not statistically significantly different. Composite CR rates (CR + CRi + MLFS) were 80.7% for AV28 versus 68.6% for AV14, and MRD negativity rates were virtually identical at 77.6% and 76.5%, respectively. Notably, marked subgroup heterogeneity emerged: patients harboring NPM1 or IDH2 mutations showed a substantially higher CR rate with AV28 (60.9% vs. 33.3%), while CR rates were equivalent (approximately 45%) in all other genomic subgroups. Although AV28 was associated with more frequent treatment interruptions, rates of grade ≥3 adverse events, count recovery after cycle 2, and early mortality did not differ meaningfully between arms. These data suggest that in a genomically unselected population of older AML patients, abbreviated venetoclax dosing cannot be considered non-inferior during induction, though certain biomarker-unselected subgroups—particularly those without NPM1 or IDH2 mutations—may tolerate AV14 with comparable outcomes. Validation in larger, molecularly stratified prospective studies is warranted, especially as triplet regimens incorporating targeted agents evolve.

### Regional Practice Variation Explains Geographic Heterogeneity in MORPHO Trial: Post-HCT Gilteritinib Benefit

A post-hoc analysis of BMT CTN 1506 (MORPHO), published in *Blood Advances*, provides new mechanistic insight into why post-HCT gilteritinib maintenance appeared to benefit North American but not European or Asian patients with FLT3-ITD AML[^6]. The analysis focused on three key variables: time from AML diagnosis to HCT, pre-HCT FLT3 inhibitor use, and pre-HCT FLT3-ITD MRD levels. Patients transplanted within 120 days from AML diagnosis and/or those treated with FLT3 inhibition pre-HCT were more likely to derive survival benefit from post-HCT gilteritinib. Critically, pre-HCT MRD levels were significantly higher in patients transplanted within 120 days of diagnosis (p = 0.001) and in those treated with FLT3 inhibition pre-HCT and transplanted early (p = 0.008). Successive courses of chemotherapy plus FLT3 inhibition prior to HCT were associated with progressively lower pre-HCT MRD, such that patients receiving more treatment cycles prior to transplantation may have sufficiently low residual disease to not require post-HCT FLT3 inhibitor maintenance. The investigators conclude that geographic differences in transplantation practice—specifically in time-to-transplant and pre-HCT FLT3 inhibitor use—likely account for the regional divergence in benefit from post-HCT gilteritinib, rather than biological differences between populations. These data have immediate implications for Asian centers (including those in Taiwan), where longer pre-HCT consolidation strategies may reduce MRD sufficiently to obviate maintenance therapy, though at the potential cost of increased early progression risk.

### G3BP2-ELF1-MCL1 Axis Confers Venetoclax Resistance in AML

A study published in *Leukemia* identifies the RNA-binding protein G3BP2 as a novel mediator of venetoclax resistance in AML, operating through a non-canonical transcriptional mechanism[^7]. Using integrated transcriptomic and proteomic analyses across multiple AML cohorts and cell lines, the authors demonstrate that G3BP2 expression is upregulated in venetoclax-resistant AML and correlates with poor clinical outcomes. Mechanistically, G3BP2 does not directly interact with MCL1 protein; instead, it binds and stabilizes ELF1 mRNA, and the transcription factor ELF1 in turn drives MCL1 promoter-mediated transcription. G3BP2 inhibition with C108, a small-molecule inhibitor, reduced MCL1 protein expression and synergized with venetoclax in primary AML cells and patient-derived xenografts. The combination of C108 and venetoclax significantly prolonged overall survival in AML model mice compared with either agent alone. These data define a druggable G3BP2→ELF1→MCL1 axis as a resistance node in Aza/Ven-treated AML, with potential therapeutic implications for patients who progress after venetoclax-based regimens.

### Adaptor-Mediated p38 MAPK Rewiring Sustains Gilteritinib Resistance

A *Leukemia* study published this week uses an unbiased kinome-wide CRISPR-Cas9 screen to identify a non-canonical resistance mechanism to gilteritinib in FLT3-mutated AML[^8]. Rather than the canonical ERK pathway, p38α (MAPK14) emerged as the predominant dependency in gilteritinib-resistant cells. Mechanistically, resistant cells exhibited adaptor-mediated rewiring wherein p46-SHC1 supported an FLT3-associated MKK3/6–p38 signaling module that sustained MYC expression and leukemic survival despite FLT3 inhibition. Pharmacologic inhibition of p38 enhanced gilteritinib sensitivity and synergized with FLT3 inhibition in both cell line and in vivo models. These findings provide a mechanistic rationale for the limited efficacy of MEK/ERK-directed combination strategies in the FLT3 inhibitor-resistant setting and suggest that targeting stress-adaptive p38 signaling may restore gilteritinib responsiveness.

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

### Subtype-Specific Relapse Patterns and MRD Prognostic Relevance in Pediatric ALL

A study published in *Leukemia* revisited classical genetic subtypes in pediatric ALL to characterize subtype-specific relapse patterns and the MRD-prognostic interaction[^9]. The analysis identified distinct relapse biology across cytogenetic subgroups, with MRD retaining independent prognostic relevance in a subtype-dependent manner. While specific HR values were not available in the abstract, the study's key finding is that MRD assessment must be interpreted within the context of the genetic subtype, as the prognostic weight of MRD negativity varies across cytogenetic categories. This has implications for risk-adapted treatment protocols, supporting subtype-stratified MRD thresholds rather than uniform cutoffs.

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

### OPTIC Trial: Five-Year Long-Term Follow-Up Confirms Benefit of Response-Based Ponatinib Dosing

The five-year outcomes of the randomized phase 2 OPTIC trial (NCT02467270) are reported in *Leukemia*, providing the most mature efficacy and safety data for response-based ponatinib dosing in tyrosine kinase inhibitor-resistant or T315I-positive chronic-phase CML (CP-CML)[^3]. Among 283 patients randomized to starting doses of 45 mg, 30 mg, or 15 mg once daily (with dose reduction to 15 mg in the 45-mg and 30-mg cohorts upon achieving ≤1% BCR::ABL1IS), median follow-up was 75–78 months. By 5 years, 60%, 41%, and 40% of patients in the 45-mg, 30-mg, and 15-mg cohorts, respectively, achieved ≤1% BCR::ABL1IS. Five-year PFS rates were 63%, 57%, and 60%, respectively, and OS exceeded 80% across all three cohorts. Among patients with the T315I mutation, 5-year rates of ≤1% BCR::ABL1IS, PFS, and OS were highest in the 45-mg cohort. Exposure-adjusted rates of adjudicated arterial occlusive events (AOEs) were 4.1, 3.8, and 2.0 patients per 100 patient-years in the 45-mg, 30-mg, and 15-mg cohorts respectively, a finding that underscores the cardiovascular risk-benefit advantage of the response-adapted dosing strategy. These long-term data reinforce the response-based ponatinib approach as the preferred strategy in third-line CP-CML, particularly in T315I-positive patients who derive the greatest absolute benefit from the higher starting dose.

### BCR::ABL1 International Scale Standardization: 15-Year Real-World Validation

A 15-year analysis of BCR::ABL1 proficiency testing data from over 200 clinical laboratories, published in *Leukemia*, provides compelling real-world validation that International Scale (IS)-based reporting is the principal driver of enhanced inter-laboratory precision in BCR::ABL1 quantitation[^10]. IS adoption increased from 8% of laboratories in 2009 to 86% by 2015 and exceeded 99% by 2019. This temporal adoption was correlated with a progressive, significant reduction in inter-laboratory standard deviations across high-, intermediate-, and low-transcript samples (high-sample r = −0.73, p = 0.004; intermediate-sample r = −0.71, p = 0.022; low-sample r = −0.84, p = 0.002), with inter-lab SDs reaching a nadir of approximately 0.2 by 2019. No other variable—including reference gene selection, RQ-PCR reagent choice, assay type, or practice setting—correlated with this improvement. These findings affirm that IS standardization is essential for clinically meaningful BCR::ABL1 monitoring and should be universally mandated, including at all CML monitoring laboratories in Taiwan.

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

### TRANSCEND CLL 004: Primary Results of Liso-cel Plus Ibrutinib in Relapsed/Refractory CLL/SLL

The primary analysis of the phase 1/2, open-label TRANSCEND CLL 004 study (NCT03331198/NCT03435796) is published in *Blood* this week, presenting results from the liso-cel plus ibrutinib combination cohort[^1]. Among 56 patients who received concurrent ibrutinib and liso-cel, the median age was 64.5 years (range, 44–77), 98% had high-risk cytogenetics, 55% had experienced both BTKi and venetoclax failure, and the median number of prior therapies was 5 (range, 1–13). Median follow-up was 24.8 months (range, 3.1–51.8). At dose level 2 (DL2; 100 × 10⁶ CAR T-positive cells, n = 51), the CR/CRi rate was 45% (95% CI, 31–60) and the overall response rate was 86% (95% CI, 74–94). Median duration of response was not reached (95% CI, 28.7–not reached) for patients achieving CR/CRi, and 41.4 months (95% CI, 23.3–not reached) for all responders. Median PFS was 31.4 months (95% CI, 20.1–not reached). The most common grade ≥3 treatment-emergent adverse events were neutropenia (52%) and anemia (41%). Cytokine release syndrome (CRS) was reported in 80% of patients (grade 3 in 4%; no grade 4/5), and neurological events (ICANS) occurred in 41% (grade 3/4 in 11%; no grade 5). Ibrutinib-related adverse events were reported in 68% of patients (grade 3/4 in 43%). These data establish liso-cel plus ibrutinib as an efficacious regimen for heavily pre-treated CLL/SLL, including the difficult-to-treat population with prior BTKi and venetoclax failure, with an expected and manageable safety profile.

### PLCG2 Exon-Skipped Variants as Novel Resistance Mechanisms to BTK Inhibition in CLL

A *Blood Advances* study delineates novel PLCG2 splice-site alterations and exon-skipped variants as resistance mechanisms to BTK inhibitor (BTKi) therapy in CLL, beyond the classical BTK or PLCG2 point mutations[^11]. A splice site mutation (c.2236-1G>T) was identified in REC-1 cells that acquired resistance to tirabrutinib through long-term treatment, generating the exon-skipped variant Δ21 with enhanced phospholipase activity. An identical deletion at the same splice site was identified in a CLL patient progressing on ibrutinib. Analysis of primary CLL samples additionally identified widespread expression of Δ22 and Δ20-22 exon-skipped variants; the active Δ20-22 variant was expressed predominantly in CLL versus healthy donor samples (p not specified). Importantly, venetoclax treatment reduced Δ20-22 expression (p = 0.02), whereas ibrutinib treatment did not, suggesting these variants may persist and confer BTKi resistance while remaining sensitive to venetoclax selection pressure. These findings have immediate clinical implications for sequencing decisions in CLL and inform the mechanistic rationale for venetoclax-based therapy following BTKi failure.

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

### Clinical and Genomic Convergence of High-Risk CCUS and Lower-Risk MDS

A prospective study of 409 patients with clonal cytopenia of undetermined significance (CCUS) and 241 with lower-risk MDS (LR-MDS), published in *Blood*, applies two independent molecular risk-stratification models—the Clonal Hematopoiesis Risk Score (CHRS) and the Clonal Cytopenia Risk Score (CCRS)—to reveal that high-risk CCUS exhibits clinical and event-rate profiles indistinguishable from LR-MDS[^12]. Harmonized diagnostic criteria, centralized pathology review, and uniform genomic profiling were applied across all 650 patients. Patients classified as high-risk CCUS by either CHRS or CCRS demonstrated event rates comparable to LR-MDS, while those with low- or intermediate-risk CCUS had markedly improved outcomes supporting conservative management. The authors propose that high-risk CCUS patients should be considered eligible for enrollment in LR-MDS clinical trials, a paradigm shift with substantial implications for trial design and regulatory strategy. These data underscore that morphology-based diagnostic thresholds alone are insufficient to capture the full risk continuum in clonal myeloid disorders.

### Germline Predisposition Testing in Real-World MDS: Systematic Evaluation in 716 Patients

A real-world investigation published in *Blood Advances* systematically evaluated germline follow-up in 716 unselected MDS patients (median age 74 years) at a routine adult MDS clinic, applying harmonized follow-up of candidate variants in CEBPA, DDX41, ETV6, GATA2, and RUNX1 identified on diagnostic targeted sequencing at variant allele frequency ≥35%[^13]. Pathogenic or likely pathogenic (P/LP) germline variants were confirmed in 19 patients (2.7%), with the majority in DDX41 (73.7%), followed by RUNX1 (10.5%), GATA2 (10.5%), and ETV6 (5.3%); germline conversion rates ranged from 5.4% to 93.3% depending on the gene. Median age at diagnosis among P/LP carriers was 73 years, with marked male predominance (3.75:1). Most P/LP variant carriers were diagnosed with MDS-EB1 or MDS-EB2 (68.4%) and had normal cytogenetics (95%). A supplementary chart review in patients <50 years identified germline predisposition in additional genes not covered by routine sequencing, revising the overall prevalence to 3.4% and to 16.7% in those under 50. These data support systematic germline follow-up as part of routine MDS diagnostic workup, particularly in younger patients, and argue for expanding tested gene panels beyond standard targeted sequencing panels.

### IPSS-M Applicability Validated in Chronic Myelomonocytic Leukemia

A multicenter retrospective study of 511 CMML patients in *Blood Advances* evaluates the prognostic utility of the IPSS-M, originally developed for MDS, in the context of CMML[^14]. IPSS-M effectively stratified patients into risk groups with significantly different OS and AML-progression rates, achieving a c-index of 0.678 for OS and 0.628 for AML progression after merging overlapping intermediate risk categories into a four-tier model. Performance was maintained in both the myelodysplastic (MD-CMML) and myeloproliferative (MP-CMML) subsets, with higher discrimination in MD-CMML for OS. When benchmarked against CPSS-Mol and the recently developed iCPSS, the adapted IPSS-M showed comparable performance to iCPSS and improved discrimination versus CPSS-Mol, while simultaneously providing external validation of the iCPSS in an independent, genetically well-characterized cohort.

### NLRC4 Inflammasome Links TET2 Loss to MDS Pathogenesis

Mechanistic work published in *Blood Advances* establishes that TET2-deficient hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells develop a hyper-inflammatory state driven by NLRC4 inflammasome activation, which initially promotes self-renewal and myeloid differentiation but ultimately accelerates HSC exhaustion and MDS development in chimeric mouse models[^15]. LPS-induced inflammation further accelerated disease in Tet2-knockout mice. Crucially, genetic depletion of Nlrc4 in Tet2-deficient mice impaired MDS development and improved survival. These results nominate the NLRC4 inflammasome as a therapeutic target in TET2-mutant clonal hematopoiesis and MDS, providing a mechanistic basis for anti-inflammatory strategies in this disease context.

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

### Molecular Age Acceleration and Early Progression in DLBCL

A study published in *Leukemia* demonstrates that tumor molecular age acceleration, assessed through epigenetic clock-based methodologies, is independently associated with early disease progression (progression-of-disease within 24 months, POD24) in DLBCL[^16]. The study positions molecular age acceleration as a feature of biologically aggressive disease beyond established genomic classifiers, though full HR and confidence interval data were not available in the accessible abstract. If validated prospectively, molecular age signatures could refine upfront risk stratification and identify patients who may benefit from intensified induction approaches.

### Real-Time Comprehensive Molecular Testing in LBCL: Feasibility and Clinical Utility

A pilot study (NCT05464823) in *Blood Advances* establishes the feasibility of integrated whole exome sequencing and RNA sequencing (WES/RNA-seq) for large B-cell lymphoma (LBCL) in routine clinical practice[^17]. Among 100 enrolled patients, samples from 71 patients (48 newly diagnosed, 23 relapsed/refractory) passed pathology quality control and underwent complete WES/RNA-seq reporting. Median turnaround time was 8 days (range, 6–22 days), with 73% of reports delivered within 10 days. Applying molecular risk classification, the high-risk event-free survival within 24 months (EFS24) signature correlated with the dark zone signature (DZsig) and lymphoma microenvironment (LME) classification but not with cell-of-origin or LymphGen classification, reflecting the heterogeneous biology of DLBCL not captured by any single molecular framework. These results support the integration of real-time comprehensive genomic profiling into LBCL management and biology-driven clinical trial design.

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

### DUSP1 as a Therapeutic Target in Hairy Cell Leukemia Persistence

Single-cell RNA sequencing of primary hairy cell leukemia (HCL) cells from long-term versus short-term cladribine responders, published in *Blood Advances*, identifies a distinct HCL subcluster characterized by increased DUSP1, FOS, and JUND expression that persists or expands at relapse and is detected in all patients[^18]. Bone marrow stromal cell (BMSC) co-culture strongly induced DUSP1 in the BRAF-mutated HAIR-M experimental model and conferred protection from BRAF inhibitor-induced apoptosis, a protective effect that was reversed by DUSP1 inhibition. These data implicate bone marrow niche-dependent DUSP1 induction as a mechanism sustaining HCL minimal residual disease after cladribine and identify DUSP1 as a candidate target for eliminating persistent disease and preventing relapse.

### DA-EPOCH-R Versus GMALL High-Intensity Therapy in Adult Burkitt Lymphoma

A retrospective cohort study in the *British Journal of Haematology* compares DA-EPOCH-R with GMALL-based high-intensity chemotherapy in adult Burkitt lymphoma[^19]. In this cohort, DA-EPOCH-R showed no signal of inferior survival or relapse risk compared with GMALL-based therapy. Outcomes were primarily driven by established prognostic factors including age, advanced disease stage, and the Burkitt Lymphoma International Prognostic Index rather than by the choice of chemotherapy backbone. Specific HR and survival figures were not reported in the available abstract. These observational data provide reassurance for institutions using DA-EPOCH-R as first-line therapy in adult Burkitt lymphoma while prospective randomized data remain unavailable.

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

### SWOG S1826 Biomarker Analysis: Nivolumab-AVD Abrogates Poor Prognosis of EBV-Positive and Non-NS Hodgkin Lymphoma

A prespecified biomarker subset analysis of the phase III SWOG S1826 trial (NCT03907488) published in *Blood Advances* is among the most clinically actionable findings of this reporting period[^4]. Of 970 eligible patients with stage III–IV classic Hodgkin lymphoma (cHL), 522 had known EBV status and 664 had slides available for histologic review. Nivolumab-AVD improved 3-year PFS to 91% versus 70% in EBV-positive patients (HR 0.30; p = 0.01) and to 90% versus 84% in EBV-negative patients (HR 0.64; p = 0.09). The benefit was particularly pronounced in non-nodular sclerosis (non-NS) histologic subtypes, where N-AVD achieved 86% versus 63% 3-year PFS compared with BV-AVD (HR 0.33; p = 0.006), and in NS histology (93% vs. 86%; HR 0.53; p = 0.01). In multivariable analysis, non-NS histology was independently associated with inferior outcomes across the entire cohort (HR 2.54; p < 0.0001), but N-AVD retained a favorable PFS advantage within this high-risk subgroup. Conversely, BV-AVD was associated with significantly worse PFS in EBV-positive patients (HR 1.97; p = 0.03) and non-NS histology (HR 3.08; p < 0.0001). These data provide compelling biological rationale for N-AVD as the preferred frontline standard of care in advanced cHL, with particularly strong justification in EBV-positive disease and non-NS histologic subtypes—disease features that are not uncommon in Asian populations.

### Immune Checkpoint Inhibition in ALK-Positive ALCL: CD4+ T Cells as Key Effectors

A preclinical mechanistic study in *Blood* employing a syngeneic ALK+ anaplastic large cell lymphoma (ALCL) mouse model demonstrates that PD-L1 blockade achieves complete remissions in approximately 50% of treated animals, with anti-tumor efficacy dependent on CD4+ T cells and NK cells[^20]. Mechanistically, PD-L1 blockade reversed Treg-mediated immunosuppression and increased circulating effector CD8+ T lymphocyte frequency, prolonging survival significantly. CD4+ T cells were found to be indispensable for sustaining immunotherapy-induced anti-tumor responses, and non-responder animals exhibited exhausted CD4+ T cells with a Th22-like transcriptomic signature, identifying persistent CD4+ exhaustion as a mechanism of immune evasion. These preclinical data provide proof-of-concept for the safe and effective use of PD-1/PD-L1 blockade in ALK+ ALCL and identify CD4+ T-cell reinvigoration as the principal effector mechanism.

### Prime Editing Enables Drug-Controllable CAR T Cells for T-Cell Lymphoma Gene Therapy

A *Blood* study describes a multiplex prime-editing platform that converts immunosuppressive drugs into tools for in vivo control of T-cell therapies, with direct application in subcutaneous panniculitis-like T-cell lymphoma (SPTCL)[^21]. The platform corrected the HAVCR2 driver mutation in primary T cells from multiple SPTCL patients, and combined gene correction with drug-resistance editing enabled selective in vivo expansion of corrected cells under pharmacologic immunosuppressive pressure in humanized mouse models. The approach extends to CAR T cells: prime-edited, drug-resistant antigen-specific and CAR T cells retained full effector function despite ongoing pharmacologic immunosuppression. These findings establish multiplex prime editing as a preclinical framework for drug-controllable cellular therapies in immunocompromised settings, a particularly relevant advance for T-cell malignancies requiring concomitant immunosuppression.

## X. NK/T-cell Lymphoma (Asia Focus)

### Atezolizumab in Relapsed/Refractory Extranodal NK/T-cell Lymphoma: Phase 2 NCCH1903/ATTACK Trial

The phase 2 NCCH1903/ATTACK trial, published in *Blood Advances*, reports the first prospective evaluation of anti-PD-L1 monotherapy with atezolizumab in relapsed/refractory extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma (ENKTL)[^5]. Fourteen patients were enrolled; among the 13 with evaluable responses (median age 72 years, range 27–80; 77% ECOG PS 1), the IRC-assessed ORR was 54% (7/13), including 4 complete responses (31%). Over a median follow-up of 24.9 months, the median duration of response was not reached, a particularly notable finding given the historically limited durability of responses in R/R ENKTL. Median PFS was 2.4 months and median OS was 10.3 months, reflecting the heterogeneity of response kinetics and the inclusion of patients who did not respond. PD-L1 structural variations (SVs), detected by targeted-capture sequencing and/or FISH in 4 of 11 evaluable patients (36%), were associated with an ORR of 75% (3/4) versus 43% in patients without PD-L1 SVs (3/7). PD-L1 expression by immunohistochemistry was positive in 5/11 evaluable patients (45%), with ORR of 80% (4/5) in PD-L1-positive versus 33% (2/6) in PD-L1-negative cases. These data establish atezolizumab as a clinically meaningful option in R/R ENKTL and nominate PD-L1 SVs and expression as candidate predictive biomarkers for patient selection. Given the higher prevalence of ENKTL in Asia, including Taiwan, these data are directly relevant to regional practice.
