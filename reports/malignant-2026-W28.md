## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

### FLT3-ITD as Immune Escape Scaffold: PKCι–STAT1–CD276 Axis

Beyond its canonical role as a constitutively active kinase, FLT3-ITD has now been demonstrated to drive immunosuppression through a kinase-independent scaffolding mechanism, a finding with immediate translational relevance[^1]. Wang et al., publishing in *Blood*, integrated single-cell RNA sequencing data from two independent datasets and multiparameter flow cytometry data from 104 primary patient samples, identifying CD8+ T-cell exhaustion as a hallmark feature of the FLT3-ITD tumor immune microenvironment. Mechanistically, FLT3-ITD assembles a ternary complex with protein kinase C iota (PKCι) and STAT1, confirmed by coimmunoprecipitation and colocalization assays. PKCι-mediated STAT1 phosphorylation at serine 727 (S727) — independent of the canonical tyrosine 701 (Y701) phosphorylation site — drives transcription of the immune checkpoint ligand CD276. Multiplex immunohistochemistry of bone marrow biopsies validated co-elevation of pS727-STAT1 and CD276 in FLT3-ITD blasts, accompanied by depletion of CD8+ T cells. In functional co-culture experiments, targeting CD276 restored CD8+ T-cell cytotoxicity by 1.2–1.7-fold, proliferation by 1.4–1.7-fold, IFN-γ secretion by 1.5–1.8-fold, and reduced inhibitory checkpoint expression by 25.4–67.6%. In patient-derived xenograft models, co-treatment with quizartinib and CD276-targeting agents achieved 72.9–80.4% tumor burden reduction, substantially outperforming quizartinib monotherapy. An accompanying commentary by Zwick and Köhler highlights that these findings reframe how immune evasion should be conceived in FLT3-ITD AML, motivating early-phase trials combining FLT3 inhibition with immune checkpoint strategies targeting CD276[^7].

### SNHG29–IGF2BP1 Axis: A Lineage-Restricted Oncogenic Circuit in Acute Megakaryoblastic Leukemia

A CRISPR interference screen reported in *Leukemia* identifies the long noncoding RNA SNHG29 as a critical lineage-specific dependency in acute megakaryoblastic leukemia (AMKL), a rare and aggressive AML subtype with a developmental origin in early childhood[^8]. Winkler et al. demonstrated that SNHG29 silencing profoundly impaired leukemic proliferation and clonogenic growth in vitro and reduced leukemic burden in vivo. RNA pulldown coupled with proteomic analysis showed that SNHG29 interacts with the oncofetal RNA-binding protein IGF2BP1, which is aberrantly expressed in AMKL and known to stabilize MYC and E2F target transcripts. Pharmacologic inhibition of IGF2BP1–RNA interactions with the small molecule BTYNB induced potent and selective cytotoxicity in patient-derived AMKL models. These findings define a co-dependency between SNHG29 and IGF2BP1 as an actionable therapeutic vulnerability and represent the first lncRNA-based target characterization in this disease entity.

### NPM1c-Directed siRNA Overcomes Menin Inhibitor Resistance In Vivo

A *Leukemia* study by Hou et al. demonstrates that allele-specific small interfering RNA (siRNA) targeting mutant NPM1 (NPM1c), delivered via lipid nanoparticles (LNPs), selectively silences the mutant allele while preserving wild-type NPM1 function[^9]. In NPM1c AML cell lines and in vivo models, LNP-delivered siNPM1c significantly reduced leukemogenesis, enhanced the efficacy of the menin inhibitor revumenib, and critically overcame revumenib resistance. Mechanistically, NPM1c recruits the histone acetyltransferases KAT7 and p300 to establish a pathogenic acetylome and open chromatin state that sustains leukemogenic transcription; siNPM1c disrupts this interaction and reverses the epigenetic landscape. These data position LNP-delivered siNPM1c as a potentially clinically translatable modality, particularly given the high unmet need in revumenib-resistant NPM1c AML.

### RSK Signaling as a Selective Therapeutic Vulnerability in FLT3-Activated AML

A complementary *Leukemia* study from Traber et al. identifies p90 ribosomal S6 kinase (RSK) as a downstream effector of FLT3 signaling whose pharmacologic inhibition by the selective RSK inhibitor PMD-026 suppresses FLT3-activated AML while sparing normal hematopoiesis[^10]. This selectivity profile may prove especially valuable in combination strategies pairing RSK inhibition with direct FLT3 inhibitors, an approach warranting early-phase clinical evaluation.

### Oral Decitabine/Cedazuridine Plus Venetoclax: Long-Term Follow-Up in Older/Unfit AML

Extended follow-up from a single-center phase II study at MD Anderson, now published in *Haematologica*, provides 32-month median follow-up data for oral decitabine/cedazuridine (35 mg/100 mg days 1–5) plus venetoclax in 68 patients (median age 79 years) with newly diagnosed AML ineligible for intensive induction[^11]. The overall response rate was 75% in de novo AML and 58% in secondary AML. Among responders, MRD negativity was achieved in 58% and 56%, respectively. Median OS was 12.7 months (95% CI, 9.1–20.3) in de novo versus 7.2 months (95% CI, 3.6–29.9) in secondary AML (p = 0.61), and median relapse-free survival was 9.2 versus 11.7 months (p = 0.56). While response rates favored de novo disease, the lack of statistically significant survival differences between the two groups — in a cohort where >50% had ECOG PS ≥2 — underscores the unmet need for novel strategies specifically in secondary AML.

### CD7-Targeted CAR-T in Relapsed/Refractory AML: Phase I Data

A phase I trial (NCT04599556) published in *Leukemia* reports early clinical outcomes for autologous or donor-derived CD7-targeted CAR-T cell therapy in patients with relapsed/refractory CD7-positive AML[^5]. Fourteen patients were enrolled across two dose levels using a 3+3 design. Cytokine release syndrome occurred in 92.9% (predominantly low-grade), grade 3–4 cytopenias in 100%, and viral reactivation in 78.6%. The objective response rate was 92.3%, with 84.6% achieving MRD-negative status. Despite these striking initial response rates, seven of the responding patients relapsed with CD7-negative disease, underscoring antigen-loss escape as a major resistance mechanism. At a median follow-up of 172.5 days, five patients remained in remission. The 1-year OS and leukemia-free survival rates were 34.3% and 34.1%, respectively — modest but meaningful in a multiply relapsed/refractory population. These results support continued investigation in frontline or earlier-line settings.

### Single-Cell DNA Methylation Maps the MDS-to-AML Epigenetic Trajectory

A *Leukemia* study by Bueno-Costa et al. applies single-cell DNA methylation profiling to map epigenetic changes during MDS-to-AML transformation, identifying progressive methylation heterogeneity and clone-specific epigenetic reprogramming as drivers of leukemic evolution[^12]. These findings have implications for biomarker development and for timing therapeutic interventions in high-risk MDS.

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

### USP22 as a MEIS1 Stabilizer in KMT2A-Rearranged Acute Leukemia

A genome-wide CRISPR/Cas9 screen in a KMT2A-rearranged B-ALL cell line, published in *Blood*, identifies the deubiquitinase USP22 — a member of the SAGA multiprotein complex — as a novel regulator of MEIS1 protein stability[^13]. Bröchtel et al. demonstrate that USP22 binds cooperatively with MEIS1 at oncogenic target gene loci, protecting MEIS1 from proteasomal degradation and thereby sustaining the leukemogenic transcriptional program downstream of KMT2A fusion proteins. Genetic or chemical inhibition of USP22 led to MEIS1 polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation, with consequent downregulation of HOXA9/MEIS1 target genes and impaired leukemic growth. These findings are particularly timely given the emergence of clinical menin inhibitor resistance, offering a mechanistically distinct target for KMT2Ar leukemias.

### FATP2-Mediated Fatty Acid Oxidation Drives CAR-T Resistance in TP53-Mutant B-ALL

A *Leukemia* study by Garcia et al. identifies the fatty acid transport protein FATP2 (encoded by SLC27A2) as a leukemia-intrinsic driver of CAR-T resistance specifically in TP53-mutant B-ALL, identified through genome-wide CRISPR/Cas9 screening[^14]. FATP2-expressing TP53-mutant cells fuel fatty acid oxidation (FAO) to sustain viability against CD19-directed CAR-T cells; pharmacologic inhibition of neutral lipolysis or CPT1 reversed this resistance in patient-derived xenograft models. High SLC27A2 expression in pediatric B-ALL patients correlated with worse survival outcomes following conventional chemotherapy. These data suggest that metabolic reprogramming — rather than antigen loss — can serve as a CAR-T resistance mechanism, opening a therapeutic window through lipid metabolism inhibition.

### Lineage Switch in Acute Leukemia: Biology, Detection, and Clinical Framework

A comprehensive review in *Leukemia* by Kovach et al. addresses the biology and clinical management of lineage switch in acute leukemia, with particular relevance in the era of antigen-directed therapies[^15]. Lineage switch — most common in KMT2A-rearranged B-ALL, which may relapse as AML — is increasingly recognized as a consequence of selective antigen pressure from CAR-T cells and bispecific T-cell engagers. The review proposes a framework for immunophenotypic and molecular monitoring to detect lineage switch early, acknowledging that optimal salvage strategies remain undefined.

### Olverembatinib in BCR::ABL1 Leukemia: A Clinical Review

A *Haematologica* review by Oehler et al. provides a comprehensive appraisal of olverembatinib (HQP1351), a third-generation BCR::ABL1 inhibitor with dual active/inactive conformation binding activity[^6]. Olverembatinib is approved in China by the NMPA for T315I-mutant chronic- or accelerated-phase CML and for CP-CML resistant/intolerant to imatinib or second-generation TKIs, and holds breakthrough designation in combination with low-intensity chemotherapy for first-line Ph+ ALL. Global phase 1b data published in 2025 support international development. The review situates olverembatinib alongside asciminib and ponatinib as a third-generation option with a distinct binding profile, noting ongoing comparative trial design as a priority.

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

### ASC4FIRST Tolerability Sub-Analysis: Asciminib vs. ATP-Competitive TKIs

A post-hoc tolerability-focused analysis of the phase 3 ASC4FIRST trial, published in *Haematologica*, examines adverse event profiles within the imatinib and second-generation TKI strata separately[^16]. Within both strata, patients receiving asciminib experienced fewer difficult-to-tolerate adverse events — including gastrointestinal toxicity, rash, and pleural effusion — and had fewer adverse events leading to dose modifications and discontinuations. The median onset of dose-modifying adverse events occurred later with asciminib versus investigator-selected TKI comparators. These tolerability data complement the efficacy outcomes previously reported from ASC4FIRST and strengthen the benefit–risk profile of asciminib as a frontline option across a broad range of newly diagnosed CML-CP patients, including those with higher cardiovascular risk enrolled in the imatinib stratum.

### CML in 2026: Introduction to the Review Series

*Haematologica* launches a dedicated review series on CML in 2026, with an introductory editorial by Radich situating the field: deep molecular response has become achievable for the majority of newly diagnosed patients, yet treatment-free remission remains elusive for those with T315I or compound mutations, and the question of optimal sequencing among three generations of TKIs — with asciminib and olverembatinib as the newest entrants — defines the current clinical research agenda[^17].

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

### Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia in CLL: Prognostic Impact Attenuated by Targeted Therapy

A retrospective analysis of 1,404 CLL patients published in *Haematologica* characterizes the clinical and molecular features of CLL-associated autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AIHA) and its prognostic significance across treatment eras[^18]. AIHA occurred in 10.4% of patients, with 69.2% classified as warm-antibody AIHA (wAIHA). Patients with AIHA were enriched for male sex, advanced stage, IGHV4-34 usage, and DNMT3A mutations, while MYD88 mutations were enriched in cold-antibody AIHA. Although AIHA conferred a significantly adverse prognostic impact on CLL outcomes in the overall cohort, this effect was markedly attenuated in patients receiving targeted therapies (BTK or BCL-2 inhibitors). Notably, unmutated IGHV status — a standard adverse prognostic marker — did not predict inferior outcomes among AIHA-positive patients, suggesting a distinct biological landscape in this subset. These findings support routine AIHA screening in high-risk CLL and the prioritization of targeted therapy when AIHA is present.

### MRD as a Surrogate Endpoint in CLL Trials: A Critical Appraisal

A methodological analysis in *Leukemia* by Simon and Al-Sawaf examines the reliability of measurable residual disease (MRD) as a surrogate endpoint for progression-free survival in CLL trials[^19]. The authors identify substantial heterogeneity in the strength of the MRD–PFS correlation across treatment regimens and patient populations, cautioning against universal application of MRD endpoints for accelerated approval and arguing for continued co-primary PFS reporting in pivotal trials.

### MYD88 Variants in CLL: Distinct Biological Behaviors

A *Leukemia* study by Yan et al. provides a systematic characterization of MYD88 variant subtypes in CLL, demonstrating that different MYD88 mutations exhibit distinct patterns of B-cell receptor signaling dependency, response to BTK inhibition, and overall prognostic implications[^20]. The findings refine the biological taxonomy of CLL and may inform targeted treatment selection.

### The Unfinished Agenda in CLL: A Curative Framework

A perspective article in *Blood* by Wu, Caligaris-Cappio, Chiorazzi, Gribben, and colleagues outlines the priority research agenda for CLL cure, emphasizing three unresolved challenges: dual BTKi/BCL-2 resistance, Richter transformation, and cancer-associated immunodeficiency[^21]. The authors propose that mechanistic studies clarifying biological heterogeneity in treatment resistance — informed by the lessons of the COVID-19 pandemic's severe impact on CLL patients — are prerequisite to designing curative combination approaches.

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

### MDM4 Haploinsufficiency: A Novel TP53-Activating Germline Syndrome with Bone Marrow Failure

A landmark translational study in *Blood* reports six unrelated individuals with variable bone marrow failure (BMF) phenotypes and hypocellular MDS harboring germline heterozygous variants in MDM4 (mouse double minute 4), presenting at a median age of 10 years (range 4 weeks to 53 years)[^22]. Using CRISPR/Cas9 deletion of MDM4 in healthy donor HSPCs and patient-specific induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) models, Sharma et al. demonstrate that MDM4 haploinsufficiency leads to enhanced p53 activation — evidenced by elevated p21 expression — impaired colony-forming capacity, and reduced xenograft engraftment potential. Complementation studies identify both the p53-binding and RING-finger domains as necessary for MDM4-mediated hematopoietic regulation. Transcriptome analysis of iPSC-derived hematopoietic cells confirmed upregulation of the p53 pathway, and one patient with MDS acquired loss-of-function TP53 mutations, consistent with maladaptive somatic rescue. These findings establish MDM4 deficiency as a new TP53-activating syndrome, expanding the differential diagnosis of pediatric and adult BMF and underscoring the central role of the MDM4–p53 axis in hematopoietic homeostasis.

### MDS Bone Marrow Microarchitecture Score Outperforms Blast Enumeration

A *Leukemia* study by Nachman et al. applies whole-slide multiplex immunofluorescence imaging with single-cell phenotyping to map bone marrow microarchitecture in MDS across 36 diagnostic biopsies, 29 longitudinal treatment samples, 13 precursor states, and 21 normal controls — comprising over 5 million spatially resolved cells[^23]. The investigators derive a composite Microarchitectural Perturbation Score (MDS-MAPS) from 82 cellular and spatial features. In leave-one-patient-out cross-validation, MDS-MAPS discriminated remission from active disease more accurately than blast percentage (AUC 0.883 vs. 0.660) and distinguished low-blast MDS from clonal cytopenia of undetermined significance (CCUS) (AUC 0.815). Mixed-effects modeling confirmed that MAPS decreased during remission independently of blast burden and re-emerged at relapse. This quantitative tissue-state biomarker approach could complement molecular and blast-based assessments and potentially serve as a trial endpoint in MDS.

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms

### TP53-Variant MPN: Evolutionary Considerations for Risk Stratification

A *Leukemia* perspective by Pan examines the evolutionary biology of TP53-variant MPNs, arguing that clonal architecture — specifically whether TP53 mutation occurs as a founding versus subclonal event, and whether bi-allelic inactivation is present — should stratify risk more precisely than TP53 variant allele frequency alone[^24]. The clinical implication is that not all TP53-mutant MPN patients carry equivalent transformation risk, and that longitudinal clone tracking is necessary for informed management decisions.

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

### Harmonizing First-Line LBCL Trial Design: A Call for Biomarker-Driven Standardization

A commentary in *Blood* by Chihara and Westin argues that the proliferating landscape of first-line LBCL trials has produced a "chaos of choice" without a principled framework for patient selection or cross-trial comparison[^25]. The authors note that while the POLARIX trial established polatuzumab vedotin-based therapy (R-CHP-pola) as superior to R-CHOP, many ongoing phase 3 studies continue to use R-CHOP as the comparator — complicating interpretation and potentially diluting biologically meaningful treatment effects. Four concrete solutions are proposed: pooled patient-level analyses, standardized biomarker and MRD platforms, AI-enabled predictive modeling integrating trial and real-world data, and regulatory approaches promoting collaborative biomarker-driven trial design.

### PD-L1 and PD-L2 Define Distinct Transcriptional States in PMBCL

A *Leukemia* study by Caillot et al. uses CRISPR-Cas9-engineered isogenic PMBCL models, immune co-culture assays, and integrative transcriptomic analyses to define non-overlapping roles for PD-L1 and PD-L2 in primary mediastinal large B-cell lymphoma[^26]. PD-L1 expression aligned with interferon-responsive programs and restraint of Th1-associated immune signaling, whereas PD-L2 was associated with preservation of B-cell lineage transcriptional identity. Combined disruption of both ligands enhanced Th1 activation while altering B-cell transcriptional states. These findings provide a conceptual framework for understanding why PD-1 blockade is highly effective in PMBCL and suggest that PD-L2 co-expression may serve as a biologically meaningful stratifier in future checkpoint inhibitor trials.

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

### MAIPI: An AI Prognostic Index for Mantle Cell Lymphoma Using H&E Histology

A *Leukemia* study from Lippl et al. introduces MAIPI (MCL Artificial Intelligence Prognostic Index), a deep learning algorithm trained on hematoxylin and eosin images of diagnostic biopsies from 428 MCL patients enrolled in clinical trials[^27]. MAIPI selects regions of interest autonomously and provides prognostic information independent of the MCL International Prognostic Index (MIPI) and Ki67 proliferation index, without requiring molecular testing or expert pathologist evaluation. Validation in an independent cohort of 140 patients treated with immunochemotherapy ± ibrutinib confirmed the algorithm's performance. MAIPI represents a step toward democratizing MCL risk stratification, particularly relevant in resource-limited settings where molecular profiling infrastructure is unavailable.

### BTK Inhibition Augments CAR-T Persistence in MCL

A *Leukemia* study by Yao et al. demonstrates that both reversible and irreversible BTK inhibitors promote stem-like functional multipotency, persistence, and antitumor efficacy of CD19-directed CAR-T cells in mantle cell lymphoma models[^28]. The mechanistic basis involves BTK inhibitor–mediated suppression of CAR-T cell terminal exhaustion and preservation of a less-differentiated memory phenotype. These preclinical findings provide rationale for prospective clinical evaluation of BTK inhibitor plus CAR-T cell combination regimens in MCL.

### Chylothorax in Follicular Lymphoma: Retroperitoneal Bulk as Predictor

A *Leukemia* report by Terziev et al. identifies retroperitoneal bulk disease as a predictor of chylothorax in follicular lymphoma and demonstrates that resolution requires systemic disease control rather than local intervention alone[^29]. This finding has practical implications for management sequencing in patients with bulky retroperitoneal FL presenting with pleural effusions.

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

### Anti-CD47 Magrolimab Plus Pembrolizumab in Relapsed/Refractory Classic Hodgkin Lymphoma

A phase 1/2 study published in *Haematologica* evaluates the anti-CD47 macrophage checkpoint antibody magrolimab in combination with the anti-PD-1 antibody pembrolizumab in relapsed/refractory classic Hodgkin lymphoma (cHL)[^30]. The combination exploits complementary phagocytic ("don't eat me" signal blockade via CD47) and adaptive immune (PD-1 blockade) mechanisms. Preliminary efficacy data are reported in this cohort; detailed response rates and follow-up are not provided in the abstract, but the trial registers a proof-of-concept signal supporting further evaluation of innate plus adaptive immune combination strategies in cHL.

### Dual Asparaginase/PD-1 Resistance in ENKTL: Dismal Outcomes with a Chidamide Signal

A multicenter retrospective cohort study in *Haematologica* provides the first comprehensive analysis of clinical outcomes in 61 patients with extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma (ENKTL) exhibiting dual resistance to both asparaginase-based regimens and PD-1 inhibitors, identified from 900 ENKTL patients across 12 Chinese academic centers[^4]. The median OS from initial diagnosis was 21.9 months (95% CI, 14.7–43.1), with a median post-resistance survival of only 4.9 months (95% CI, 2.8–9.9). Rechallenge with asparaginase-based regimens yielded a median PFS of 3.2 months (95% CI, 1.4–5.0), and PD-1 inhibitor rechallenge yielded 2.7 months (95% CI, 2.1–3.3). Other novel agents including XPO1 inhibitors, PI3K inhibitors, JAK1 inhibitors, and brentuximab vedotin did not demonstrate a significant survival benefit. Chidamide-containing regimens were the sole strategy associated with a significantly longer post-resistance survival (HR 0.46; 95% CI, 0.24–0.88; p = 0.019). While the retrospective design and small numbers preclude definitive conclusions, chidamide — an HDAC inhibitor with established single-agent activity in T-cell lymphomas — represents the most promising salvage backbone for this devastating disease state and warrants prospective evaluation.

### CircZBTB46 Mediates Crizotinib Resistance in ALK+ ALCL

A *Leukemia* mechanistic study by Babin et al. identifies the circular RNA circZBTB46 as a driver of resistance to the ALK inhibitor crizotinib in ALK-positive anaplastic large-cell lymphoma[^31]. CircZBTB46 expression is restricted to ALK+ ALCL cells and is driven by the NPM1::ALK/STAT3 oncogenic axis. Functionally, circZBTB46 acts as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA), sponging miR-25-3p and thereby de-repressing PIP5K1C — a downstream effector sustaining ALK inhibitor resistance. Silencing circZBTB46 restored crizotinib sensitivity in resistant ALCL cells both in vitro and in vivo. This represents the first identification of a circRNA-based resistance mechanism in a mature T-cell lymphoma.

## X. NK/T-cell Lymphoma (Asia Focus)

The dual asparaginase/PD-1 resistance analysis described in Section IX is of particular importance to Asian centers, where ENKTL comprises a substantially higher proportion of lymphoma diagnoses than in Western populations[^4]. For centers at NCKUH and other academic institutions in Taiwan, this study provides the most granular outcomes data to date for this refractory subset and identifies chidamide — available in Taiwan — as the current best salvage strategy pending prospective data. Taiwan's NHI coverage for chidamide in ENKTL should be reviewed in the context of these findings.

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

### Pavurutamab (AMG 701): Phase 1/1b Data for BCMA-Targeting Bispecific T-Cell Engager

A fully published phase 1/1b study of pavurutamab in *Blood* provides the most comprehensive efficacy and safety dataset yet for this BCMA-directed bispecific T-cell engager in triple-class relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma[^3]. Among 172 patients receiving at least one dose, the ORR was 46.5%. Among 73 patients treated at the recommended phase 2 dose (RP2D; 18,000 μg), the ORR was 65.8%, with ≥VGPR achieved in 60.3%. At a median follow-up of 17.2 months, the median duration of response was 36.6 months (95% CI, 22.3–not estimable) — a notably durable response in this heavily pretreated population. Median PFS for all patients was 5.5 months (95% CI, 2.8–10.1) and 16.8 months (95% CI, 5.0–not estimable) at the RP2D. The most frequent treatment-emergent adverse events included CRS (74.4%), anemia (61.0%), neutropenia (47.1%), and hypophosphatemia (45.3%); grade ≥3 infections occurred in 34.9%. High soluble BCMA levels correlated with lower drug exposure, a pharmacokinetic variable warranting consideration in patient selection. An accompanying perspective by van de Donk and Zweegman maps pavurutamab within the rapidly evolving BCMA landscape, now including teclistamab, elranatamab, and belantamab mafodotin[^32].

### Concurrent BCMA + GPRC5D CAR-T Co-Infusion: Phase I Safety and Preliminary Efficacy
