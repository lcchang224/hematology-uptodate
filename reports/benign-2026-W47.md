## Summary

This week's non-malignant output is smaller than W46. It is led by one Phase 3 complement-inhibitor dataset and three large observational studies, each with direct implications for long-term patient management. The indole-3-lactic acid ITP study, which also appeared in this cycle's CrossRef feed, was fully reported in W46 and is not repeated.

1. **Ravulizumab in pediatric HSCT-TMA.** The global, single-arm, open-label Phase 3 ALXN1210-TMA-314 trial (NCT04557735) enrolled 41 children with HSCT-associated thrombotic microangiopathy (TMA) that persisted for at least 72 hours despite initial management. Ravulizumab produced a complete TMA response in 17.1% and a partial response in 53.7%. Estimated overall survival (OS) was 92.6% at Day 100, 87.2% at Week 26 and 73.4% at Week 52. No meningococcal infections were reported[^1].

2. **Renal decline in adult β-thalassemia.** The Italian Webthal network followed 994 adults with β-thalassemia for up to 16 years. eGFR declined by −1.13 mL/min/1.73 m²/year (95% CI −1.20 to −1.07), with a steeper slope in transfusion-dependent (TDT) than non-transfusion-dependent (NTDT) disease. Baseline hyperfiltration (22%) did not predict faster subsequent decline[^2].

3. **BCOR/BCORL1 mutations in bone marrow failure (BMF).** In 11,340 sequenced patients (418 *BCOR/L1*-mutated), these mutations behaved as ancestral, low-VAF, often contracting clones in BMF. In BMF they were associated with PNH clones and a trend towards favourable immunosuppressive therapy (IST) response. In myeloid neoplasms (MN), by contrast, they were secondary events linked to inferior survival[^3].

4. **Antepartum VTE in sickle cell disease (SCD).** In a two-centre cohort of 289 pregnancies, 6% were complicated by VTE. Prior VTE (33% vs 4%, p < 0.001), pre-conception acute chest syndrome (80% vs 37%, p = 0.001) and prenatal acute chest syndrome (47% vs 10%, p < 0.001) were the strongest associations. These factors define a phenotype in which thromboprophylaxis trials are warranted[^4].

5. **Coagulation diagnostics and inherited thrombocytopenia.** A *BJH* educational piece revisits interpretation of the thrombin time and reptilase time beyond simple heparin contamination[^5]. A correspondence exchange continues debate on the cytoskeletal mechanism of platelet dysfunction in *ETV6*-related thrombocytopenia[^6][^7].

## I. Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP)

_No new signal this week._ The only primary ITP paper in this cycle's feed was the indole-3-lactic acid/Th17–Treg study, already reported in full in W46. No new findings or numbers are available, so it is not restated.

Of adjacent relevance to the ITP differential diagnosis, *BJH* published a comment[^6] and an author response[^7] on the previously reported mechanistic work in *ETV6*-related thrombocytopenia (ETV6-RT). That work attributed impaired platelet shape change and defective thrombus formation to altered cytoskeletal integrity. Neither item carries an abstract, so the substance of the disagreement cannot be summarised here.

The clinical relevance for ITP clinics remains unchanged. ETV6-RT is an autosomal dominant inherited thrombocytopenia that is frequently misdiagnosed as chronic ITP and carries a predisposition to haematological malignancy. In patients labelled "refractory ITP" who have a family history of thrombocytopenia or leukaemia, germline panel testing should precede escalation to further immunosuppression.

## II. TTP / HUS / Thrombotic Microangiopathy

HSCT-associated TMA has lacked any prospectively validated, approved therapy. The ALXN1210-TMA-314 trial is the first global Phase 3 dataset of a C5 inhibitor in children with this complication[^1].

**Design.** The study was open-label and single-arm. It enrolled patients aged 28 days to under 18 years with HSCT-TMA diagnosed within 12 months of transplant that persisted for at least 72 hours despite initial management. Patients received weight-based ravulizumab plus best supportive care for 26 weeks, followed by 26 weeks of off-treatment follow-up. The primary endpoint was complete TMA response during treatment, a composite requiring improvement in proteinuria, platelet count, and LDH/schistocytes.

**Results.** Of 41 participants (mean age 7.6 years; 51.2% female), 28 (68.3%) completed treatment and 24 (58.5%) completed the study. Seven participants (17.1%) achieved complete TMA response and 22 (53.7%) achieved partial response. Overall, 29 (70.7%) improved in at least one response component. Investigators judged serious treatment-emergent adverse events to be ravulizumab-related in 3 participants (7.3%). No new safety signals and no meningococcal infections were reported[^1].

| Endpoint (ALXN1210-TMA-314, n = 41) | Result |
|---|---|
| Complete TMA response (primary) | 7 (17.1%) |
| Partial TMA response | 22 (53.7%) |
| Improvement in ≥1 TMA component | 29 (70.7%) |
| Estimated OS, Day 100 | 92.6% |
| Estimated OS, Week 26 | 87.2% |
| Estimated OS, Week 52 | 73.4% |
| Completed 26-week treatment | 28 (68.3%) |
| Ravulizumab-related serious TEAEs | 3 (7.3%) |
| Meningococcal infections | 0 |

**Interpretation.** Three caveats apply.

- The complete response rate of 17.1% is modest. The composite endpoint is stringent, however. Proteinuria normalisation in particular is confounded post-transplant by calcineurin-inhibitor nephrotoxicity, sepsis and GVHD. The 70.7% single-component improvement rate is probably the more clinically intuitive figure.
- There is no concurrent comparator. "Favourable survival" therefore rests on comparison with historical series, which mostly describe untreated or variably treated high-risk HSCT-TMA. Selection effects are likely: eligibility required persistence beyond 72 hours, not refractory multiorgan failure.
- The OS curve continues to fall after treatment stops (87.2% at Week 26 vs 73.4% at Week 52). This raises the question of whether a fixed 26-week course is optimal or whether some patients need longer complement blockade. The abstract does not separate TMA-attributable from relapse- or infection-attributable deaths.

**Practical points.** Meningococcal protection in recently transplanted children cannot rely on vaccination alone, because vaccine responses are blunted. Antibiotic prophylaxis for the full duration of C5 blockade, plus a period after the last dose, remains essential. The absence of meningococcal events in 41 patients should not be read as evidence that prophylaxis can be relaxed.

For our transplant colleagues, these data support earlier, protocolised C5 inhibition in high-risk HSCT-TMA. Diagnosis should rely on harmonised criteria: elevated LDH, proteinuria, hypertension, schistocytes, rising transfusion requirement and elevated sC5b-9 where available.

No new data were published this week for immune TTP (caplacizumab, recombinant ADAMTS13) or for complement-mediated aHUS outside the transplant setting.

## III. Aplastic Anemia

The largest characterisation to date of *BCOR* and *BCORL1* mutations across myeloid neoplasms and bone marrow failure was published in *AJH*. It was not covered in W46[^3].

**Cohort and molecular differences.** Among 11,340 sequenced patients, 418 carried *BCOR/L1* mutations. Compared with MN, *BCOR/L1*-mutated BMF patients were younger and more often had a normal karyotype. Their mutations were more frequently missense and had significantly lower variant allele frequencies.

**Clonal behaviour.** Single-cell DNA sequencing and VAF-based clonal reconstruction showed that *BCOR/L1* mutations were usually secondary events in MN but primary (ancestral) events in BMF. On longitudinal sequencing, *BCOR/L1*-mutant clones frequently contracted or disappeared over time, even in patients who progressed. Progression instead tracked with rising overall mutational burden, particularly *RUNX1*. This argues for limited intrinsic clonal fitness of *BCOR/L1* mutations themselves[^3].

**Prognosis and mechanism.** In MN, *BCOR/L1* mutations were associated with older age, more co-mutations and inferior survival. In BMF, they had no prognostic impact and tended to correlate with a favourable IST response. RNA sequencing showed reduced *BCOR/L1* expression in BMF, and *BCOR* knockout reduced HLA class I expression. The authors propose an immune-escape mechanism analogous to 6p copy-neutral LOH and *PIGA*-mutant clones[^3].

**Clinical implication.** This confirms and extends the long-held view, carried over from earlier aplastic anemia cohorts, that *BCOR/BCORL1* (together with *PIGA*) are "good-risk" somatic lesions in acquired aplastic anemia. They should not be equated with *ASXL1*, *DNMT3A*-high-VAF, *RUNX1* or monosomy 7 clones.

For NCKUH practice, a low-VAF *BCOR* or *BCORL1* mutation on an NGS panel in a newly diagnosed severe aplastic anemia patient should not by itself redirect management away from IST (hATG/CsA ± eltrombopag) towards a myeloid-neoplasm pathway. Serial NGS should focus on emergence of *RUNX1* and other high-risk lesions rather than on *BCOR* VAF kinetics.

## IV. PNH — Paroxysmal Nocturnal Hemoglobinuria

No new primary PNH trial data were published this week.

The *BCOR/L1* study adds a mechanistic point of PNH relevance. In BMF, *BCOR/L1* mutations were associated with a higher prevalence of PNH clones[^3]. This is consistent with a shared immune-escape selection pressure in which *PIGA*-mutant (GPI-anchor-deficient) and *BCOR*-mutant (HLA class I-reduced) haematopoietic stem cells are both spared from autoimmune T-cell attack. Concurrent flow-cytometric PNH clone screening (FLAER-based) and myeloid NGS at aplastic anemia diagnosis therefore remain complementary rather than redundant.

## VII. Thalassemia (alpha & beta; Taiwan-relevant)

A large *AJH* cohort from the Italian Webthal network characterises long-term renal trajectories in adult β-thalassemia, an increasingly important topic as survival extends into the sixth and seventh decades[^2].

**Cohort and overall decline.** The study included 994 adults (806 TDT, 188 NTDT) followed for up to 16 years, with eGFR estimated by the race-free CKD-EPI 2021 equation and analysed by mixed-effects modelling. Baseline glomerular hyperfiltration was present in 22% and was not associated with significantly faster subsequent decline. Overall eGFR fell by −1.13 mL/min/1.73 m²/year (95% CI −1.20 to −1.07), with a steeper trajectory in TDT than NTDT[^2].

**Haemoglobin and phenotype.** In a subset of 877 patients contributing 5,529 observations, models using one-year-lagged, time-varying haemoglobin and baseline eGFR showed that haemoglobin–eGFR associations differed by transfusion phenotype. In "phenoconverted" patients (those whose transfusion phenotype changed during follow-up), higher lagged haemoglobin was associated with a more negative age-related eGFR slope. This held in sensitivity analyses adjusting for baseline eGFR. Adjusting for haemoglobin did not eliminate phenotype-related slope differences.

**Chelation.** In the long-term chelation monotherapy subgroup, chelation regimen was not associated with slope differences[^2].

**Caveats.** The authors acknowledge that observational data cannot distinguish attenuation of hyperfiltration from genuine progressive renal injury. Creatinine-based equations are also problematic in thalassemia, where low muscle mass and hyperfiltration can mask early loss of nephron mass. The reassuring chelation result is limited to patients who stayed on a single agent long term. It therefore does not negate the known dose-related tubular and glomerular effects of deferasirox in individual patients.

**Application at NCKUH.** For our adult TDT clinic, the decline is roughly 1.1 mL/min/1.73 m² per year, which approaches the rate of physiological ageing in the general population but begins from a hyperfiltering baseline. Annual eGFR is insufficient on its own. Trend analysis with urine albumin- and protein-to-creatinine ratios, and periodic cystatin C where available, is more informative, particularly in patients on deferasirox.

## XI. Thrombosis & Coagulation (VTE, APS, inherited thrombophilia)

**Antepartum VTE in sickle cell disease.** Most data on pregnancy-related thrombosis in SCD come from administrative datasets with limited clinical granularity. A two-centre US retrospective cohort in *BJH* addresses this gap with chart-level data on 289 singleton pregnancies in 197 women with SCD delivered between 1990 and 2021[^4]. VTE occurred in 6% of pregnancies.

The investigators compared the first VTE-complicated pregnancy (15 cases) with the first pregnancy of women without pregnancy-related VTE (182 controls). The associated pre-conception factors were:

- prior VTE (33% vs 4%, p < 0.001);
- acute chest syndrome (80% vs 37%, p = 0.001);
- acute care for vaso-occlusive crisis in the preceding year (80% vs 43%, p = 0.006).

The associated prenatal factors were acute chest syndrome (47% vs 10%, p < 0.001) and vaso-occlusive crisis (93% vs 65%, p = 0.026). VTE was also associated with earlier delivery, at a median of 37.0 (IQR 34.0–38.1) versus 38.3 (36.9–39.3) weeks (p = 0.004)[^4].

| Risk factor | VTE cases (n = 15) | Controls (n = 182) | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prior VTE (pre-conception) | 33% (5/15) | 4% (7/182) | < 0.001 |
| Acute chest syndrome (pre-conception) | 80% (12/15) | 37% (68/182) | 0.001 |
| Acute VOC care, year before pregnancy | 80% (12/15) | 43% (70/182) | 0.006 |
| Prenatal acute chest syndrome | 47% (7/15) | 10% (19/182) | < 0.001 |
| Prenatal VOC | 93% (14/15) | 65% (119/182) | 0.026 |

With only 15 events, multivariable adjustment was not feasible, and several factors (ACS, VOC) are collinear markers of disease severity. The clinical message is nonetheless coherent: a "high-acuity" SCD phenotype, especially with prior VTE or recent ACS, identifies pregnancies in which antenatal LMWH prophylaxis deserves explicit consideration. This is a population that current obstetric VTE risk scores do not specifically weight. SCD is uncommon in Taiwan, but such patients do present to tertiary centres, and the principle extends to other haemolytic, hypercoagulable states in pregnancy.

**Thrombin time and reptilase time.** A *BJH* educational article titled "Interpreting thrombin and reptilase times: Beyond heparin contamination" was published without an abstract[^5]. The underlying framework is standard. The thrombin time is prolonged by:

- unfractionated heparin;
- direct thrombin inhibitors, notably dabigatran, to which it is exquisitely sensitive;
- hypofibrinogenemia and dysfibrinogenemia;
- high concentrations of fibrin(ogen) degradation products;
- paraproteins interfering with fibrin polymerisation.

The reptilase (batroxobin) time is insensitive to heparin and dabigatran. A prolonged thrombin time with a normal reptilase time therefore points to heparin or a thrombin inhibitor. Prolongation of both points to a fibrinogen quantity or quality defect, or to fibrin polymerisation interference. This pairing remains underused in our laboratory workup of isolated prolonged APTT or thrombin time, particularly when sampling from central lines and in suspected congenital dysfibrinogenemia.

## XIII. Taiwan Clinical Context

**Thalassemia.** Thalassemia remains the dominant inherited red-cell disorder in Taiwan, with carrier rates of approximately 4–5% for α-thalassemia and 1–2% for β-thalassemia. Southern Taiwan, including the NCKUH catchment, follows a long-term adult TDT cohort that is ageing on chronic chelation.

The Webthal renal trajectory data[^2] support a structured approach for these patients:

- annual eGFR trend review;
- urine albumin- and protein-to-creatinine ratio monitoring;
- deferasirox dose re-evaluation when eGFR declines faster than about 1 mL/min/1.73 m²/year or when proteinuria emerges, rather than reacting to a single creatinine value.

NHI reimbursement criteria for chelators require periodic renal function documentation, and these data provide a reference rate of decline against which to judge individual patients.

**G6PD deficiency.** G6PD deficiency affects roughly 2–3% of the Taiwanese population, with newborn screening in place nationally. There was no new G6PD signal this week.

**Complement inhibitors and HSCT-TMA.** Eculizumab is reimbursed under NHI for PNH and aHUS with prior-authorisation criteria. Ravulizumab is registered in Taiwan for complement-mediated indications, but HSCT-TMA is not a labelled or reimbursed indication for either agent. The ALXN1210-TMA-314 data[^1] are an important evidence base for case-by-case applications and for discussions with transplant teams. They are not yet a basis for routine use. Colleagues should verify the current NHI reimbursement status before planning treatment.

**Other NHI-covered agents.** Emicizumab and eltrombopag remain NHI-covered under their respective criteria. There were no coverage changes this week.

**Strongyloides.** A Hong Kong *BJH* image report highlights opportunistic *Strongyloides* hyperinfection[^8]. No abstract text was available, but the topic is regionally pertinent. *Strongyloides stercoralis* remains endemic in parts of Southeast Asia and is encountered in Taiwan, particularly among older rural residents and migrant workers. Benign haematology patients commonly receive high-dose corticosteroids (ITP, AIHA) or intensive immunosuppression (ATG for aplastic anemia). Serological screening, or empirical ivermectin in epidemiologically at-risk patients before prolonged steroids, is a low-cost safeguard worth reinforcing in our protocols.

## XIV. Key Takeaways

**HSCT-TMA.** Ravulizumab is now the first C5 inhibitor with global Phase 3 data in pediatric HSCT-TMA. The complete response rate was modest at 17.1%, but 70.7% of patients improved in at least one component and estimated OS was 73.4% at Week 52[^1]. The single-arm design and the post-treatment survival decline mean that optimal duration remains open.

**Adult β-thalassemia.** eGFR declines by about 1.1 mL/min/1.73 m²/year, faster in TDT. Hyperfiltration at baseline does not predict accelerated loss, and chelation regimen did not modify slope in long-term monotherapy patients[^2]. Proteinuria-inclusive renal surveillance is recommended.

**Aplastic anemia NGS.** *BCOR/BCORL1* mutations in aplastic anemia are ancestral, low-VAF, often transient clones that associate with PNH clones and favourable IST response. They should not trigger a myeloid-neoplasm management pathway on their own; *RUNX1* acquisition and rising mutational burden are the progression signals to watch[^3].

**SCD pregnancy.** Prior VTE and acute chest syndrome identify SCD pregnancies at highest antepartum VTE risk (6% overall)[^4]. Prospective thromboprophylaxis studies are needed.

**Coagulation workup.** The paired thrombin and reptilase times remain a simple, underused tool for separating heparin or thrombin-inhibitor effects from fibrinogen disorders[^5].

**Inherited thrombocytopenia.** "Refractory ITP" with a family history warrants germline testing, including *ETV6*, before further immunosuppression[^6][^7].
