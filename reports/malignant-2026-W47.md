## Summary

This week brings several practice-relevant readouts. *Blood* published mature EPCORE NHL-2 arm 1 data (epcoritamab plus R-CHOP in high-IPI LBCL) and the randomized OPTI-AML trial, which tested 14-day versus 28-day venetoclax. The 8-year FORTE update in transplant-eligible myeloma also appeared in *Blood*. *JCO* reported a positive randomized phase III trial of sitagliptin for acute GVHD prevention in ATG-based alternative-donor transplantation, and an EBMT GoCART analysis defined outcomes of allogeneic HSCT after CD19 CAR-T in B-ALL.

**(1)** In arm 1 of the phase 1b/2 EPCORE NHL-2 trial, 47 patients with newly diagnosed LBCL and IPI 3–5 received fixed-duration epcoritamab plus R-CHOP. ORR was 98% (95% CI 89–100) and CR rate 85% (95% CI 72–94). At a median follow-up of 44.2 months, estimated 2-year PFS was 80% (95% CI 65–89) and 2-year OS 87% (95% CI 73–94)[^1].

**(2)** The randomized phase 2 OPTI-AML trial (n=169, age ≥60) found that 14-day venetoclax with azacitidine did **not** meet non-inferiority versus the approved 28-day schedule. CR within 2 cycles was 43% versus 49.4% (difference 6.4%, 90% CI −6.1% to 19.0%). The deficit was concentrated in NPM1/IDH2-mutated patients (CR 33.3% vs 60.9%)[^2].

**(3)** Long-term FORTE follow-up (median 99 months from first randomization) confirmed the advantage of KRd-ASCT. Median PFS was 98 months, versus 70 months with KRd12 (HR 0.71, p=0.027) and 67 months with KCd-ASCT (HR 0.66, p=0.006). An OS benefit versus KCd-ASCT has now emerged (8-year OS 76% vs 68%; HR 0.65, p=0.04)[^3].

**(4)** In a randomized phase III trial (n=190), sitagliptin was added to ATG-based GVHD prophylaxis after myeloablative haploidentical or unrelated-donor HSCT. Day +100 grade 2–4 aGVHD fell from 31.6% to 14.7% (sHR 0.41, p=0.005), with no difference in relapse, NRM, or OS[^4].

**(5)** An EBMT GoCART coalition analysis included 112 patients with B-ALL who underwent HSCT after CD19 CAR-T. Two-year LFS was 54% overall. LFS was markedly worse with rescue HSCT for frank relapse (41%) than with pre-emptive (74%) or consolidative (78%) HSCT (p=0.002), and haploidentical donors carried higher risk (HR 2.21, 95% CI 1.15–4.25)[^5].

## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

The most consequential AML publication this week is OPTI-AML, the first prospective randomized test of abbreviated venetoclax exposure during induction with azacitidine[^2]. Patients aged ≥60 with newly diagnosed AML were enrolled without genomic selection. They received azacitidine 75 mg/m² for 7 days plus venetoclax for either 28 days (AV28, n=83) or 14 days (AV14, n=86) during cycles 1–2.

The primary endpoint was CR within 2 cycles. CR was 49.4% with AV28 versus 43% with AV14 (difference 6.4%; 90% CI −6.1% to 19.0%), which failed the prespecified non-inferiority margin. Composite CR showed a larger numerical gap (80.7% vs 68.6%). MRD negativity among responders was virtually identical (77.6% vs 76.5%).

The subgroup signal is clinically important. In NPM1- or IDH2-mutated AML, which are the most venetoclax-sensitive genotypes, CR was 60.9% with AV28 versus 33.3% with AV14. Other subgroups had equivalent CR rates (~45%). AV28 caused more treatment interruptions. However, count recovery after cycle 2, grade ≥3 adverse events, and early mortality did not differ between arms, which undercuts the main rationale for routine abbreviation.

The accompanying editorial argues that, although the CR difference was not statistically significant, the trial cannot license a 14-day default during initial therapy[^6]. Shortening may remain appropriate as an individualized, post-remission toxicity-management manoeuvre.

On the translational side, a mutation-agnostic NRAS antisense oligonucleotide was tested in gilteritinib-resistant FLT3-mutated AML. It resensitized cell lines, cell line-derived xenografts, and primary samples that carried multiple distinct NRAS mutations, and it outperformed downstream MEK inhibition[^7]. Efficacy did not depend on NRAS-mutant VAF, implicating wild-type NRAS in resistance. Multi-omic profiling (transcriptomic, proteomic, phosphoproteomic) showed that NRAS knockdown reversed the monocytic cell state that drives resistance. NRAS-mutant venetoclax-resistant primary cells were also resensitized to venetoclax.

This connects directly with last week's MEK/HDAC (trametinib–quisinostat) work in NRAS-driven AML, and with the clinical observation that monocytic differentiation confers resistance to venetoclax and IDH inhibitors. An editorial frames NRAS depletion as a "cell-state reset" strategy[^8]. Separately, a *Haematologica* letter describes teclistamab use in patients with multiple myeloma and coexisting MDS or AML; no quantitative outcomes were available in the abstract[^9].

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

An AIEOP-BFM analysis of 159 children with PAX5-rearranged (PAX5-r) B-ALL treated between 2001 and 2024 characterizes this newly recognized subtype[^10]. Within the AIEOP-BFM ALL 2017 cohort, PAX5-r patients (n=96) were compared with non-PAX5-r patients (n=1948). PAX5-r patients had more hyperleukocytosis (22.9% vs 8%, p<0.001) and enrichment of IKZF1plus (14.7% vs 7.8%, p=0.0015). MRD-based response and risk assignment did not differ between the groups.

Overall 4-year EFS was 72.6% ± 5.7% and OS 95.4% ± 2.3%. The prognostic penalty was entirely MRD-dependent:

- **Standard-risk:** 4-year EFS 100%.
- **Medium-risk:** 63.4% ± 9.7%.
- **High-risk:** 63.3% ± 10%.

In other words, PAX5-r is unfavorable only when end-of-induction MRD is positive. Whole-transcriptome sequencing showed high FLT3 expression. Patient-derived xenograft drug screens identified FLT3-inhibitor sensitivity, and gilteritinib was synergistic with dexamethasone ex vivo. An editorial highlights this as a rationale for FLT3-directed trials in MRD-positive PAX5-r ALL[^11].

The GoCART/EBMT post-CAR-T transplant analysis is detailed in Section XII[^5]. Its key ALL-relevant message is that children had superior post-HSCT LFS compared with adults (63% vs 38%). In addition, a planned pre-emptive or consolidative HSCT strategy substantially outperformed rescue HSCT after overt relapse.

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

No new randomized CLL data appeared this week. TRANSCEND CLL 004 was covered in full last week. The accompanying *Blood* editorial on BTK-inhibitor "priming" of CAR-T cells adds commentary but no new outcome numbers[^16].

Translationally, nurse-like cells (NLCs) in the CLL microenvironment express high folate receptor beta (FOLR2), which correlates with leukemic-cell survival in vitro[^12]. CLL cells acquire functional FOLR2 from NLCs through trogocytosis, which increases folate uptake. Under CD40L/IL-15 microenvironmental stimulation, FOLR2⁺ CLL cells made up the predominant actively cycling population. Contact-independent transfer, possibly vesicle-mediated, was also observed. Multiplex immunofluorescence of lymph nodes showed FOLR2⁺ NLCs enriched in more proliferative cases, nominating FOLR2 as a marker of protective NLCs and a potential target.

Three brief *Haematologica* reports without extractable outcome statistics merit tracking:

- A flow-cytometric assay for rapid identification of the high-risk IGLV3-21^R110 subgroup[^13], which could make this stereotype-independent risk marker accessible without sequencing.
- A time-varying analysis linking GLP-1 receptor agonist exposure to lower secondary malignancy risk during watch-and-wait[^14]. This is hypothesis-generating and observational, and residual confounding is likely.
- A case of paraneoplastic spongiotic dermatitis heralding progression to treatment-requiring CLL[^15].

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

The International Consortium for MDS (icMDS) published a systematic analysis of MDS therapeutic trial manuscripts published from 2015 to 2024, together with a modified-Delphi position statement[^17]. Reporting was highly inconsistent:

- Baseline ANC was reported in only 16% of lower-risk and 23% of higher-risk MDS trials.
- Baseline marrow blast percentage was reported in only 55% of higher-risk MDS manuscripts.
- Six distinct definitions of RBC transfusion independence were used in lower-risk trials.
- Seven different ORR definitions were used in higher-risk trials.

The consensus recommendations are intended as reporting templates. Practically, they matter when comparing response rates across luspatercept, imetelstat, and HMA-combination trials, where definitional drift can easily exceed between-arm differences.

A prospective cohort of 409 patients with CCUS and 241 with lower-risk MDS used harmonized diagnostic and progression criteria, centralized pathology, and uniform genomic profiling[^18]. Patients with high-risk CCUS by either the clonal hematopoiesis risk score (CHRS) or the clonal cytopenia risk score (CCRS) had clinical features and event rates comparable to LR-MDS. Low- and intermediate-risk CCUS had markedly better outcomes. The authors argue that high-risk CCUS should be eligible for LR-MDS trials and that risk scores should supplement morphologic thresholds; an editorial endorses this convergence model[^19].

In humanized sickle cell disease mice, chronic erythropoietic regenerative stress produced premature myeloid-biased "aging-like" hematopoiesis and higher clonal hematopoiesis burden. A high proportion of mice developed MDS-like dysplasia, and serial HSCT further expanded clones carrying oncogenic drivers[^20]. This offers a mechanistic framework for the excess therapy-related MDS/AML seen after gene therapy or autologous HSCT in SCD. A *Blood* image describes paravertebral extramedullary CMML mimicking myeloid sarcoma[^21].

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

A preclinical study is the most conceptually important MPN paper of the week. Investigators developed hAB2, a high-affinity humanized antibody specific to the mutant-CALR C-terminal neoepitope, and used it as the binding domain for CAR-T cells[^22]. Functional murine and human MPN HSCs reliably displayed surface CALRmut in vitro and in vivo.

In a newly developed MPN patient-derived xenograft model, hAB2 CAR-T cells selectively eliminated CALRmut HSCs. They abolished all detectable MPN-propagating activity in serial transplantation while sparing coexisting normal human and host hematopoiesis. This is the clearest evidence to date that a transplant-free, clone-eradicating immunotherapy is biologically feasible in CALR-mutant ET/MF. Note that no clinical data exist yet.

For sapablursen (IMPRSSION), the cohort A phlebotomy-rate reduction and median phlebotomy counts were reported in W46 and are not repeated. The full *Blood* publication adds three new elements[^23]:

- **Cohort B (40 mg) phlebotomy rate:** reduced by −0.10 (95% CI −0.14 to −0.06; n=17; p=0.0001).
- **Cohort B median phlebotomies:** fell from 5.0 to 1.5 over the 20-week window.
- **MPN-SAF total symptom score at week 37:** improved by −6.21 points in cohort A (95% CI −10.56 to −1.85; p=0.0052). Cohort B showed a non-significant −2.71 (95% CI −8.33 to 2.91; p=0.34).

The symptom benefit therefore appears dose-dependent, whereas phlebotomy control was similar across doses. An editorial places sapablursen and rusfertide within a "new Iron Age" of hepcidin-axis therapy in PV[^24].

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

EPCORE NHL-2 arm 1 now provides mature data for epcoritamab plus 6 cycles of R-CHOP, with epcoritamab continued to 1 year total. The trial enrolled 47 patients with newly diagnosed CD20⁺ LBCL and IPI 3–5: median age 64, 89% stage IV, 53% bulky (≥7 cm)[^1].

Efficacy was strong. ORR was 98% (95% CI 89–100) and CR 85% (95% CI 72–94). At a median follow-up of 44.2 months, median DOCR, PFS, and OS were not reached. Estimated 2-year PFS was 80% (95% CI 65–89) and OS 87% (95% CI 73–94). All MRD-evaluable patients achieved MRD negativity.

Toxicity was dominated by:

- **Neutropenia:** 74%.
- **Anemia:** 72%.
- **CRS:** 60%, predominantly grade 1–2, with all events resolved.

For context, 2-year PFS with R-CHOP in IPI 3–5 historically sits around 60–65%, but cross-trial comparison in a 47-patient single-arm cohort is hazardous. The ongoing phase 3 evaluation is the real test. The companion editorial asks whether epcoritamab is "the X in R-CHOP+X", noting that previous X candidates failed in randomized settings despite promising phase 2 data[^25].

Molecular classification advanced with LymphGen-Sig (LGsig), published in *JCO*. LGsig is a 294-gene nearest-shrunken-centroid classifier trained on paired genomic and transcriptomic data (NCI/BCCA, n=764) to assign every DLBCL to MCD, BN2, EZB, or ST2 signatures[^26]. LG-unclassified cases reassigned by LGsig mirrored the biology and outcomes of their LG counterparts. A53 (aneuploid/TP53) cases were redistributed into more therapeutically meaningful clusters.

When applied to 678 POLARIX archival samples, LGsig identified subtypes with survival benefit from pola-R-CHP over R-CHOP in both LG-classified and LG-unclassified tumors. The abstract does not report subtype-specific HRs. In a complementary approach, the transfer-learning BRIDGE-Cox model borrows population-level SEER information to strengthen small genomic cohorts. It achieved a C-index of 0.70 versus 0.66 for IPI-based and 0.63 for R-IPI-based Cox models, with gains maintained within genomic subgroups and confirmed on external validation[^27].

In the second-line axi-cel setting, the French DESCAR-T registry analyzed 566 patients with early-relapsing or refractory LBCL. Of these, 469 (83%) received chemotherapy-based holding/bridging therapy (H/BT) and 97 (17%) received chemo-free H/BT or none[^28]. After propensity matching, H/BT responders had better OS (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.51–0.96, p=0.029). The EFS advantage lost significance (HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.70–1.07, p=0.17). A composite score of H/BT response, IPI, LDH, and CRP at infusion separated two EFS risk groups.

The US CUBIC consortium compared CD3×CD20 bispecifics (epcoritamab or glofitamab) in relapsed/refractory transformed indolent NHL (n=74) versus de novo DLBCL (n=166)[^29]. Efficacy was comparable:

- **ORR/CR:** 50%/31% in tiNHL versus 51%/34% in de novo DLBCL.
- **12-month PFS:** 25% (95% CI 15–39) versus 34% (95% CI 27–43); HR 1.15 (95% CI 0.83–1.61).
- **12-month OS:** 38% versus 47%; HR 1.08 (95% CI 0.73–1.60).

Toxicity was not different, which supports bispecific use in tiNHL despite its exclusion from many pivotal trials.

Finally, an Italian Group of Haematopathology position paper reaffirms surgical excision biopsy as the preferred diagnostic sample for lymphoma. It reserves core needle biopsy for high-surgical-risk or inaccessible lesions and recommends re-biopsy when core results are inconclusive or discordant[^30].

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

A population-based Israeli cohort compared 1007 young-adult HL survivors (age 18–50, diagnosed 1995–2021) with 20,140 matched controls over a median 12 years of follow-up[^31]. Mental and functional disorders, including fibromyalgia, irritable bowel syndrome, and insomnia, were already more prevalent before HL diagnosis (15.3% vs 10.2%). This underscores the need for survivorship programmes that address pre-existing as well as treatment-emergent morbidity; the post-diagnosis incidence HRs were truncated in the available abstract.

A correspondence on real-world N-AVD (S1826 regimen) in older adults argues for preserving anthracycline exposure, potentially using liposomal formulations, and for reconsidering consolidative radiotherapy in advanced-stage cHL in the elderly[^32]. In T-cell disease, an editorial discusses bendamustine for high-burden T-large granular lymphocytic leukemia[^33]. A response letter continues the debate on checkpoint inhibitors in pediatric and adolescent lymphomas[^34]. None of these provides new comparative statistics.

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

The FORTE long-term update now has a median of 99 months from first randomization (R1) and 85 months from maintenance randomization (R2)[^3].

**Induction/consolidation (R1).** KRd-ASCT produced a median PFS of 98 months, versus 70 months with KRd12 (HR 0.71, p=0.027) and 67 months with KCd-ASCT (HR 0.66, p=0.006). KRd-ASCT also improved PFS2 versus both comparators (HR 0.65, p=0.02 vs KRd12; HR 0.68, p=0.04 vs KCd-ASCT). For the first time, an OS advantage appears versus KCd-ASCT (8-year OS 76% vs 68%; HR 0.65, p=0.04).

**Maintenance (R2).** Carfilzomib for 2 years plus lenalidomide (KR) improved PFS versus lenalidomide alone (HR 0.67, p=0.02). The benefit persisted up to 2 years after carfilzomib stopped, but there was no PFS2 or OS difference. KR increased MRD conversion during active therapy (sHR 0.47, p=0.03), reduced MRD resurgence (sHR 0.53, p=0.001), and prolonged median duration of MRD negativity (50.5 vs 38 months). MRD-negative patients derived durable PFS benefit from KR (HR 0.60, p=0.031), whereas MRD-positive patients benefited mainly while on carfilzomib (HR 0.46, p=0.05). The cumulative incidence of second primary malignancy was 6%.

The key interpretive point is the KRd12 comparison. Even with a highly active carfilzomib quadruplet-era backbone, upfront ASCT still adds roughly 28 months of median PFS. This argues against deferring transplant on the basis of response depth alone, pending quadruplet-era data with anti-CD38 antibodies.

In the relapsed/refractory setting, pooled phase 1/1b data for etentamig were reported in 146 triple-class-exposed patients (median 4 prior lines, range 3–23; median follow-up 13 months). Etentamig is a BCMA×CD3 bispecific with a low-affinity CD3 arm, a bivalent BCMA binder, and retained FcRn binding that enables every-4-week dosing after a single step-up dose[^35].

- **Efficacy:** ORR 66% and ≥VGPR 54%; median DOR and PFS were not reached.
- **Safety:** grade 3/4 neutropenia 38% and infection 23%.
- **CRS with the phase 1b single step-up dose (n=23):** 30% overall (26% grade 1, 4% grade 2), with median onset 22.3 hours and resolution in 20.7 hours.

The infection rate is numerically lower than that typically reported with teclistamab or elranatamab. Follow-up is short, however, and any comparison is cross-trial.

Additional items without extractable comparative data include:

- A report of teclistamab activity in myeloma with coexisting MDS/AML[^9].
- An analysis of shared high-risk transcriptional programs spanning MGUS/smoldering-to-active myeloma that associate with progression[^36].
- Correspondence on IVIG for infection prevention in daratumumab-treated patients[^37].

## XII. Transplant & Cellular Therapy (CAR-T / HSCT / GVHD)

**Sitagliptin for aGVHD prevention.** This multicenter, open-label, randomized phase III trial enrolled 190 adults aged 18–60 receiving myeloablative haploidentical or unrelated-donor HSCT. Patients received ATG/calcineurin inhibitor/methotrexate/MMF prophylaxis with or without sitagliptin, at the dose stated in the abstract of 600 mg orally every 12 hours from day −1 to +14[^4]. Median follow-up was 29.8 months.

Grade 2–4 aGVHD by day +100 fell from 31.6% to 14.7% (sHR 0.41, p=0.005). Exploratory endpoints were also lower: grade 3–4 aGVHD (6.3% vs 18.9%; sHR 0.30, p=0.010) and intestinal aGVHD (12.6% vs 29.5%; p=0.004). Day +180 GRFS improved (86.3% vs 72.6%; HR 0.44, p=0.016). There were no differences in 2-year cGVHD, NRM, relapse-free survival, OS, CMV/EBV reactivation, engraftment, or adverse events.

These results contrast with the negative randomized PTCy-tacrolimus-MMF-based sitagliptin experience. They suggest DPP-4 inhibition may add value specifically on an ATG-based platform, which is widely used for haploidentical transplant in Chinese and other Asian centers.

**HSCT after CD19 CAR-T in B-ALL.** The EBMT GoCART study analyzed 112 patients transplanted a median 6.1 months after CAR19 infusion[^5]. Transplant indications were:

- **Rescue for relapse:** 64%.
- **Pre-emptive for loss of B-cell aplasia:** 19%.
- **Consolidative:** 17%.

Overall 2-year LFS was 54% and cumulative incidence of relapse 32%. Older age predicted inferior LFS (HR 1.30 per 10 years, p=0.01), as did haploidentical donors (HR 2.21, 95% CI 1.15–4.25, p=0.02). The difference between rescue (41%) and pre-emptive or consolidative HSCT (74% and 78%) supports serial B-cell aplasia and MRD monitoring with early transplant referral for high-risk patients, rather than waiting for overt relapse.

**Conditioning neurotoxicity.** A Dutch single-center series of αβTCR/CD19-depleted allo-HSCT compared fludarabine-thiotepa-melphalan (n=55) with fludarabine-busulfan (n=47)[^38]. Overall neurological complications were similar (31% vs 30%, mostly mild peripheral neuropathy). CNS toxicity, however, occurred exclusively with Flu-Thio-Mel (n=7), including 3 fatal leukoencephalopathies. Grade 3–5 CNS toxicity reached 9%, with older age, renal impairment, and higher fludarabine exposure as putative risk factors. This argues for renal-adjusted fludarabine dosing and caution in vulnerable patients.

**Mechanistic and review papers.** Endothelial IDO1-driven kynurenine metabolism, via the KYNU–anthranilic acid–Wnt/β-catenin axis, was identified as a mediator of blood–brain barrier disruption in ICANS[^39]. Patients with ICANS had elevated plasma IDO1 activity, and IDO1 blockade restored barrier integrity in mice. A related commentary calls for prospective evidence in steroid-refractory ICANS management[^40]. Other papers this week:

- A review traces cytokine-armored CAR-T design from IL-12 to IL-18, including the recent clinical activity of IL-18-secreting constructs[^41].
- Systemic short-chain fatty acids (butyrate, propionate) mitigated murine cGVHD by suppressing pathogenic B-cell expansion and alloantibody production[^42].
- A review synthesizes CNS GVHD pathophysiology and barrier-directed therapeutic strategies[^43].
- A correspondence addresses functional DNase assessment in pediatric post-transplant lung injury[^44].

## XIV. Taiwan Clinical Context

**Venetoclax + azacitidine (OPTI-AML).** Ven-Aza is reimbursed by NHI for unfit newly diagnosed AML under restricted criteria. Many Taiwanese centers, including NCKUH, already shorten venetoclax in later cycles to manage cytopenias. OPTI-AML supports keeping the full 28-day schedule in cycles 1–2, particularly for NPM1- or IDH2-mutated disease, and reserving abbreviation for post-remission cycles guided by marrow assessment.

**Sitagliptin (aGVHD prevention).** This trial is directly relevant to Taiwan. ATG-based haploidentical platforms remain common at NTUH, VGH-Taipei, CGMH, CMUH, and NCKUH, and sitagliptin is an inexpensive, widely available generic. Note, however, that the dose reported (600 mg every 12 hours) is roughly 12-fold the diabetic dose. It is off-label, requires a protocol and pharmacy agreement, and its benefit has been shown only on an ATG backbone, not with PTCy.

**Epcoritamab and FORTE regimens.** Before considering epcoritamab plus R-CHOP outside trials, readers should verify its current TFDA and NHI status for each indication. Frontline carfilzomib (KRd) is, to our knowledge, not NHI-reimbursed in Taiwan, where carfilzomib coverage is restricted to the relapsed setting. FORTE therefore informs maintenance and transplant decisions more than induction choice locally. The persistent ASCT benefit over KRd12 supports maintaining upfront ASCT referral pathways.

**NK/T-cell lymphoma.** NK/T-cell lymphoma remains disproportionately prevalent in Taiwan and East Asia, but no new data appeared this week.

**Access barriers.** A US claims study found that only 45.5–54.5% of patients ultimately obtained and filled newly prescribed specialty oral anticancer drugs for blood cancers[^46]. This contrasts with Taiwan's NHI model, where barriers arise from prior-authorization criteria rather than out-of-pocket cost.

## XV. Key Takeaways

1. **Epcoritamab + R-CHOP in high-IPI LBCL.** The regimen yields 2-year PFS of 80% with universal MRD negativity among evaluable patients[^1]. It remains investigational pending phase 3, and the history of R-CHOP+X failures warrants restraint[^25].

2. **Venetoclax duration in AML.** Do not routinely abbreviate venetoclax to 14 days during induction. OPTI-AML failed non-inferiority, and NPM1/IDH2-mutated patients lost nearly half their CR probability with shortened exposure[^2].

3. **FORTE in myeloma.** Upfront ASCT after KRd continues to outperform KRd12, now with PFS2 benefit and an OS signal versus KCd-ASCT. KR maintenance improves MRD durability without OS gain[^3].

4. **Sitagliptin for GVHD prevention.** Sitagliptin halved grade 2–4 aGVHD on an ATG-based alternative-donor platform without compromising relapse or infection outcomes[^4].

5. **HSCT after CAR-T in B-ALL.** Post-CAR-T HSCT in B-ALL should be planned pre-emptively or as consolidation rather than as rescue (2-year LFS 74–78% vs 41%)[^5].

6. **PAX5-rearranged pediatric ALL.** Prognosis is poor only when end-of-induction MRD is positive, and FLT3 is a candidate target[^10].

7. **CCUS risk stratification.** High-risk CCUS by CHRS/CCRS behaves like LR-MDS and should be considered for LR-MDS trials[^18]. The icMDS reporting standards should be applied when appraising MDS trials[^17].

8. **CALR-mutant MPN.** Mutant-CALR CAR-T eradicated MPN-propagating HSCs in xenografts, a first step toward transplant-free curative therapy[^22].

9. **Editorial perspective.** The *Blood* editor-in-chief's call to prioritize breakthrough over incremental therapies, with rigorous dose and duration optimization, frames OPTI-AML and EPCORE NHL-2 well[^45].
