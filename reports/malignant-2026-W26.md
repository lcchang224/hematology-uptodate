## Summary

Five signals define this week's literature. **(1)** The UK NCRI AML18 trial published in *Blood* demonstrates that quizartinib added to intensive chemotherapy in patients aged >60 years improves OS specifically in the FLT3-mutated subgroup (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.37–0.93, p = 0.024) but paradoxically increases non-relapse mortality in the unselected population (HR 1.64, 95% CI 1.04–2.59, p = 0.032), validating strict FLT3 selection for quizartinib intensification in older AML patients[^1]. **(2)** A large-scale single-cell multiomic study in *Blood* resolves the genotype–immunophenotype landscape of NPM1-mutated AML clonal evolution, demonstrating that signaling mutations drive increased clonal complexity specifically at relapse — a complexity that correlates with inferior overall survival — and that leukemic lineage trajectories appear hard-wired by the specific mutational complement present[^2]. **(3)** A phase III *Blood* trial from China randomizes 407 patients undergoing haploidentical PBSC transplantation to low-dose ATG/PTCy combination, standard ATG, or PTCy-alone GVHD prophylaxis, finding comparable grade 2–4 acute GVHD and survival across all arms, but demonstrably superior hematopoietic recovery kinetics with the ATG/PTCy combination[^3]. **(4)** A *Blood Advances* multicenter LYSA study demonstrates that ctDNA MRD negativity after four cycles of therapy in primary mediastinal large B-cell lymphoma predicts 1-year PFS of 98.4% versus 33.3% for MRD-positive patients (HR 78.1, 95% CI 9.5–641.8), and that MRD has a higher positive predictive value for progression than PET (89% vs. 50%)[^4]. **(5)** A *Blood Advances* real-world multicenter analysis establishes that TKI maintenance following brexucabtagene autoleucel in Ph+ ALL patients who achieved MRD-negative CR is associated with a markedly lower 1-year cumulative incidence of relapse (HR 0.12, 95% CI 0.02–0.88, p = 0.037) and improved PFS (HR 0.19, 95% CI 0.04–0.85, p = 0.029)[^5].

## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

### Quizartinib in Older Patients with AML Regardless of FLT3 Status: NCRI AML18 Final Results

The UK NCRI AML18 trial, now reported with a median follow-up of 76 months in *Blood*, provides important and cautionary data on the indiscriminate use of quizartinib in older AML patients[^1]. In this open-label phase III study, 463 patients with a median age of 68 years — encompassing both FLT3-mutated (n = 116) and FLT3 wild-type (n = 314) disease — were randomized to receive quizartinib 40 mg for 14 days following chemotherapy courses 2 and 3, with subsequent randomization to long (12 additional maintenance cycles) or short (no further treatment) quizartinib. The primary endpoint of OS in the unselected population was entirely negative (HR 0.99, 95% CI 0.79–1.24, p = 0.937). More concerning, quizartinib was associated with a significantly increased non-relapse mortality in the unselected cohort (HR 1.64, 95% CI 1.04–2.59, p = 0.032). In a pre-planned subgroup analysis, however, FLT3-mutated patients derived a significant OS benefit from quizartinib (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.37–0.93, p = 0.024), driven by a reduced relapse risk (HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.35–0.91, p = 0.017). The short-duration quizartinib regimen showed a numerically greater OS benefit in FLT3-mutated patients (HR 0.49, 95% CI 0.24–1.02, p = 0.055) compared with the long-duration arm, though neither reached significance on the maintenance randomization alone. These findings stand in contrast to the QuANTUM-First trial reported last week — which used quizartinib continuously from induction in FLT3-ITD patients — and cumulatively confirm that FLT3 mutation status is an absolute prerequisite for quizartinib benefit in older AML patients receiving intensive chemotherapy.

### Clonal Evolution and Immunophenotypic Trajectories in NPM1-Mutated AML

A single-cell multiomic study published in *Blood* this week performed simultaneous single-cell molecular profiling and immunophenotyping on 43 samples from 32 patients with NPM1-mutated AML across diagnosis, remission, and relapse timepoints[^2]. The analysis reveals that while diagnosis and relapse AML samples display broadly similar clonal architecture patterns, the acquisition of signaling mutations — including in FLT3, RAS pathway genes, and others — drives progressively increased clonal complexity specifically at relapse. Critically, this increased clonal complexity at relapse correlated with inferior overall survival. A key mechanistic insight is that unique genotype–immunophenotype relationships were identified that persisted regardless of disease state, suggesting that leukemic lineage differentiation trajectories are effectively hard-wired by the mutations present at any given point — a finding with implications for understanding why surface phenotype changes predictably accompany mutational evolution. Longitudinal analysis of patients on frontline therapy demonstrated dynamic clonal and immunophenotypic changes consistent with these hardwired genotype–immunophenotype relationships, supporting the use of combined genomic and immunophenotypic monitoring to anticipate and detect relapse clones. A companion editorial contextualizes this as a framework for interpreting single-cell data in the clinical monitoring of AML[^6].

### Prognostic Impact of t(9;11) and del(7q) After Allo-HCT: EBMT Registry Analysis

A retrospective EBMT registry study published in *Haematologica* this week examined the prognostic significance of two ELN 2022 intermediate-risk cytogenetic abnormalities — t(9;11)(p21.3;q23.3) and isolated del(7q) — in the context of allogeneic HCT[^7]. Among 141 patients with t(9;11) who underwent allo-HCT in CR1, 23% had additional adverse cytogenetic abnormalities (ACA), primarily complex karyotype. After a median follow-up of three years, 2-year relapse incidence (22% vs. 18.2%, p = 0.85), leukemia-free survival (66% vs. 76%, p = 0.42), and overall survival (72% vs. 75%, p = 0.68) did not differ significantly between those with and without ACA, supporting preservation of the intermediate-risk designation for t(9;11) in the allo-HCT setting. In contrast, among 84 patients with del(7q) compared with 95 patients with monosomy 7 and 71 with del(5q) — all without additional ACA — 2-year leukemia-free survival was comparable across groups (61% vs. 59% vs. 52%, respectively), suggesting del(7q) behaves more like monosomy 7 than its ELN 2022 intermediate-risk classification would imply when transplanted in CR1. These findings have direct implications for pre-transplant risk counseling in these cytogenetically defined subsets.

### Long-Read Sequencing for Rapid Karyotyping in AML

A *Blood Advances* study this week evaluated low-coverage whole-genome sequencing (lcWGS) via Oxford Nanopore Technology as an alternative to conventional metaphase karyotyping in 100 AML diagnostic samples[^8]. The platform demonstrated 93% sensitivity, specificity, and overall accuracy for chromosomal aberration detection, with particularly robust performance for complex karyotype (AUC 0.971). Reproducibility between two independent laboratories was excellent (R = 0.99). Median turnaround from sample receipt to bioinformatics interpretation was approximately 34 hours, enabling actionable karyotype results within 72 hours — substantially faster than the typical 10–14-day conventional cytogenetic turnaround. Patients with complex karyotypes identified by lcWGS had significantly shorter OS and relapse-free survival, mirroring outcomes defined by conventional cytogenetics. This platform addresses a genuine unmet need for time-sensitive risk stratification in AML, and its adoption at NCKUH and other institutions with active AML programs warrants consideration.

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

### TKI Maintenance After Brexucabtagene Autoleucel in Ph+ ALL

A multicenter real-world analysis spanning 19 U.S. institutions, published in *Blood Advances*, retrospectively evaluated outcomes in 51 adults with relapsed/refractory Ph+ ALL who achieved MRD-negative CR following commercial brexu-cel and subsequently received either TKI maintenance (n = 20) or no maintenance (n = 31)[^5]. Treating TKI maintenance as a time-varying covariate to mitigate immortal time bias, TKI maintenance was associated with a markedly lower 1-year cumulative incidence of relapse (HR 0.12, 95% CI 0.02–0.88, p = 0.037), which translated into improved progression-free survival (HR 0.19, 95% CI 0.04–0.85, p = 0.029) and a trend toward improved OS (HR 0.34, 95% CI 0.07–1.62, p = 0.18). Non-relapse mortality did not differ between the groups (HR 0.83, 95% CI 0.12–5.87, p = 0.85). Although the small cohort size, retrospective design, and heterogeneity in TKI choice preclude definitive conclusions, the magnitude of the relapse reduction is biologically plausible — residual Ph+ clones not eliminated by CAR T-cell-mediated CD19 targeting may be suppressed by ongoing ABL1 inhibition. These data provide the first real-world evidence supporting routine TKI maintenance in MRD-negative Ph+ ALL patients following brexu-cel, and a prospective study is warranted.

### CD19 CAR T-Cells as Definitive Consolidation in Older Adults with B-ALL in CR1

A phase 1 study published in *Blood Advances* enrolled 18 patients aged ≥55 years (median 64 years) with B-ALL in first complete remission, all MRD-negative at lymphodepletion, to receive memory-enriched CD19 CAR T-cells as definitive consolidation without subsequent allo-HCT[^9]. No dose-limiting toxicities occurred; there was no grade ≥2 CRS and no ICANS of any grade. Estimated 18-month event-free survival was 84% and 18-month OS was 100%. CAR T-cell expansion was documented in both blood and cerebrospinal fluid despite patients' MRD-negative status pre-infusion. Comparative analysis against a historical R/R B-ALL cohort revealed that blood and CAR T-cell products from R/R patients were hyper-inflammatory and hyper-immunometabolic, respectively, potentially explaining differences in toxicity profiles. While the single-arm nature and small size limit conclusions, this study provides meaningful early evidence that first-line CAR T-cell consolidation is safe and may extend remission in older adults with B-ALL who are poor candidates for allo-HCT.

### NG2-ITGA4 Axis in KMT2A-Rearranged B-ALL: Natalizumab as Potential Adjuvant

A *Blood* study characterizes the NG2–ITGA4 (α4 integrin) signaling axis as a driver of leukemic aggressiveness in KMT2A-rearranged B-ALL[^10]. NG2 — a direct transcriptional target of KMT2A fusions previously associated with poor prognosis and glucocorticoid resistance — promotes proliferation and migration in KMT2A-r B-ALL via ITGA4-dependent Rho GTPase activity. Combined genetic ablation of NG2 and ITGA4 significantly delayed disease onset and prolonged survival in immunodeficient mouse models. Notably, natalizumab — an FDA/EMA-approved anti-ITGA4 monoclonal antibody used in multiple sclerosis — delayed leukemia progression and potentiated standard chemotherapy efficacy in KMT2A-r B-ALL patient-derived xenograft models. These preclinical findings support drug repurposing strategies targeting this axis in a very high-risk leukemia subtype where CD19-targeted therapies have limited long-term impact.

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

### Bosutinib Second-Line for CP-CML: Final 10-Year Phase I/II Results

The final, ≥10-year report of second-line bosutinib in 284 patients with chronic-phase CML after imatinib failure was published in *Haematologica* this week, representing the longest follow-up of any TKI after imatinib failure to date[^11]. Median follow-up was 53.7 months and median treatment duration was 25.6 months. Cumulative rates of complete cytogenetic response, major molecular response (MMR), and MR4 were 49.6%, 42.1%, and 37.1%, respectively. Kaplan–Meier probabilities of maintaining these responses at year 11 were 58.3%, 56.1%, and 55.7%, respectively. The cumulative incidence of on-treatment disease progression or death was 24.6%; 15 patients (5.3%) had on-treatment transformation to accelerated or blast phase, with one occurring after year 5. The 11-year Kaplan–Meier OS rate was 70.5%. No new safety signals emerged in the extended follow-up period. These data establish the durable efficacy and acceptable late-phase safety of bosutinib as a second-line standard, with stable molecular response durability beyond a decade in responding patients.

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

### CD49d Governs BCR Immune Synapse Formation in CLL

A mechanistic study published in *Blood* this week establishes CD49d — the α4 integrin subunit whose expression dichotomizes CLL into high- and low-risk prognostic groups — as an active regulator of B-cell receptor (BCR) signaling functionality, not merely a passive prognostic marker[^12]. Using CD49d-knockout TCL1 transgenic mouse models and primary human CLL stratified by CD49d expression, the study demonstrates that CD49d is required for bone marrow infiltration and shapes leukemic cell positioning within splenic niches. CD49d-deficient murine CLL cells and human CD49d-low cells failed to form efficient immune synapses with antigen-presenting membranes. Transcriptomic analyses identified CD49d-dependent regulation of actin-associated pathways, and CD49d-low CLL cells displayed aberrant actin remodeling following BCR stimulation. Kinome profiling linked impaired antigen-induced BCR responses in CD49d-deficient murine cells to altered kinase activity. A downstream desynchronization of BCR-related Syk and PLCγ2 activation was identified in human CD49d-low CLL cells, with the CD49d–BCR interplay converging on a focal adhesion kinase–actin axis. A companion editorial contextualizes these findings within the broader pathophysiology of CLL[^13]. This mechanistic framework helps explain why CD49d expression is such a robust prognostic variable and raises the possibility that CD49d–actin pathway inhibition could be therapeutically relevant.

### Outcomes of Patients Discontinuing Covalent BTK Inhibitors in CLL Due to Adverse Events

A *Leukemia* study this week examined outcomes in CLL patients who discontinued covalent BTKi (ibrutinib or acalabrutinib) specifically due to adverse events rather than disease progression[^14]. Details from the abstract were limited, but the study addresses the clinically critical question of whether patients who discontinue for toxicity — rather than resistance — have a more favorable subsequent disease course than those progressing on BTKi. This population has become increasingly common as BTKi use expands, and understanding their prognosis guides selection of subsequent therapy including pirtobrutinib or venetoclax-based regimens.

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

### MBD1 Aberrant Splicing as a Mutation-Independent Driver of MDS

A translational study published in *Blood* this week identifies an MDS-associated isoform of MBD1 (MBD1-L) as a disease-driving splice variant that operates independently of specific splicing factor mutations — a conceptually important advance because it represents a convergent target across the molecular heterogeneity of MDS[^15]. Rather than originating from a mutant splicing factor, MBD1-L production is driven by reduced WTAP expression in MDS. Overexpression of MBD1-L in healthy human HSPCs recapitulated archetypal MDS defects including reduced terminal erythroid differentiation and impaired in vivo reconstitution. An integrated multiomics approach demonstrated that the MBD1-L isoform refocuses MBD1's heterochromatin-promoting activity from methylated to unmethylated CpGs, causing broad downregulation of CpG-rich promoters and secondary epigenetic dysregulation via its downstream target BCOR. Critically, directly reversing abnormal MBD1 splicing across primary human MDS samples using nanoparticle-encapsulated antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) enhanced in vitro erythroid differentiation, establishing a proof-of-concept for RNA-based therapeutic correction of this isoform switch. This positions MBD1-L as a candidate target for ASO-based therapy across a broad spectrum of MDS.

### Clonal Monocytosis of Undetermined Significance: Population-Level Prevalence and High-Risk Stratification

A large *Blood* study this week analyzed 431,531 UK Biobank participants to characterize CMUS (clonal monocytosis of undetermined significance) and CCMUS (clonal cytopenia and monocytosis of undetermined significance) at a population level[^16]. CMUS with absolute monocytosis and CCMUS were strongly associated with incident myeloid neoplasia, cardiovascular disease, and renal disease. Amending the definition of CMUS/CCMUS to incorporate sex-specific monocyte thresholds (accounting for higher baseline monocyte counts in men) and excluding isolated DNMT3A mutations significantly strengthened the association with incident myeloid neoplasia. The authors developed MoSAIC, a machine-learning classifier that infers SRSF2 mutations — associated with high MN risk — from complete blood count indices alone, and validated findings in 625,328 Danish primary care patients. A companion editorial contextualizes these findings within the spectrum of clonal hematopoiesis[^17]. These data support the clinical utility of recognizing CMUS/CCMUS as distinct high-risk entities and refining their diagnostic criteria, with implications for hematologists encountering unexplained monocytosis in routine practice.

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

### Galectin-1 as a Driver of Monocyte-Mediated Inflammation in MPNs

A study published in *Blood* this week identifies galectin-1 (Gal-1, encoded by *LGALS1*) as an upregulated inflammatory mediator specifically in MPN monocytes, with functional and therapeutic implications[^18]. Single-cell RNA sequencing of human MPN samples and mouse models revealed significant enrichment of galectin gene expression in MPN monocytes, with cell–cell communication analysis positioning monocytes as pivotal mediators of intercellular interactions. Mass cytometry (CyTOF) profiling demonstrated that recombinant Gal-1 significantly increased multiple inflammatory cytokines specifically in monocytes without affecting other cell types. Mechanistically, crosstalk between the TLR4 and Gal-1 signaling pathways was demonstrated via 3D protein modeling and co-immunoprecipitation, with NF-κB identified as the key downstream effector activated in a TLR4-dependent manner. In an MPLW515L-driven murine MPN model, genetic abrogation of *Lgals1* ameliorated key MPN disease features including leukocytosis and splenomegaly. Additionally, Gal-1 inhibition suppressed carrageenan-induced thrombosis and inflammation in vivo. These findings position Gal-1 as a tractable therapeutic target for the inflammatory and thrombotic complications that drive MPN-related morbidity, a pathway distinct from current JAK inhibitor-based strategies.

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

### CD79B Expression Gradient in DLBCL Corresponds to Germinal Center Differentiation Stage

A *Haematologica* study examined CD79B protein expression by immunohistochemistry in 590 de novo DLBCL cases with external validation in 272 cases, identifying a CD79B expression gradient according to cell-of-origin (COO) subtype[^19]. ABC-DLBCL showed the lowest CD79B expression, followed by GCB-DLBCL and dark-zone signature-positive (DZsigpos) cases in ascending order (p < 0.0001). Single-cell proteomic and transcriptomic analyses of 2,447 normal germinal center B-cells revealed a progressive decrease in CD79B expression as GC B-cells transitioned toward terminal differentiation, providing the biological explanation for the COO-dependent gradient. This COO-dependent CD79B gradient has practical implications for polatuzumab vedotin — the CD79B-targeted ADC now standard of care in frontline DLBCL — as it suggests that GCB or DZsigpos tumors may actually carry higher target antigen density despite ABC-DLBCL deriving greater clinical benefit in pola-R-CHP analyses. The biological paradox of greater clinical benefit in lower CD79B-expressing ABC tumors warrants further investigation.

### ctDNA MRD Versus PET in Primary Mediastinal Large B-Cell Lymphoma

A prospective multicenter LYSA study published in *Blood Advances* evaluated the clinical relevance of ctDNA-based MRD assessment in 84 patients with newly diagnosed PMBL treated with R-CHOP14 or R-ACVBP[^4]. Baseline ctDNA was detected in 98% of patients. After four cycles of therapy, 87.7% had undetectable MRD. Persistence of MRD after 4 cycles (MRD4 positivity) was associated with dramatically shorter PFS (HR 78.1, 95% CI 9.5–641.8), with 1-year PFS of 98.4% (95% CI 95.4–100%) for MRD4-negative versus 33.3% (95% CI 13.2–84.0%) for MRD4-positive patients (p < 10⁻⁴). Compared with PET at cycle 4, ctDNA MRD4 demonstrated a higher positive predictive value for disease progression (89% vs. 50%) while maintaining a similar negative predictive value (100% vs. 93%). In multivariate analysis, only the combined PET4⁻/MRD4⁻ status retained independent association with PFS (adjusted HR 0.07, 95% CI 0.01–0.90). These data position ctDNA MRD as a superior positive predictive biomarker to PET in PMBL, with direct implications for future risk-adapted treatment designs.

### Meta-Analysis of ctDNA MRD and PET Response in LBCL

Complementing the PMBL data above, a *Blood Advances* meta-analysis pooled three studies comprising 367 LBCL patients to quantify the prognostic superiority of phased-variant ctDNA MRD over PET response at end of frontline treatment[^20]. The pooled HR for PFS comparing undetectable versus detectable MRD was 14.02 (95% CI 7.35–26.74), versus 5.09 (95% CI 3.29–7.88) for PET complete metabolic response versus non-CMR (p = 0.01 for group difference). Two-year PFS rates by combined MRD/PET status were 95.7% (MRD⁻/CMR), 91.3% (MRD⁻/no CMR), 40.2% (MRD⁺/CMR), and 23.4% (MRD⁺/no CMR) (p < 0.0001). The MRD⁺/CMR group — patients who would be classified as responding by PET but carry residual ctDNA — had only a 40.2% 2-year PFS, highlighting the false reassurance conferred by PET alone in a substantial proportion of patients.

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

### Diagnosis-to-Treatment Interval as an Independent Prognostic Factor in Follicular Lymphoma

A *Blood* study by Vodička et al. using international discovery and validation cohorts demonstrates that a short diagnosis-to-treatment interval (DTI) independently predicts inferior outcomes in follicular lymphoma treated with immunochemotherapy, with prognostic value beyond established indices such as FLIPI[^21]. The finding has immediate implications for clinical trial design and patient stratification — DTI may be a surrogate for biological aggressiveness not captured by conventional scoring systems. Trials that enrich for short-DTI patients may inadvertently create unfavorable comparison cohorts, and risk-stratification algorithms in FL should consider DTI as an additional variable.

### RENOIR Phase III: R² vs. R Maintenance in Elderly Relapsed/Refractory FL

The RENOIR trial, a phase III FIL study published in *Blood Advances*, randomized 129 elderly patients (median age 71 years) with R/R follicular lymphoma who had responded to rituximab-based induction to maintenance with R² (rituximab plus lenalidomide, n = 64) versus R alone (n = 65)[^22]. The primary endpoint of 2-year PFS improvement with R² was not met (HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.46–1.16, p = 0.183), with 2-year PFS of 73% versus 64%. Overall survival was similar between arms (2-year OS: R² 80% vs. R 89%; HR 1.12). An unplanned, hypothesis-generating subgroup analysis suggested a greater benefit with R² in patients aged <70 years (HR 0.35; 2-year PFS 96% vs. 69%), whereas the oldest patients derived no benefit and faced higher toxicity. Grade 3/4 adverse events — predominantly neutropenia and gastrointestinal disorders — were more frequent with R². These data indicate that R² maintenance cannot be uniformly recommended in elderly R/R FL patients, though the age interaction warrants prospective validation.

### Lenalidomide Maintenance After Methotrexate-Based Induction in PCNSL

A multicenter phase II trial published in *Haematologica* evaluated lenalidomide 15 mg on days 1–21 of 28-day cycles for up to 12 cycles as maintenance in 31 patients with PCNSL (median age 72 years) who were ineligible for WBRT or ASCT[^23]. At a median follow-up of 20.7 months, the 2-year PFS was 58.8% (95% CI 43.4–79.8%), numerically exceeding the pre-specified alternative threshold of 55%, but the formal one-sample log-rank test did not reach significance (p = 0.084). The 2-year OS was 83.9%. Grade 3–4 neutropenia occurred in 53.3%; one grade 5 pneumonia was observed. These results are hypothesis-generating and suggest that lenalidomide maintenance warrants evaluation in a randomized trial for this population with limited consolidation options.

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

### Gonadal Function After Nivolumab-AVD in Classic HL: GHSG NIVAHL Phase 2 Data

A gonadal function substudy from the GHSG NIVAHL phase 2 trial, published in *Blood*, assessed 82 patients with classic HL receiving frontline 4×N-AVD (nivolumab plus doxorubicin, vinblastine, and dacarbazine)[^24]. Stable gonadal hormone levels were observed across both sexes up to 24 months after N-AVD completion, suggesting preserved gonadal function and fertility potential after this chemotherapy-free/reduced-chemotherapy regimen. These data are reassuring for young patients with cHL for whom fertility preservation is a treatment priority, and provide an additional argument for checkpoint inhibitor-inclusive frontline approaches over bleomycin-containing regimens that carry higher gonadotoxic risk.

### TCRVβ-Targeting ADC: A Clone-Restricted Strategy for CTCL

A *Blood Advances* study provides proof-of-concept for TCR variable β-chain (TCRVβ)-specific antibody-drug conjugates as a clone-restricted strategy to eliminate malignant T cells in cutaneous T-cell lymphoma (CTCL), exploiting the clonal TCR with minimal collateral damage to the normal T-cell repertoire[^25]. Analysis of TCR sequencing from 104 CTCL patients showed TCR Vβ2 (TRBV20-1) as the most common TCRVβ. An anti-TCRVβ2 ADC selectively killed malignant cells from patients with TCRVβ2+ leukemic CTCL and significantly inhibited tumor growth in a MOLT-16 mouse model. This represents a conceptually novel precision approach for T-cell lymphomas, where pan-T-cell targeting strategies inevitably cause profound immunosuppression.

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

### IRF2 as an Essential Transcription Factor and Therapeutic Target in Multiple Myeloma

A CRISPR-Cas9 library screening study in *Blood* identified 22 essential transcription factors in multiple myeloma cells, among which IRF2 emerged as critically important alongside the well-established IRF4[^26]. CUT&RUN experiments demonstrated that IRF2 binds extensively to chromatin independently and in cooperation with IRF1 and IRF4, with IRF2-unique regions predominantly associated with active promoters. Functionally, IRF2 contributes to MM cell survival by suppressing necroptosis and promoting cell migration. IRF2-dependent transcriptional dysregulation was evident even in precursor conditions (MGUS and smoldering MM), suggesting a role in early disease evolution. In multivariate analyses adjusting for common MM genetic risk factors, higher IRF2 expression was independently associated with worse PFS and OS. A companion editorial frames IRF2 alongside IRF4 as dual therapeutic targets within the IRF family in MM[^27].
