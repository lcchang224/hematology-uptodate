## Summary

This week's literature is dominated by *Blood Advances* publications spanning hemophilia A gene therapy and model development, pediatric AIHA (an expanded companion analysis to last week's report), von Willebrand disease pharmacoeconomics, and a methodologically important thrombophilia-testing commentary, alongside several *RPTH* papers addressing antiphospholipid syndrome complement biology, DOAC monitoring, and VTE epidemiology. First, a *Blood Advances* study in hemophilia A mice demonstrates that antiplatelet therapy with aspirin or clopidogrel does not substantially impair the hemostatic efficacy of platelet-targeted FVIII gene therapy, addressing a long-standing concern about combining platelet-delivered factor replacement with concurrent cardiovascular antiplatelet regimens[^1]. Second, a novel humanized FIX/FX double-knockin hemophilia A mouse model (HuHA) is described, enabling in vivo functional evaluation of human-specific FVIIIa-mimetic bispecifics such as emicizumab without requiring protein infusion, with thrombin generation assays showing dose-dependent peak thrombin improvement with both recombinant human FVIII and emicizumab[^2]. Third, the full peer-reviewed publication of the 399-patient, 15-center pediatric AIHA cohort appears in *Blood Advances*, now reporting that 28% of warm AIHA patients (72/257) remained on active treatment at a median follow-up of 4.9 years and that the median number of treatments for the first episode was 2 (range 0–17), extending last week's secondary-etiology and recurrence-risk findings with new longitudinal treatment-burden data[^3]. Fourth, a trial-based cost-effectiveness analysis of plasma-derived VWF/FVIII (Wilate) prophylaxis versus on-demand therapy in type 3 von Willebrand disease estimates annual payer-perspective savings of $10,898 per patient overall, $53,264 among adults, and $125,712 among adult women, with societal-perspective savings reaching $148,555 annually in women[^4]. Fifth, a *Blood Advances* editorial commentary directly interrogates whether thrombophilia testing meaningfully affects clinical risk stratification, a timely counterpoint to the large meta-analysis on thrombophilia-subtype VTE recurrence risk reported last week[^5],[^6].

## II. TTP / HUS / Thrombotic Microangiopathy

A brief *AJH* commentary this week, "Schistocytes Are a Clue, Not a Gatekeeper in Thrombotic Microangiopathy," argues against over-reliance on schistocyte quantification as a diagnostic gatekeeper for TMA, instead emphasizing that clinical and laboratory gestalt (ADAMTS13 activity, LDH, platelet count trajectory) should drive urgent plasma exchange decisions even when schistocyte counts are borderline or initially absent[^8]. This conceptual piece complements last week's prospective MRI cohort demonstrating progressive cerebral small vessel disease in iTTP survivors despite hematologic remission[^9], reinforcing a broader theme this cycle: that conventional single-parameter diagnostic and remission thresholds in TMA may understate ongoing disease activity and end-organ risk. A methodologic scoping review of 143 published mouse models of VWF/ADAMTS-13–related thrombosis identifies TTP as the most frequently modeled systemic thrombotic phenotype (21 of 143 studies, 14.7%), while highlighting substantial heterogeneity in strain choice (32.6% C57BL/6), sex inclusion (32.7% male-only), and injury methodology (ferric chloride in 31.5%, laser injury in 9.8%), which the authors argue undermines cross-study comparability of preclinical VWF-targeted therapeutics research relevant to TTP and related TMAs[^10].

## V. Hemophilia A & B (including gene therapy)

This week brings three complementary preclinical hemophilia A papers in *Blood Advances*. First, a study using platelet-targeted FVIII gene therapy in hemophilia A mice examined the impact of concurrent antiplatelet therapy (aspirin, clopidogrel) on hemostatic efficacy, an important translational question given that platelet-based gene therapy approaches deliver FVIII from α-granules upon platelet activation and could theoretically be blunted by antiplatelet agents used for cardiovascular comorbidities in an aging hemophilia population; the hemostatic benefit of platelet-targeted FVIII gene therapy was preserved despite concurrent antiplatelet exposure[^1]. Second, a novel humanized FIX/FX double-knockin hemophilia A (HuHA) mouse model was generated by replacing murine FIX/FX genes with human homologs on an FVIII-null background, with constitutive expression of functional human FIX and FX at or above physiologic human plasma concentrations; thrombin generation assays demonstrated dose-dependent improvement in peak thrombin with both recombinant human FVIII and emicizumab, and tail-clip bleeding assays suggested hemostatic benefit from both agents in vivo, providing a new platform for preclinical evaluation of human-specific FVIIIa-mimetic therapeutics without reliance on acquired-hemophilia nonhuman primate models[^2]. Third, a mechanistic mouse model study characterized hemarthropathy-associated pain in FVIII-knockout mice, demonstrating that subpatellar knee injury produced nocifensive behaviors, mechanical and deep-tissue hyperalgesia, and gait changes persisting through 8 weeks, accompanied by elevated circulating serum amyloid P and interleukin-6 and joint-level increases in neutrophil elastase, myeloperoxidase, mast cell degranulation, substance P, and calcitonin gene-related peptide; recombinant FVIII treatment produced only partial improvement in pain behaviors, suggesting that pain may persist in hemophilic arthropathy despite adequate factor replacement and identifying protease-activated receptor 2 signaling as a potential therapeutic target[^11]. Separately, an *RPTH* laboratory study assessed six FVIII assays for monitoring efanesoctocog alfa (an ultra-extended half-life FVIII) in patient plasma versus spiked samples, finding that plasma-calibrated chromogenic assays overestimated FVIII activity by 37–94% in spiked samples while efanesoctocog-specific calibration improved agreement (+2% to +16%), but that in actual patient samples no single assay remained within the acceptable 80–120% recovery range across the full FVIII spectrum, with one-stage assays overestimating and chromogenic assays underestimating activity at trough levels — a finding of direct relevance to clinical laboratories monitoring patients transitioning to extended half-life products[^12].

## VI. Von Willebrand Disease

A trial-based cost-effectiveness analysis compared plasma-derived VWF/FVIII concentrate (Wilate) prophylaxis against on-demand therapy in type 3 VWD in the United States, using clinical inputs from phase 3 studies and incorporating both payer and societal perspectives across 1-year and lifetime horizons. From the payer perspective, prophylaxis was associated with estimated annual savings of $10,898 per individual in the overall type 3 VWD cohort, $53,264 among adults, and $125,712 among adult women; societal-perspective savings were larger, reaching $148,555 annually per individual among women, driven primarily by reduced productivity losses during bleeding events. The primary cost drivers identified were bleeding event rates and on-demand treatment costs, supporting broader adoption of VWF-based prophylaxis in this severely affected subgroup[^4]. Complementing this economic analysis, the methodologic scoping review of 143 VWF/ADAMTS-13 mouse thrombosis models noted above provides a critical resource for investigators designing future preclinical VWF-targeted therapeutic studies, though it identifies considerable need for standardization[^10].

## VIII. Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia (AIHA)

The full peer-reviewed *Blood Advances* publication of the multicenter pediatric AIHA study (399 patients, 15 centers) provides additional granularity beyond what was captured in last week's preliminary summary. Beyond the previously reported secondary-etiology distribution (Evans syndrome 37%, other autoimmunity 22%, inborn errors of immunity 18%) and recurrence-risk associations, this week's full-text data add that genetic testing for IEI was performed in only 109 of 348 eligible patients (31%), with pathogenic findings in 32% of those tested, underscoring substantial underutilization of genetic evaluation in this population. Of patients with warm AIHA, 31% received steroid-sparing therapy during their first episode, and the median number of treatments required for the first AIHA episode was 2 (range 0–17), reflecting marked interpatient heterogeneity in treatment burden. At a median follow-up of 4.9 years (range 0–19.4), 72 of 257 patients with warm AIHA (28%) continued to have active disease on treatment, and all 10 deaths in the cohort occurred among patients with secondary diagnoses, reinforcing that mortality risk in pediatric AIHA is concentrated in those with underlying immune dysregulation rather than primary/idiopathic disease[^3]. These findings collectively argue for routine, expansive immune evaluation — including genetic testing for IEI — at AIHA diagnosis rather than reserving such workup for refractory or recurrent cases.

## IX. Red Cell Disorders (G6PD, PK deficiency, hereditary spherocytosis)

A *Blood Advances* CRISPR-Cas9 screen in enucleated red blood cells derived from primary human hematopoietic stem/progenitor cells identified Chloride Intracellular Channel 3 (CLIC3) and Vesicle-associated membrane protein 8 (VAMP8) as genes required for terminal erythroid enucleation, with CLIC3 knockdown producing p53/p21 upregulation and cell-cycle alterations culminating in impaired enucleation, while VAMP8-depleted cells arrested at the orthochromatic erythroblast stage with disorganized actin and impaired nuclear polarization[^13]. While primarily a basic-science contribution, this work provides mechanistic insight relevant to disorders of erythroid maturation and may eventually inform understanding of dyserythropoietic phenotypes seen in hereditary red cell membrane and enzyme disorders. No new clinical data on G6PD deficiency, pyruvate kinase deficiency, or hereditary spherocytosis were identified this week.

## X. Iron Deficiency & Nutritional Anemias

An *AJH* brief report questions whether soluble transferrin receptor (sTfR) and the sTfR/log ferritin index offer diagnostic advantages over ferritin alone in distinguishing anemia of chronic disease from iron deficiency anemia, a methodologically relevant challenge to a widely used adjunctive testing strategy, though granular comparative performance data were not available in the abstract[^14].

## XI. Thrombosis & Coagulation (VTE, APS, inherited thrombophilia)

Several *RPTH* papers this week extend understanding of VTE risk stratification and antiphospholipid syndrome pathophysiology. A UK Biobank analysis of 447,681 participants examined the sarcopenia index (SI, based on serum creatinine-to-cystatin C ratio) and polygenic risk in relation to incident VTE over a median 13.4-year follow-up (12,008 events), finding that higher SI was associated with lower VTE risk in a dose-dependent fashion (fully adjusted HR 0.88, 95% CI 0.83–0.92 for Q2 vs Q1; HR 0.77, 95% CI 0.72–0.82 for Q4 vs Q1), while high genetic risk nearly tripled VTE risk (HR 2.56, 95% CI 2.42–2.72); participants with both low SI and high genetic risk had the highest combined risk (HR 3.60, 95% CI 3.14–4.12 versus high-SI/low-genetic-risk participants), suggesting SI may serve as an accessible adjunct risk marker[^15]. A multiplex complement profiling study in 58 APS patients (51 with microvascular APS) found significantly elevated plasma Ba, C3a, C4a, and soluble C5b-9 and reduced C1q, C2, and factor P compared with 20 healthy controls (all p<0.05), with cluster analysis identifying three distinct complement activation phenotypes and a combined factor P/C3a predictive model achieving an AUC of 0.898 (sensitivity 82.4%, specificity 88.9%) for microvascular APS identification, supporting a role for complement-targeted therapy in this subgroup[^16]. In the VTE epidemiology space, a matched registry analysis of 1,792 Hispanic and 5,376 non-Hispanic patients from the RIETE registry found Hispanic ethnicity associated with increased adjusted odds of 90-day recurrent VTE (OR 1.90, 95% CI 1.10–3.28) and major bleeding (OR 1.73, 95% CI 1.09–2.75), though not mortality (OR 1.05, 95% CI 0.68–1.64), with the authors cautioning that absolute differences were small and residual confounding cannot be excluded[^17]. A population-based Norwegian cohort study (Tromsø Study and Trøndelag Health Study, 2,071 incident VTE patients, 310 recurrences over median 2.8 years follow-up) found that coffee consumption was associated with a nonlinear reduction in VTE recurrence risk (HR 0.49, 95% CI 0.30–0.81 for 1–2 cups/day versus non-drinkers), an association more pronounced after provoked index VTE (HR 0.50, 95% CI 0.30–0.82) than after unprovoked VTE (HR 1.01, 95% CI 0.53–1.94)[^18]. Finally, an editorial commentary in *Blood Advances* directly challenges the clinical utility of thrombophilia testing in guiding anticoagulation duration decisions, serving as an important counterpoint to last week's meta-analysis quantifying twofold-to-threefold recurrence risk elevations by thrombophilia subtype, and will warrant close attention as practice guidelines evolve[^5],[^6]. On the anticoagulant monitoring front, a five-year single-center observational study of 584 bleeding or non-elective procedural events in DOAC-treated patients found 79% guideline compliance with no association between compliance and outcomes, while rivaroxaban concentrations were higher among bleeding patients with adverse outcomes than without (243 ng/mL, IQR 74–367, versus 88 ng/mL, IQR 35–199)[^19], and a complementary review summarized the limitations of current DOAC activity assays and the potential role of emerging point-of-care testing in time-sensitive scenarios such as acute ischemic stroke and major bleeding[^20].

## XIII. Taiwan Clinical Context

For NCKUH practice, several of this week's findings carry direct relevance. The efanesoctocog alfa assay discrepancy data[^12] are pertinent as extended half-life FVIII products become more accessible under Taiwan's NHI hemophilia reimbursement pathway; local coagulation laboratories should be aware that neither one-stage nor chromogenic assays reliably track efanesoctocog alfa activity across the clinically relevant range, and product-specific calibrators should be requested from reference laboratories when monitoring is clinically indicated. The pediatric AIHA data reinforce that patients presenting with Evans syndrome or recurrent AIHA warrant referral for expansive immune and genetic evaluation; given Taiwan's growing access to next-generation sequencing panels for inborn errors of immunity through NHI-supported genetic testing programs at major medical centers, this cohort's 32% pathogenic yield in genetically tested patients supports lowering the threshold for genetic workup in recurrent or syndromic pediatric AIHA presentations. Von Willebrand disease prophylaxis cost-effectiveness data, while US-specific, may inform future NHI reimbursement discussions for VWF concentrate prophylaxis in severe type 3 VWD patients, a population that remains predominantly on-demand-managed in Taiwan. Thalassemia carrier prevalence in Taiwan (approximately 4–5% for alpha-thalassemia and 1–2% for beta-thalassemia) remains an important background consideration for antenatal screening programs, though no new thalassemia-specific data were published this cycle.

## XIV. Key Takeaways

The week's strongest signal is methodological and translational: two new hemophilia A mouse models (platelet-targeted gene therapy with antiplatelet co-administration, and the humanized FIX/FX HuHA model) substantially expand the preclinical toolkit for evaluating next-generation FVIII replacement and mimetic strategies, directly complementary to last week's Inno8 oral bispecific data. The pediatric AIHA full publication confirms and extends prior preliminary findings, emphasizing persistent disease activity in 28% of warm AIHA patients at nearly 5 years and underutilization of genetic IEI testing (31% tested despite high diagnostic yield). In thrombosis, the juxtaposition of a mechanistic APS complement-profiling study supporting complement-targeted intervention against the editorial skepticism regarding thrombophilia testing utility highlights an active tension in the field between molecular stratification and pragmatic clinical decision-making — a debate NCKUH hematologists should follow closely as it may influence future anticoagulation-duration guidelines.
