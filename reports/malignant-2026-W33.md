## Summary

Five signals define this week's literature. **(1)** The phase 3 MonumenTAL-3 trial published in the *New England Journal of Medicine* demonstrates that talquetamab-based combinations (Tal-DP and Tal-D) deliver striking progression-free survival benefit over daratumumab-pomalidomide-dexamethasone in relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma after as few as one prior line, with 24-month PFS estimates of 81.3% and 77.6% versus 51.2% and hazard ratios of 0.28 and 0.33 respectively (both p<0.001), establishing a new benchmark for GPRC5D-directed therapy in earlier relapse[^1]. **(2)** A phase I/II study of sonrotoclax (BGB-11417), a next-generation BCL-2 inhibitor more potent and selective than venetoclax, published in the *Journal of Clinical Oncology*, achieves an independent review committee-assessed ORR of 52.4% (95% CI, 42.4–62.4%) in BTK inhibitor-pretreated relapsed/refractory mantle cell lymphoma, including responses in TP53-mutated patients (59.1%), with a median DOR of 15.8 months[^2]. **(3)** A multicenter phase 3 randomized trial published in *The Lancet Haematology* demonstrates that low-dose ruxolitinib substituted for mycophenolate mofetil in post-haploidentical HSCT GVHD prophylaxis reduces the cumulative incidence of grade II–IV acute GVHD from 36.9% to 6.8% by day 100 (subdistribution HR 0.15; 95% CI, 0.07–0.34; p<0.0001)[^3]. **(4)** The largest registry-based analysis of HCT for relapsed/refractory classical Hodgkin lymphoma (n=19,498), published in *The Lancet Haematology*, demonstrates that 2-year PFS and OS after auto-HCT improved from 63%/85% (2010–14) to 73%/93% (2019–22), and similarly after allo-HCT from 44%/66% to 62%/72%, validating modern transplant approaches across eras[^4]. **(5)** A prospective multicenter study in *Blood Advances* reports real-world outcomes with epcoritamab in 157 patients with relapsed/refractory aggressive B-cell lymphomas, showing an ORR of 49.6%, a CRR of 31.9%, and a 1-year PFS of 29% (95% CI, 22–37%), with higher toxicity rates than pivotal trials, underscoring the challenge of translating bispecific antibody therapy to unselected patients[^5].

## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

### Venetoclax with Cytarabine in Pediatric R/R AML: Five-Year Results from VENAML

The combination of venetoclax with intensive chemotherapy has reshaped adult AML therapy, but prospective pediatric data remain limited. The VENAML phase 1 expansion study, published this week in *The Lancet Haematology*, enrolled 61 patients aged 2–24 years with relapsed or refractory AML at three US research hospitals between 2017 and 2022, with a median follow-up of 5.3 years (IQR 4.2–6.3)[^6]. Among 44 patients treated at the recommended phase 2 dose of venetoclax 360 mg/m² (maximum 600 mg daily for 27 days) combined with high-dose cytarabine 1,000 mg/m² every 12 hours for eight doses (cohort A), with or without idarubicin (cohort B) or azacitidine (cohort C), composite complete response after one cycle was achieved in 25 patients (57%; 95% CI, 41–72%), of whom 19 were MRD-negative. Common grade 3–4 adverse events included febrile neutropenia (52%), gastrointestinal disorders (32%), and infections (25%); two grade 5 events due to sepsis and multi-organ failure were observed. These five-year results are notably durable and support the ongoing phase 3 randomized pediatric trial (NCT05183035) that was prompted by this data. For practicing hematologists, the 57% composite CR rate in a heavily pretreated pediatric population compares favorably with historical salvage benchmarks and positions venetoclax-cytarabine as a legitimate bridge-to-transplant platform in children.

### RUNX1 Mutations as Diagnostic and MRD Markers in AML Around Allo-SCT

A multicenter analysis published in *Haematologica* this week characterizes the prognostic impact of *RUNX1* mutations at diagnosis and as MRD markers before and after allogeneic stem cell transplantation in AML[^7]. The study systematically evaluated *RUNX1* mutation tracking by high-sensitivity sequencing at pre- and post-transplant timepoints, demonstrating that *RUNX1*-MRD positivity before allo-SCT was independently associated with inferior relapse-free survival, while clearance to MRD-negativity after transplant conferred a substantially improved prognosis. These findings add *RUNX1* to the growing panel of mutation-based MRD targets applicable in the peri-transplant window and have direct implications for post-transplant intervention timing decisions.

### Real-World Treatment Intensity and Prognostic Model Performance in Older AML

The French ALFA-PPP cohort analysis published in *Leukemia* this week examined real-world patterns of treatment intensity and the calibration of established prognostic models in elderly AML patients[^8]. The study identifies significant discordance between model-predicted and observed outcomes in routine practice, particularly in patients aged ≥75 years receiving hypomethylating agent-based regimens, and demonstrates that current ELN2022 risk stratification may over-estimate the benefit of intensive induction in borderline-fit older patients. These findings are directly relevant to the decision-making framework used at academic centers across Asia, where the proportion of elderly patients with AML is rising.

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

### KMT2A::AFF1 Fusion Circular RNA Regulates Mitochondrial Function in t(4;11) B-ALL

A translational study published in *Haematologica* identifies a novel recurrent *KMT2A::AFF1*-derived back-splicing junction (AK_7_3) functioning as a fusion circular RNA (f-circRNA) in t(4;11)-positive B-ALL[^9]. AK_7_3 was detected in 12 of 18 pediatric and 17 of 24 adult t(4;11)-positive B-ALL patients, including paired diagnosis-relapse samples, but was absent in 23 t(4;11)-negative patients. Knockdown of AK_7_3 in B-ALL cell lines induced reactive oxygen species overproduction, increased mitochondrial membrane potential and respiration, and triggered apoptosis, establishing an anti-apoptotic role for this f-circRNA in *KMT2A::AFF1*-driven leukemia. These findings open a new mechanistic dimension for one of the highest-risk ALL cytogenetic subgroups and suggest potential therapeutic targeting of mitochondrial metabolic pathways.

### KSR2 as a Novel Oncogene in TAL1-Subtype T-ALL

A study in *Haematologica* characterizes *KSR2* as a novel oncogene in the TAL1 subtype of T-cell ALL, with prognostic implications for risk stratification[^10]. *KSR2* overexpression cooperated with *TAL1* to promote leukemogenesis in preclinical models, and KSR2 expression levels correlated with clinical outcomes in patient cohorts, suggesting its potential utility as both a biomarker and therapeutic target in this high-risk T-ALL subset.

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

### FLT3 and KIR3DL1 in CML Treatment-Free Remission: An Emerging Immune Axis

A correspondence published in *Leukemia* proposes a mechanistic framework in which FLT3 signaling and the inhibitory NK-cell receptor KIR3DL1 converge on a shared immune surveillance axis governing treatment-free remission (TFR) maintenance in CML[^11]. The authors hypothesize that FLT3-mediated dendritic cell maturation and KIR3DL1-regulated NK-cell activity cooperate to maintain immunological control over residual leukemic stem cells during TFR. While hypothesis-generating, this framework provides a rational basis for prospective studies combining TKI discontinuation protocols with NK-cell immunomodulatory strategies, which may be particularly relevant for centers in Taiwan where a substantial proportion of CML patients achieve deep molecular response and are candidates for TFR attempts.

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

### ZMYM3 Mutations: A Novel Adverse Prognostic Factor Cooperating with NOTCH1 in CLL

A comprehensive translational study published in *Blood Advances* characterizes *ZMYM3* mutations in a cohort of 487 CLL patients and establishes them as an independent adverse prognostic factor[^12]. *ZMYM3* variants, predominantly loss-of-function, were identified in 6.6% of patients and were strongly enriched for *NOTCH1* pathway mutations, with 60% of *ZMYM3*-mutated patients also harboring *NOTCH1* mutations. Clinically, *ZMYM3* mutations were associated with significantly shorter time to first treatment (median 35 vs. 53 months; p=0.010) and inferior outcomes in early-stage patients (median time to treatment 48 vs. 108 months; p=0.002), confirmed as an independent adverse factor on multivariable analysis. Functionally, *ZMYM3* mutations cooperated with *NOTCH1* mutations to cause transcriptional dysregulation, reduced chromatin accessibility through impaired histone acetylation, and caspase-mediated apoptosis evasion. Importantly, primary *ZMYM3*-mutated CLL cells demonstrated increased sensitivity to BCL-XL inhibition and ibrutinib, suggesting precision-stratified therapeutic opportunities.

### BTK Inhibitors Reduce Secondary Myeloid Neoplasm Risk Compared with Chemoimmunotherapy

A propensity score-matched analysis published in *Leukemia* demonstrates that BTK inhibitor-treated CLL patients have a significantly lower risk of developing therapy-related myeloid neoplasms and non-melanoma skin cancer compared with patients treated with chemoimmunotherapy regimens[^13]. This population-level safety signal has increasing relevance as BTK inhibitors are deployed earlier in the CLL treatment course, and the finding supports the long-term oncological safety advantage of targeted therapy over cytotoxic approaches in CLL.

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

### MDS Without Clonal Marker: Depth over Breadth in Sequencing

A perspectives piece in *Leukemia* addresses the diagnostic challenge of MDS without detectable clonal markers by conventional next-generation sequencing, arguing that ultra-deep targeted sequencing substantially outperforms broader but shallower panel approaches in identifying low-level somatic mutations that anchor clonal diagnoses[^14]. The authors present data demonstrating that error-corrected deep sequencing (>10,000× coverage) at loci including *SF3B1*, *SRSF2*, *U2AF1*, *ASXL1*, and *TET2* enables clonal confirmation in a meaningful fraction of morphologically and clinically diagnostic MDS cases that would otherwise remain genetically unclassified. This has implications for NHI reimbursement eligibility and clinical trial enrollment at Taiwanese centers where MDS diagnosis frequently depends on ancillary molecular data.

### SLF2 and SMC5 Dysfunction: A Novel Inherited Bone Marrow Failure Syndrome Predisposing to MDS

A study in *Leukemia* describes a new inherited bone marrow failure syndrome caused by biallelic *SLF2* and *SMC5* dysfunction, components of the SMC5/6 complex involved in DNA repair[^15]. The authors demonstrate that germline mutations in *SLF2* or *SMC5* drive accelerated HSC aging, DNA damage accumulation, and progressive predisposition to MDS. This expands the landscape of genetic bone marrow failure syndromes and warrants consideration in young patients presenting with MDS and family history of cytopenias.

### FOLR2-IRF2BP2 Axis in Folic Acid–Mediated Erythropoiesis Impairment in MDS

Mechanistic work published in *Leukemia* delineates the FOLR2-IRF2BP2 signaling axis as a mediator of erythropoiesis impairment caused by excess folic acid in MDS[^16]. The study demonstrates that supraphysiologic folic acid paradoxically suppresses erythroid differentiation in MDS progenitors through this pathway, challenging the routine supplementation practice in MDS patients and warranting prospective evaluation of folate level optimization.

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

### PV-RR6: A Dynamic Prognostic Model for Ruxolitinib-Treated Polycythemia Vera

A novel prognostic model for polycythemia vera patients receiving ruxolitinib following hydroxyurea failure is reported in *Blood Advances* from the observational PV-ARC study (NCT06134102)[^17]. Among 178 PV patients with a median follow-up of 3.5 years from ruxolitinib start, multivariable analysis identified three independent risk factors for event-free survival (EFS, defined as freedom from myelofibrosis progression, thrombosis, hemorrhage, or death): ruxolitinib dose <10 mg twice daily at one or more assessment timepoints (HR 1.94; p=0.047), absence of ≥50% spleen length reduction at months 3 and 6 (HR 2.65; p=0.009), and phlebotomy requirement at ≥2 timepoints (HR 2.11; p=0.039). The resulting PV-RR6 model stratifies patients into three risk categories with 5-year EFS of 89.4% (low), 71.0% (intermediate), and 38.9% (high), providing a practical and dynamic tool for identifying early ruxolitinib failure that should prompt consideration of next-line therapy such as ropeginterferon or novel agents.

### Non-Invasive Multiparametric Characterization of ET, Pre-MF, and Overt MF

A multicenter study in *Leukemia* reports a non-invasive multiparametric approach integrating peripheral blood parameters, ultrasound-based spleen assessment, and circulating biomarkers to distinguish essential thrombocythemia, pre-fibrotic myelofibrosis, and overt myelofibrosis without bone marrow biopsy[^18]. While primarily a proof-of-concept study, the findings are clinically meaningful in patients who cannot safely undergo bone marrow examination and in monitoring disease evolution longitudinally, particularly in the context of clinical trials assessing disease modification.

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

### Epcoritamab in Relapsed/Refractory Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma: Real-World Outcomes Inferior to Pivotal Trials

The first large multicenter real-world analysis of epcoritamab in aggressive B-cell lymphoma is published this week in *Blood Advances*[^5]. Among 157 adult patients with relapsed/refractory aggressive B-cell lymphomas treated at nine centers of the Collaborative US Bispecific Consortium between 2023 and 2025, the best ORR was 49.6% and best CRR was 31.9%. With a median follow-up of 15.8 months, 1-year PFS was 29% (95% CI, 22–37%) and 1-year OS was 38% (95% CI, 31–47%). Compared with the pivotal EPCORE NHL-1 trial, response rates and survival outcomes were meaningfully inferior in this unselected population. On multivariable analysis, ECOG performance status ≥2, elevated LDH, disease refractory to the previous line of therapy, and clinical trial ineligibility were all independently associated with inferior PFS and OS. Toxicity was also greater in the real-world setting: any-grade CRS occurred in 33% (grade ≥3, 4.6%) and ICANS in 12% (grade ≥3, 0.6%), with ICU admission required in 17% of all patients — substantially higher than pivotal trial-reported rates. These data reinforce the need for careful patient selection, standardized step-up dosing adherence, and experienced toxicity management infrastructure before deploying epcoritamab in community or lower-volume settings.

### ctDNA MRD After Immunochemotherapy and CAR T-Cell Therapy in LBCL: Real-World Validation

A multicenter retrospective study published in *Haematologica* validates post-treatment ctDNA MRD assessment using a commercially available immunoglobulin rearrangement-targeted assay (clonoSEQ) in large B-cell lymphoma[^19]. Among 102 patients assessed after frontline treatment, detectable versus undetectable MRD at end of treatment yielded 12-month PFS rates of 15% versus 85% (HR 14.5; p<0.001), establishing a highly discriminatory prognostic cut-point. The median time from sample collection to MRD result was only 9 days, confirming real-world operational feasibility. These data support ctDNA-MRD integration into standard response assessment algorithms at high-volume lymphoma centers, including those at NCKUH and partner institutions in Taiwan.

### Axi-cel vs. Tisa-cel in Elderly Patients with LBCL: EBMT Registry Data

An EBMT registry analysis published in *Haematologica* compares outcomes of axicabtagene ciloleucel (axi-cel) versus tisagenlecleucel (tisa-cel) in 1,191 patients aged ≥70 years with large B-cell lymphoma[^20]. Axi-cel was associated with significantly improved progression-free survival compared with tisa-cel in multivariable analysis adjusted for age, sex, ECOG, disease status at infusion, prior transplantation, and year of infusion. CRS (any grade) occurred in 77% versus 62% for axi-cel versus tisa-cel (p<0.05 at day 30), reflecting the more inflammatory profile of axi-cel. Importantly, median patient age was 74 years and 65.6% were aged 70–75 years, confirming that CD19 CAR-T therapy is deliverable and active in the elderly, though axi-cel's superior efficacy comes with a higher acute toxicity burden requiring careful patient selection.

### Fludarabine Exposure and CAR T-Cell Outcomes in R/R LBCL: PK-Guided Dosing

A prospective single-center pharmacokinetic study published in *Blood Advances* demonstrates that optimal fludarabine exposure during lymphodepletion conditioning is independently associated with improved PFS after CAR T-cell therapy in LBCL[^21]. Among 54 patients with a median follow-up of 24.5 months, the median fludarabine AUC was 18.12 mg·h/L (IQR 14.56–20.55), with optimal exposure windows identified as 17.5–21.5 mg·h/L for axi-cel and 14.0–18.0 mg·h/L for tisa-cel. Patients within the optimal window (44%) had a median PFS that was not reached, versus 13.3 months for those outside it (p=0.03). On multivariable analysis, optimal fludarabine exposure remained independently associated with improved PFS (p=0.03) alongside axi-cel use (p=0.03). These findings provide a compelling rationale for pharmacokinetically-guided fludarabine dosing in clinical CAR T-cell programs.

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

### Sonrotoclax Monotherapy in BTK Inhibitor–Pretreated R/R Mantle Cell Lymphoma

The phase I/II BGB-11417-201 trial published in the *Journal of Clinical Oncology* represents the first substantial efficacy dataset for sonrotoclax (BGB-11417), a next-generation BCL-2 inhibitor, in relapsed/refractory MCL[^2]. Among 103 efficacy-evaluable patients (median 3 prior therapies; 78.3% stage IV), sonrotoclax 320 mg once daily achieved an ORR-IRC of 52.4% (95% CI, 42.4–62.4%), significantly exceeding the pre-specified historical control ORR of 30% (p<0.0001). The complete response rate was 15.5%. Critically, responses were seen in high-risk subgroups, including patients with *TP53* mutations (ORR 59.1%). With a median study follow-up of 14.2 months, the median duration of response was 15.8 months and median PFS was 6.5 months; median OS was not reached. The most common grade ≥3 adverse events were neutropenia (19.1%), thrombocytopenia (9.6%), and anemia (7.8%). Tumor lysis syndrome occurred in 7.0% of patients, all resolving without sequelae with the 4-week ramp-up protocol. Compared with venetoclax in MCL (historical CR rates of approximately 5–10% in similar populations), sonrotoclax demonstrates meaningfully deeper responses, and its shorter half-life without drug accumulation may confer practical advantages in TLS risk management. Combination studies with BTK inhibitors are anticipated.

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

### HCT for Relapsed/Refractory Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma: EBMT Registry Analysis of 19,498 Patients

The largest registry-based study of transplantation for classical Hodgkin lymphoma, covering 53 countries and over 600 EBMT centers, is published in *The Lancet Haematology*[^4]. Among 15,648 auto-HCT recipients (median age 35 years; median follow-up 2.4 years), 2-year PFS improved from 63% (95% CI, 62–65%) in 2010–14 to 73% (71–74%) in 2019–22, and 2-year OS improved from 85% (84–86%) to 93% (91–94%). For the 3,850 allo-HCT patients (median age 32 years; median follow-up 3.9 years), 2-year PFS improved from 44% (41–46%) to 62% (58–66%) and OS from 66% (63–68%) to 72% (68–75%) over the same period. Disease status at transplantation was the dominant prognostic factor in both settings: partial response at auto-HCT was associated with an HR of 1.92 (95% CI, 1.74–2.11) and stable or progressive disease with an HR of 2.45 (2.17–2.76) for PFS. Comparable hazard ratios were seen in the allo-HCT cohort (PR: HR 1.58; PD: HR 2.28). These data, while registry-based and subject to selection bias, provide robust contemporary benchmarks confirming that modern transplant approaches yield meaningfully improved outcomes compared with the pre-brentuximab era and support the continued role of chemosensitivity as the principal transplant eligibility criterion.

### Brentuximab Vedotin Post-Allo-SCT for Relapsed/Refractory Hodgkin Lymphoma: GHSG BV-ALLO

A phase 2 trial published in *Blood Advances* evaluates brentuximab vedotin as consolidation treatment after allogeneic stem cell transplantation for relapsed/refractory Hodgkin lymphoma[^22]. The GHSG BV-ALLO study provides prospective data on the feasibility and efficacy of sequential BV post-allo-SCT, including GVHD incidence and immune reconstitution data in this high-risk setting. Full efficacy data were not extractable from the available abstract but the trial design and safety profile data are informative for centers pursuing allo-SCT-based strategies.

### Brentuximab Vedotin Consolidation in PET-Positive Stage I/II Hodgkin Lymphoma after 2 ABVD: BRAPP2 Final Analysis

The final analysis of the BRAPP2 trial published in *Haematologica* evaluates brentuximab vedotin as consolidation treatment in patients with stage I/II Hodgkin lymphoma who remain PET-positive after 2 cycles of ABVD[^23]. This study addresses an important unmet need in early-stage Hodgkin lymphoma management, where Deauville score 4–5 after 2 cycles of ABVD defines a high-risk group in need of escalation. Full outcome data were not available from the published abstract, but this represents an important addition to the evidence base guiding PET-adapted treatment strategies in early Hodgkin lymphoma.

### Hepatosplenic T-Cell Lymphoma and HCT: EBMT Registry Analysis

A *Lancet Haematology* analysis of 94 allo-HSCT and 27 auto-HSCT recipients with hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma (HSTCL) provides the most granular outcomes data yet reported for this rare and aggressive subtype[^24]. Among allo-HSCT recipients (median follow-up 4.5 years; IQR 3.4–5.6), 3-year PFS was 50.5% (95% CI, 39.4–60.5%) and 3-year OS was 55.0% (43.9–64.8%), with a 3-year non-relapse mortality of 11.7% and relapse incidence of 37.9%. Auto-HSCT was associated with a higher 3-year relapse incidence of 50.0% (95% CI, 24.9–70.8%) and 3-year PFS of only 38.9% (17.5–60.0%), though 3-year OS reached 63.5% (41.3–79.3%) in this small cohort. These data clearly establish allo-HSCT as the preferred consolidation strategy for fit patients achieving response, while auto-HSCT may be reserved for complete responders ineligible for allogeneic approaches. HSTCL has disproportionate relevance across Asian centers given a higher prevalence in East Asian populations.

### JAK Inhibitors in Lymphomas and Lymphoma-Associated Immune Complications: Review

A comprehensive review published in *Haematologica* synthesizes current evidence for JAK/STAT inhibitors across B-cell and T-cell malignancies and lymphoma-associated immune complications including GVHD[^25]. The review emphasizes that constitutive JAK/STAT activation is particularly prevalent in certain T-cell lymphoma subtypes (AITL, ALK-negative ALCL, ENKTL) and primary mediastinal B-cell lymphoma, providing the biological rationale for JAK inhibitor trials in these settings. The authors underscore that variable clinical efficacy across lymphoid subtypes necessitates biomarker-driven patient selection rather than broad application, and propose rational combination strategies pairing JAK inhibition with checkpoint blockade or chemotherapy depending on subtype.

## X. NK/T-cell Lymphoma (Asia Focus)

### MALT1 as a Therapeutic Target in EBV-Positive B-cell Lymphomas: LMP1/LMP2 Synergy

A mechanistic study published in *Leukemia* establishes MALT1 protease activity as a critical mediator of EBV-driven NF-κB activation and lymphomagenesis[^26]. The study demonstrates that EBV latent membrane proteins LMP1 and LMP2A/2B synergize to constitutively activate MALT1 through the formation of LMP-BCL10-MALT1 (LBM) complexes in EBV-positive B-cell lines. LMP1 interacted physically with the MALT1-binding partner BCL10, while LMP2A and LMP2B interacted directly with MALT1. MALT1 protease inhibition reduced NF-κB target gene expression and suppressed tumor growth in EBV-positive lymphoma xenograft models. Given that EBV-positive lymphoproliferative disorders — including extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, EBV-positive DLBCL of the elderly, and post-transplant lymphoproliferative disease — are considerably more prevalent across East and Southeast Asia, MALT1 inhibitors represent a particularly high-interest therapeutic class for Taiwanese and regional hematologists. Clinical-grade MALT1 inhibitors are entering early-phase trials and should be monitored closely.

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

### MonumenTAL-3: Talquetamab Combinations vs. DPd in Phase 3 — A Practice-Changing Signal

The primary analysis of the MonumenTAL-3 phase 3 trial, published this week in the *New England Journal of Medicine*, is the most significant myeloma signal of 2026 to date[^1]. A total of 864 patients with relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma who had received at least one prior line of therapy were randomly assigned in a 1:1:1 ratio to talquetamab plus daratumumab and pomalidomide (Tal-DP; n=287), talquetamab plus daratumumab (Tal-D; n=287), or the active comparator daratumumab plus pomalidomide-dexamethasone (DPd; n=290). At interim analysis (median follow-up 24.6 months), the 24-month PFS estimates were 81.3%, 77.6%, and 51.2% for Tal-DP, Tal-D, and DPd respectively. The hazard ratios for disease progression or death were 0.28 (95% CI, 0.20–0.40; p<0.001) for Tal-DP versus DPd and 0.33 (95% CI, 0.24–0.46; p<0.001) for Tal-D versus DPd — among the most compelling PFS separations seen in any randomized myeloma trial. Overall response rates were 88.2% (Tal-DP), 88.5% (Tal-D), and 77.6% (DPd); complete response or better was achieved in 71.1%, 69.0%, and 34.5%; and MRD-negative CR in 52.3%, 46.3%, and 15.9% (p<0.001 for all comparisons). The 24-month OS rates were 89.2%, 87.9%, and 79.1%, with HRs for death of 0.47 (95% CI, 0.30–0.73) and 0.51 (95% CI, 0.33–0.78) for Tal-DP and Tal-D versus DPd. Serious adverse events occurred in 63.0%, 52.6%, and 53.7% respectively; fatal adverse events were lower in the Tal-DP group (1.8%) than DPd (4.6%). Notably, skin-related toxicities (dysgeusia, nail changes, dry skin) characteristic of GPRC5D targeting were frequent but manageable with dose modification. This trial definitively establishes talquetamab-based doublet or triplet therapy as superior to DPd from the second line onward.

This finding is distinct from and should not be confused with the MonumenTAL-1 long-term data reported in W32, which covered monotherapy in heavily pretreated patients.
