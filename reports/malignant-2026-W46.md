## Summary

This week's literature is dominated by *Blood*'s primary results from TRANSCEND CLL 004 (liso-cel plus ibrutinib in relapsed/refractory CLL/SLL), a mechanistic CD70 relapse-prediction study in follicular lymphoma with a dual CD19-CD70 CAR-T construct, a phase 2a sapablursen (TMPRSS6 antisense oligonucleotide) trial in polycythemia vera, and a cluster of *Blood Advances* papers on teclistamab-daratumumab combination therapy (TRIMM-2) and MPN-associated splanchnic vein thrombosis management. **(1)** In the phase 1/2 TRANSCEND CLL 004 cohort combining lisocabtagene maraleucel with ibrutinib in 56 heavily pretreated relapsed/refractory CLL/SLL patients (98% high-risk cytogenetics, median 5 prior lines), the dose level 2 cohort achieved a CR/CRi rate of 45% (95% CI 31–60) and overall response rate of 86% (95% CI 74–94), with median PFS of 31.4 months (95% CI 20.1–not reached)[^1]. **(2)** A translational study in follicular lymphoma identified CD70 overexpression at diagnosis on tumor cells and CD8+/CD4+ T follicular helper cells as a predictor of inferior progression-free survival, and engineered dual CD19-CD70 CAR T cells achieved complete tumor clearance in spleen and bone marrow in FL xenograft models, outperforming monotargeted CD19 CAR-T[^2]. **(3)** The phase 2a IMPRSSION trial of sapablursen, a TMPRSS6-targeting antisense oligonucleotide, in phlebotomy-dependent polycythemia vera showed a reduction in weekly phlebotomy rate of −0.10 (95% CI −0.13 to −0.07, p<0.0001) in the 120/80 mg cohort, reducing the median number of phlebotomies from 5.0 to 0 over the 20-week efficacy window[^3]. **(4)** The phase 1b TRIMM-2 study of teclistamab plus daratumumab in triple-class-exposed relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma (median 5 prior lines) reported an overall response rate of 68.9% (≥CR 44.3%) with median PFS of 26.3 months at weight-based recommended phase 2 dosing, though a fixed-dose cohort was terminated early after a fatal infection safety signal[^4]. **(5)** A GASTRO-MPN consortium retrospective analysis of 757 patients with MPN-associated splanchnic vein thrombosis found cytoreductive therapy, modeled as a time-dependent covariate, associated with reduced risk of recurrent thrombosis (HR 0.67, 95% CI 0.50–0.89, p=0.01), driven primarily by reduced splanchnic-bed extension/recurrence (HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.42–0.90, p=0.01)[^5].

## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

Beyond last week's mature AML18 quizartinib report, this week adds mechanistic and biomarker data without new randomized outcomes. A high-throughput viability screen across 56 genetically diverse primary AML samples identified AML874, a selective CDK8/CDK19 (Mediator complex) inhibitor, as disrupting Mediator-dependent transcription with particular sensitivity in FLT3-mutated AML, and CDK8 inhibition synergized with venetoclax in cell lines and patient-derived xenograft models, nominating Mediator kinase blockade as a combination strategy with established BCL2-directed therapy[^6]. A companion mechanistic study detailed trametinib plus quisinostat (MEK/HDAC dual inhibition) as reprogramming CD4/CD8 T-cell transcriptomes via STAT1 activation, downregulating immune checkpoint ligands while upregulating MHC-I/II on NRAS/ASXL1-driven AML cells, directly enhancing MHC-dependent T-cell killing in leukemia:T-cell cocultures — extending the mechanistic basis reported last week for this combination's survival benefit[^7]. A clinically focused review discussed pivekimab sunirine, the anti-CD123 antibody-drug conjugate approved by the FDA on May 27, 2026 for blastic plasmacytoid dendritic cell neoplasm (BPDCN) based on the CADENZA study, with implications for overlapping AML biology given shared CD123 expression[^8]. Separately, a Korean-Beat AML genomic comparison cohort and a study characterizing FLT3-ITD microclones in elderly patients receiving less-intensive therapy were published but without reportable comparative outcome statistics in the available abstracts.

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

An international expert panel published a cross-age consensus guideline on measurable residual disease (MRD) assessment in ALL, achieving consensus on 38 of 41 Delphi statements spanning pre-analytical standards, nomenclature, and MRD-based remission/relapse definitions; bone marrow was endorsed as the reference specimen and MRD was prioritized over cytomorphology in cases of discordance, while anticoagulant choice for PCR-based MRD and the exclusive use of MRD to define remission remained areas without consensus[^9]. A real-world implementation study of blinatumomab across six pediatric oncology centers in India, Pakistan, and Vietnam (106 patients, relapsed/refractory or MRD-positive B-ALL) reported 6-month event-free survival and overall survival of 61.5% ± 5.8% and 89.9% ± 3.6%, respectively, and 12-month EFS/OS of 58.1% ± 7.1% and 82.8% ± 5.5%; MRD-negative status after cycle 1 was associated with superior 12-month EFS (p<0.0001) and OS (p=0.0366), supporting feasibility of immunotherapy access expansion in lower-middle-income countries[^10]. A separate Haematologica spotlight review cautioned that blinatumomab-induced CD19+ B-cell depletion combined with dexamethasone premedication may compound immune suppression and infection risk in B-ALL patients post-consolidation, calling for dedicated studies on optimal corticosteroid dosing rather than empiric neuroprotection regimens[^11].

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

Following last week's structural resistance mapping for asciminib, this week's *Leukemia* issue introduces relcobatinib (ELVN-001), a next-generation ATP-competitive ABL1 inhibitor with a distinctive P-loop folded-in binding mode designed for improved selectivity over existing active-site TKIs. Preclinically, relcobatinib showed robust activity against native BCR::ABL1 and a broad panel of clinically relevant resistance mutants, and in a BCR::ABL1 xenograft model combination with asciminib produced durable tumor regression beyond either agent alone, supporting a rational active-site/allosteric inhibitor combination strategy for sequencing in resistant CML[^12]. A companion genomic/transcriptomic study examined features associated with response to third-generation TKI therapy in CML, though granular outcome statistics were not available in the abstract. A Haematologica analysis of 101 long-term (median 13.8 years) survivors after allogeneic HCT found clonal hematopoiesis in 28.7% of patients, with transplantation for CML as an independent risk factor (OR 7.13, 95% CI 1.38–36.8) alongside donor age at sampling (OR 1.06, 95% CI 1.00–1.12) and moderate/severe chronic GVHD (OR 5.49, 95% CI 1.05–28.7), though CH status was not associated with three-year overall survival (94.2% vs. 92.9%, p=0.800) or second cancer incidence[^13].

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

The headline result this week is the primary analysis of TRANSCEND CLL 004's liso-cel plus ibrutinib cohort, detailed above[^1], establishing durable CAR-T efficacy (CR/CRi 45%, median PFS 31.4 months) in a BTK-inhibitor combination strategy for heavily pretreated R/R CLL/SLL, with manageable toxicity (grade ≥3 CRS in 4%, no grade 4/5; neurological events in 41%, grade 3/4 in 11%). A companion *Blood* editorial frames BTK inhibitor priming as mechanistically enhancing CAR T-cell fitness in CLL, consistent with the combination's efficacy signal[^14]. Complementing this clinical result, a mechanistic study of terminally differentiated CD8+ T cells in treatment-naive CLL patients found a CD27⁻CD28⁻CD57⁺KLRG1⁺ population exhibiting senescence-associated features without canonical irreversible senescence or exhaustion; these cells mounted transient but robust proliferative bursts and were poorly compatible with CAR T-cell manufacturing, yet retained potent acute cytotoxicity exploitable by bispecific T-cell engagers — offering a biological rationale for why BiTEs may outperform CAR-T in certain CLL T-cell states[^15]. A separate study demonstrated that nurse-like cells in the CLL lymphoid niche transfer functional folate receptor beta (FOLR2) to leukemic cells via trogocytosis, with FOLR2+ CLL cells representing the predominant actively cycling leukemic population and enrichment of FOLR2+ nurse-like cells correlating with more aggressive, proliferative disease in lymph node tissue[^16]. A pharmacoepidemiologic time-varying analysis reported that GLP-1 receptor agonist exposure during watch-and-wait was associated with lower secondary malignancy risk in CLL patients, though effect size was not specified in the available abstract[^17].

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

A prospective comparison of 409 CCUS patients and 241 lower-risk MDS (LR-MDS) patients applying harmonized diagnostic criteria and two independent risk models (clonal hematopoiesis risk score [CHRS] and clonal cytopenia risk score [CCRS]) found that high-risk CCUS patients exhibited clinical features and event rates comparable to LR-MDS, whereas low-/intermediate-risk CCUS patients had markedly improved outcomes supporting conservative management — suggesting high-risk CCUS patients should be considered for enrollment in LR-MDS-directed trials[^18]. Mechanistically, a study using integrated ATAC-seq and RNA-seq profiling showed that MDS/secondary-AML-derived mesenchymal stromal cells induce chromatin remodeling in NK cells, driving mitochondrial fragmentation and apoptosis that suppresses NK-cell cytotoxic function, proposing mitochondrial-resilience-directed therapies to restore immune surveillance in this bone marrow microenvironment[^19]. A separate *Blood Advances* paper identified the NLRC4 inflammasome as a driver of MDS pathogenesis in Tet2-deficient mouse models, where Nlrc4 depletion impaired MDS development and improved survival, nominating inflammasome blockade as a therapeutic avenue in TET2-mutant disease[^20].

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

Sapablursen's phase 2a IMPRSSION data in polycythemia vera, detailed above[^3], represents this week's major MPN signal, with the companion editorial framing TMPRSS6/hepcidin-axis targeting as a "new iron age" in PV management complementary to JAK2-directed cytoreduction[^21]. In contrast, a separate phase 2a trial of sapablursen in non-transfusion-dependent β-thalassemia intermedia failed to meet its primary or secondary endpoints of increased hemoglobin or reduced liver iron content, despite similar dosing (30–160 mg), illustrating that disease-specific iron/erythroferrone biology (elevated baseline liver iron content and serum erythroferrone in thalassemia) can blunt the TMPRSS6-hepcidin mechanism that proved effective in PV[^22]. The GASTRO-MPN consortium's splanchnic vein thrombosis analysis (detailed above)[^5] provides the most robust evidence to date for cytoreductive therapy's role in preventing SVT recurrence, addressing a long-standing evidence gap from underpowered prior studies. Mechanistically, a study of CALR-mutant MPN models found NK cells retain preserved — even enhanced — degranulation capacity but express high levels of the checkpoint molecule TIGIT, with CD34+ MPN cells overexpressing the TIGIT ligand CD112; TIGIT blockade reduced platelet counts in a transplant mouse model, providing rationale for TIGIT-directed immunotherapy specifically in CALR-mutant disease[^23].

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

A phase II single-arm study of orelabrutinib (BTK inhibitor) plus R-CHOP in 49 newly diagnosed non-GCB double-expressor DLBCL patients reported 2-year EFS, PFS, and OS rates of 82.0% (95% CI 71.2–94.3%), 82.0% (95% CI 71.2–94.3%), and 97.9% (95% CI 94.0–100.0%) respectively, with end-of-treatment complete response rate of 89.8% and manageable grade ≥3 toxicity (51.0%, predominantly neutropenia 38.8%) — a notable efficacy signal in a historically poor-prognosis DLBCL subgroup treated with standard R-CHOP alone[^24]. A phase 1 dose-escalation study (BP43131) of the novel CD19-CD28 costimulatory bispecific antibody RO7443904 combined with glofitamab in 52 patients with relapsed/refractory B-NHL reported ORR/CRR of 63.6%/42.4% in aggressive lymphoma and 89.5% for both endpoints in indolent lymphoma, with maximum tolerated dose not reached and predominantly low-grade CRS (53.8% any grade); this represents a novel mechanistic strategy of adding CD28 costimulation to enhance T-cell engager efficacy[^25]. A *Blood* perspective discussed the clinical readiness of circulating tumor DNA for MRD detection in large B-cell lymphoma, noting its emerging but still-unvalidated role as an adjunct to PET-based response assessment for molecular-only residual disease[^26]. A Czech national registry study examined survival improvement among DLBCL patients receiving third-line-and-beyond therapy in the recent treatment era, though comparative hazard ratios were not available in the abstract.

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

The CD70-FL relapse biomarker study and its dual CD19-CD70 CAR-T preclinical rationale, detailed in the Summary[^2], is this week's principal signal in indolent lymphoma, accompanied by an editorial emphasizing the paradox of CD70 as both an immune-activation marker and an immune-escape mechanism exploited by relapsing FL clones[^27]. Separately, immune-monitoring data from a phase 2 trial of fixed-duration (6-month) subcutaneous mosunetuzumab in previously untreated follicular or marginal zone lymphoma demonstrated that increased peripheral CD8+ T-cell clonality at midtreatment (3 months) was associated with early clinical complete response, and higher NK-cell counts at midtreatment were similarly associated with complete response, with mosunetuzumab inducing CD56dim/CD16bright NK-cell skewing — providing early immune correlates that may guide future fixed-duration dosing strategies[^28].

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

A transcriptomic analysis of 178 nodular lymphocyte-predominant Hodgkin lymphoma (NLPHL) and 22 T-cell/histiocyte-rich large B-cell lymphoma (THRLBCL) cases found that variant immuno-architectural pattern NLPHL cases exhibited THRLBCL-like transcriptional profiles, particularly with splenic involvement and high Lymphocyte-Predominant International Prognostic Score; tumor microenvironment deconvolution revealed a gradient from B-cell/T-helper-enriched classic NLPHL through intermediate variant NLPHL to macrophage/cytotoxic-T-cell-enriched, immune-checkpoint-high THRLBCL, with higher follicular helper T-cell content associated with shorter event-free survival, supporting a continuous biological spectrum rather than discrete entities[^29]. Separately, a prime-editing platform study demonstrated correction of the HAVCR2 driver mutation in T cells from patients with subcutaneous panniculitis-like T-cell lymphoma (SPTCL), with multiplexed gene correction and drug-resistance editing enabling selective in vivo expansion of corrected cells under immunosuppressive pressure in humanized mouse models[^30].

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

The TRIMM-2 teclistamab-daratumumab combination data, detailed in the Summary[^4], anchors this week's myeloma literature, with the weight-based recommended phase 2 dose cohort (teclistamab 1.5 mg/kg weekly or 3.0 mg/kg every other week plus daratumumab) achieving ORR 68.9% and ≥CR 44.3% at median follow-up of 12.0 months in patients with median 5 prior lines of therapy; notably, a fixed-dose teclistamab cohort (100–300 mg) was terminated early following a fatal infection safety signal, underscoring the importance of weight-based dosing and early immunoglobulin replacement in bispecific-based combination regimens. A Haematologica report examined teclistamab use specifically in multiple myeloma patients with coexisting MDS or AML, addressing an important but previously underexplored overlap population, though comparative outcome data were not detailed in the abstract[^31]. A review of amyloidogenic versus "innocent" light chain discrimination in systemic amyloidoses highlighted emerging mass spectrometry, flow cytometry, and next-generation sequencing-based MRD detection tools, alongside LC-intrinsic biomarkers (N-glycosylation, germline gene usage, reduced stability) that may enable pre-symptomatic intervention in patients with apparently benign monoclonal gammopathies at risk for AL amyloidosis[^32]. An editorial reinforced that myeloma MRD assessment should be conceptualized as a longitudinal "journey" rather than a single timepoint snapshot, consistent with evolving guidance on serial MRD monitoring[^33]. A Haematologica study identified shared high-risk transcriptional programs spanning multiple myeloma disease stages that associate with progression and poor outcome, though specific hazard ratios were not provided in the abstract[^34].

## XII. Transplant & Cellular Therapy (CAR-T / HSCT / GVHD)

A European Society for Blood and Marrow Transplantation registry analysis of 4,695 patients undergoing 9/10 mismatched unrelated donor allo-HCT examined HLA evolutionary divergence (HED), a metric of peptide-binding-site amino acid variability, finding that in the DRB1-mismatched subgroup, HED-mismatch at that locus independently predicted inferior relapse-free survival, while in HLA-B-mismatched pairs higher DRB1 recipient HED was associated with worse overall survival and relapse-free survival and higher relapse risk — demonstrating that HED-derived metrics capture clinically meaningful heterogeneity beyond conventional dichotomous mismatch classification[^35]. A two-center retrospective study of CD34+-selected stem cell boost for poor graft function after allo-HSCT (116 patients, 86.8% haploidentical) reported an overall response rate of 67.24% (CR 62.93%), with active infection at time of boost the sole independent predictor of lower response (OR 3.786, 95% CI 1.584–9.048, p=0.003), and responders showing markedly superior overall survival versus nonresponders (81.65% vs. 11.67%, p<0.001)[^36]. In a first proof-of-concept for CAR-T in Waldenström macroglobulinemia, a CD20-directed autologous CAR-T construct in BTK-inhibitor-refractory, heavily pretreated patients achieved a CR/VGPR rate of 80%, supporting evaluation in larger trials for a disease with no currently approved cellular therapy[^37]. A multiplex prime-editing platform was also shown capable of generating drug-controllable, pharmacologically resistant CAR T-cell products retaining effector function despite ongoing immunosuppression, with direct relevance to transplant recipients requiring CAR-T therapy under continued immunosuppressive regimens[^30].

## XIV. Taiwan Clinical Context

For Taiwan practice, the TRANSCEND CLL 004 liso-cel/ibrutinib data[^1] is of limited immediate relevance given the low population incidence of CLL in Taiwan relative to Western cohorts, though BTK inhibitors (ibrutinib, acalabrutinib, zanubrutinib) are NHI-reimbursed for relapsed CLL/SLL and CAR-T access remains restricted to designated centers (NTUH, LinKou CGMH, TVGH) under compassionate/named-patient pathways. The TRIMM-2 teclistamab-daratumumab combination data[^4] is relevant as teclistamab monotherapy has recently entered Taiwan's expanded-access framework for triple-class-exposed myeloma, though combination regimens with daratumumab are not yet NHI-reimbursed and would require clinical trial enrollment at CGMH or NTUH myeloma programs; the fatal-infection signal in the fixed-dose cohort reinforces the importance of infection prophylaxis protocols already standard at Taiwan transplant/cellular-therapy units. The orelabrutinib plus R-CHOP data in double-expressor DLBCL[^24] is notable since double-expressor status is routinely assessed by immunohistochemistry in Taiwan pathology practice, and orelabrutinib (approved in China) may become accessible via regional trials given CMUH and CGMH's existing BTK-inhibitor trial infrastructure. Sapablursen's polycythemia vera data[^3] offers a potential future NHI-reimbursement candidate for phlebotomy-dependent PV patients who remain symptomatic despite hydroxyurea, a population currently underserved in Taiwan given limited ruxolitinib second-line reimbursement criteria.

## XV. Key Takeaways

This week consolidates a shift toward combination immunotherapy strategies that pair established small-molecule or antibody backbones with next-generation cellular or bispecific platforms — BTK inhibitor plus CAR-T in CLL, CD28-costimulatory bispecific plus glofitamab in B-NHL, and dual-antigen CAR-T design in follicular lymphoma — each demonstrating that rational biological combination can meaningfully extend efficacy beyond single-agent ceilings. In parallel, the sapablursen data illustrate both the promise and the disease-specificity limits of TMPRSS6/hepcidin-axis manipulation, effective in polycythemia vera but ineffective in β-thalassemia intermedia due to divergent baseline iron physiology. The CCUS/LR-MDS convergence study and HED-based transplant risk stratification both point toward increasingly granular, biomarker-driven reclassification of conventionally morphology- or mismatch-based diagnostic and risk frameworks across MDS and transplant medicine.
