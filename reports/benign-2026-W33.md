## Summary

The five most clinically actionable signals this week are as follows. First, a *Blood Advances* guideline from the McMaster ITP Emergency Management Guideline Group provides the first formal evidence-informed recommendations for the emergency management of critical bleeds in adults and children with ITP, issuing strong GRADE recommendations for combined use of high-dose corticosteroids, high-dose intravenous immunoglobulin, platelet transfusions, tranexamic acid, and thrombopoietin receptor agonists simultaneously, while advising against recombinant factor VIIa due to thrombosis risk[^1]. Second, a prospective cohort study published in *Blood* challenges the prevailing paradigm that provoked venous thromboembolism (VTE) carries uniformly low recurrence risk, demonstrating a 24-month recurrence rate of 19.7% (95% CI, 11.0–30.2) after major transient risk factors — a finding that led to early study termination and that directly supports individualized decisions on anticoagulation duration[^2]. Third, a *Blood* "How I Treat" article by Lavin, Abdul Kadir, and Sidonio provides the first comprehensive practical framework for managing bleeding in women and girls with haemophilia (WGH) and symptomatic haemophilia carriers across the lifespan, operationalising the 2021 ISTH reclassification and addressing the persistently underrecognised bleeding burden in this population[^3]. Fourth, a *Blood* mechanistic study identifies histidine-rich glycoprotein (HRG) — downregulated by repeated heparin exposure via the FGFR-ERK-Elk1 hepatic axis — as a pivotal endogenous inhibitor of platelet activation and immune complex formation in heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT), offering a mechanistically novel target for therapeutic intervention[^4]. Fifth, a comprehensive 2026 update in *AJH* on clinical trials in β-thalassemia documents the current approved landscape (luspatercept, mitapivat, betibeglogene autotemcel, exagamglogene autotemcel) alongside emerging platforms, while highlighting the increasing importance of genetic disease modifiers in treatment planning and the critical unresolved challenges of access, cost, and pediatric expansion[^5].

## I. Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP)

**Emergency Management of Critical ITP Bleeds — First Formal Guideline.** The absence of a standardised approach to critical bleeds in ITP — defined as intracranial haemorrhage or bleeding causing haemodynamic instability — has long been a recognised gap in haematological emergency management. This week, *Blood Advances* published a GRADE-based guideline developed by the McMaster ITP Emergency Management Guideline Group, comprising five adult ITP experts, three paediatric ITP experts, two emergency physicians, one emergency nurse, two methodologists, and four patient partners[^1]. The recommendations were informed by a dedicated multicenter retrospective cohort study and systematic reviews. Despite low or very low certainty in the evidence base — reflecting the rarity and acuity of the condition — the panel issued strong recommendations for simultaneous use of five interventions: high-dose corticosteroids, high-dose intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG), platelet transfusions, tranexamic acid, and thrombopoietin receptor agonists (TPO-RAs). The rationale for strong recommendations despite low-certainty evidence was the life-threatening nature of the condition and the favourable risk-to-benefit profile of each agent. The panel issued a conditional recommendation supporting urgent splenectomy when all other therapies have failed. Importantly, a conditional recommendation against recombinant factor VIIa was issued, based on its thrombotic risk profile. The inclusion of TPO-RAs in the acute emergency setting is clinically important — while their platelet count-raising effect is typically delayed by several days, early initiation during the critical bleed event is supported on the basis that TPO-RA exposure prepares the marrow for subsequent platelet production and may have additional non-thrombopoietic haemostatic effects. This guideline provides NCKUH practitioners with the first systematic, evidence-referenced algorithm for ITP emergencies applicable to both adults and children.

**Platelet-Reactive Antibody Burden and Platelet Functional Exhaustion in Chronic ITP.** A *BJH* study by Hosseini et al. reports a novel approach to quantifying anti-platelet antibody burden in chronic ITP using a modified platelet immunofluorescence test (PIFT) and correlating results with platelet function and clinical outcomes[^6]. Thirty-one patients with chronic ITP and 20 healthy controls were enrolled. Platelet function was characterised by P-selectin expression, PAC-1 binding (reflecting GPIIb/IIIa activation), and reactive oxygen species generation. Elevated platelet-reactive IgG burden correlated with reduced platelet responsiveness to agonist stimulation and with bleeding severity (p < 0.01), but — importantly — not with platelet count alone. Non-responders to ITP therapy exhibited significantly higher antibody burden than responders, and receiver operating characteristic analysis identified a PIFT cut-off of ≥3.85 with an AUC of 0.877 (sensitivity 80%, specificity 87%) for discrimination between responder groups. These findings reinforce the clinical concept that platelet count is an imperfect surrogate for haemostatic competence in ITP, and suggest that antibody burden quantification may contribute to treatment response prediction. For NCKUH clinicians managing patients with persistent bleeding despite apparently modest thrombocytopenia, this assay framework is hypothesis-generating with respect to identifying the functional component of bleeding risk independent of count.

**Anti-Thyroperoxidase Antibodies in Adult ITP.** Duhamel et al. published a single-centre cohort study in *BJH* investigating the clinical relevance of anti-thyroperoxidase (anti-TPO) antibodies in adult ITP[^7]. Full numerical data were not available in the digest for this week's release, but the study addresses the recognised clinical association between thyroid autoimmunity and ITP — a finding with direct implications for workup, as detection of anti-TPO antibodies may identify a subset of ITP patients with a broader autoimmune diathesis warranting thyroid monitoring and potentially informing treatment sequencing. This paper is flagged for full review upon journal access.

## II. TTP / HUS / Thrombotic Microangiopathy

**Monoclonal Gammopathy of Thrombotic Significance — Proposed Framework.** Ho, Zanwar, Kumar, and Rajkumar published in *AJH* a conceptual framework proposing "monoclonal gammopathy of thrombotic significance" (MGTS) as a discrete subcategory within the spectrum of monoclonal gammopathy of clinical significance (MGCS)[^8]. The authors define MGTS as encompassing thrombotic disorders in which there is definitive, mechanistically supported evidence that the M-protein directly contributes to thrombosis. Based on their analysis of current evidence, monoclonal protein–induced immune thrombocytopenia and thrombosis is the only disorder currently meeting their stringent criteria for MGTS. In contrast, thrombotic microangiopathy and antiphospholipid syndrome occurring in the setting of a monoclonal protein are classified as "thrombotic syndromes associated with M-proteins" pending causal validation, reflecting biologically plausible but not yet directly proven M-protein–mediated mechanisms. This nosological framework is clinically important for haematologists at NCKUH who encounter patients with monoclonal proteins presenting with TMA or thrombocytopenia, as it encourages a systematic mechanistic evaluation rather than empirical treatment of the underlying plasma cell or B-cell disorder alone.

**AI-Based Mortality Prediction in Immune TTP — Persistent Limitations.** A *Blood Advances* commentary by Abou-Ismail et al. addresses the challenge of mortality prediction in immune TTP despite advances in artificial intelligence-based analytical tools[^9]. The paper highlights that even with improved computational methods, short-term mortality prediction in acute immune TTP remains difficult due to the heterogeneity of presentation and the dynamic nature of ADAMTS13 inhibitor-mediated pathophysiology. This commentary, while primarily methodological, serves as a timely caution against over-reliance on prognostic scores for triage decisions in acute TTP, reinforcing that clinical judgement and prompt therapeutic plasma exchange initiation remain paramount.

## III. Aplastic Anemia

**Somatic Alterations in the Diagnosis and Management of Hypocellular Bone Marrow Failure — "How I Treat" Framework.** Groarke, Gutierrez-Rodrigues, and Patel published in *Blood* a comprehensive "How I Treat" review addressing the clinical use of somatic alterations in hypocellular bone marrow failure (BMF)[^10]. The review distinguishes immune aplastic anaemia (IAA), hypoplastic MDS, and inherited BMF syndromes (IBMFS) — a distinction of major clinical importance given the divergent management implications. The authors demonstrate that disease-specific clonal signatures can actively aid in resolving this differential: for example, somatic BCOR/BCORL1 and DNMT3A mutations emerging in the context of IAA generally represent clonal haematopoiesis without necessarily conferring malignant transformation risk, whereas somatic mutations in genes such as RUNX1, TP53, or spliceosomal genes in a hypocellular marrow should prompt consideration of hypoplastic MDS. The review also addresses the use of somatic alterations to estimate risk for secondary myeloid neoplasms and to guide surveillance intensity, and in select cases — such as PIG-A clone expansion conferring a PNH phenotype — to support specific therapeutic decisions. For practitioners at NCKUH, this framework operationalises next-generation sequencing data in the evaluation of unexplained cytopenias with hypocellular marrow, moving beyond morphology alone.

**PTCy-Based Haplo-BMT for Paediatric and Young Adult Severe Aplastic Anaemia.** Dionysiou et al. published in *Blood Advances* a single-institution retrospective series reporting outcomes of 31 consecutive paediatric, adolescent, and young adult patients (age ≤21 years) with acquired severe aplastic anaemia (SAA) who underwent allogeneic bone marrow transplantation between 2014 and 2024[^11]. The PTCy cohort (n = 23) received reduced-intensity conditioning haploidentical BMT with post-transplant cyclophosphamide, mycophenolate mofetil, and tacrolimus; the standard-of-care cohort (n = 8) received matched sibling donor BMT with cyclophosphamide/antithymocyte globulin conditioning and calcineurin inhibitor/methotrexate GVHD prophylaxis. Overall survival was 97% across the entire cohort (PTCy group 96%, SOC group 100%). Notably, GVHD rates were low in the PTCy cohort, with only one case of grade 2 acute GVHD and one case of extensive chronic GVHD. No patients developed clonal haematopoiesis post-transplant. Robust donor chimerism was achieved in the PTCy group, in contrast to uniformly mixed chimerism in the SOC group. The authors conclude that reduced-intensity conditioning haplo-BMT with PTCy represents a safe and curative frontline option even when matched sibling donors are available, substantially expanding the potential donor pool for paediatric SAA patients in Taiwan where HLA-identical sibling availability is constrained by small family sizes.

## V. Hemophilia A & B (including gene therapy)

**Bleeding in Women and Girls with Haemophilia — First Comprehensive "How I Treat."** Lavin, Abdul Kadir, and Sidonio published in *Blood* the first comprehensive "How I Treat" review specifically addressing bleeding management in women and girls with haemophilia (WGH) and symptomatic haemophilia carriers across the lifespan[^3]. The 2021 ISTH reclassification established that women and girls with FVIII or FIX levels <0.40 IU/mL should be classified as WGH rather than merely "carriers," and that those with excessive bleeding despite normal factor levels should be termed symptomatic carriers. The authors note that despite this paradigm shift, historical misconceptions persist widely in both families and healthcare providers, resulting in delayed diagnosis and inadequately managed bleeding episodes. The review departs from prior publications that focused principally on pregnancy management; instead, it systematically addresses bleeding across all life stages, including: the diagnostic delay in childhood before menarche, management of heavy menstrual bleeding as the sentinel event in many WGH, joint bleeding and its long-term musculoskeletal consequences, reproductive tract bleeding including miscarriage and post-partum haemorrhage, and the unique challenges of perioperative management. The review provides a practical decision framework for haematology treatment centres to ensure access and appropriate services for all haemophilia carriers and WGH throughout their lives. For NCKUH, this is directly relevant to haemophilia treatment centre practice: female family members of known haemophilia patients should be prospectively offered factor level assessment, ISTH bleeding assessment tool scoring, and — where factor levels are ≤0.40 IU/mL or bleeding score is elevated — formal WGH designation with individualised management plans.

**Bone Mineral Density Deficits in Haemophilia and Von Willebrand Disease.** Citla-Sridhar et al. published in *Blood Advances* a three-group cross-sectional observational study examining bone mineral density (BMD), bone remodelling biomarkers, and haemostatic correlates in persons with haemophilia (PwH; n = 34), persons with von Willebrand disease (PwVWD; n = 45), and healthy controls (n = 30), in participants aged 5–45 years[^12]. PwVWD had significantly lower BMD z-scores compared with controls, while PwH showed a similar but non-significant trend. Both groups had significantly lower bone mineral content. In multivariable regression models incorporating simultaneous thrombin-plasmin generation (STP) assay parameters, higher thrombin generation was independently associated with higher BMD z-scores, and higher plasmin generation was associated with lower BMD z-scores — a hypothesis-generating finding suggesting that the haemostatic system may directly modulate skeletal homeostasis. Importantly, STP parameters accounted for a greater proportion of BMD variance than models including only clinical factors or bone turnover markers. This study is the first to systematically characterise BMD deficits in VWD, which has historically been overlooked relative to haemophilia in this context. For NCKUH clinicians, it supports a lower threshold for DXA screening in VWD patients, particularly those with severe disease or prolonged joint bleeding history.

**BSH/UKHCDO Good Practice Paper on Multidisciplinary Haematology-Gynaecology Services.** Biss et al. published in *BJH* a British Society for Haematology and UK Haemophilia Centre Doctors' Organisation Good Practice Paper on implementing multidisciplinary haematology-gynaecology services for individuals with bleeding and thrombotic disorders[^13]. While a practice framework document rather than clinical trial data, it formalises the infrastructure required to deliver gender-appropriate haemostasis care — directly complementing the WGH "How I Treat" article published in the same week — and provides an implementation template applicable to specialist centres across Asia.

## VII. Thalassemia (alpha & beta; Taiwan-relevant)

**2026 Clinical Trials Update in β-Thalassemia — Comprehensive Landscape Review.** Musallam, Locatelli, Algeri, Cappellini, and colleagues published in *AJH* a comprehensive 2026 update on the clinical trial landscape for β-thalassemia, spanning both non-transfusion-dependent (NTDT) and transfusion-dependent (TDT) disease[^5]. Luspatercept, the activin receptor ligand trap, and mitapivat, the pyruvate kinase (PK) activator, are now approved in multiple jurisdictions for their respective indications and have demonstrated clinically meaningful improvements in haemoglobin levels and transfusion burden reduction. Additional PK activators, notably etavopivat, are undergoing clinical evaluation. On the curative side, betibeglogene autotemcel (gene addition via lentiviral vector) and exagamglogene autotemcel (CRISPR-based BCL11A enhancer editing) have achieved high rates of durable transfusion independence in pivotal trials; emerging platforms including base editing aim to further improve the precision and safety profile of genome modification. The review explicitly acknowledges that several approaches targeting fetal haemoglobin induction, iron metabolism, and ineffective erythropoiesis have failed to translate from preclinical proof of concept to clinical benefit, highlighting the complexity of the therapeutic development landscape. A separate *Blood Advances* companion article by Papasavva et al. — referenced in the same coverage period — reviews genetic disease modifiers (GDMs) in β-haemoglobinopathies, emphasising that modifiers such as BCL11A, HBG2 XmnI polymorphisms, and KLF1 variants strongly influence both disease severity and therapeutic responsiveness, and must be incorporated into treatment planning[^14]. Future priorities identified include refining patient selection for curative therapies, generating real-world comparative effectiveness data, developing standardised response criteria, expanding paediatric access, and addressing the profound cost and accessibility disparities — particularly relevant for Taiwan's NHI system and for Taiwanese patients of Southeast Asian ancestry.

**Polycomb EZH2 Exon-14 Skipping as a Strategy for Selective HbF Reactivation.** Kaminski et al. published in *Blood* a CRISPR screen-based mechanistic study identifying a region in EZH2 (the catalytic subunit of PRC2) in which Cas9-induced exon 14 skipping (EZH2Δ14) relieves HbF repression while largely preserving cellular fitness[^15]. The EZH2Δ14 isoform lacks a portion of the CXC domain, retains H3K27 methylation capacity for a subset of PRC2 target genes, but is unable to maintain the HBG repressor programme. Crucially, this selective disruption demonstrates that partial PRC perturbation — rather than complete loss of PRC function, which would be toxic — can yield therapeutically useful phenotypes. Experiments in mice bearing humanised β-globin genes confirmed that this mechanism operates in a physiologically relevant switching model. This study provides a novel strategy for HbF reactivation specifically relevant to β-thalassemia and sickle cell disease, and is conceptually important for the ongoing development of non-nuclease-based epigenetic editing platforms that could be applied without permanent DNA cleavage.

**Two-Decade HSCT Experience in Transfusion-Dependent β-Thalassemia.** Al-Jefri et al. published in *AJH* a large single-centre retrospective series evaluating haematopoietic stem cell transplantation outcomes in children with transfusion-dependent β-thalassemia over 20 years[^16]. Full numerical data were not available in the digest, but the study provides long-term HSCT outcome data relevant to resource settings where gene therapy is not yet accessible or affordable — which describes the current NHI situation in Taiwan for most paediatric TDT patients, where matched sibling donor HSCT remains the standard curative pathway.

## IX. Red Cell Disorders (G6PD, PK deficiency, hereditary spherocytosis)

**IMP1 as a Posttranscriptional Regulator of Embryonic Globin Expression.** MacInnes et al. published in *Blood Advances* a study characterising the RNA-binding protein IMP1 as a developmentally restricted posttranscriptional regulator in early erythroid cells[^17]. IMP1 is expressed in fetal but repressed in adult erythroid cells, being among the most differentially expressed proteins between developmental stages. Knockdown of IMP1 in the HUDEP-1 fetal erythroid line did not alter expansion or differentiation but produced significant proteome changes consistent with fetal erythroid specification. Notably, IMP1 knockdown markedly reduced embryonic ζ- and ε-globin protein levels, supporting direct posttranscriptional regulation of embryonic globins by IMP1 — but had no significant effect on γ-globin (HbF) levels, suggesting that the HbF regulatory axis operates independently of this mechanism. IMP1 was most abundant in iPSC-derived embryonic erythroid surrogates. While the immediate clinical applicability is limited, the identification of IMP1 as an embryonic globin regulator adds a novel posttranscriptional layer to the globin switching regulatory network — a finding potentially relevant to therapeutic reactivation of embryonic globins in haemoglobinopathies such as β-thalassemia and sickle cell disease.

## X. Iron Deficiency & Nutritional Anemias

**Iron Deficiency — Clinical Care and Research Gaps.** Wang, Cuker, and Goshua published a commentary in *Blood Advances* identifying unmet clinical needs and research gaps in iron deficiency management[^18]. While full numerical data are not available in the digest, the piece addresses the persistent underdiagnosis and undertreatment of iron deficiency in multiple clinical settings — including among patients with bleeding disorders and haematological malignancies receiving immunotherapy. This commentary is timely given the BJH observation (Hwa et al., referenced in the malignant context but peripherally relevant) that nearly 50% of newly diagnosed myeloma patients receiving daratumumab-based therapy develop iron deficiency at one year, compared with none in the VRD group — though the primary focus of that analysis is oncology rather than nutritional haematology. The gaps identified by Wang et al. include lack of consensus on optimal ferritin cut-offs in different disease contexts, underutilisation of intravenous iron in appropriate patients, and the need for longitudinal iron status monitoring protocols.

## XI. Thrombosis & Coagulation (VTE, APS, inherited thrombophilia)

**Provoked VTE Recurrence Risk Is Not Uniformly Low — Early-Terminated Prospective Cohort.** Eichinger, Eischer, Kaider, Heinze, and colleagues published in *Blood* a prospective cohort study that fundamentally challenges the assumption that venous thromboembolism provoked by transient risk factors carries uniformly low recurrence risk[^2]. The study used the International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis (ISTH) classification of major and minor transient risk factors to prospectively stratify 242 patients with symptomatic DVT and/or PE who had completed ≥3 months of anticoagulation, excluding patients with cancer, major thrombophilia, pregnancy-associated VTE, or indications for extended anticoagulation. Patients were followed after anticoagulation discontinuation with symptomatic recurrent VTE as the primary outcome. Critically, the study was terminated early due to unexpectedly high recurrence risk among patients with major provoking factors. At 24 months, the overall recurrence risk was 9.5% (95% CI, 6.0–14.0). Stratified by type of provoking factor, the 24-month recurrence risk was 19.7% (95% CI, 11.0–30.2) after major transient risk factors and 5.0% (95% CI, 2.2–9.5) after minor transient risk factors. Strikingly, no recurrence occurred among the 81 women with hormone-associated VTE; after their exclusion, the recurrence risk among other minor risk factor patients rose to 9.1% (95% CI, 4.0–17.0). These data carry immediate clinical implications: the category of "major provoked VTE" — which includes surgery, trauma, or hospitalisation — should not be considered uniformly low-risk, and individual decisions about anticoagulation duration require careful review of the specific provoking factor type rather than blanket application of the 3-month rule. For NCKUH, this study provides quantitative risk estimates directly usable in shared decision-making conversations with patients about extended anticoagulation after major provoked VTE.

**Hepatic FGFR-ERK-HRG Axis in Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia — Novel Pathogenic Mechanism.** Zhou, Wang, Neves, Liu, and colleagues published in *Blood* a mechanistic study identifying histidine-rich glycoprotein (HRG) as a pivotal regulator of HIT pathogenesis[^4]. The authors demonstrated that repeated heparin administration over five days progressively enhanced platelet activation, and plasma proteomic analysis identified a significant reduction in circulating HRG levels following successive heparin exposure. Mechanistically, heparin and PF4 suppressed HRG expression by inhibiting the FGFR-ERK-Elk1 hepatic signalling axis — Elk1 directly binds the HRG promoter to regulate liver expression, and heparin/PF4-mediated suppression of this axis results in reduced HRG output. Physiological concentrations of HRG potently inhibited platelet activation, procoagulant activity, spreading, and aggregation, and significantly reduced thrombus formation under flow conditions. HRG was shown to bind activated αIIbβ3 integrin in a zinc-dependent manner, blocking its ligand-binding capacity. Beyond direct platelet inhibition, HRG also inhibited the formation of ultralarge PF4-heparin complexes (ULCs), disrupted the immune complexes comprising the HIT-like antibody KKO and ULCs, and attenuated autoantibody generation and neutrophil extracellular trap formation in both in vitro and murine HIT models. This study repositions HRG from a relatively obscure plasma protein to a central mechanistic brake on HIT pathogenesis, and identifies the hepatic FGFR-ERK axis as a potential therapeutic target. Exogenous HRG supplementation or FGFR pathway modulation to restore HRG levels may represent a novel preventive strategy in patients at risk for HIT.

## XIII. Taiwan Clinical Context

Several findings this week have direct Taiwan-specific relevance. The critical ITP bleed guideline from *Blood Advances*[^1] is immediately applicable to NCKUH emergency practice; the recommendation to co-administer high-dose IVIG, corticosteroids, platelet transfusions, tranexamic acid, and TPO-RAs simultaneously at first assessment of a critical bleed should be incorporated into local institutional protocols. Taiwan's NHI currently reimburses eltrombopag and romiplostim for chronic ITP; however, the acute TPO-RA use scenario (as an emergency adjunct rather than maintenance agent) may require additional clinical justification in reimbursement discussions.

For the VTE recurrence data[^2], Taiwanese vascular surgery and haematology services should note that the 19.7% two-year recurrence risk following major provoked VTE substantially exceeds the threshold typically used to justify extended anticoagulation in international guidelines, and this dataset — derived from an ISTH-classified prospective cohort — provides a quantitative foundation for individualising decisions beyond the standard 3-month course.

Regarding β-thalassemia[^5], Taiwan has a population α-thalassaemia carrier rate of approximately 4–5% and β-thalassaemia carrier rate of approximately 1–2%, predominantly of Southeast Asian and indigenous ancestry, with a substantial prevalence of HbH disease. Luspatercept is not yet available under NHI coverage for thalassaemia in Taiwan as of mid-2026, and the gene therapy products (betibeglogene autotemcel and exagamglogene autotemcel) remain unavailable outside clinical trial access. The 2026 update identifying unresolved accessibility and cost challenges globally resonates acutely in the Taiwan NHI context, and NCKUH participation in international gene therapy trials represents the primary access pathway for eligible TDT patients. The EZH2Δ14 HbF reactivation strategy[^15] is a preclinical platform that could eventually complement or reduce the required therapeutic haemoglobin increment for NHI-covered disease-modifying therapies.

For haemophilia, the WGH "How I Treat" article[^3] is directly applicable: NCKUH haemophilia treatment centre practice should proactively screen female relatives of known haemophilia patients for factor levels and assign WGH designation where FVIII or FIX is <0.40 IU/mL. Emicizumab is currently NHI-reimbursed for severe haemophilia A with inhibitors in Taiwan, but WGH with FVIII <0.01 IU/mL may also qualify; individual assessment is warranted.

## XIV. Key Takeaways

The most immediately practice-changing findings from 2026-W33 are the following. The McMaster ITP emergency guideline establishes the first GRADE-based standard for critical ITP bleeds, recommending five simultaneous interventions including TPO-RAs, and advising against recombinant factor VIIa. The provoked VTE recurrence data — including a 19.7% two-year risk after major transient risk factors and early study termination — fundamentally challenge blanket application of 3-month anticoagulation for all provoked VTE and demand individualised risk assessment. The WGH "How I Treat" article operationalises the 2021 ISTH reclassification, requiring haemophilia centres to proactively identify and formally manage female haemophilia carriers with factor levels <0.40 IU/mL as WGH. The identification of the FGFR-ERK-HRG hepatic axis as a suppressor of HIT pathogenesis represents a mechanistic breakthrough that positions HRG as both a biomarker of HIT susceptibility and a therapeutic target. Finally, the 2026 β-thalassemia trials update provides the most current inventory of an approved and emerging therapeutic landscape that is highly relevant to Taiwan's substantial thalassaemia-affected population, while highlighting that equitable access remains the dominant unresolved challenge.
