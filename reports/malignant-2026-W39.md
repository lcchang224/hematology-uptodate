## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

New data this week strengthen the case for genotype-directed intensification and for MRD-guided post-transplant decision-making, complementing last week's RAS-resistance findings without duplicating them. A retrospective multicenter analysis of 212 adults with de novo BCR::ABL1+ AML (excluding patients with prior CML history or TKI exposure) compared intensive chemotherapy alone (n=89) against intensive chemotherapy plus a BCR-ABL1 TKI (n=123) between 1999 and 2024. CR/CRi rates rose from 68% to 89% (p<0.0001), median OS was 20.5 months with chemotherapy alone versus not reached with the TKI combination, and 3-year/5-year OS rates improved from 42.1%/38.5% to 70.9%/62.9% (p<0.0001). Multivariate analysis confirmed TKI addition as an independent predictor of both CR/CRi (odds ratio 4.74, 95% CI 2.17–10.35, p<0.001) and OS (HR 0.40, 95% CI 0.27–0.62, p<0.001), with TKI addition and allo-HSCT in CR1 both independently prognostic for relapse-free survival (HR 0.42, 95% CI 0.23–0.75, p=0.004 and HR 0.31, 95% CI 0.17–0.55, p<0.0001, respectively)[^3]. The authors propose that BCR::ABL1+ AML should be reconsidered for removal from the ELN adverse-risk category given this magnitude of benefit.

A *Blood* "How I Treat" article addresses the practical application of MRD testing to personalize allogeneic HCT decisions in adult AML, covering patient selection for transplant, conditioning intensity calibration, post-transplant surveillance, and identification of candidates for maintenance or pre-emptive therapy, illustrated through four representative clinical scenarios; the authors emphasize the gap between promising translational claims and the practical realities of currently available MRD assays[^6]. A companion Haematologica Spotlight Review surveys the evolving landscape of leukemia-restricted immunotherapy targets beyond CD33/CD123/CLEC12A, highlighting subtype-specific antigens such as FOLR1 in CBFA2T3::GLIS2 AML, CLEC2A in KMT2A-rearranged AML, and intracellular neoantigens derived from NPM1 mutations and fusion proteins as candidates for a new generation of biologically precise immunotherapies[^7]. Mechanistic work also identifies MECOM and BRD4 as actionable dependencies downstream of mutant ASXL1 in a CRISPR-engineered human HSPC model, with ASXL1-mutant AML samples showing increased MECOM chromatin activity consistent with the gene-editing findings[^8]. The prior week's CPX-351 pediatric AAML1831 futility result and the RAS-pathway/FLT3-BCL2i resistance biology remain the definitive findings on those topics and are not restated here.

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

The principal new signal this cycle is a prospective phase 2 study (NCT05660473) of venetoclax added to pediatric-inspired chemotherapy as first-line therapy in 167 adolescents and adults (14–60 years) with newly diagnosed Philadelphia chromosome-negative ALL. The primary endpoint of post-induction MFC-MRD negativity was met, with 73.0% of responders achieving MRD negativity and an overall CR rate of 91.0%. After a median follow-up of 19.3 months, median OS and disease-free survival were not reached, with estimated 2-year rates of 78.5% and 76.7%, respectively; a propensity score–matched comparison against historical chemotherapy-only controls confirmed superior survival with the venetoclax-containing regimen. Grade ≥3 adverse events were predominantly hematologic toxicities and infections, at an incidence comparable to the historical cohort[^1]. This adds venetoclax to the growing list of targeted agents being layered onto pediatric-inspired backbones for adult Ph-negative ALL.

In transplant conditioning, a retrospective single-center comparison of 101 adults with ALL undergoing first allo-HSCT after reduced-dose (8 Gy) versus standard-dose (12 Gy) fractionated TBI found that despite an older, higher-comorbidity, higher-risk 8 Gy cohort, unadjusted non-relapse mortality (NRM), relapse incidence, and OS did not differ between groups; in multivariable analysis, 12 Gy TBI was independently associated with increased NRM (subdistribution HR 5.97, 95% CI 1.77–20.15, p=0.004) without a corresponding reduction in relapse incidence, a finding that was robust across sensitivity analyses including propensity score overlap weighting[^9]. The authors call for prospective evaluation of reduced-dose TBI regimens in adult ALL. A CD38-CD3 bispecific T-cell engager, XmAb18968, also reported early efficacy in a phase 1 trial enrolling both relapsed/refractory AML and T-ALL patients (see Section XII for full dosing and toxicity details).

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

The final analysis of the international phase III CLL6 RESIDUUM study (ALLG/FILO groups) reports on 2 years of lenalidomide maintenance versus observation in 143 patients with CLL and residual disease after initial immunochemotherapy. Median PFS was 64.6 months with lenalidomide (95% CI 47.7 to not reached) versus 42.4 months with observation (95% CI 32.3–61.6, p=0.039), and the PFS benefit persisted for 3 years after maintenance cessation, though no OS difference was observed; more patients receiving lenalidomide achieved MRD negativity, especially those completing at least 20 maintenance cycles, at the cost of increased cytopenias, infections, and gastrointestinal toxicity[^10]. Separately, mechanistic work in *Leukemia* identifies aberrant MERTK expression as constitutively active in leukemic cells from approximately 65% of 100 CLL patients studied, forming a signalosome with CD79a/LYN that activates BTK independent of canonical BCR ligation; ibrutinib-resistant CLL cells showed elevated MERTK levels, and the MERTK inhibitor UNC-2025 synergized with venetoclax to induce apoptosis, nominating this combination as a candidate strategy for relapsed/refractory disease[^11].

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

The phase II LODEFI study evaluated low-dose deferasirox in lower-risk MDS patients after erythropoiesis-stimulating agent failure, though detailed efficacy figures were not available in the abstract accessed this week[^12]. Mechanistic interest also continues around juvenile myelomonocytic leukemia (JMML), a pediatric MDS/MPN overlap disorder; a *Blood* study using a Shp2E76K/+ mouse model identifies an IL-17A/PTGS2/NLRP3 inflammasome axis driving marrow inflammation and immune suppression in PTPN11-mutant disease, with combined NLRP3/PTGS2 inhibition plus MEK blockade reducing leukemic burden and improving survival in patient-derived xenograft models[^13].

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

Two significant biology papers advance the mechanistic understanding of MPN inflammation and fibrosis this week, distinct from the JAK2/CALR signaling literature already covered previously. First, single-cell RNA sequencing of MPN samples identifies galectin-1 (Gal-1, LGALS1) as markedly upregulated in MPN monocytes, where recombinant Gal-1 drives inflammatory cytokine secretion through TLR4-dependent NF-κB activation; genetic Lgals1 ablation in MPLW515L-driven murine MPN ameliorated leukocytosis and splenomegaly, and Gal-1 inhibition suppressed carrageenan-induced thrombosis and inflammation in vivo, nominating Gal-1/TLR4 blockade as a novel therapeutic avenue for MPN-associated inflammation[^14]. Second, a companion *Blood* paper reframes bone marrow fibrosis pathogenesis in JAK2V617F-mutated myelofibrosis, showing that hematopoietic-derived "fibrocytes" of monocyte lineage — rather than myofibroblasts alone — account for nearly two-thirds of collagen-producing cells in the marrow; a high-throughput screen using Jak2V617F transgenic mouse-derived fibrocytes identified statins as inhibitors of fibrocyte proliferation, and in vivo pitavastatin treatment reduced fibrocyte numbers, ameliorated marrow fibrosis, and improved anemia, with reduced TGF-β1 production and downstream myofibroblast expansion; human peripheral blood–derived fibrocytes from JAK2V617F-mutated MPN patients showed similar in vitro statin sensitivity[^15]. In genotype-selective signaling biology, CALR-mutant and MPLW515K-mutant (but not JAK2V617F-mutant) MPN cells show constitutive TYK2 phosphorylation; the TYK2 inhibitor deucravacitinib reduced viability and STAT3/5 phosphorylation selectively in CALR/MPL-mutant cells, with enhanced efficacy combined with the JAK2-selective inhibitor fedratinib, and in primary patient samples deucravacitinib suppressed colony growth in ET and primary myelofibrosis but not PV, selectively reducing CALR- but not JAK2V617F-mutant allele burden[^5].

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

A *Blood* perspective piece calls attention to the proliferating complexity of first-line LBCL trial design, noting that despite POLARIX establishing R-CHP-polatuzumab as superior to R-CHOP, many ongoing phase 3 trials continue to use R-CHOP as comparator, and "R-CHOP + X" strategies tested in biomarker-unselected populations risk producing fragmented, noncomparable regimens; the authors propose pooled patient-level analyses, standardized biomarker/MRD platforms, and AI-enabled predictive modeling as a path toward a harmonized treatment framework[^16]. Mechanistically, DOT1L is shown to cooperate with EZH2 to maintain germinal-center B-cell identity in GCB-DLBCL via regulation of noncanonical PRC1 composition and H2AK119 monoubiquitination at PRC1/2 target genes enriched for plasma-cell differentiation signatures; DOT1L-dependent downregulation of USP7 was associated with increased sensitivity to EZH2 inhibition across multiple GCB-DLBCL cell lines, supporting combination epigenetic targeting strategies[^17]. In real-world outcomes, a pooled analysis of the REPRIME-FIL and CART-SIE studies in relapsed/refractory primary mediastinal B-cell lymphoma found that CD19 CAR T-cell access (post-2019 treatment era) was associated with significantly superior OS versus other salvage regimens in a 6-month landmark analysis (adjusted HR 0.34, 95% CI 0.17–0.66, p=0.001), and versus the subgroup of patients undergoing ASCT specifically (adjusted HR 0.27, 95% CI 0.13–0.66, p=0.001), across a pooled cohort of 191 patients with 3-year OS of 62% (95% CI 55–69) from first-line failure[^18]. A retrospective analysis of 344 patients with histologic transformation of follicular lymphoma in the LEO CReWE consortium reports a median EFS from transformation of only 9 months (95% CI 7–11), 2-year EFS of 30%, and median OS of 4.9 years, with relapse occurring in 60% of patients (70% relapsing as DLBCL)[^19].

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

Building on last week's SYMPATICO and TRIANGLE data (not repeated here), this cycle adds a chemotherapy-free frontline strategy for indolent lymphoma. A phase 2 trial of mosunetuzumab followed by response-adapted polatuzumab vedotin plus obinutuzumab in 42 previously untreated patients with follicular lymphoma (88%) or marginal zone lymphoma achieved an end-of-treatment overall response rate of 100% and CR rate of 86%, with mosunetuzumab alone yielding a 71% CR rate; at 34 months median follow-up, 2-year PFS was 89% (95% CI 80–100) and 2-year OS was 100%, with cytokine release syndrome occurring in 64% of patients but confined to grade 1 severity and no tocilizumab requirement[^20]. A systematic review and meta-analysis examines rituximab maintenance following frontline bendamustine-rituximab in MCL, though comparative effect estimates were not detailed in the available abstract[^21].

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

A retrospective cohort of 145 classical Hodgkin lymphoma patients treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors between 2015 and 2024 examined the feasibility of ICI rechallenge after immune-related adverse event (irAE)–driven interruption. Among 130 patients treated within the same line (cohort 1), PFS and duration of response did not differ significantly between rechallenged and non-rechallenged patients (PFS HR 1.7, 95% CI 0.31–9.3, p=0.55; DOR HR 2.7, 95% CI 0.24–31.2, p=0.42), and recurrent irAEs occurred in 20% overall (same irAE recurrence in 7%). In a separate cohort of 15 patients rechallenged in a subsequent treatment line, median PFS was 7.4 months (refractory subgroup) versus 13.5 months (responders subsequently rechallenged at progression), supporting rechallenge as feasible in selected patients[^22]. In peripheral T-cell lymphoma, the phase IIa AVAIL-T trial of the anti-PD-L1 antibody avelumab in 32 treated relapsed/refractory PTCL patients reported a modest overall response rate of 14.3%, median PFS of 2.8 months (95% CI 1.8–3.4), and median OS of 10.3 months (95% CI 6.0–12.3), though the five responders showed durable benefit with a 12-month duration-of-response rate of 60%[^23]. Mechanistic work in *Blood* identifies IGF2BP2, an N6-methyladenosine reader, as consistently overexpressed across nodal PTCL subtypes in a 196-patient RNA-seq cohort, where it stabilizes endosome-related transcripts to promote tumor growth and suppress CD8+ T-cell infiltration; the small-molecule inhibitor CWI1-2 suppressed endocytosis and tumor growth in patient-derived xenograft models, providing rationale for RNA-modification-targeted immunomodulatory therapy in PTCL[^24].

## X. NK/T-cell Lymphoma (Asia focus)

A British Journal of Haematology report finds that ICI-containing salvage therapy improves outcomes in relapsed/progressive NK/T-cell lymphoma after first-line treatment failure, an important signal for a disease with historically poor salvage options and particular relevance to Asian patient populations, though detailed response and survival figures were not available in the accessed abstract[^25].

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

This is the most data-dense section this week. The phase 3 SeaLAND trial (ALLG MM23, ACTRN12620000291987) randomized 142 newly diagnosed, transplant-eligible myeloma patients post-ASCT to selinexor plus lenalidomide maintenance versus lenalidomide alone, closing early for futility. The ≥CR rate was numerically but not significantly higher with the combination (67% vs 53%, p=0.09), and at 24-month median follow-up PFS did not differ (HR 1.22, 95% CI 0.62–2.41, p=0.56; 24-month PFS 70% vs 73%). Lenalidomide relative dose intensity was substantially reduced in the combination arm (68% vs 81%, p=0.002), and grade ≥3 adverse events were markedly more frequent with selinexor-lenalidomide (85% vs 45%, p<0.001), including higher rates of infection (19% vs 6%, p<0.01) and gastrointestinal toxicity (14% vs 3%, p<0.05); the authors conclude selinexor-based maintenance cannot be recommended, even in high-risk disease[^2].

On the biology front, two companion *Blood* papers focus on high-risk cytogenetic subsets. NSD2-ligand-directed degrader (NSD2-LDD), a cereblon-recruiting degrader selective for PWWP1-domain-containing NSD2 isoforms, eliminates the oncogenic H3K36me2 mark driving t(4;14) transcriptional programs, suppressing stromal adhesion and 3D colony growth, and extending survival in xenograft and genetically engineered t(4;14) mouse models[^26]. In del(17p)/TP53-deficient myeloma, PKMYT1 was nominated as a targetable vulnerability by integrating RNA-seq and genetic dependency data; the selective inhibitor RP-6306 induced DNA damage, micronucleus formation, and mitotic catastrophe preferentially in del(17p) MM cells while sparing del(17p)-negative and healthy cells, reducing tumor burden and extending survival in both xenograft and TP53-deficient syngeneic models[^4]. A global WBMT registry analysis of 8,269 newly diagnosed myeloma patients transplanted 2013–2017 found 3-year OS in a 6-month landmark analysis was higher with lenalidomide maintenance (86%) than with thalidomide (80%), bortezomib (83%), or no maintenance (79%)[^27]. Risk stratification also advances via an integrated SNP-array/FISH analysis of 694 Chinese newly diagnosed myeloma patients, in which a modal chromosome count >49 predicted superior OS (p=0.0062) and PFS (p=0.00037) among hyperdiploid patients, with gain of chromosome 3 independently favorable for both OS (p=0.0024) and PFS (p=0.00078); autologous HSCT benefit was restricted to hyperdiploid patients without high-risk cytogenetic abnormalities and with >49 chromosomes (OS p=0.014)[^28]. In cellular therapy, a study of 20 relapsed/refractory myeloma patients receiving BCMA-targeted CAR T-cell salvage after prior anti-GPRC5D CAR T failure reported 13 responses (9 stringent CR, 4 PR) with median duration of response 10.2 months and median PFS 9.2 months (95% CI 3.0–13.6), statistically comparable to a prior cohort receiving GPRC5D CAR T after BCMA CAR T failure (ORR 65% vs 84%, p=0.18; PFS 9.2 vs 4.5 months, p=0.93), supporting bidirectional target-switching as salvage strategy[^29]. Finally, a prospective analysis of 352 newly diagnosed myeloma patients using combined bone marrow histology, MRI, PET, and serum sBCMA identifies soluble BCMA as the strongest independent prognostic marker, more predictive of early treatment failure than physical tumor volume, with a novel four-phenotype classification (Dual-High, Sero-dominant, Macro-dominant, Dual-Low) proposed for risk stratification[^30].

## XII. Transplant & Cellular Therapy (CAR-T / HSCT / GVHD)

A phase 1 dose-escalation trial of expanded minor histocompatibility antigen (mHAg)–specific donor regulatory T cells for GVHD prevention in matched related donor allo-HCT enrolled 15 patients across four dose levels (up to 4×10^5/kg, identified as the maximum tolerated dose), with no dose-limiting toxicities observed. Day 100 cumulative incidence of grade II–IV acute GVHD was 13%, 1-year moderate/severe chronic GVHD was 6.7% (rising to 20% by 3 years), and overall survival was 73% at a median follow-up of 41.7 months; infused Treg clones demonstrated persistence and expansion through 1 year post-transplant, supporting further development of this cellular GVHD-prevention strategy[^31]. In infectious complications, a single-center retrospective study of 2,072 HCT and 545 CAR T-cell recipients found a 7.4% cumulative incidence of respiratory viral infection within 100 days, highest in CAR T-cell recipients (12.7%) versus allogeneic (7.8%) and autologous (4.7%) HCT recipients; SARS-CoV-2 was the most common pathogen (43%), and severe lower respiratory tract infection was independently associated with tocilizumab use within 30 days (OR 4.84, 95% CI 1.12–20.96, p=0.035)[^32]. A Haematologica review addresses the rare but regulatory-scrutinized phenomenon of secondary T-cell lymphoma after CAR T-cell therapy, reporting incidences of approximately 0.03–0.3% across registries and large institutional series — far rarer than therapy-related myeloid neoplasms or solid tumors in this heavily pretreated population — and noting that most molecularly characterized cases lack evidence of CAR vector-driven transformation, instead reflecting pre-existing or therapy-selected clonal T-cell populations; the authors conclude current evidence does not undermine the overall favorable benefit-risk profile of CAR T-cell therapy but supports standardized long-term surveillance[^33]. In the multitarget AML/T-ALL space, the CD38-CD3 bispecific T-cell engager XmAb18968 was evaluated in a phase 1 trial of 22 heavily pretreated patients (13 AML, 9 T-ALL; median age 63, median 3 prior lines including 77.3% with prior venetoclax exposure); no grade ≥3 cytokine release syndrome or neurotoxicity occurred, two AML patients achieved MRD-negative CR and one achieved PR, four of six evaluable T-ALL patients showed meaningful disease burden reduction, and median OS was 8.5 months (AML) and 8.7 months (T-ALL)[^34].

## XIV. Taiwan Clinical Context

Several findings this week carry direct relevance for practice at NCKUH and other Taiwanese centers. The BCR::ABL1+ AML TKI-intensification data reinforce a treatment approach already broadly adopted locally, where dasatinib or imatinib is commonly co-administered with induction chemotherapy for Ph+ AML under NHI reimbursement pathways analogous to those for Ph+ ALL; the magnitude of OS benefit reported (5-year OS 62.9% vs 38.5%) supports continued advocacy for early TKI access in this rare AML subset[^3]. The venetoclax plus pediatric-inspired chemotherapy data in adult Ph-negative ALL are particularly relevant given Taiwan's established use of pediatric-inspired (Hyper-CVAD-adjacent and BFM-based) protocols in AYA ALL populations at CGMH and NTUH; venetoclax is NHI-reimbursed for CLL and select AML indications in Taiwan, and this new phase 2 signal may support future compassionate or trial-based access extension to ALL. For multiple myeloma, the SeaLAND negative trial is a useful cautionary data point as selinexor carries NHI restrictions in Taiwan limited to later-line use, and this result argues against extrapolating selinexor to maintenance settings outside of clinical trials. The NK/T-cell lymphoma ICI-salvage signal is of above-average local relevance given the markedly higher incidence of extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, nasal type, in Taiwan and East Asia compared with Western populations; ICI-based salvage regimens (e.g., pembrolizumab, sintilimab) are used off-label or through NHI special review after L-asparaginase-based regimen failure at CGMH and NTUH, and this new evidence base should inform local salvage algorithm updates. Finally, the secondary T-cell lymphoma-after-CAR-T review is relevant as Taiwan's CAR-T experience (tisagenlecleucel and axicabtagene ciloleucel, both NHI-reimbursed for defined DLBCL/ALL indications) matures beyond 3–5 years of follow-up, reinforcing the case for structured long-term registry surveillance at treating centers.

## XV. Key Takeaways

The venetoclax-pediatric chemotherapy ALL data and BCR::ABL1+ AML TKI intensification results both reinforce a maturing theme across acute leukemias: genotype- and MRD-guided combination intensification continues to outperform historical chemotherapy-only backbones with manageable added toxicity. The SeaLAND trial's futility for selinexor maintenance in myeloma, alongside prior negative or marginal maintenance intensification signals, underscores that not every combinable novel agent translates into maintenance benefit, and toxicity/dose-intensity tradeoffs (reduced lenalidomide RDI in the combination arm) can undermine efficacy even when response rates trend favorably. The PKMYT1/del(17p) myeloma and Gal-1/fibrocyte MPN biology papers both exemplify a broader trend toward microenvironment- and lineage-specific vulnerability targeting (fibrocytes rather than myofibroblasts in myelofibrosis; monocyte-restricted Gal-1/TLR4 signaling in MPN inflammation) as the next wave of mechanistically rational drug development beyond canonical JAK2/BCL2/BTK axes.
