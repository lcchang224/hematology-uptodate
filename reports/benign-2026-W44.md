## Summary

This cycle is dominated by a wave of *Blood* publications extending last week's two biggest storylines — ITP cellular/molecular biology and PF4-driven antiphospholipid immunothrombosis — alongside fresh real-world PNH data and sickle cell/red cell biology of tangential relevance. First, a *Blood* phase-translatable report demonstrates that BCMA-directed CAR T-cell therapy produces durable drug-free complete remission lasting up to 15 months in 4 patients with refractory primary ITP, with rapid and sustained platelet recovery and a manageable safety profile, representing a genuinely novel cellular-therapy approach for a disease historically managed with rituximab, TPO-RAs, or splenectomy[^9]. Second, a mechanistic *Blood* paper identifies a reduced SRSF1-LCK splicing axis in CD4+ naive T cells from ITP patients, showing that SRSF1 deficiency drives LCK exon 8 skipping, impairs TCR signaling and FOXP3-dependent Treg induction, and that adoptive transfer of exon-8-skipped Lck-expressing T cells into CD61-knockout mice significantly reduces Treg proportions and platelet counts in an active ITP model — a new molecular explanation for regulatory T-cell failure in ITP[^10]. Third, building directly on last week's PF4:β2GPI:NET immunocomplex discovery, the full primary dataset (now indexed with its own DOI) confirms thrombus reduction with ADAMTS13 or DNase I infusion and abrogation of thrombosis in PF4-knockout and anti-PF4 monoclonal antibody-blocked murine models, cementing PF4 as a central immunothrombotic hub in antiphospholipid syndrome[^2]. Fourth, a 151-patient multicenter UK/France real-world cohort of iptacopan monotherapy in PNH reports hemoglobin rising from 104 g/L at baseline to 127 g/L at 3 months, with 76% achieving hemoglobin ≥120 g/L by 12 months (versus 21.9% at baseline), transfusion independence in 88%, breakthrough hemolysis in 6.6%, and no thromboses or iptacopan-related deaths over a median 238-day treatment duration — the largest post-approval iptacopan safety/efficacy dataset to date, with no significant difference in response between patients with and without a history of bone marrow failure[^11]. Fifth, a low-incidence pediatric aplastic anemia genetic-screening study from *Blood* found that among 149 children with severe aplastic anemia undergoing comprehensive standard-of-care evaluation, 75.8% had no clinical or laboratory features suggestive of inherited bone marrow failure syndrome and none of these harbored a pathogenic IBMFS variant, arguing against routine delay of treatment while awaiting broad genetic testing in low-suspicion cases[^12].

## I. Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP)

ITP biology and novel cellular therapeutics dominate this week's literature. The most clinically provocative report is a *Blood* phase-translatable series describing BCMA-directed CAR T-cell therapy (registered as NCT06519565) in 4 patients with refractory primary ITP, in which treatment produced manageable safety, rapid and sustained platelet recovery, and durable drug-free complete remission lasting up to 15 months, raising the prospect of a plasma-cell-depleting cellular approach for autoantibody-driven ITP analogous to CAR T strategies now being explored across autoimmune disease more broadly[^9]. An accompanying *Blood* editorial, "ITP: a new CAR for an old road," frames this as proof-of-concept that deep B-cell/plasma-cell depletion can achieve long-term treatment-free remission in a subset of ITP patients refractory to conventional immunosuppression and thrombopoietic agents, while cautioning that the experience remains limited to 4 patients and requires confirmation in larger controlled cohorts[^13]. Complementing this clinical signal, a second *Blood* mechanistic paper identifies reduced SRSF1 expression in CD4+ naive T cells from ITP patients as the driver of LCK exon 8 skipping, demonstrating via minigene, RNA pull-down mass spectrometry, and RNA immunoprecipitation assays that SRSF1 directly regulates LCK splicing, that SRSF1 overexpression restores in vitro Treg induction capacity, and that antisense oligonucleotide blockade of SRSF1 binding enhances exon 8 skipping and attenuates TCR activation in Jurkat cells; in vivo, adoptive transfer of CD4+ naive T cells expressing exon-8-skipped murine Lck into CD61-knockout mice significantly reduced both Treg proportions and platelet counts in an active ITP model, establishing a novel SRSF1-LCK splicing axis as a candidate regulator of Treg development failure in ITP[^10]. A companion *Blood* commentary, "Splicing mishap in ITP: exon skipping dysregulates T cells," situates this finding within the growing recognition that post-transcriptional and splicing abnormalities, rather than purely antibody-centric mechanisms, contribute meaningfully to ITP pathogenesis[^14]. Together with last week's ASH 2026 focused guideline update recommending combination first-line therapy[^1] and the CD19+CD38+ regulatory B-cell biomarker data[^7], this week's findings reinforce a rapidly maturing mechanistic and therapeutic ITP landscape moving well beyond empiric corticosteroid-centric management.

## III. Aplastic Anemia

A *Blood* retrospective study addresses a long-debated diagnostic question: whether comprehensive germline genetic testing should be routinely performed in pediatric severe aplastic anemia (SAA) beyond cases with clinical suspicion of an inherited bone marrow failure syndrome (IBMFS). Among 153 patients who underwent enhanced whole-exome sequencing tailored to IBMFS genes, complete clinical and laboratory data were available for 149 patients; 113 patients (75.8%) had no clinical or laboratory features suggestive of IBMFS, and none of these harbored a pathogenic or likely pathogenic IBMFS variant. Among the remaining 36 patients (24.2%) flagged by clinical evaluation, one harbored a heterozygous pathogenic SAMD9L variant with a consistent clinical history, and a second had two pathogenic ERCC6L2 variants of undetermined phase[^12]. The authors conclude that for pediatric SAA patients with a negative comprehensive standard-of-care clinical and laboratory workup, the yield of broad genetic testing for cryptic IBMFS is low, and that the marginal diagnostic benefit must be weighed against the risk of delaying time-sensitive treatment such as immunosuppressive therapy or transplant — a finding with direct relevance to the companion *Haematologica* editorial from last week advocating broader proactive genetic screening in ITP/thrombocytopenia workups, suggesting that the optimal screening threshold may be disease- and phenotype-specific rather than uniformly maximalist[^6].

## IV. PNH — Paroxysmal Nocturnal Hemoglobinuria

The largest real-world iptacopan dataset published to date substantially extends the evidence base beyond pivotal trial populations. This retrospective multicenter UK/France study enrolled 151 patients with PNH who received iptacopan monotherapy between July 2021 and July 2025, with a median treatment duration of 238 days[^11]. Mean hemoglobin rose from 104 g/L at baseline to 127 g/L at 3 months, with improvements sustained through 24 months; 76% of patients achieved hemoglobin ≥120 g/L by 12 months compared with only 21.9% at baseline, and transfusion independence was achieved in 88% of patients. Critically, there was no statistically significant difference in hematologic response between patients with and without a history of bone marrow failure, addressing a key real-world uncertainty not fully resolved by the pivotal APPLY-PNH and APPOINT-PNH trials. Breakthrough hemolysis occurred in 6.6% of patients, with a mean hemoglobin fall of 24 g/L during these episodes; suspected bacterial infections occurred at a rate of 0.18 per patient-year with no meningococcal or pneumococcal infections reported, and there were no thromboses and no iptacopan-related deaths[^11]. This dataset meaningfully complements last week's 227-patient non-transplant-access PNH cohort data from regions without complement inhibitor availability[^5], together bracketing the full spectrum of modern PNH management options from allogeneic HSCT in resource-limited settings to oral factor B inhibition in complement-inhibitor-accessible health systems.

## V. Hemophilia A & B (including gene therapy)

A *Blood* preclinical paper describes a novel "AutoRegulated" (AR) FVIII-mimetic bispecific antibody engineered by inserting a thrombin-cleavable peptide into the hinge region of both emicizumab arms, addressing the recognized class-effect liability of FVIII-mimetic antibodies: excessive and context-insensitive thrombin generation, particularly problematic when combined with bypassing agents such as activated prothrombin complex concentrate (aPCC)[^15]. AR-emicizumab retained FVIII-mimetic EC50 potency and corrected blood loss in hemophilia A mice comparably to parental emicizumab, but unlike the parental molecule, underwent thrombin-responsive proteolytic inactivation upon exposure to supraphysiological thrombin levels. In thrombin generation assays, AR-emicizumab significantly attenuated the excessive thrombin peak seen with co-administered aPCC, and in vivo, AR-emicizumab prevented platelet-consumptive coagulopathy and reduced microvascular pulmonary thrombosis compared with parental emicizumab, without affecting systemic antibody levels[^15]. This represents an early but mechanistically elegant strategy to decouple FVIII-mimetic efficacy from thrombotic risk when bypassing agents are co-administered — a scenario of ongoing clinical relevance in inhibitor-positive hemophilia A patients who may still require emergency bypassing therapy.

## IX. Red Cell Disorders (G6PD, PK deficiency, hereditary spherocytosis)

An *AJH* multi-omic study provides the first integrated molecular characterization of hexokinase-1 (HK1) deficiency, a rare cause of hereditary nonspherocytic hemolytic anemia. Using population-scale genetics from the REDS RBC-Omics cohorts alongside deep metabolomic, lipidomic, oxylipin, and proteomic profiling of an individual carrying the HK1 p.Arg12Ter loss-of-function variant, the authors demonstrate disruption of glycolysis, the pentose phosphate pathway, and glutathione metabolism, accompanied by ATP depletion, impaired antioxidant defenses, and extensive membrane lipid remodeling with increased oxidized phospholipids and lysophospholipids[^17]. Proteomic signatures revealed redox imbalance and altered proteostasis, while reticulocytosis, increased mitochondria-positive erythrocytes, elevated acylcarnitines, and polyamines indicated severe compensatory erythropoietic stress with incomplete reticulocyte maturation. This establishes a mechanistic framework linking both common and rare HK1 variation to defined molecular phenotypes in human erythrocytes, with implications for understanding chronic compensated hemolysis in other glycolytic enzymopathies relevant to the differential diagnosis of hereditary spherocytosis and G6PD deficiency in the Taiwanese population[^17].

## XI. Thrombosis & Coagulation (VTE, APS, inherited thrombophilia)

The primary dataset underlying last week's reported discovery of PF4-centric APS immunothrombosis is now independently indexed in *Blood*, confirming and detailing the full experimental findings: platelet factor 4 (PF4) binds β2-glycoprotein I (β2GPI) and bridges it to neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs), forming a multimolecular PF4:β2GPI:NET immunotarget recognized by pathogenic APS antibodies[^2]. Dynamic light-scattering studies using isolated IgG from 4 triple-positive APS patients confirmed formation of antibody-reactive PF4:β2GPI:NET complexes, and in a microfluidic whole-blood thrombosis model, platelet-, fibrin-, and complement-rich thrombi bound APS antibodies only in the presence of PF4, with thrombus size reduced upon infusion of either ADAMTS13 or DNase I. In murine laser-injury cremaster arteriole/venule models, wild-type and human PF4-transgenic mice (with or without FcγRIIA) developed significantly more intense neutrophil rolling and more extensive thrombus formation than PF4-knockout mice, and three antigenically distinct anti-human-PF4 monoclonal antibodies blocked both thrombosis in vitro and neutrophil rolling/thrombosis in vivo[^2]. These data, together with last week's companion VITT/HIT procoagulant-response comparison[^4], collectively nominate PF4-targeted interventions (ADAMTS13, DNase I, or anti-PF4 monoclonal antibodies) as a plausible nonanticoagulant therapeutic class for APS, HIT, and VITT, pending clinical translation.

## XIII. Taiwan Clinical Context

The emerging BCMA CAR-T data in refractory ITP[^9] and the SRSF1-LCK splicing mechanism[^10] are of direct interest to NCKUH and other Taiwanese tertiary centers managing corticosteroid- and TPO-RA-refractory ITP, a population in which rituximab access remains constrained by NHI reimbursement criteria and for whom cellular therapy — if validated in larger cohorts — could eventually offer a drug-free remission pathway; close monitoring of trial expansion is warranted given Taiwan's established CAR-T manufacturing and administration infrastructure for B-cell malignancies. The iptacopan real-world safety data[^11], showing sustained hemoglobin normalization independent of bone marrow failure history and no thromboses over a median 238-day follow-up, are directly relevant to Taiwan's NHI-reimbursed PNH population, where iptacopan and eculizumab/ravulizumab access pathways coexist; the low breakthrough hemolysis rate (6.6%) and favorable infection profile should inform local switching algorithms from C5 inhibitors. The hexokinase-1 deficiency multi-omic characterization[^17] is relevant background for the differential diagnosis of hereditary nonspherocytic hemolytic anemia in Taiwan, where G6PD deficiency carrier rates are well characterized (approximately 3% in the general population, higher in certain southern Taiwan subpopulations) but rarer glycolytic enzymopathies such as HK1 and pyruvate kinase deficiency remain underdiagnosed due to limited access to specialized enzymatic and multi-omic testing; thalassemia carrier screening (alpha ~4–5%, beta ~1–2% in Taiwan) continues to warrant consideration of overlapping red cell enzymopathy phenotypes in atypical or transfusion-dependent cases that do not fully correlate with thalassemia genotype.

## XIV. Key Takeaways

The ITP field is undergoing a mechanistic and therapeutic inflection point, with cellular therapy (BCMA CAR-T) and splicing-factor biology (SRSF1-LCK) both pointing toward B-cell/T-cell-intrinsic drug-free remission strategies that could eventually displace chronic immunosuppression in refractory disease, though both lines of evidence currently rest on small human cohorts (4 patients) or murine models and require substantial validation. The PF4-centric reframing of antiphospholipid syndrome, now independently confirmed in its full primary dataset, is solidifying into a credible nonanticoagulant drug-development target spanning APS, HIT, and VITT. Real-world iptacopan data in 151 PNH patients provide reassuring long-term safety and efficacy signals that should support broader, confidence-based adoption outside pivotal trial settings, including in bone marrow failure-associated PNH. Finally, this week's pediatric aplastic anemia genetic-screening data offer a pragmatic counterpoint to calls for maximalist genetic testing in ITP, suggesting that screening thresholds should remain tailored to pretest clinical probability rather than applied uniformly across all cytopenic disorders.
