## Summary

Five signals dominate this week's literature. **(1)** The phase 3 CLL13/GAIA trial publishes its 5-year final analysis, demonstrating that GIV (venetoclax–obinutuzumab–ibrutinib) achieves a 5-year PFS of 81.3% versus 69.8% for GV, 57.4% for RV, and 50.7% for CIT — with GIV superior to GV (p = 0.0046) — while no overall survival differences have yet emerged across arms, establishing that triple therapy provides the deepest long-term disease control but at the cost of delayed quality-of-life benefit[^1]. **(2)** A *Blood* article from the EURO-SKI Biomarker Study provides comprehensive transcriptomic and immunologic characterization of treatment-free remission (TFR) in CML, identifying erythroid regulon activation (GATA1, KLF1, MYBL1) and preserved innate-adaptive immune communication as hallmarks of sustained TFR, and an FLT3 expression threshold as a predictor of TFR loss — data with direct implications for TKI discontinuation candidate selection[^2]. **(3)** The 10-year RELEVANCE trial analysis confirms that R² (rituximab–lenalidomide) produces comparable 10-year PFS (46.4% vs 46.6%) and OS (82.4% vs 81.1%) to immunochemotherapy in previously untreated follicular lymphoma, validating the chemotherapy-free approach as a definitive long-term alternative[^3]. **(4)** A *Blood Advances* subset analysis of QuANTUM-First demonstrates that quizartinib maintenance in FLT3-ITD AML patients who did not undergo allo-HCT confers a significant OS benefit (HR 0.401, 95% CI 0.192–0.838), even in those who were MRD-negative at maintenance onset (OS HR 0.194, 95% CI 0.056–0.676), suggesting possible de-escalation of transplant in selected MRD-negative patients[^4]. **(5)** A phase 2 study of anbalcabtagene autoleucel (anbal-cel), a dual PD-1/TIGIT knockdown CD19 CAR T-cell product, in R/R LBCL reports a CR rate of 67.1%, 18-month OS of 57.3%, and greater CAR T-cell expansion in responders — providing the first clinical data for this checkpoint-ablated CAR T construct in LBCL[^5].

## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

### Quizartinib Maintenance After Consolidation Without Allo-HCT: QuANTUM-First Subanalysis

A prespecified exploratory and post-hoc analysis of the QuANTUM-First trial published in *Blood Advances* this week interrogated the impact of quizartinib maintenance therapy on outcomes stratified by allo-HCT receipt and FLT3-ITD MRD status[^4]. Among patients who did not undergo allo-HCT, quizartinib maintenance was associated with a statistically significant OS benefit compared with placebo maintenance (HR 0.401, 95% CI 0.192–0.838). Crucially, this benefit extended to MRD-negative patients: among those who achieved FLT3-ITD MRD negativity at maintenance onset and did not receive allo-HCT, the OS HR was 0.194 (95% CI 0.056–0.676), and patients achieving MRD negativity after consolidation had 89.1% (95% CI 70.0–96.4) 3-year survival with quizartinib maintenance. Conversely, no OS benefit from quizartinib maintenance was demonstrable in the allo-HCT subgroup. Notably, 71% of quizartinib-treated patients versus 55% of placebo-treated patients who had undergone allo-HCT received maintenance therapy. These data provocatively suggest that for carefully selected FLT3-ITD AML patients who achieve MRD negativity after induction and consolidation with quizartinib, maintenance therapy may substitute for allo-HCT in some, though the post-hoc nature of this analysis and absence of statistical powering for these comparisons demand that this hypothesis be prospectively tested.

### METTL1/m7G Epitranscriptomic Axis as a Leukemia Stem Cell Target

A study in *Blood* this week identifies METTL1, the enzyme catalyzing N7-methylguanosine (m7G) modification on tRNA, as a critical regulator of leukemia stem cell (LSC) self-renewal and bone marrow homing in AML[^6]. Mechanistically, METTL1 promotes m7G modification on tRNA^Phe^GAA, supporting translation of transcripts enriched in Phe codons — most notably hematopoietic cell kinase (HCK), which sustains CXCR4 signaling required for LSC bone marrow homing. Loss of METTL1 reduced m7G abundance and steady-state tRNA^Phe^GAA levels, resulting in translational suppression of HCK, disrupted CXCR4 signaling, and impaired LSC function across multiple AML models. A small-molecule METTL1 inhibitor (NSC137443) identified through high-throughput screening reduced LSC frequency and delayed leukemogenesis in xenograft models, prolonging survival without evident toxicity to normal hematopoiesis. A companion editorial contextualizes METTL1 as a druggable epitranscriptomic dependency warranting clinical translation[^7].

### BRAF Mutations in AML: Clinical Landscape and Therapeutic Implications

A molecular epidemiology study in *Leukemia* provides the largest dedicated analysis of BRAF-mutated AML, identifying canonical and non-canonical BRAF mutations in approximately 1% of 5,779 consecutively annotated patients at two major US cancer centers (50/5,779; 21 newly diagnosed, 9 relapsed/refractory, 20 newly diagnosed secondary AML)[^8]. BRAF mutations were enriched in myelodysplasia-related AML (AML-MR), and most mutations occurred outside the classic V600 hotspot. Single-cell multiomic profiling revealed that BRAF mutations and co-occurring signaling mutations can reside within the same leukemic cell — a key divergence from RAS-mutant AML biology. Patients with BRAF-mutant AML had poor OS with currently available regimens including venetoclax-based therapy. Drug sensitivity data suggest that BRAF/MEK inhibitor strategies may offer therapeutic avenues, supporting further study of MAPK pathway targeting in this molecularly defined subset.

### FLT3-ITD Insertion Site Determines Metabolic and Autophagy Vulnerabilities

A mechanistic study in *Leukemia* demonstrates that the intracellular insertion site of FLT3-ITD (juxtamembrane vs. tyrosine kinase domain) determines autophagy response to midostaurin, mitochondrial metabolic architecture, and lipid dependency[^9]. Organelle proteomics revealed that midostaurin induces subcellular redistribution of approximately 2,500 proteins involved in cell cycle, autophagy, and metabolism. The ITD insertion site modulates organelle architecture and ATP production even at steady state. Most importantly, the investigators identified lipid restriction as a site-independent vulnerability: lipid restriction enhanced FLT3 trafficking to the plasma membrane and markedly reduced viability of midostaurin-resistant FLT3-ITD cells, identifying lipid-mediated compartmentalization control as a therapeutically actionable resistance mechanism.

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

### CD7-Directed BCL-XL ADC Overcomes Platelet Toxicity Barrier in T-ALL

A *Leukemia* study this week reports the preclinical development of ADC-CD7-BCL-XLi, a novel anti-CD7 monoclonal antibody conjugated to a BCL-XL-selective inhibitor, designed to circumvent the on-target thrombocytopenia that has prevented clinical deployment of systemic BCL-XL inhibitors in T-ALL[^10]. Adult T-ALL overall survival remains below 50% with current intensive chemotherapy, and BCL-XL dependence in T-ALL is well established. ADC-CD7-BCL-XLi efficiently killed most T-ALL cell lines and demonstrated potent anti-leukemic activity in patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models without platelet toxicity — a critical advantage over free BCL-XLi. Synergy with venetoclax (BCL-2 inhibitor) further prolonged remission and improved survival in PDX models, and the triple combination with chemotherapy produced cures in some animals. These data provide strong preclinical rationale for clinical evaluation, including as bridging therapy to HSCT.

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

### EURO-SKI Biomarker Study: Immune Architecture of Treatment-Free Remission

The EURO-SKI Biomarker Study, published in *Leukemia*, reports whole-transcriptome and NanoString nCounter analyses of peripheral blood from 240 CML patients on the final day of TKI intake, with the aim of identifying molecular predictors of sustained TFR versus molecular relapse[^2]. In the training cohort (n = 122) validated in an independent cohort (n = 118), patients who achieved sustained TFR displayed activation of GATA1, KLF1, and MYBL1 regulons characteristic of erythroid progenitor cell differentiation — a pattern shared with healthy controls but absent in patients who later lost TFR. Sustained TFR was further characterized by preserved intercellular communication between innate immunity (NK cells, dendritic cells) and adaptive immunity (CD8+, CD4+, γδ T cells), mirroring healthy control immune architecture. This intercellular communication was systematically disrupted at time of TFR loss. An FLT3 expression threshold identified two patient groups with significantly different TFR loss probability. These findings establish a multi-layered immune signature — erythroid progenitor regulon activation plus innate-adaptive immune connectivity — as the basis of durable TFR, and suggest that strategies reinforcing this immune communication could expand the pool of patients able to discontinue TKI therapy.

### Bosutinib 10-Year Final Analysis: Durable Second-Line Efficacy After Imatinib

The final ≥10-year analysis of a phase I/II study of second-line bosutinib in chronic phase CML after imatinib failure (n = 284) — the longest follow-up published for any TKI in this setting — is reported in *Haematologica*[^11]. The median follow-up was 53.7 months (range 0.5–171.6) and median treatment duration was 25.6 months. Cumulative rates of complete cytogenetic response, major molecular response, and MR4 were 49.6%, 42.1%, and 37.1%, respectively, with Kaplan–Meier probabilities of maintaining each response at year 11 of 58.3%, 56.1%, and 55.7%. At 11 years, cumulative on-treatment disease progression or death was 24.6%; 15 patients (5.3%) had transformed to accelerated or blast phase, of whom one did so after year 5. The 11-year OS rate was 70.5%. At the start of year 11, 19.4% of patients remained on bosutinib, and 52.7% of those still on therapy were receiving ≥500 mg once daily. No new safety signals emerged beyond year 5. These data confirm that bosutinib provides durable long-term disease control as a second-line TKI after imatinib and underpin its continued role in this setting.

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

### CLL13/GAIA Trial: Five-Year Final Analysis

The phase 3 CLL13/GAIA trial — a four-arm comparison of GIV (venetoclax–obinutuzumab–ibrutinib), GV (venetoclax–obinutuzumab), RV (venetoclax–rituximab), and CIT (FCR or BR) in fit, TP53-aberration-negative patients with untreated CLL — publishes its 5-year final analysis in *Blood* with a median observation time of 63.8 months[^1]. Among 926 randomized patients (GIV n = 231, GV n = 229, RV n = 237, CIT n = 229), 5-year PFS rates were 81.3% for GIV, 69.8% for GV, 57.4% for RV, and 50.7% for CIT. Both GV and GIV demonstrated superior PFS to CIT and RV (p < 0.001 for each), and GIV was superior to GV (p = 0.0046). Despite these PFS differences, no OS advantage was detected among any arm (5-year OS: GIV 94.3%, GV 93.6%, RV 94.7%, CIT 90.7%), almost certainly reflecting the efficacy of venetoclax-based re-treatment at progression (2-year treatment-free survival >80% from second-line for venetoclax-treated patients). Severe infection rates were highest with CIT, while cardiac events were most frequent with GIV — the latter attributable to the continuous ibrutinib component. Patients treated with GV or RV reported significantly greater and earlier quality-of-life improvements versus CIT, whereas GIV patients experienced clinically relevant QoL improvement only at month 15 following end of treatment, consistent with higher on-treatment symptom burden. A companion editorial contextualizes these data with respect to when MRD truly matters in treatment selection and sequencing[^12].

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

### TP53-Mutated MDS/AML Evolution to Complex Karyotype

A *Haematologica* cohort study examines the natural history of mono-allelic TP53-mutated MDS/AML, focusing on trajectories to complex karyotype disease — an event associated with especially poor outcomes[^13]. The study addresses an unmet need in understanding who requires pre-emptive intervention and when, particularly as TP53-directed agents enter earlier treatment lines. Clinicians should note that complex karyotype evolution appears to represent a critical transition point that alters therapeutic eligibility and prognosis in this already high-risk population.

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

### FAP-1 Deficiency and Myelofibrosis-Like Pathology: A New Mechanism

A *Blood* study from a Taiwan-affiliated research group reports that FAP-1 (Fas-associated phosphatase-1)-deficient mice develop megakaryocyte hyperplasia, defective platelet function, and myelofibrosis-like features with aging[^14]. Mechanistically, FAP-1 deficiency induces sustained Src kinase activation and cofilin inactivation, impairing the perinuclear actin remodeling required for demarcation membrane system (DMS) expansion in megakaryocytes, resulting in a pre-DMS arrest. Approximately half of aged FAP-1-deficient mice developed extramedullary hematopoiesis, hepatosplenomegaly, anemia, and thrombocytopenia; a subset progressed to frank fibrosis. Bone marrow transplantation demonstrated that these abnormalities are hematopoietic cell-intrinsic. In clinical validation, patients with primary myelofibrosis showed reduced FAP-1 expression associated with pre-DMS megakaryocyte accumulation, Src activation, and abnormal actin organization. Dasatinib attenuated FAP-1-deficiency-related defects in mice, linking this pathway to therapeutically relevant targets in MF. These findings identify a novel FAP-1→Src→cofilin→actin axis governing megakaryocyte maturation with potential translational relevance to MF pathobiology.

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

### Anbalcabtagene Autoleucel (PD-1/TIGIT Knockdown CD19 CAR-T) in R/R LBCL

A phase 1/2 study of anbalcabtagene autoleucel (anbal-cel), a CD19-directed CAR T-cell therapy engineered with dual PD-1 and TIGIT knockdown using CRISPR-based gene editing to enhance persistence and anti-tumor function, was published in *Blood*[^5]. In the phase 2 cohort, 79 patients with R/R LBCL received anbal-cel; 73 were evaluable for efficacy. The CR rate was 67.1% and PR rate 8.2%, for an ORR of 75.3%. Median PFS was 6.04 months (95% CI 4.34–16.46), with 6-, 12-, and 18-month PFS rates of 50.9%, 41.1%, and 35.2%, respectively. Median OS was not reached; 12- and 18-month OS rates were 66.6% and 57.3%. CAR T-cell expansion (Cmax) was significantly greater in responders versus non-responders (median 20,403 vs. 8,580 copies/μg). Patients achieving sustained CR at 6 months (long-term responders) showed reduced PD-1 and TIGIT expression on CAR-positive T cells — confirming that checkpoint downregulation on CAR T cells correlates with durability of response. Grade ≥3 AEs were nearly universal (97.5%), dominated by neutropenia (93.7%), thrombocytopenia (41.8%), and anemia (30.4%). CRS occurred in 57.0% (grade 3: 8.9%; no grade 4), and neurologic events in 13.9% (grade ≥3: 3.8%). Grade 5 infection occurred in 3 patients. These phase 2 data establish anbal-cel's CR rate and CAR T-expansion profile in LBCL and provide proof of concept for checkpoint knockdown engineering as a strategy to improve durability.

### EBV-Positive DLBCL: Convergent Immunosuppressive Mechanisms

A *Leukemia* mechanistic study characterizes the immunobiology of EBV-positive DLBCL (EBV+DLBCL) through integrated functional antiviral T-cell profiling and high-dimensional spatial proteomics[^15]. Both EBV+ and EBV-negative DLBCL patients exhibited broad impairments in antiviral T-cell responses — affecting latent and lytic EBV antigens as well as non-EBV viral targets — with deficits most pronounced in EBV+ patients. Spatial proteomics revealed a profoundly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment in EBV+DLBCL, characterized by relative loss of intratumoural CD8+ T cells, expansion of PD-1+ regulatory and exhausted T-cell populations, and dense aggregates of PD-L1+/IDO1+ macrophages around LMP1-expressing tumor cells. Compared with EBV+ classical Hodgkin lymphoma and infectious mononucleosis, EBV+DLBCL harbored the most marked macrophage-associated immunosuppressive signature and lowest T-cell density. These findings identify convergent systemic antiviral immune dysfunction and LMP1-driven suppressive myeloid niches as dual determinants of EBV+DLBCL pathogenesis, with direct implications for checkpoint inhibitor and LMP1-targeting strategies in this Asia-enriched subtype.

### DLBCL Genomic Classification: LySeqST Targeted Panel Validated

A *Blood Advances* study reports the design and validation of LySeqST, a targeted sequencing panel capturing all genomic features required for LymphGen classification of DLBCL[^16]. In silico and real-world validation versus whole-exome sequencing (WES) demonstrated accurate classification using both LymphGen and DLBclass systems. In an unselected population-based cohort, the panel confirmed that only tumors expressing the dark zone gene expression signature are consistently associated with inferior outcomes with R-CHOP. The development of a validated, clinically implementable panel for LymphGen subtyping is a prerequisite for the subtype-stratified clinical trials now in design, making LySeqST a potentially practice-enabling tool.

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

### RELEVANCE Trial: 10-Year Final Analysis

The 10-year final analysis of the multinational phase 3 RELEVANCE trial (NCT01476787/NCT01650701) — comparing R² (rituximab + lenalidomide, n = 513) to rituximab-based immunochemotherapy (R-Chemo, n = 517) in previously untreated advanced follicular lymphoma — is published in *Blood*[^3]. With 120 months of follow-up, median PFS was 110.6 months for R² versus 102.8 months for R-Chemo, with 10-year PFS rates of 46.4% and 46.6%, respectively — identical to the point estimate. Median OS and TTNLT were not reached in either arm; 10-year OS rates were 82.4% (R²) and 81.1% (R-Chemo), and 10-year TTNLT rates were 62.2% (R²) and 66.3% (R-Chemo). Patients with POD24 had substantially inferior prognosis (HR 6.215, p < 0.0001) but this was balanced between arms. The incidence of second primary malignancies was 2.11 per 100 patient-years (95% CI 1.80–2.46). Only 9 lymphoma transformations occurred after 24 months. The comparable decade-long outcomes confirm R² as a genuine chemotherapy-free alternative to immunochemotherapy in this population, with particular relevance for patients preferring to avoid cytotoxic therapy. A companion editorial contextualizes the long-term significance of these data[^17].

### Radiotherapy for Indolent Primary Cutaneous B-Cell Lymphoma: ILROG Multicenter Analysis

An international multicenter ILROG analysis in *Blood* evaluates outcomes after radiotherapy in 535 patients with limited-stage primary cutaneous marginal zone or follicle center lymphoma treated between 1995 and 2023[^18]. Complete responses were achieved in 91% at a median of 3.6 months after a median RT dose of 24 Gy. No statistically significant difference was observed between ≤4 Gy and >4 Gy for initial complete or overall response rates (p = 0.077 and p = 0.056), but low-dose ≤4 Gy was associated with significantly inferior 5-year local control (73% ± 12% vs 96% ± 2% for >4 Gy; p < 0.001). Higher doses did not translate into an OS benefit, and toxicities rarely exceeded grade 2, though they were more frequent with >4 Gy. These data support a nuanced approach: conventional-dose RT (>4 Gy) for patients in whom durable local control is paramount, and very low-dose RT (≤4 Gy) as a reasonable, lower-toxicity palliative approach in selected cases.

### Lenalidomide Maintenance After Chemoimmunotherapy in Consolidation-Ineligible PCNSL

A multicenter phase 2 trial in *Haematologica* assessed lenalidomide 15 mg/day (days 1–21 of 28-day cycles, up to 12 cycles) as maintenance therapy in 31 patients with primary CNS lymphoma (PCNSL) who achieved at least partial response after methotrexate- and rituximab-based induction but were ineligible for WBRT or ASCT (median age 72 years)[^19]. The primary endpoint, 2-year PFS from maintenance initiation, was 58.8% (95% CI 43.4–79.8%) — exceeding the pre-specified alternative threshold of 55%, though the one-sample log-rank test did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.084). Two-year OS was 83.9%. Grade 3–4 neutropenia occurred in 53.3%; non-hematologic toxicities were predominantly grade 1–2. Treatment was discontinued for toxicity in 13%. One grade 5 pneumonia occurred and no thromboembolic events were observed. While hypothesis-generating and limited by the single-arm design, these data provide a framework for lenalidomide maintenance as consolidation strategy in elderly/unfit PCNSL patients, warranting randomized evaluation.

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

### Gonadal Function After Nivolumab-AVD in cHL: GHSG NIVAHL Data

A *Blood* report from the phase 2 GHSG NIVAHL trial (NCT03004833) characterizes gonadal function in 82 patients with classic Hodgkin lymphoma treated with frontline 4×N-AVD (nivolumab + doxorubicin, vinblastine, dacarbazine)[^20]. Stable gonadal hormone levels were observed up to 24 months after N-AVD, suggesting that first-line checkpoint inhibitor-based chemoimmunotherapy preserves gonadal function and fertility potential — an important consideration for young patients making treatment decisions when weighing N-AVD against BV-AVD.

### Early-Stage Peripheral T-Cell Lymphoma: Outcomes by Stage and Treatment

A multicenter retrospective cohort study in *Haematologica* examined 132 patients with stage I–II nodal T-cell lymphoma (PTCL-NOS, nodal TFHL, ALK-negative ALCL) treated from 2001–2022[^21]. For the entire cohort, 5-year PFS and OS were 56% and 68%, respectively. Stage I disease showed particularly favorable outcomes: 5-year PFS 66% and OS 79%. Among stage I patients who responded to chemotherapy and received chemoradiation, outcomes were especially encouraging (5-year PFS 70%, OS 86%). Stage II patients had inferior outcomes compared with stage I regardless of treatment strategy. These data provide the most comprehensive description yet of outcomes in early-stage nodal PTCL, supporting chemoradiation as an excellent consolidation approach for stage I disease. ASCT was received by 14% of patients and appeared to add limited benefit over chemoradiation in stage I.

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

### AL Amyloidosis Stage IIIB: EMN22 Phase 2 Trial of Daratumumab Monotherapy

The prospective phase 2 EMN22 trial reports daratumumab monotherapy in 40 previously untreated patients with Mayo 2004/European cardiac stage IIIB AL amyloidosis — a population with extremely limited treatment options and historically dismal outcomes[^22]. The primary endpoint, 6-month OS rate, was 65.0% (95% CI 48.2–77.6), with median OS of 10.4 months. The best hematologic response rate (partial response or better) up to 6 months was 75.0%, with very good partial response or better in 47.5% and CR in 12.5%. Median time to first hematologic response was one week and to best response was 2.3 months. Cardiac response rate at 6 months was 30.0%. Ten patients (25.0%) received additional bortezomib and dexamethasone after cycle 3 for inadequate response. Common serious AEs were cardiac failure (25.0%), sudden cardiac death (10.0%), and acute kidney injury (7.5%). Quality of life remained stable throughout treatment. Compared with historical cohorts, these data support daratumumab as a feasible and effective backbone for anti-clonal therapy in this ultra-high-risk population.

### Teclistamab Resistance in MajesTEC-1: Longitudinal Immune Profiling

A *Haematologica* correlative study from the MajesTEC-1 trial characterizes longitudinal peripheral blood and tumor microenvironment immune dynamics associated with teclistamab response, resistance, and relapse in triple-class-exposed RRMM[^23]. Responders exhibited greater T-cell margination, recovery, and activation in the periphery compared with non-responders. Non-responders showed elevated and sustained checkpoint marker expression on both CD4+ and CD8+ T cells — indicative of chronic T-cell activation without productive response — and persistently elevated immunosuppressive regulatory T-cell proportions. At relapse, bone marrow and peripheral T cells showed increased expression of dysfunction markers (CD39, CD57) and higher regulatory T-cell proportions relative to baseline, alongside a reduction in BCMA receptor density on tumor plasma cells. These mechanistic data identify dual immune exhaustion and BCMA antigen modulation as convergent escape mechanisms, providing a rationale for combinations with immune-restorative agents and earlier deployment of teclistamab before T-cell exhaustion is established.

### CBP/p300 Degradation in t(4;14) Multiple Myeloma

A *Leukemia* study reports that CBPD-409, a selective PROTAC-mediated degrader of CBP/p300, demonstrates robust anti-myeloma activity in t(4;14) MM models — directly relevant to the ~10–15% of MM patients harboring this translocation[^24]. The study links NSD2 overexpression in t(4;14) MM to H3K36me2-mediated antagonism of H3K27me3 at distal regulatory elements, creating a chromatin landscape permissive for oncogenic transcription factor expression. CBP/p300 degradation selectively disrupts this permissive chromatin state and drives anti-myeloma lethality, identifying a synthetic vulnerability between NSD2 overexpression and CBP/p300 dependency in this molecularly defined subgroup.

### Solitary Bone Plasmacytoma with Marrow Involvement: High-Risk Progression

A multicenter *Blood Advances* study of 141 SBP and 102 SBPmm patients demonstrates that SBPmm is a substantially higher-risk entity than SBP: 92% of SBPmm patients progressed to MM versus 76% of SBP (p = 0.01), with a median time to progression of 15 months (95% CI 14–21) for SBPmm versus 43 months (95% CI 34–55) for SBP (p < 0.0001)[^25]. Two-thirds of SBPmm patients progressed within 2 years of diagnosis. Del(17p) and 1q+ were associated with particularly rapid progression in SBPmm, with 2-year progression rates of 100% and 82%, respectively. Multifocal lytic bone lesions were the dominant myeloma-defining event in both groups. These findings strongly support risk-stratified management of SBPmm, with prospective trials of frontline systemic therapy warranted in this population.

## XII. Transplant & Cellular Therapy (CAR-T / HSCT / GVHD)

### HLA-E Single-Chain Trimer for NK Cell Evasion in Allogeneic CAR T Cells

A *Blood Advances* study presents a refined strategy for allogeneic CAR T-cell engineering that addresses the critical barrier of NK cell-mediated rejection caused by MHC-I deletion (missing-self response)[^26]. The investigators demonstrated that HLA-E presentation of the signal peptide VMAPRTLIL (HLA-ESP-1C) confers resistance to NKG2A+ NK cell populations without activating NKG2C+ populations. Combined with CD54 and CD58 ablation on UCART cells, HLA-ESP-1C expression provided optimal resistance to heterogeneous NKG2A+/C+ NK cell populations. In a humanized mouse model reconstituted with allogeneic NK cells, UCART19 cells constructed with this approach promoted stringent tumor control, matching autologous CAR T-cell performance. This work represents a meaningful advance toward scalable, off-the-shelf CAR T products.
