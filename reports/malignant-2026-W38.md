## Summary

This cycle brings a dense *Blood* issue on FLT3/BCL2-inhibitor resistance biology in AML, mature first-line data for ibrutinib-venetoclax in TP53-mutant/older mantle cell lymphoma, five-year survival for liso-cel in TRANSCEND NHL 001, and a negative pediatric AML phase III readout for CPX-351. **(1)** In the phase 3 SYMPATICO open-label cohort, first-line ibrutinib plus venetoclax in nonblastoid MCL patients ≥65 years or with TP53 mutations achieved a 69% CR rate and 95% overall response rate, with median PFS of 40.2 months and 3-year OS of 79%, though outcomes remained markedly worse in the TP53-mutant subgroup (median PFS 22.0 months in older patients, 15.4 months in younger patients)[^1]. **(2)** Five-year follow-up of TRANSCEND NHL 001 confirms durable curative potential for lisocabtagene maraleucel in relapsed/refractory large B-cell lymphoma, with estimated 5-year overall survival of 38% (leukapheresed set 33%) and 5-year disease-specific survival of 52%, rising to 78% and 92%, respectively, among long-term-follow-up survivors alive at study completion[^2]. **(3)** Single-cell multiomic profiling of the venetoclax-gilteritinib phase 1b trial in FLT3-mutant AML identifies RAS-pathway activation — via mutant clone selection, nonmutational transcriptional upregulation, or monocytic differentiation — as the convergent resistance mechanism to FLT3/BCL2 inhibitor doublets, nominating RAS-pathway inhibition as a resensitization strategy[^3]. **(4)** The Children's Oncology Group phase III AAML1831 trial was stopped early for futility after CPX-351 induction showed inferior 2-year event-free survival compared with standard daunorubicin/cytarabine in pediatric FLT3 wild-type AML (51.2% vs 62.2%; p=0.011), driven by significantly worse disease-free survival and higher relapse incidence in low-risk patients (2-year DFS 57.5% vs 73.8%; p=0.001)[^4]. **(5)** The TRIANGLE trial secondary analysis shows rituximab maintenance added to ibrutinib-containing therapy (with or without ASCT) significantly prolongs PFS in younger, treatment-naïve MCL patients (4-year PFS 85% vs 73% without ASCT, p=0.003; 90% vs 75% with ASCT, p<0.001), at the cost of higher grade 3–5 infectious toxicity[^5].

## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

This week's *Blood* issue is anchored by a companion pair of papers dissecting resistance to FLT3/BCL2 inhibitor combinations. Single-cell DNA/protein and RNA/protein multiomic profiling of patients treated on the phase 1b venetoclax-gilteritinib trial (NCT03625505) demonstrates that although the doublet effectively eliminates FLT3-mutant clones, relapse is driven by convergent RAS-pathway activation achieved through multiple independent routes — selection of pre-existing RAS-mutant subclones, nonmutational upregulation of RAS transcriptional programs, and a shift toward RAS-associated monocytic AML differentiation — and that RAS-pathway inhibition resensitizes resistant cells to the doublet in vitro[^3]. An accompanying editorial frames this as evidence that RAS signaling functionally "seals fate" in AML besieged by dual FLT3/BCL2 blockade, reinforcing the case articulated in prior weeks for triplet or RAS-directed combination strategies rather than doublets alone[^6].

Metabolic vulnerability data add a further axis to venetoclax-resistance biology. A *Blood* study identifies isocitrate dehydrogenase 3 (IDH3), specifically its catalytic IDH3A subunit, as a TCA-cycle enzyme selectively required for leukemic stem cell (LSC) maintenance relative to healthy HSPCs; IDH3A knockdown reduces TCA flux, causes intracellular citrate accumulation, activates AMPK, suppresses mTORC1, and enhances venetoclax sensitivity in vitro and in vivo, while LSCs from patients clinically resistant to venetoclax-azacitidine show transcriptomic signatures of active TCA cycle and glycolysis — nominating the IDH3A-citrate axis as a therapeutic target to overcome Ven/Aza resistance[^7].

On the clinical trial front, the Children's Oncology Group phase III AAML1831 trial delivers a cautionary, disease-context-specific result. In 721 eligible patients with newly diagnosed FLT3 wild-type pediatric AML randomized to CPX-351 versus standard daunorubicin/cytarabine (DA) induction (both arms also receiving gemtuzumab ozogamicin in induction 1), a protocol-specified interim analysis triggered the futility rule and stopped randomization: 2-year event-free survival from study entry was 62.2% for DA versus 51.2% for CPX-351 (p=0.011). The inferiority was concentrated in the low-risk stratum, where 2-year disease-free survival from end-of-induction-1 was 73.8% (DA) versus 57.5% (CPX-351; p=0.001) and cumulative incidence of relapse was 23.6% versus 39.9% (p=0.001), whereas outcomes were comparable in high-risk patients proceeding to allogeneic HSCT[^4]. This contrasts with CPX-351's established benefit in adult secondary/therapy-related AML and underscores that liposomal daunorubicin/cytarabine's advantage does not generalize to de novo pediatric FLT3-wild-type disease, a point reinforced by an accompanying editorial noting the "unexpected" lack of efficacy in this population[^8].

A French DATAML-IPC-AURAML consortium real-world comparison of salvage regimens in 317 patients with AML in first relapse after intensive chemotherapy found no significant difference in CR2 rate between intermediate/high-dose cytarabine-based salvage (66.0%) and venetoclax-azacitidine (66.7%; p=0.96), nor in relapse-free survival (13.2 vs 13.7 months) or overall survival (14.5 vs 12.3 months; p=0.41). Multivariable analysis suggested a nuanced age-cytogenetic interaction: venetoclax-azacitidine favored OS in relapsing patients ≥60 years with low/intermediate-risk cytogenetics (p=0.002), while cytarabine-based salvage was superior for younger patients with poor-risk cytogenetics (p=0.002), supporting biomarker-guided salvage selection pending randomized confirmation[^9].

A CD38xCD3 bispecific T-cell engager, XmAb18968, was evaluated in a phase 1 trial of 22 heavily pretreated patients with relapsed/refractory AML (n=13) and T-ALL (n=9), most previously exposed to venetoclax (77.3%). No grade ≥3 cytokine release syndrome or neurotoxicity occurred; among 17 evaluable patients, 2 of 11 AML patients achieved MRD-negative CR and 1 achieved PR, while 4 of 6 T-ALL patients had meaningful disease-burden reduction with 1 MRD clearance. Median OS was 8.5 months (AML) and 8.7 months (T-ALL), supporting further investigation of CD38-directed bispecific engagement in this refractory setting[^10].

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

A retrospective analysis of 636 adults with ALL undergoing first allogeneic HCT at two US centers (2011–2021) found 1- and 3-year cumulative incidences of post-transplant CNS relapse of only 3% and 6%, respectively. A composite low-risk profile — absence of pre-HCT CNS involvement, pre-HCT MRD negativity, and total body irradiation-based conditioning — was associated with markedly reduced CNS relapse risk (HR 0.31, 95% CI 0.15–0.68; p=0.0032), while post-HCT CNS relapse itself carried a striking mortality signal (HR 7.33, 95% CI 5.12–10.49; p<0.0001), raising the question of whether low-risk patients could safely de-escalate CNS-directed prophylaxis[^11]. Separately, a German retrospective series of 101 adults with ALL undergoing first allo-HSCT after TBI-based conditioning found that 12 Gy (vs 8 Gy) total body irradiation was associated with significantly increased non-relapse mortality (subdistribution HR 5.97, 95% CI 1.77–20.15; p=0.004) without a compensatory reduction in relapse incidence, supporting prospective evaluation of reduced-dose TBI regimens in this population[^12].

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

Five-year follow-up data from two Japan Adult Leukemia Study Group phase 2 trials (N-STOP216, nilotinib, n=51; D-STOP216, dasatinib, n=49) refine the treatment-free remission (TFR) landscape after frontline second-generation TKI discontinuation. Both trials met their primary endpoints (12-month TFR 76.5% nilotinib vs 55.1% dasatinib), and 5-year treatment-free survival rates were 68.6% (nilotinib) and 50.9% (dasatinib), with no disease progression observed in either cohort. Immune profiling revealed a divergent "immune paradox" in the dasatinib cohort — expansion of effector memory T cells and mature CD57+CD56dim NK cells alongside depletion of naïve/central memory "immune reservoir" populations — while elevated pre-discontinuation IL-1β and IL-6 were associated with molecular relapse across both trials, suggesting 2G-TKI-specific immunomodulatory off-target effects influence TFR durability[^13].

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

A University of Washington/Fred Hutchinson retrospective study of 339 patients with CLL/SLL receiving modern frontline therapy (continuous BTKi n=179, bendamustine-rituximab n=66, time-limited BCL2i n=94) examined the clinical significance of progression defined solely by absolute lymphocyte count increase (ALC PD) versus other progression modalities. ALC-only progression was more frequent with bendamustine-rituximab than BTKi or BCL2i (p=0.021) and carried a substantially better prognosis: median OS from progression was 116 months for ALC PD versus 71 months for other PD (HR 3.15, 95% CI 1.52–6.53; p=0.0012), with 5-year OS rates of 83% versus 56%. Time to next treatment or death was also longer for ALC PD (median 14 vs 2.2 months; p<0.0001), suggesting future response criteria may benefit from reporting ALC-only progression as a distinct, more favorable category[^14].

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

An international cohort of 682 patients with isolated deletion 5q MDS was used to derive a disease-specific prognostic tool, IPSS-del(5q), after demonstrating that existing IPSS-R and IPSS-M systems show only modest discriminatory ability in this entity (concordance indices ≈0.5 for both). Independent adverse factors included age ≥70 years, male sex, hemoglobin ≤10 g/dL, platelet count ≤100×10⁹/L, one additional chromosomal abnormality, ≥2 gene mutations, SF3B1 mutation, and high-risk TP53 status; the resulting six-variable model stratified patients into standard-risk (74.3%) and high-risk (25.7%) groups with significantly different leukemia-free survival (69.2 vs 32.0 months; p<0.01), reclassifying 19.1% of IPSS-R lower-risk and 14.6% of IPSS-M lower-risk patients into high risk, albeit with only modest discrimination (C-index 0.60)[^15]. In a separate nationwide Japanese study of 1767 adult MDS patients undergoing single-unit cord blood transplantation, cord blood unit CD34+ cell dose and colony-forming unit-granulocyte/macrophage dose were prognostically important for disease-free survival only in high-risk MDS, not low-risk MDS, supporting a risk-adapted approach to cord blood unit selection[^16].

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

Mechanistic work in a transgenic PV mouse model identifies NK cells as the primary immune effector mediating interferon-alfa's therapeutic efficacy: IFN-α expands CD27+ NK cells in bone marrow, and NK cell depletion abrogates the antileukemic effect, while in patients with PV or ET undergoing IFN-α therapy, CD56bright NK cell frequency correlates with molecular response; NK cells preferentially kill JAK2V617F-mutant HSPCs via TNF-α rather than IFN-γ or NKG2D, and mice lacking type-I interferon receptor on NK cells show accelerated disease progression even without exogenous IFN-α[^17]. In a Chinese multicenter cohort of 1728 patients with essential thrombocythemia, adolescents and young adults (15–39 years, n=328) showed a distinct clinicomolecular profile — enriched for CALR mutations, lower non-driver mutation burden (TET2, DNMT3A, ASXL1, SH2B3), fewer cardiovascular risk factors, and superior overall, myelofibrosis-free, and leukemia-free survival compared with non-AYA patients (≥40 years), although CALR mutations in this younger group showed an age-specific association with progression to post-ET myelofibrosis[^18]. An Italian study of 664 ET/PV patients found that each 1 m/s increase in estimated pulse wave velocity, a surrogate of arterial stiffness, was independently associated with major adverse cardiovascular events (HR 1.16, 95% CI 1.02–1.32; p=0.025), with 72.1% of the cohort meeting criteria for early vascular ageing[^19].

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

A prospective phase 2 study of selinexor combined with R-CHOP (S-RCHOP) as first-line therapy for newly diagnosed double-hit/triple-hit lymphoma enrolled 13 patients (9 DHL, 4 THL) and achieved a 100% overall response rate (76.9% CR); at median follow-up of 25.4 months, 2-year PFS and OS were 67.7% and 67.1%, respectively, with only one CNS relapse observed. Post-treatment circulating cell-free DNA negativity, achieved in 72.7% of patients, correlated with PET-CT-confirmed CR, and BCL6/IGLL5 mutations were frequently detected on NGS, though hematologic toxicity (leukopenia/neutropenia 55.1%) was substantial[^20]. A single-cell RNA-seq/BCR-seq/TCR-seq atlas of 63 large B-cell lymphoma biopsies identified five conserved malignant transcriptional archetypes, with high abundance of a memory B-cell/quiescence-marked "Archetype 4" independently correlating with poor event-free survival after standard immunochemotherapy, validated by bulk RNA-seq deconvolution in independent cohorts — offering a novel cellular-state-based stratification framework for DLBCL[^21].

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

The phase III SYMPATICO trial's open-label cohort provides the most mature first-line data yet for ibrutinib plus venetoclax in nonblastoid MCL patients aged ≥65 years or with TP53 mutations. Among 78 enrolled patients (65 ≥65 years, 11 with TP53 mutation) treated with 2 years of combination therapy followed by ibrutinib maintenance, the overall CR rate was 69% (95% CI, 58–79) and ORR was 95% (95% CI, 87–99), with median duration of response 37.1 months and median PFS 40.2 months (95% CI, 29.4–NE); 3-year OS was 79% (95% CI, 68–86). Outcomes diverged sharply by TP53 status: in patients ≥65 years, CR rates were 76% without TP53 mutation versus 44% with mutation, mPFS 40.2 versus 22.0 months, and 3-year OS 85% versus 66%; adult patients <65 years with TP53 mutation had a 73% CR rate but mPFS of only 15.4 months and 3-year OS of 73%[^1]. An accompanying editorial contextualizes this as grounds for "sympathy" for the TP53-mutant subgroup, whose outcomes, while improved relative to chemoimmunotherapy historical controls, remain markedly inferior[^22].

Building on the same MCL population, the TRIANGLE trial's secondary analysis of rituximab maintenance shows that among responders to ibrutinib-containing induction (with or without ASCT), rituximab maintenance significantly prolonged PFS regardless of transplant use: 4-year PFS was 85% with maintenance versus 73% without in the non-transplant arm (p=0.003) and 90% versus 75% in the transplant arm (p<0.001), with trends toward improved OS, though at the cost of markedly higher grade 3–5 infectious toxicity (34% vs 11% non-transplant arm; 41% vs 18% transplant arm)[^5].

In indolent B-cell lymphoma, a chemotherapy-free sequential strategy using the CD3/CD20 bispecific mosunetuzumab followed by response-adapted polatuzumab vedotin/obinutuzumab was tested in 42 previously untreated FL/MZL patients (88% FL). End-of-treatment ORR and CR rates were 100% and 86%, respectively, with mosunetuzumab alone achieving 100% ORR/71% CR; at median follow-up of 34 months, 2-year PFS was 89% (95% CI, 80–100) and 2-year OS was 100%. Cytokine release syndrome occurred in 64% of patients but was uniformly grade 1, with no tocilizumab required[^23]. In relapsed/refractory FL, the phase III RENOIR trial did not meet its primary endpoint: rituximab plus lenalidomide (R2) maintenance versus rituximab alone in older patients showed 2-year PFS of 73% versus 64%, a numerical but not statistically significant benefit, though an unplanned subgroup analysis suggested a substantial benefit in patients <70 years (R2 96% vs R 69%)[^24].

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

A mechanistic *Blood* study identifies the N6-methyladenosine reader IGF2BP2 as a clinically relevant oncogenic driver in peripheral T-cell lymphoma, showing consistently high expression across common nodal PTCL subtypes in three external cohorts and an internal RNA-seq dataset of 196 newly diagnosed patients; IGF2BP2 stabilizes endosome-related transcripts (RAB4, VPS35, RAB9, STAM), promoting tumor proliferation and suppressing CD8+ T-cell-mediated cytotoxicity, and therapeutic targeting with the small molecule CWI1-2 suppressed endocytosis and tumor growth in patient-derived xenograft models — providing rationale for RNA-modification-based dual tumor/immune-targeting strategies in PTCL[^25].

## X. NK/T-cell Lymphoma (Asia focus)

A British Journal of Haematology study reports that immune checkpoint inhibitor-containing salvage therapy improves outcomes in patients with relapsed or progressive NK/T-cell lymphoma (NKTCL) after first-line treatment failure, an outcome of particular relevance given the higher prevalence of extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma in East Asian populations, including Taiwan[^26].

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

Two companion *Blood* papers this week nominate PKMYT1 as a novel therapeutic target in del(17p)/TP53-deficient multiple myeloma. Integrating RNA sequencing from patient MM cells with genetic dependency data from cell lines identified PKMYT1 as selectively required in del(17p) MM cells; the selective inhibitor RP-6306 triggered DNA damage accumulation, micronucleus formation, and mitotic catastrophe preferentially in del(17p) cells while sparing del(17p)-negative MM cells and healthy tissue, reducing tumor burden and extending survival in xenograft and TP53-deficient syngeneic models[^27], with an accompanying editorial characterizing PKMYT1 as an "Achilles' heel" in this genomically defined high-risk subgroup[^28].

Refining risk stratification further, an analysis of the 2025 IMS-IMWG consensus genomic staging (CGS) system in 631 patients from the NICHE cohort found that circulating tumor cells (CTC), detectable in 63.9% of 410 newly diagnosed patients, refine prognostic stratification specifically within the CGS high-risk subgroup: patients with CGS high-risk/CTC-high disease had the shortest PFS, defining a disseminated genomic high-risk phenotype in 12.4% of evaluable patients, with a CTC threshold of 0.38% independently predicting inferior PFS after multivariable adjustment[^29]. Spatial transcriptomics of extramedullary myeloma biopsies revealed a suppressive, T-cell-excluded tumor microenvironment dominated by M2-polarized macrophages and exhausted (LAG3+/TIGIT+) CD8+ T cells, with three recurrent immune niches (excluded, suppressed, permissive) identified via cell-cell interaction modeling showing plasma cells signaling through PGE2/VEGFB while receiving prosurvival signals via CD38, CXCR4, and BCMA[^30]. On the CAR-T durability front, single-cell RNA sequencing of 40 idecabtagene vicleucel infusion products (184,398 cells) identified an NF-κB-driven transcriptional program in CD4 CAR-T cells — characterized by prosurvival circuitry, tonic/chemokine signaling, and elevated CAR transgene expression — as an intrinsic hallmark of durable response independent of baseline clinical characteristics, with pharmacologic NF-κB inhibition abrogating CAR-T cytotoxicity and cytokine production in vitro[^31].

A real-world VA health system study of 20,135 newly diagnosed MM patients (2000–2024) found that survival has improved across successive treatment eras, but lower socioeconomic status (by area deprivation index) was independently associated with increased mortality despite the VA's equal-access structure, underscoring persistent structural barriers to optimal MM care even where financial cost is not a limiting factor[^32].

## XII. Transplant & Cellular Therapy (CAR-T / HSCT / GVHD)

Five-year survival data from TRANSCEND NHL 001 provide the most mature curative-intent evidence yet for lisocabtagene maraleucel in relapsed/refractory large B-cell lymphoma. Among 270 treated patients (257 efficacy-evaluable), median OS was 27.5 months (95% CI, 16.2–47.3) with estimated 5-year OS of 38% (95% CI, 32–45); median disease-specific survival was 67.8 months with 5-year DSS of 52%. Among the subset of long-term-follow-up survivors alive at study completion (n=84), 5-year OS and DSS reached 78% and 92%, respectively, and most deaths occurred within 2 years of infusion with no new late safety signals, including low rates of late severe infection and second primary malignancy[^2]. An accompanying editorial, "Time Will Tell," reflects on the durability signal these five-year data provide for CAR-T as potentially curative therapy[^33].

A Cell Therapy Consortium analysis of 444 patients with relapsed/refractory LBCL treated with CD19 CAR-T across 8 academic centers characterizes late cytopenia (≥30 days post-infusion) as a common, clinically consequential toxicity: grade ≥3 cytopenias were observed in 47%, 34%, 19%, 21%, and 11% of patients at 1, 2, 3, 6, and 12 months, respectively, and late cytopenia was associated with higher 1-year non-relapse mortality (11% vs 4.4%; p=0.038), worse 2-year PFS (38% vs 67%; p=0.035), and worse 2-year OS (60% vs 76%; p=0.016); a high CAR-HEMATOTOX score (≥2) predicted cytopenia at 3 months, while bridging chemotherapy and axicabtagene ciloleucel use predicted cytopenia at 6 months[^34]. Complementary data from a 176-patient axi-cel cohort demonstrate that robust day-7 CAR-T expansion (≥48 cells/μL), combined with pre-lymphodepletion LDH and bridging response in a multivariable model, was independently associated with improved PFS (HR 0.43, 95% CI 0.22–0.83; p=0.01), though at the cost of increased CRS severity and ICANS incidence, supporting dynamic post-infusion risk stratification[^35].

In the GVHD space, a review of CD4+CD25+FOXP3+ regulatory T cells in allogeneic HCT — timed to the 2025 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine recognizing foundational immune tolerance mechanisms — surveys the translational path from preclinical models to clinical Treg-based GVHD prophylaxis strategies[^36]. A Japanese study of 118 patients undergoing first allo-HSCT found that a high serum thioredoxin level at day 0 was independently associated with reduced risk of acute GVHD (HR 0.48; p=0.031), with a validation cohort of 47 patients showing a consistent, if not statistically significant, trend, suggesting a role for TXN in predictive modeling of acute GVHD[^37].

## XIV. Taiwan Clinical Context

The maturing SYMPATICO and TRIANGLE data on ibrutinib-venetoclax combinations and rituximab maintenance in MCL are directly actionable in Taiwan, where ibrutinib and venetoclax are both NHI-reimbursed for relapsed/refractory MCL and CLL indications; extension of reimbursement discussions to first-line use in elderly or TP53-mutant patients, as supported by this week's data, would be a natural next step for NHI policy review, particularly given Taiwan's aging population and the transplant-ineligible subset commonly seen at CGMH and NTUH lymphoma clinics. The NK/T-cell lymphoma checkpoint-inhibitor salvage data are of outsized relevance locally, as extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, nasal type, remains disproportionately prevalent in Taiwan and East Asia relative to Western populations; incorporation of ICI-containing salvage regimens into local NKTCL relapse algorithms — building on existing experience with asparaginase-based regimens at CMUH and VGH — merits prospective evaluation in Taiwanese cohorts. Liso-cel and axi-cel CAR-T programs, both available under Taiwan's managed-access CAR-T pathways at major transplant centers, should incorporate the late-cytopenia monitoring framework and CAR-HEMATOTOX risk stratification highlighted this week to guide post-infusion surveillance protocols locally.

## XV. Key Takeaways

The week's dominant theme is convergence: multiple independent datasets (single-cell resistance profiling in AML, PKMYT1 vulnerability in myeloma, five-year CAR-T survival in lymphoma) point toward genomically and immunologically defined subgroups — TP53-mutant, del(17p), RAS-activated — as the loci where next-generation combination or targeted strategies must concentrate. The negative AAML1831 result is a reminder that positive adult AML data (CPX-351 in secondary/therapy-related disease) do not automatically transfer across age and molecular subgroups, reinforcing the need for population-specific pediatric trials rather than extrapolation. Finally, the durable TRANSCEND five-year OS/DSS data and the TRIANGLE rituximab-maintenance PFS benefit both support continued escalation of long-term follow-up as a standard expectation for cellular and BTKi-based regimens in aggressive and indolent lymphomas alike.
