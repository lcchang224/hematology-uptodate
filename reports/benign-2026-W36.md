## Summary

This week delivers a landmark guideline publication alongside substantive new data in ITP mechanism, hemophilia gene therapy, sickle cell disease, and coagulation factor pharmacology. First and most significant, the American Society of Hematology has published its **2026 clinical practice guidelines for severe and very severe acquired aplastic anemia**, comprising 33 recommendations and 4 good practice statements developed via GRADE methodology; the panel endorses incorporating eltrombopag into first-line immunosuppressive regimens and prioritizes hematopoietic cell transplantation for younger patients with an available matched sibling or unrelated donor, while explicitly noting that certainty of evidence was low or very low for most recommendations due to reliance on small, nonrandomized studies[^1]. Second, the phase 3 HOPE Kids 2 trial of voxelotor in children with sickle cell disease and conditional cerebral blood flow velocities met its primary endpoint, with a significantly greater reduction in CBFV versus placebo (least-squares mean difference −7.77 cm/s, 95% CI −13.18 to −2.37, p=0.0048) sustained through Week 48, but the program was terminated in September 2024 after an imbalance of deaths (8 vs 2) emerged, underscoring a cautionary signal that tempers the efficacy finding[^2]. Third, a *Blood Advances* preclinical study demonstrates that fusing an extended half-life albumin domain to hyperactive factor IX-Padua (FIX-R338L-ALB[QMP]) in an AAV gene therapy construct produces a sustained fourfold increase in FIX antigen and threefold increase in activity compared to standard FIX-R338L vectors in hemophilia B mice, achieved without increased immunogenicity, liver toxicity, or thrombogenic risk — a potential strategy to reduce vector dose requirements in clinical gene therapy[^3]. Fourth, a *Haematologica* study in refractory ITP reveals that non-responders to daratumumab exhibit interleukin-17-associated inflammation alongside distinct B-cell remodeling patterns, adding a second mechanistic layer (beyond last week's romiplostim/anergic B-cell data) to the emerging picture of B-cell-directed therapy resistance in ITP[^4]. Fifth, an *AJH* review article makes the case for BAFF/BAFF-R pathway targeting in both ITP and warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia, framing elevated BAFF levels as a shared mechanistic driver of durable non-response in these historically B-cell-mediated autoimmune cytopenias and outlining a rationale for emerging BAFF-directed agents[^5].

## I. Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP)

Two complementary reports this week extend the mechanistic ITP narrative beyond last week's romiplostim/anergic B-cell data. A *Haematologica* study of daratumumab (anti-CD38) in refractory ITP identifies interleukin-17-associated inflammation as a distinguishing feature of non-responders, alongside characteristic B-cell remodeling patterns following treatment[^4]; while granular response-rate figures were not available in the abstract, this establishes IL-17 pathway activity as a candidate resistance biomarker complementing the anergic B-cell expansion signature reported last week for romiplostim. Separately, a comprehensive *AJH* review by Mahévas, Cooper, and colleagues articulates the biological rationale for targeting BAFF and its receptor BAFF-R in both ITP and warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia (wAIHA), noting that BAFF binding to BAFF-R drives B-cell differentiation, proliferation, and survival, and that BAFF levels are elevated in both conditions[^5]. The review frames the persistent lack of durable remission after treatment discontinuation — even with agents achieving high initial response rates — as an unmet need directly addressable by BAFF/BAFF-R pathway inhibition, positioning this axis as a unifying therapeutic target across autoimmune cytopenias. Separately, an *AJH* cross-sectional study documents cognitive impairment, depressive symptoms, and cerebral microbleeds in patients with ITP, though quantitative comparisons with controls were not detailed in the available abstract, flagging an under-recognized neuropsychiatric and cerebrovascular comorbidity burden warranting further prospective characterization[^6].

## III. Aplastic Anemia

The dominant development this week is the publication of the **ASH 2026 clinical practice guideline** for severe and very severe acquired aplastic anemia, representing the most consequential aplastic anemia document of the year[^1]. Developed by a multidisciplinary panel including content experts, methodologists, and a patient representative using GRADE evidence-to-decision frameworks, the guideline issues 33 recommendations and 4 good practice statements spanning diagnostic testing, treatment selection, and supportive care. Key recommendations include prioritizing allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplantation for younger patients with an available matched sibling or matched unrelated donor as first-line therapy, and reserving transplantation as a second-line option after immunosuppressive therapy failure for those without a well-matched donor upfront. Notably, the panel recommends **incorporating eltrombopag into immunosuppressive regimens**, consistent with adult trial data, and endorses antimicrobial (antibacterial and antifungal) prophylaxis for patients with severe neutropenia at high infectious risk. The panel candidly acknowledges that certainty of evidence was rated low or very low for the majority of clinical questions, reflecting the field's continued reliance on small, nonrandomized cohorts — a limitation directly relevant to interpreting last week's pediatric hetrombopag RCT, which found no benefit from routine TPO-RA/IST combination in children[^8], and this week's guideline recommendation for eltrombopag being derived predominantly from adult evidence. Taken together, these two publications delineate an increasingly clear age-stratified picture: TPO-RA/IST combination therapy has robust adult evidentiary support but should not be routinely extrapolated to pediatric SAA outside of adolescents nearing adult physiology.

## V. Hemophilia A & B (including gene therapy)

Building on last week's whole-genome sequencing report on FVIII clearance modifiers, this week brings a preclinical gene therapy advance for hemophilia B. Investigators engineered AAV vectors encoding a chimeric transgene combining the hyperactive factor IX-Padua variant (FIX-R338L) with a mutant human albumin domain with extended half-life properties (FIX-R338L-ALB[QMP]), hypothesizing that this fusion could overcome the substantial interpatient variability seen in current hemophilia B gene therapy trials, where up to 13% of patients return to prophylaxis[^3]. In wild-type and hemophilia B mice, the fusion construct produced a sustained fourfold increase in FIX antigen levels and a threefold increase in FIX activity compared with the non-fused FIX-R338L control vector, translating into durable correction of the bleeding phenotype. Critically, no anti-FIX antibodies, alanine transaminase elevations, or hepatic immune infiltration were observed, and immune tolerance was achieved in most treated animals even after active immunization challenge with FIX protein and adjuvant — an effect mediated by regulatory T cells. D-dimer levels remained stable, arguing against increased thrombogenic risk despite the hyperactive FIX-Padua backbone. An accompanying editorial frames this as a "doing more with less" strategy, potentially allowing lower vector doses to achieve therapeutic FIX levels and thereby mitigating the dose-dependent immune responses that currently necessitate immunosuppression in AAV-based hemophilia B gene therapy[^9]. Separately, the ADVANCE Japan real-world data on arterial thrombotic risk in aging hemophilia patients reported last week[^10] is complemented this week by a comprehensive multicenter laboratory study characterizing the impact of FXI(a) inhibitors on coagulation assays, extending last week's findings by confirming that lupus anticoagulant testing via dilute Russell viper venom time is unaffected by FXI(a) inhibitors, though LA-sensitive aPTT reagents may yield false-negative results, and that protein C anticoagulant activity is overestimated while protein S activity is unaffected — refining the practical assay-interpretation framework for laboratories preparing for these agents' clinical introduction[^11].

## VI. Von Willebrand Disease

A *Haematologica* study provides mechanistic insight into acquired ADAMTS13/VWF imbalance in trauma-associated hemorrhagic shock, distinct from congenital VWD but mechanistically informative for the broader VWF-ADAMTS13 axis. In 100 hemorrhagic shock patients, investigators identified a reduced rate of VWF cleavage accompanied by increased oxidation of specific methionine residues on VWF, rendering it more resistant to ADAMTS13-mediated proteolysis and exacerbating hyperadhesive VWF activity[^12]. In a mouse polytrauma model, a single dose of recombinant human ADAMTS13 decreased VWF adhesive activity, enhanced VWF cleavage, and improved parameters of lung dysfunction, suggesting recombinant ADAMTS13 supplementation as a potential therapeutic strategy for trauma-induced endotheliopathy — a concept with plausible extension to acquired TTP and severe VWD-adjacent consumptive states, though not yet studied in inherited VWD populations.

## VII. Thalassemia (alpha & beta; Taiwan-relevant)

A *BJH* phase 4, open-label, single-center trial from China reports outcomes of haploidentical hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (Haplo-HSCT) in 134 patients with transfusion-dependent thalassemia (median age 8.7 years, IQR 6.0–11.7), using a uniform busulfan/cyclophosphamide/fludarabine/ATG conditioning regimen with MTX/MMF/tacrolimus GVHD prophylaxis[^13]. At a median follow-up of 23.4 months, 2-year overall survival was 93.4%, event-free survival 92.6%, and GVHD-free relapse-free survival 77.5%. Cumulative incidence of any-grade acute GVHD was 47.8%, with grade 2–4 aGVHD in 30.9% and grade 3–4 in 12.5%. Donor-specific antibodies and male-recipient/female-donor pairing were independently associated with inferior OS (p=0.021 and p=0.010, respectively) and EFS (p=0.040 and p=0.020), while hepatomegaly ≥5 cm predicted inferior OS (p=0.037). This large single-center experience reinforces haploidentical HSCT as a viable curative option for transfusion-dependent thalassemia patients lacking a matched donor, a scenario directly relevant to Taiwan's thalassemia population where fully matched sibling donors are frequently unavailable given typical family sizes; the identification of DSA and donor-recipient sex mismatch as modifiable/screenable risk factors is of direct practical value for transplant planning.

## XI. Thrombosis & Coagulation (VTE, APS, inherited thrombophilia)

Beyond the FXI(a) inhibitor assay-interference data covered in Section V, this week's *AJH* literature includes a randomized, active-controlled phase 2 trial of SHR-2004, a humanized anti-factor XI monoclonal antibody, for VTE prevention after ovarian cancer surgery in 198 Chinese patients across 25 sites[^14]. By Day 28, VTE occurred in 12.6% of SHR-2004-treated patients versus 20.2% of those receiving enoxaparin-rivaroxaban (absolute difference −8.4%, 95% CI −19.9 to 3.1), with comparable major/clinically-relevant-nonmajor bleeding rates (2.2% vs 1.9%). While this is an oncology-surgical population rather than classic non-malignant thrombophilia, the biweekly dosing schedule and favorable bleeding profile are directly relevant to the broader FXI(a) inhibitor development landscape covered extensively in recent weeks' reports. Separately, a *Blood Advances* systematic review and meta-analysis addresses secondary anticoagulation prophylaxis in pediatric antiphospholipid syndrome, though granular pooled effect estimates were not available in the abstract provided; this fills a long-standing evidence gap in an area where pediatric-specific guidance has been largely extrapolated from adult APS literature[^15]. Additionally, a *Haematologica* analysis of incidental findings in clinical exomes performed for unrelated indications identifies inherited thrombosis and hemostasis disorder variants as a non-trivial secondary finding category, with an accompanying editorial emphasizing the clinical actionability challenges when genotype precedes phenotype in bleeding and thrombotic disorders[^16].

## XIII. Taiwan Clinical Context

The ASH 2026 aplastic anemia guideline's endorsement of upfront eltrombopag incorporation into immunosuppressive regimens is directly actionable in Taiwan, where eltrombopag carries NHI reimbursement for severe aplastic anemia in adults; the guideline's caveat regarding low-certainty evidence in pediatric populations reinforces the message from last week's pediatric hetrombopag RCT that routine extrapolation of adult TPO-RA practice to children requires caution, an important consideration for pediatric hematology units at NTUH, CGMH, and VGH managing pediatric SAA. The Haplo-HSCT thalassemia data are highly relevant given Taiwan's beta-thalassemia carrier prevalence of approximately 1–2% and alpha-thalassemia carrier prevalence of 4–5%; with a well-established haploidentical transplant program at CGMH Kaohsiung and NTUH, the identification of donor-specific antibodies and donor-recipient sex mismatch as prognostic factors should inform pre-transplant screening protocols for Taiwanese transfusion-dependent thalassemia patients considering haploidentical donors, particularly given the relatively small family sizes typical in contemporary Taiwanese households that limit matched sibling donor availability. Regarding factor XI(a) inhibitors, as abelacimab, milvexian, and asundexian progress through phase 3 development, Taiwanese coagulation laboratories should anticipate the need to adopt the interpretive frameworks described in this week's and last week's reports — particularly regarding aPTT prolongation, lupus anticoagulant testing pitfalls with LA-sensitive reagents, and protein C activity overestimation — well ahead of eventual NHI reimbursement decisions.

## XIV. Key Takeaways

The ASH 2026 aplastic anemia guideline represents this week's most immediately practice-relevant publication, though its candid acknowledgment of low-certainty evidence across most recommendations underscores the persistent evidence gap in this rare disease space, a gap partially addressed but also complicated by last week's negative pediatric hetrombopag RCT. The HOPE Kids 2 voxelotor trial delivers a cautionary tale in pediatric sickle cell disease: a statistically robust primary efficacy endpoint (CBFV reduction, p=0.0048) was ultimately overshadowed by a mortality imbalance that triggered program-wide discontinuation, illustrating the importance of long-term safety surveillance even for biomarker-positive interventions. In hemophilia, the shift toward optimizing gene therapy transgene design (extended half-life fusion constructs) rather than simply increasing vector dose represents a promising strategy to address the dose-dependent immunogenicity that has constrained AAV-based hemophilia B programs. Finally, the accumulating mechanistic data across ITP (anergic B cells, IL-17 signaling, BAFF/BAFF-R) suggest the field is converging on B-cell tolerance induction as the central unifying mechanism of durable remission, opening rational combination or sequencing strategies for future trials.
