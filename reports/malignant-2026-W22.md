## Summary

Five signals dominate this week's literature. **(1)** A phase 1 trial of briquilimab — an anti-CD117 monoclonal antibody — combined with nonmyeloablative conditioning in 32 older adults with AML/MDS demonstrates 1-year EFS of 69.2% in AML-CR and 53.8% in MDS, with no graft failure or dose-limiting toxicities, establishing a new antibody-based conditioning platform for frail patients ineligible for intensive regimens[^1]. **(2)** A smMIP-based NGS-MRD assay applied pre-transplant in 93 AML patients identifies MRD positivity as an independent predictor of inferior post-transplant OS (HR 4.58; p = 0.002) at an estimated library preparation cost of ~€8 per sample, offering a scalable precision tool for resource-limited settings[^2]. **(3)** Single-cell multiomic profiling of 99 serial samples from 34 CTCL patients identifies STAT3 gain-of-function mutation D661Y, CCR4, and EZH2 as recurrent progression-associated alterations, with therapeutic implications for EZH2 inhibition in this incurable disease[^3]. **(4)** A phase 2 study of ropeginterferon alfa-2b in early myelofibrosis reports clinicohematologic complete response rates of 63.8% and 63.5% at weeks 24 and 48, with a 26% rate of bone marrow fibrosis improvement and molecular responses in >50% at week 24, with only 4.2% treatment discontinuations[^4]. **(5)** A high-dimensional spatiotemporal single-cell atlas of the CML bone marrow microenvironment using a 54-marker CODEX panel across 2,033,725 cells reveals progressive vascular disorganization, PD-1 upregulation on CD8+ T cells, and megakaryocyte morphologic disruption as key features of leukemic niche remodeling reversed by TKI therapy[^5].

## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

### CD117-Targeted Conditioning for Older Adults

A phase 1 trial published in *Blood* this week evaluates briquilimab — a monoclonal antibody blocking stem cell factor binding to CD117 (c-Kit) — as a novel non-cytotoxic conditioning agent for allogeneic HCT in older patients with AML or MDS (NCT04429191)[^1]. Thirty-two patients (median age 70 years) with a mix of AML in complete remission (n=13), AML in relapse (n=3), and MDS (n=16) were enrolled; most had detectable MRD at screening. Briquilimab was infused 10–14 days before transplant day 0, followed by nonmyeloablative fludarabine 30 mg/m² and 2–3 Gy TBI. GVHD prophylaxis consisted of tacrolimus, sirolimus, and mycophenolate mofetil. Remarkably, there were no briquilimab infusion reactions, no dose-limiting toxicities, and no primary graft failure events. Marrow sampling confirmed mean HSPC depletion of 62.4% ± 22.7% after briquilimab, accompanied by a threefold rise in serum SCF. Among patients with AML in CR, 1-year EFS was 69.2% and 1-year OS was 75%; in the MDS cohort, 1-year EFS was 53.8% and 1-year OS was 76.4%. These outcomes are notably favorable given the advanced age and MRD-positive status of most participants. One of three patients with active AML relapse experienced a transient response. This study provides proof-of-concept for immunologic HSC niche depletion as a conditioning strategy, and a companion editorial in the same issue contextualizes the broader promise of CD117-targeted HCT conditioning across myeloid malignancies[^6].

### NGS-MRD Pre-Transplant Risk Stratification: A Cost-Efficient smMIP Platform

A study in *Haematologica* this week reports a cost-efficient NGS-MRD assay using single-molecule molecular inversion probes (smMIPs) targeting 92 loci in 33 AML driver genes, applied to 93 AML patients in remission prior to allo-SCT[^2]. MRD positivity, defined as ≥1 non-DTA variant at ≥0.5% VAF, was present in 30% of patients. In multivariable analysis, NGS-MRD positivity was an independent predictor of inferior post-transplant OS (HR 4.58; 95% CI not fully reported; p = 0.002), and was also associated with significantly shorter OS on univariable analysis (p < 0.001). Importantly, conditioning intensity did not modify outcomes in MRD-positive patients, reinforcing the view that pre-transplant molecular disease burden trumps transplant-preparative regimen in determining relapse risk. Three cases exhibited multiple low-VAF clustered variants in RUNX1 and TET2 that were absent at diagnosis, raising concern for treatment-induced mutagenesis. At an estimated library preparation cost of approximately €8 per sample, the smMIP approach represents a potentially transformative reduction in per-assay cost compared to commercial error-corrected sequencing platforms. These findings complement but do not overlap with previously reported pre-transplant MRD data from the CIBMTR (W21 report), which focused on flow cytometry-based MRD and its center-level heterogeneity rather than NGS.

### CIBMTR Real-World Flow Cytometry MRD Heterogeneity

In a large observational CIBMTR analysis published this week in *Haematologica*, pre-transplant MFC-MRD was evaluated in 2,544 AML patients (median age 58 years) undergoing allo-HCT in first CR between 2013 and 2019[^7]. Eleven percent had detectable MRD prior to alloHCT. MRD positivity was independently associated with inferior OS (HR 1.27; 95% CI 1.06–1.51; p = 0.009), inferior DFS (HR 1.31; 95% CI 1.11–1.53; p = 0.001), and increased relapse risk (HR 1.42; 95% CI 1.17–1.72; p < 0.001), but not with non-relapse mortality. The 1-year cumulative incidence of relapse was 35% in MRD-positive versus 25% in MRD-negative patients. Critically, substantial center-level variability in the prognostic impact of MRD was observed across the 12 largest transplant centers — a finding that underscores the urgent need for standardized assay thresholds and reporting, and argues for platform-agnostic MRD integration into pre-transplant risk scores only when methodology is defined.

### Mesothelin as Predictor of Extramedullary Disease in AML

A study in *Blood Advances* characterizes mesothelin (MSLN) expression as an independent risk factor for extramedullary disease (EMD) in a cohort of 118 de novo AML patients, 27 of whom (22.88%) developed EMD at various sites[^8]. By multivariate analysis, MSLN positivity at the transcript level carried an HR of 3.43 (95% CI 1.44–8.20; p = 0.006) for EMD risk, while MSLN positivity detected on leukemic blasts by flow cytometry carried an HR of 3.28 (95% CI 1.37–7.86; p = 0.008). Mechanistically, MSLN promoted EMD through interaction with MUC16, activating PI3K signaling and upregulating neural CAMs CD56 and NCAM2. As MSLN is an established target in solid tumors (e.g., anti-MSLN ADCs), these findings open a rationale for exploring MSLN-directed therapeutics in the AML-EMD context.

### Mutant IDH1 Blocks Neutropoiesis via Epigenetic Repression of CEBPE

A mechanistic study in *Blood* demonstrates that IDH1-mutant AML harbors neutrophil lineage-specific epigenetic alterations not replicated by IDH2 mutations[^9]. Using a genetically engineered inducible mouse model with heterozygous Idh1 mutation, the investigators show a cell-intrinsic pre-leukemic block in neutrophil differentiation caused by repression of myeloid transcription programs — most critically, impaired expression of *Cebpe*, a key transcription factor for neutrophil differentiation. Restoration of CEBPE expression, either by CEBPA overexpression or hypomethylating agent treatment, reversed the differentiation block, suggesting the mechanism is epigenetically plastic and potentially reversible by ivosidenib treatment in clinical settings.

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

### TCF3::HLF B-ALL: Self-Reinforcing IL-1β Signaling Axis

Although published online during the overlap window between W21 and W22, the full-text article characterizing the TCF3::HLF–IL-1β axis in aggressive B-ALL was formally indexed in *Blood* this week[^10]. Using a new murine model that faithfully recapitulates human TCF3::HLF B-ALL including osteolytic lesions, genetic deletion of IL1B or its receptor IL1R1 suppressed leukemic growth and ameliorated bone destruction in vivo. Single-cell RNA-seq of patient samples confirmed strong IL1B induction at relapse compared to diagnosis. TCF3::HLF was found to directly activate an intronic regulatory element in the IL1B locus. These data position anakinra or canakinumab as testable therapeutic approaches in this otherwise universally fatal subtype, particularly relevant given the small number of patients and lack of active international trials.

### PAX5 Fusion Landscape in B-ALL: GSE1 as a Novel Partner

A molecular characterization study in *BJH* identifies PAX5::GSE1 as a novel in-frame fusion in a B-ALL patient and, by analyzing 330 PAX5-rearranged B-ALL cases, maps 76 distinct in-frame partner genes[^11]. Integrated transcriptomic analysis reveals that PAX5-r induces a convergent expression profile characterized by dysregulation of the RB1/p53 pathways and NPM1 overexpression — therapeutic vulnerabilities of direct relevance given current CDK4/6 inhibitor and MDM2 inhibitor trial programs. PAX5::GSE1 transcript levels correlated with treatment response in the index patient, supporting its utility as an MRD biomarker. Given the Asian ancestry of several enrolled patients, these findings are relevant for institutional genomic profiling programs at NCKUH and other Taiwan centers.

### Pediatric B-ALL Relapse: Thiopurine Resistance and MMR Deficiency

A real-world WES study from *BJH* of 75 pediatric B-ALL relapse cases (2018–2024) provides important insight into maintenance therapy failure mechanisms[^12]. SBS87 (thiopurine-related), SBS6/15 (MMR-related), or Thio-dMMR signatures were detected in 24% of cases. Thiopurine resistance clones in NT5C2 (n=13), PRPS1 (n=2), or TP53 (n=7) were present in 25% of cases; germline or somatic MMR gene clones were found in 25%. Low median weekly 6-MP dose (<45 mg/m²) was significantly associated with higher hazard for developing these signatures (HR 7.09; 95% CI 1.77–28.34; p = 0.006), as was ≥4 weeks of dose interruptions (HR 6.21; 95% CI 1.66–23.32; p = 0.007). The clinical message is unambiguous: suboptimal maintenance dosing drives acquired resistance and MMR deficiency at relapse, underscoring the importance of pharmacokinetic dose optimization, TPMT/NUDT15 genotyping prior to therapy initiation, and minimizing unnecessary interruptions.

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

### 25 Years of TKI Therapy: Landmark Perspective and Spatiotemporal Bone Marrow Atlas

Two important CML publications anchor this week's section. The first, a comprehensive historical and forward-looking perspective co-authored by Hochhaus, Talpaz, Saglio, and Cortes in *Leukemia*, marks 25 years since the introduction of imatinib and provides a structured synthesis of TKI generation evolution, treatment-free remission (TFR) achievements, and remaining challenges[^13]. Approximately half of eligible patients maintain TFR after discontinuation; the article identifies novel PROTAC degraders, immunomodulatory strategies, and combination approaches as the next frontier for expanding TFR eligibility and overcoming resistance. The piece is accompanied by a journal editorial commemorating the milestone[^14]. These are considered retrospective/perspective articles; no new trial data are introduced.

The second, a high-resolution spatiotemporal atlas published in *Blood*, profiled 2,033,725 cells across 55 regions of interest in CML and wild-type mouse femurs at days 7, 14, and 21 post-induction, using a 54-marker CODEX panel and identifying 41 cell types[^5]. During CML progression, the investigators observed expansion of myeloid and progenitor cells, upregulation of PD-L1 on leukemic cells, PD-1 upregulation on both CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, and profound loss of B cells, plasma cells, and bone cells. Advanced CML showed striking expansion of immature, pericyte-deficient vasculature that disrupted hematopoietic stem/progenitor cell positioning. Spatial mapping revealed leukemia-specific cellular neighborhoods enriched with PD-1+CD8+ T cells suggesting localized immune exhaustion — a finding with direct implications for testing PD-1/PD-L1 checkpoint inhibition as a TKI add-on in persistent disease. Laser-capture microdissected MKs from newly diagnosed CML patients had reduced cytoskeleton gene expression, which was reversed in TKI-treated cases. A parallel analysis of AML marrow showed reduced and morphologically normal MKs, in contrast to the irregular CML-specific MK phenotype, highlighting disease-specific niche remodeling biology.

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

### Acalabrutinib: Final Phase 1/2 Long-Term Results

The definitive long-term phase 1/2 results of acalabrutinib monotherapy in CLL/SLL are reported in *Blood Advances* this week, covering 99 treatment-naïve (TN) and 134 relapsed/refractory (R/R) patients[^15]. At final data cutoff (median follow-up 73.7 months TN; 52.6 months R/R), 71% of TN and 31% of R/R patients remained on treatment. ORR was 97.0% (TN) and 94.8% (R/R). In the TN cohort, median PFS was not reached and the 72-month PFS rate was 86.7% (95% CI 77.0–92.5). For R/R patients, median PFS was 66.1 months and the 72-month PFS rate was 45.1% (95% CI 35.6–54.1). Atrial fibrillation occurred in 6.1% (TN) and 9.0% (R/R), major bleeding in 8.1% and 8.2%, respectively. Importantly, the incidence of most adverse events decreased over time, and no new long-term safety signals emerged. These data confirm durable benefit across both frontline and relapsed settings with reassuring long-term tolerability.

### AI-Enhanced Flow Cytometry for CLL MRD Detection

An AI pipeline for automated CLL MRD detection by flow cytometry, designated CCADDAS, is reported in *Leukemia* this week[^16]. Applied to 166 MRD-positive and 61 MRD-negative cases using a single-tube 10-color panel, the pipeline achieved results highly concordant with expert manual review (R² = 0.98) and distinguished CLL MRD from background B cells with an AUC of 0.98. Downsampling reduced cellularity by 85% and file size by 78% while retaining low-level MRD events. Manual analysis time decreased from 9.0 to 0.9 minutes per case — a 90% reduction. The cloud-based, software-agnostic architecture could substantially lower the barrier for high-sensitivity MRD testing in centers without dedicated hematopathology expertise, directly relevant to NCKUH and regional centers in Taiwan.

### Duvelisib Plus Venetoclax in R/R CLL and Richter Transformation

A phase 1/2 study in *Blood Advances* reports outcomes of duvelisib (PI3Kδ/γ inhibitor) plus venetoclax in 35 patients with R/R CLL/SLL and 9 with Richter transformation (RT)[^17]. Among CLL patients, 77% had unmutated IGHV, 46% had TP53 aberrancy, and 60% had prior BTK inhibitor exposure. At the recommended phase 2 dose (duvelisib 25 mg BID plus venetoclax 400 mg daily), best CR and ORR were 60% and 91%, respectively. At cycle 13, peripheral blood and bone marrow uMRD at 10⁻⁴ were 43% and 40%, respectively, and the median PFS for CLL/SLL was 46 months with a 3-year PFS of 67% (95% CI 0.51–0.86). Four patients with Richter syndrome achieved a response including 3 CRs. Grade ≥3 neutropenia occurred in 91%, with febrile neutropenia in 6%, and diarrhea ≥ grade 3 in 14%, reflecting the known immunologic toxicity profile of dual PI3K inhibition. While the MRD depth and PFS are encouraging, the toxicity burden limits broad adoption; the RT signal warrants further prospective investigation.

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

### Ropeginterferon Alfa-2b in Early Myelofibrosis: Phase 2 Efficacy and Molecular Response

A phase 2 study of ropeginterferon alfa-2b (ropeg) in prefibrotic primary MF and low/intermediate-1–risk fibrotic PMF or secondary MF requiring cytoreduction is reported in *Blood Advances* this week (NCT04988815)[^4]. Seventy-one patients (median age 60 years; range 31–86) were enrolled and followed for a median of 119 weeks after substituting hydroxyurea with ropeg (500 μg every 2 weeks from week 4). Clinicohematologic complete response (CHCR) was achieved in 63.8% at week 24 (pre-PMF 74%, fibrotic PMF 20%, SMF 42.9%), 63.5% at week 48, and 70% at week 104. No thrombo-hemorrhagic events occurred after hydroxyurea discontinuation. JAK2V617F VAF reduction was observed in 53.1% at week 24 and 67.6% at week 104; CALR mutant VAF reduction was noted in 52.6% at week 24 and 40% at week 104. Among 50 evaluable patients at week 104, 13 (26%) had reduced bone marrow reticulin fibrosis. Only 3 patients (4.2%) discontinued treatment due to adverse events. These data position ropeg as a particularly promising cytoreductive option for pre-PMF patients — where it achieved the highest CHCR — with the additional advantage of molecular disease modification not seen with hydroxyurea.

### Blood-Based Machine Learning Model for MF Detection in JAK2V617F-Positive MPNs

A multicentre pilot proof-of-concept study in *BJH* develops an interpretable, non-invasive machine learning model using peripheral blood parameters to detect moderate-to-severe myelofibrosis in 336 JAK2V617F-positive MPN patients (training set), with external validation in 92[^19]. The best-performing support vector machine model yielded an AUC of 0.916 (95% CI 0.83–0.99) with good calibration and highest clinical utility by decision curve analysis. SHAP analysis identified haemoglobin, IL-1β, and age as the three most influential features. Preclinical experiments confirmed IL-1β upregulation during fibrosis progression, reversed by ruxolitinib or pegylated interferon-α but not hydroxyurea. While bone marrow biopsy remains the reference standard for fibrosis grading, this blood-based tool could serve as a screening and monitoring adjunct to triage patients for biopsy in resource-constrained or high-volume settings.

### Clonal Dynamics in Concurrent MPN and CML

A case series/perspective in *Leukemia* examines clonal dynamics and therapeutic pressure in the rare clinical scenario of concurrent MPN with CML, analyzing the implications of JAK2-directed therapy and co-occurring mutations[^20]. This highlights the underrecognized biology of BCR::ABL1 and JAK2V617F co-existence and its therapeutic complexity, although no new trial data are presented.

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

### CSNK1E Mediates Drug Persistence in DLBCL via a Memory B-Cell–like Program

A translational study in *Blood* this week provides the first systematic characterization of drug-persistent DLBCL cells at single-cell resolution, with paired scRNA-seq and BCR-seq from 8 R/R patients validated in 3 independent cohorts[^21]. Drug-persistent cells exhibited a transcriptional profile consistent with a less-differentiated, memory B-cell–like program with enhanced stemlike properties, which correlated with unfavorable clinical outcomes across multiple DLBCL cohorts. Casein kinase 1ε (CSNK1E), a WNT signaling regulator, was upregulated in these stemlike persistent cells — in part via APRIL–TNFRSF13B axis activation. CSNK1E inhibition impaired growth and tumor-initiating capacity of drug-persistent cells and potentiated R-CHOP efficacy both in vitro and in vivo. A companion editorial in the same issue contextualizes the finding[^22]. CSNK1E joins a growing list of actionable stemlike-persistence mediators (alongside DUSP1 in hairy cell leukemia, and IFITM2 in MCL) being characterized this week.

### Zanubrutinib Plus Lenalidomide in R/R DLBCL: Phase 1 Final Results

The *Blood Advances* phase 1 study of zanubrutinib 160 mg BID plus lenalidomide in 66 R/R DLBCL patients (BGB-3111-110) establishes the RP2D as lenalidomide 25 mg[^23]. No dose-limiting toxicities occurred. At the RP2D, ORR was 58% and complete response rate was 42%, with a median time to response of 2.8 months, median duration of response of 14.9 months, and median PFS of 5.5 months (95% CI 2.9–11.1); the 12-month event-free rate was 34% (95% CI 21–48). Grade ≥3 decreased neutrophil count occurred in 58% of patients, and 11% discontinued treatment due to adverse events. Approximately 67% of patients had non-GCB/ABC-type DLBCL. While the ORR and CRR are encouraging — particularly in a heavily pretreated, largely non-GCB population that has already received prior lines — the PFS of 5.5 months reflects the underlying aggressive biology. The combination appears rationally designed to exploit BTK + cereblon co-inhibition and merits phase 2 evaluation with companion biomarker selection.

### Early PET Response After 2L Axi-cel Predicts Post-CAR T Outcomes

A national cohort study from the United Kingdom published in *Blood Advances* evaluates the prognostic utility of early FDG-PET response at 1 month following second-line axicabtagene ciloleucel in 302 patients with large B-cell lymphoma[^24]. Among 245 evaluable patients, those with DS4 and DS5 responses at 1 month had a 50% and 73% risk of progression by month 6, respectively, compared with 25% for DS1-3/DS4RT responders (DS4: HR 3.07, 95% CI 1.85–5.07; DS5: HR 5.20, 95% CI 2.95–9.16; p < 0.0001). In a risk model incorporating DS category and pre-infusion LDH ≥ 2× ULN, a high-risk group was identified with an HR of 5.21 (95% CI 3.27–8.29; p < 0.001) for early failure; responding high-risk patients had ≥70% risk of progression by month 6. These data argue for routine post-CAR T PET assessment at day 30 to identify candidates for early intervention with bispecific antibodies, retreatment strategies, or novel consolidation.

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

### CAR-T in Follicular Lymphoma: Axi-cel vs Tisa-cel Real-World Data

A multicenter retrospective study in *Blood Advances* compares real-world outcomes of axicabtagene ciloleucel (axi-cel; n=100) versus tisagenlecleucel (tisa-cel; n=36) in 136 R/R FL patients treated between 2021 and 2024[^25]. In the unweighted analysis, axi-cel was associated with higher ORR (96% vs 80%; p = 0.007), higher CRR (88% vs 71%; p = 0.024), and longer median PFS (30.5 vs 11.9 months; p = 0.021). Median OS did not differ significantly (NR vs 23.6 months; p = 0.061). CRS rates were comparable (75% vs 75%), but ICANS was more frequent with axi-cel (42% vs 17%; p = 0.008). After inverse probability treatment weighting to adjust for age differences (median 60 vs 68 years) and lymphodepletion regimen (bendamustine more frequent with tisa-cel), efficacy outcomes were largely similar but axi-cel toxicity remained higher. Real-world median PFS was inferior to clinical trial benchmarks for both products, underscoring the impact of patient selection criteria.

### IFITM2 as a CAR-T Persistence Mechanism in Mantle Cell Lymphoma

A methodologically innovative *Blood Advances* study introduces Live-cell Pick-Seq (LiP-Seq), a live-cell imaging-coupled single-cell transcriptomics platform capable of profiling viable lymphoma cells at frequencies as low as 10⁻⁶[^26]. Applying LiP-Seq to MCL patients post-CD19 CAR-T therapy, the investigators identify recurrent upregulation of IFITM2 — an interferon-induced transmembrane protein — in the persistent cell fraction. Functional validation demonstrated that IFITM2 overexpression conferred protection against CAR-T cytotoxicity in vitro. IFITM2 inhibition may thus represent a rational strategy to eliminate CAR-T–resistant minimal residual disease in MCL, complementing emerging data on CD19 antigen escape.

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

### Nivolumab in Elderly, Frail HL Patients: NIVINIHO Phase 2 Final Results

The LYSA phase 2 NIVINIHO trial reports final results for 64 treatment-naïve HL patients aged ≥61 years (median age 75 years) with comorbidities contraindicating standard chemotherapy, treated with nivolumab induction followed by nivolumab ± vinblastine consolidation[^27]. Of 56 evaluable patients, 16 (28.6%) achieved complete metabolic response (CMR) at end of treatment. Median PFS was 9.8 months (95% CI 4.2–12), and the 2-year OS rate was 74.1% (95% CI 58.9%–84.4%). Grade ≥3 adverse events occurred in 50% of patients; treatment discontinuation due to nivolumab-related AEs was required in 29.7%. The CMR rate of 28.6% and PFS of 9.8 months indicate that nivolumab monotherapy, while administratively feasible in frail patients, provides modest disease control in this unmet-need population. These data support exploring combinations — including low-dose or sequential BV — in upcoming trials.

### BV Maintenance Post-ASCT: Impact of Novel Agents in Prior Therapy

A large retrospective study of 1,091 R/R cHL patients undergoing ASCT between 2010–2022 published in *Blood Advances* assesses whether prior exposure to PD-1 antibodies or brentuximab vedotin (BV) modifies the benefit of post-ASCT BV maintenance[^28]. Among 608 propensity-score matched patients, 3-year PFS and OS were 73% (95% CI 70–78%) and 95% (95% CI 93–97%), respectively. BV maintenance significantly improved PFS only in patients with no prior novel agent exposure and ≥2 modified AETHERA risk factors (HR 0.40; 95% CI 0.25–0.65; p < 0.001). In contrast, BV maintenance was not associated with a significant PFS benefit in any patient subgroup who received novel agents before ASCT — including those treated with prior BV (0-1 factors: HR 1.43; p = 0.48; 2+ factors: HR 0.79; p = 0.58) or prior PD-1 mAb. Patients achieving CR after one PD-1–based salvage line and proceeding to ASCT had a 2-year PFS of 95–100%, with no additional benefit from BV maintenance. These findings challenge the routine use of BV maintenance in the modern era and suggest that omission should be considered for patients who have responded to PD-1–based salvage.

### PTCL: Critical Reappraisal of CHOP-Plus Strategies

A comprehensive review in *Blood Advances* critically examines two decades of CHOP-plus strategies in peripheral T-cell lymphoma, concluding that — with the sole exception of BV-CHOP in ALK-negative ALCL — augmented frontline regimens have not improved outcomes and add toxicity in most PTCL subtypes[^29]. The authors emphasize the need to abandon the CHOP-plus paradigm and design biology-driven trials based on molecular subtype stratification. Concurrent mechanistic data from this week support this shift: the multiomics CTCL study identifying EZH2, CCR4, and STAT3-D661Y as therapeutic targets (Section IX, below) and the IGF2BP2 study in nodal PTCL (see below) both provide rationale for targeted approaches.
