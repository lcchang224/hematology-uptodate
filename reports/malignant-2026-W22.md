## Summary

Five signals dominate this week's literature. **(1)** Final phase 1/2 results of acalabrutinib monotherapy in 233 patients with treatment-naïve and relapsed/refractory CLL/SLL demonstrate a 72-month PFS rate of 86.7% in the treatment-naïve cohort (median not reached) and 45.1% in the relapsed/refractory cohort (median PFS 66.1 months), with no new safety signals at a median follow-up exceeding 6 years.[^1] **(2)** A phase 2 study of ropeginterferon alfa-2b in 71 patients with early-stage myelofibrosis achieves a clinicohematologic complete response rate of 63.8% at week 24 — improving to 70% at week 104 — with JAK2 V617F VAF reduction in 67.6% and histologic fibrosis regression in 26% at two years.[^2] **(3)** A real-world multicenter cohort of 136 patients with relapsed/refractory follicular lymphoma treated with commercial axi-cel or tisa-cel demonstrates superior ORR (96% vs 80%), CRR (88% vs 71%), and median PFS (30.5 vs 11.9 months) with axi-cel, though ICANS was significantly more frequent (42% vs 17%).[^3] **(4)** A 302-patient national cohort shows that early PET response at one month post-axi-cel in second-line large B-cell lymphoma powerfully predicts subsequent failure: Deauville score 5 carries a progression risk of 73% by month 6 (HR 5.20, 95% CI 2.95–9.16 vs DS1-3).[^4] **(5)** A comprehensive 25-year review of TKI therapy in CML synthesises the trajectory from imatinib to contemporary STAMP inhibitors and frames the residual challenges of treatment-free remission eligibility, drug affordability, and novel resistance mechanisms.[^5]

## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

Three papers extend the AML evidence base this week, each approaching the disease from a distinct translational angle. A retrospective Italian multicentre study published in *BJH* evaluates outcomes in patients with de novo and secondary AML treated with frontline hypomethylating agent (HMA) plus venetoclax combinations, providing granular real-world complement to the VIALE-A trial data.[^6] The analysis stratifies by fitness criteria definitions — highlighting that "unfit" is a heterogeneous designation — and demonstrates that patients categorised as unfit by strict criteria (e.g., ECOG ≥3, severe organ dysfunction) have meaningfully inferior outcomes compared with those designated unfit by softer criteria, with complete remission rates diverging substantially across subgroups. An accompanying editorial in the same issue cautions against applying HMA/venetoclax uniformly to all AML patients deemed ineligible for intensive chemotherapy and calls for prospective fitness-stratified trial design.[^7]

Separately, a large cohort study in *Blood Cancer Journal* analysing 2,500 AML patients systematically characterises the prognostic impact of RAS pathway mutations across molecular subtypes.[^8] The report finds that KRAS and NRAS mutations carry heterogeneous prognostic effects that are highly context-dependent: in NPM1-mutated AML without FLT3-ITD, RAS mutations confer a significantly increased relapse risk, whereas in KMT2A-rearranged AML, they appear broadly neutral. These subtype-dependent interactions have direct implications for ELN risk classification and for informing MEK/RAS inhibitor trial eligibility. A companion translational paper from the ALFA-1200 cohort in *Blood Cancer Journal* demonstrates that ultra-high-sensitivity NGS-based MRD assessment (variant allele frequency threshold 0.001%) after intensive induction in older AML patients independently predicts relapse-free survival and OS, adding to the accumulating evidence that flow cytometry-based MRD thresholds may underestimate residual disease burden in this population.[^9]

A further *Blood Advances* study investigates mesothelin (MSLN) expression as an independent predictor of extramedullary disease (EMD) in a cohort of 118 de novo AML patients, of whom 22.88% developed EMD.[^10] Multivariate analysis identified MSLN transcript positivity (HR 3.43, 95% CI 1.44–8.20, p = 0.006) and MSLN positivity by flow cytometry on leukemic blasts (HR 3.28, 95% CI 1.37–7.86, p = 0.008) as independent EMD risk factors. Mechanistically, MSLN promotes extramedullary dissemination through MUC16 binding, activating PI3K signalling and upregulating neural cell adhesion molecules CD56 and NCAM2. MSLN is already a validated therapeutic target in solid tumours (e.g., mesothelin-directed ADCs), and this work raises the possibility of redirecting such agents toward EMD-prone AML — a subgroup with historically dismal prognosis.

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

A *BJH* study from India reports real-world genomic characterisation of thiopurine resistance in 75 paediatric B-ALL relapse cases subjected to whole exome (WES; n = 70) or deep targeted sequencing (n = 5).[^11] Acquired thiopurine resistance clones — involving NT5C2 (n = 13), PRPS1 (n = 2), or TP53 (n = 7) — were found in 25% of relapses, and MMR gene aberrations (germline or somatic) were present in a further 25%. Critically, both low median weekly 6-MP dose (<45 mg/m²) and prolonged dose interruptions (≥4 weeks) were independently associated with higher hazard of developing thiopurine-related (SBS87) or thiopurine-deficient mismatch repair (Thio-dMMR) mutational signatures at relapse (HR 7.09, 95% CI 1.77–28.34, p = 0.006 and HR 6.21, 95% CI 1.66–23.32, p = 0.007, respectively). These findings underscore the clinical imperative for minimising maintenance dose interruptions and optimising 6-MP dose intensity to forestall resistance clonal evolution in paediatric ALL — a message particularly relevant for resource-constrained settings.

A cost-effectiveness analysis from a Hong Kong public-sector perspective evaluates blinatumomab in combination with chemotherapy for paediatric standard-risk B-ALL using a 10-year Markov model.[^12] At a willingness-to-pay threshold of 162,721 USD/QALY, second-line blinatumomab yielded an ICER of 10,534–12,375 USD/QALY and first-line blinatumomab yielded 50,312–54,799 USD/QALY in both high- and average-risk relapse subgroups — both below the threshold. Probabilistic sensitivity analysis favoured the blinatumomab first-line strategy in 71.4–90.3% of simulations. While not directly applicable to Taiwan NHI pricing, this regional Asian cost-effectiveness data provides a valuable reference for regional reimbursement discussions.

Additionally, a *BJH* study from China characterises PAX5::GSE1 as a novel in-frame fusion gene in B-ALL, accompanied by analysis of 330 patients with PAX5-rearranged ALL revealing 76 distinct in-frame partner genes.[^13] Integrated transcriptomic analysis demonstrates that PAX5 rearrangements converge on RB1 and p53 pathway dysregulation with NPM1 overexpression, irrespective of fusion partner. Transcript levels of PAX5::GSE1 correlated with treatment response, supporting its use as an MRD biomarker in this subtype.

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

Two landmark reviews published this week in *Leukemia* mark the 25th anniversary of imatinib's clinical introduction, together representing the most comprehensive synthesis of TKI-era CML data in recent literature. The first, authored by Hochhaus, Talpaz, Saglio, Cortes, and colleagues, traces the evolution from imatinib's transformation of a uniformly fatal disease into one with near-normal life expectancy for responding patients, through the second- and third-generation TKIs developed to overcome resistance and intolerance, to the current paradigm shift toward treatment-free remission (TFR) as a realistic therapeutic goal.[^5] Landmark discontinuation studies have established that approximately 50% of patients meeting deep molecular response criteria can sustain TFR, yet the review emphasises that a substantial proportion of patients worldwide remain unable to access even first-generation TKIs due to cost constraints — a challenge particularly acute in low- and middle-income settings.

Complementing this, a focused review in *Blood Cancer Journal* by Haddad and Kantarjian presents an updated CML-CP treatment algorithm incorporating recent data on STAMP inhibitors (asciminib) and investigational agents including olverembatinib, ELVN-001, TGRX-678, and TERN-701.[^14] The review challenges several established practices, including the reflexive switching of TKIs upon toxicity (advocating dose reduction as the preferred first step), and provides a framework for selecting among approved second- and third-line options — ponatinib, asciminib, and second-generation TKIs — based on mutation status and comorbidity profile. The authors note that post-approval studies across multiple TKIs consistently demonstrate that reduced-dose strategies achieve molecular response rates comparable to standard approved doses with substantially improved tolerability.

Extended follow-up data for low-dose dasatinib (DAVLEC phase 2 trial) in older CML-CP patients is also published this week in *Blood Cancer Journal*, providing further support for individualised dose optimisation strategies in this comorbid population.[^15]

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

Two clinically significant papers in CLL appear this week. The first — the final analysis of the phase 1/2 acalabrutinib monotherapy trial (NCT02029443) — reports long-term outcomes in 99 treatment-naïve (TN) and 134 relapsed/refractory (R/R) CLL/SLL patients at a median follow-up of 73.7 and 52.6 months respectively.[^1] In the TN cohort, the overall response rate was 97.0%, with 71% of patients still on therapy at data cutoff and a 72-month PFS rate of 86.7% (95% CI 77.0–92.5) — with the median still not reached. In the R/R cohort, the ORR was 94.8%, the median PFS was 66.1 months, and the 72-month PFS rate was 45.1% (95% CI 35.6–54.1). Responses were consistent across standard- and high-risk genomic features. Atrial fibrillation occurred in 6.1% (TN) and 9.0% (R/R), and hypertension in 29.3% and 23.1%, respectively. Most adverse events decreased in frequency over time, and no new late-emerging safety signals were identified. These data represent the most mature single-agent BTK inhibitor dataset in CLL and provide an important benchmark for combination regimens.

A phase 1/2 study of duvelisib (PI3Kδ/γ inhibitor) plus venetoclax in 35 R/R CLL/SLL and 9 Richter transformation (RT) patients (NCT03534323) is published in *Blood Advances*.[^16] The recommended phase 2 dose was established as duvelisib 25 mg twice daily plus venetoclax 400 mg daily; the maximum tolerated dose was not reached. The best CR rate was 60% and ORR 91% in the CLL/SLL cohort; at cycle 13, peripheral blood and bone marrow uMRD at 10⁻⁴ were 43% and 40%, respectively. The median PFS was 46 months and the 3-year PFS was 67% (95% CI 0.51–0.86). Four RT patients achieved a response (3 CRs). However, toxicity was substantial: ≥grade 3 neutropenia occurred in 91%, febrile neutropenia in 6%, diarrhoea ≥grade 3 in 14%, and elevated AST ≥grade 3 in 9%. While efficacy — particularly in a heavily pretreated cohort (60% with prior BTK inhibitor, 46% with TP53 aberrancy) — is impressive, the immune-mediated toxicity profile will require careful risk-benefit assessment before broader adoption.

An AI-enhanced flow cytometry platform (CCADDAS) for automated CLL MRD detection is reported in *Leukemia*.[^17] Applied to 166 MRD-positive and 61 MRD-negative cases, the pipeline reduced manual analysis time by 90% (from 9.0 to 0.9 min per case), cell file size by 78%, while achieving R² = 0.98 concordance with expert review. An AI-generated aberrancy scale distinguished CLL MRD from background B cells with AUC = 0.98. This technology has direct operational implications for MRD-guided treatment discontinuation protocols in CLL.

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

A time-driven activity-based costing study published in *BJH* prospectively maps the true per-unit cost of red cell transfusion in MDS at an Australian tertiary centre, arriving at a total cost of US$440.47 per unit (annual per-patient cost US$6,607.12), with hospital process costs — particularly equipment (16%) and staffing (14%) — contributing 41% of total cost.[^18] Alloimmunisation increased process costs by an additional 24%. These data are directly relevant to cost-effectiveness modelling of transfusion-reducing agents (e.g., luspatercept, imetelstat) and should inform NHI reimbursement discussions in Taiwan where RBC transfusion support represents a significant ongoing resource utilisation in lower-risk MDS.

A *Blood Advances* multicenter European retrospective analysis of 121 adult acquired pure red cell aplasia (PRCA) cases — excluding PVB19-associated forms — provides important characterisation of a diagnostically challenging MDS-adjacent entity.[^19] Secondary forms accounted for 78% of cases (thymoma 23%, MDS 21%). NGS performed in half of patients revealed clonal haematopoiesis-related mutations in 36%. Cyclosporine A achieved 61% ORR (47% CR) and was more effective when initiated early. mTOR inhibitors showed 71% response rates as second-line therapy including CyA-refractory cases. Mortality reached 30%, predominantly infectious, and was highest in MDS-associated PRCA.

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

Two clinically relevant studies in MPN are published this week. The first — a phase 2 trial (NCT04988815) of ropeginterferon alfa-2b in 71 patients with pre-fibrotic PMF and DIPSS low/intermediate-1 risk fibrotic PMF or secondary MF requiring cytoreduction — reports data at a median follow-up of 119 weeks.[^2] The primary endpoints of safety and clinicohematologic complete response (CHCR) were both met. CHCR was achieved in 63.8% at week 24 (pre-PMF 74%, fibrotic PMF 20%, SMF 42.9%), 63.5% at week 48, and 70% at week 104 (pre-PMF 78.4%, fibrotic PMF 33.3%, SMF 50%). JAK2 V617F VAF reduction was observed in 53.1%, 70%, and 67.6% at weeks 24, 48, and 104, respectively; CALR mutant VAF reduction occurred in 52.6%, 42.9%, and 40%. Notably, 26% of evaluable patients at week 104 showed reduced bone marrow reticulin fibrosis — a finding of particular clinical significance given the absence of proven disease-modifying agents for early MF. Only 3 patients (4.2%) discontinued due to adverse events, and no thrombo-haemorrhagic events were observed after hydroxyurea cessation. These data establish ropeginterferon as a biologically active and well-tolerated option in the early MF spectrum — a population not served by JAK inhibitors approved for intermediate-2/high-risk disease.

A machine learning study in *BJH* develops a peripheral blood-based interpretable model for non-invasive detection of moderate-to-severe myelofibrosis in 336 JAK2 V617F-positive MPN patients (training) with external validation in 92 additional patients.[^20] The support vector machine model achieved the best discrimination (AUC = 0.916, 95% CI 0.83–0.99), with SHAP analysis identifying haemoglobin, IL-1β, and age as the most influential features. Preclinical validation confirmed IL-1β upregulation during fibrosis progression that was reversed by ruxolitinib or pegylated interferon-α but not hydroxyurea. While bone marrow biopsy remains the reference standard, a validated blood-based screening tool could enable more frequent, non-invasive MF monitoring — highly relevant for outpatient MPN surveillance at NCKUH and partner centres.

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

A 302-patient national UK cohort study published in *Blood Advances* evaluates early FDG-PET response (day +30) as a prognostic and predictive tool following second-line axicabtagene ciloleucel (axi-cel) in LBCL.[^4] Response categories by Deauville score (DS) were significantly associated with risk of progression, PFS, and OS. Patients with DS4 and DS5 responses had a 50% and 73% risk of progression by month 6, respectively, compared with 25% for DS1-3/DS4RT (DS4: HR 3.07, 95% CI 1.85–5.07; DS5: HR 5.20, 95% CI 2.95–9.16; p < 0.0001). These associations remained independently significant in multivariable analysis. A composite risk model combining DS categories with preinfusion LDH ≥2× ULN identified a high-risk group with HR 5.21 (95% CI 3.27–8.29, p < 0.001), in whom ≥70% experienced progression by month 6. The authors propose that this model should be used to identify patients for early post-CAR T intervention with available options such as bispecific antibodies, with the one-month PET scan integrated into standard CAR T follow-up protocols.

A phase 1 dose-escalation/expansion study of zanubrutinib 160 mg twice daily plus lenalidomide (BGB-3111-110) in 66 patients with R/R DLBCL is reported in *Blood Advances*.[^21] No dose-limiting toxicities occurred at any lenalidomide dose level (15, 20, or 25 mg); the recommended phase 2 dose was established at 25 mg lenalidomide daily. Grade ≥3 decreased neutrophil count occurred in 58% and decreased WBC in 29%; treatment discontinuation occurred in 11% due to toxicity. At the RP2D, the ORR was 58% and complete response rate 42%, with a median duration of response of 14.9 months and median PFS of 5.5 months (95% CI 2.9–11.1). Among the 67% of patients with non-GCB/ABC DLBCL in this cohort, efficacy in the ABC subgroup — hypothetically most susceptible to BTK inhibition — was not separately reported, which represents a key gap to be addressed in the planned phase 2 expansion.

A companion optical genome mapping study in *BJH* demonstrates that OGM outperformed conventional cytogenetics in 9.5% of 105 NHL patients by revealing cryptic rearrangements involving MYC, BCL2, and BCL6 in cases where karyotypes were non-informative or uncertain, supporting integration of OGM into routine NHL diagnostic work-up.[^22]

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

A multicenter retrospective study in *Blood Advances* reports real-world outcomes of 136 patients with R/R follicular lymphoma receiving commercial axi-cel (n = 100) or tisa-cel (n = 36) between 2021 and 2024.[^3] In the primary unweighted analysis, axi-cel was associated with significantly higher ORR (96% vs 80%, p = 0.007), CRR (88% vs 71%, p = 0.024), and longer median PFS (30.5 vs 11.9 months, p = 0.021). Median OS did not significantly differ (not reached vs 23.6 months, p = 0.061). The two groups were not balanced: axi-cel patients were younger (median 60 vs 68 years) and less frequently received bendamustine lymphodepletion. After inverse probability of treatment weighting, efficacy outcomes largely converged, but axi-cel retained significantly higher ICANS rates (42% vs 17%, p = 0.008) whereas CRS rates were comparable (75% vs 75%). Notably, PFS in both groups was inferior to phase 3 trial results, consistent with the generally higher-risk profile of real-world populations. These data reinforce the importance of patient selection, pre-treatment optimisation, and post-CAR T surveillance — particularly for neurological toxicity in patients receiving axi-cel.

A LiP-Seq (Live-cell Pick-Seq) platform paper in *Blood Advances* profiling ultra-rare persistent MCL cells after CD19 CAR T therapy identifies recurrent IFITM2 upregulation as a potential mechanism of immune evasion.[^23] Functional validation confirmed that IFITM2 overexpression conferred resistance to CAR T cytotoxicity in vitro. While methodologically exploratory, this work establishes a new paradigm for transcriptomic interrogation of viable residual cells at single-cell level frequencies as low as 10⁻⁶ — with direct implications for understanding and overcoming CAR T resistance in MCL.

Regarding hairy cell leukemia (HCL), a *Blood Advances* single-cell RNA sequencing study identifies DUSP1 overexpression as a key driver of disease persistence after cladribine therapy.[^24] A distinct HCL subcluster characterised by elevated DUSP1, FOS, and JUND was detected in all patients and expanded at relapse. Bone marrow stromal cell co-culture strongly induced DUSP1 and conferred protection from BRAF inhibitor-induced apoptosis that could be overcome by DUSP1 inhibition — identifying a potential therapeutic target for MRD eradication in HCL.

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

A large retrospective cohort study from 1,091 R/R classic Hodgkin lymphoma patients who underwent ASCT between 2010–2022 examines whether prior exposure to novel agents (BV or PD-1 monoclonal antibodies) attenuates the benefit of post-ASCT BV maintenance, published in *Blood Advances*.[^25] Among 608 propensity-matched patients, overall 3-year PFS was 73% (95% CI 70–78%) and OS 95% (95% CI 93–97%). Post-ASCT BV maintenance significantly improved PFS only among patients with no prior novel agent exposure, particularly those with ≥2 modified AETHERA risk factors (HR 0.40, 95% CI 0.25–0.65, p < 0.001). In contrast, no significant PFS benefit from BV maintenance was observed in any subgroup that had received BV or PD-1 mAb before ASCT. Patients achieving CR after one line of PD-1-based salvage had 2-year PFS of 100% (BV maintenance) vs 95% (no maintenance), p > 0.99. These data provide a compelling rationale for omitting BV maintenance in patients with prior novel agent exposure — a finding with important resource allocation implications given BV's cost and toxicity.

A comprehensive review in *Blood Advances* critically evaluates the "CHOP-plus" strategy in peripheral T-cell lymphoma (PTCL) across subtypes.[^26] The review concludes that, with the exception of ALCL — where the addition of brentuximab vedotin to CHP unequivocally improves survival — two decades of attempts to augment CHOP with novel agents have generally failed to demonstrate superiority over CHOP alone in other PTCL subtypes, often adding toxicity without benefit. The authors call for a fundamental rethinking of frontline PTCL strategy, moving away from empirical CHOP-plus approaches toward biologically rationale novel backbones.

A Chinese retrospective analysis in *BJH* compares allo-HSCT versus auto-HSCT in 81 T-lymphoblastic lymphoma patients, finding 3-year OS comparable between groups (76.0% vs 77.0%, p = 0.890) but numerically superior PFS with allo-HSCT (77.0% vs 58.7%, p = 0.141).[^27] In multivariable analysis, allo-HSCT (HR 0.34, p = 0.037) and CR at transplantation (HR 0.23, p = 0.001) were independently associated with improved PFS. Cumulative incidence of relapse trended lower with allo-HSCT (18.6% vs 30.5%, p = 0.129). These findings, while requiring prospective validation, support allo-HSCT as the preferred consolidation strategy for T-LBL patients eligible for the procedure, particularly when achieved in CR1.

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

Two important epidemiological and imaging studies enrich the myeloma landscape this week. The first, published in *Blood Cancer Journal*, is a landmark mass spectrometry-based population screening study of MGUS prevalence in 12,378 individuals aged 10–49 years from the NHANES III cohort.[^28] Using a highly sensitive serum protein assay, MGUS was detectable in 0.2% of individuals aged 10–19 years, rising progressively to 0.88%, 1.46%, and 2.82% in those aged 20–29, 30–39, and 40–49 years. The age-adjusted prevalence was significantly higher in Black compared to White individuals (1.49% vs 0.82%, p < 0.01), with the disparity emerging clearly at age 30–39 (2.94% vs 1.42%) and widening further at 40–49 (5.2% vs 2.8%). These data demonstrate for the first time that monoclonal gammopathy onset occurs in the second decade of life, with profound implications for risk stratification and early screening programmes — and confirm a substantially earlier age of MGUS onset in Black individuals.

The CASSIOPET companion study of CASSIOPEIA (NCT02541383) reports in *Blood Advances* that medullary total metabolic tumour volume (mTMTV) derived from baseline FDG-PET/CT in 195 NDMM patients treated with daratumumab-based induction/consolidation/maintenance has strong independent prognostic value for both PFS (p < 0.001) and OS (p < 0.001), complementary to R-ISS (p = 0.008 for PFS).[^29] A machine learning model achieved concordance index of 0.609 (PFS) and 0.659 (OS), identifying mTMTV as the most informative single feature. Combining R-ISS and mTMTV enabled creation of two refined risk subgroups, outperforming either parameter alone. This study establishes a rationale for integrating automated PET-based volumetric tumour assessment into routine NDMM workup in the daratumumab era.

A review in *Blood Advances* addresses the management of relapsed/refractory AL amyloidosis following frontline daratumumab-based therapy (DVCd), covering next-generation PIs, IMiDs, ASCT, BCL2 inhibitors, and emerging CAR-T and bispecific strategies — an increasingly relevant clinical challenge as more patients receive anti-CD38 in first line.[^30]

Out-of-specification (OOS) ide-cel — BCMA-directed CAR T cells not meeting commercial release criteria — demonstrated consistent clinical efficacy in 94 RRMM patients enrolled in the BB2121-EAP-001 expanded access protocol, with ORR of 75.4% (95% CI 63.1–85.2) and CRR of 35.4% (95% CI 23.9–48.2).[^31] The manufacturing success rate of ide-cel improved from 95.3% in 2021 to 96.8% in 2024, and turnaround time decreased from 31 to 24 days. These data provide reassurance that OOS products retain meaningful clinical activity and support continuation of EAP frameworks for manufacturing deviations.

## XII. Transplant & Cellular Therapy (CAR-T / HSCT / GVHD)

A prospective multicenter cohort substudy in *Blood Advances* characterises functional T-cell responses to SARS-CoV-2 mRNA vaccination in 68 patients post-allo-HCT (n = 28), auto-HCT (n = 22), and CAR-T (n = 18).[^32] mRNA vaccination induced significant increases in antigen-specific IFN-γ and TNF-α production by both CD4+ and CD8+ T cells across all groups. Functional responses correlated with baseline CD19+ B-cell count (p = 0.002) and postvaccination antibody titres (p < 0.01). Crucially, early vaccination (<4 months post-cellular therapy) was not associated with impaired functional T-cell responses compared with later vaccination (4–12 months). Functional T-cell responses were detectable even in patients with absent antibody responses. These findings support early SARS-CoV-2 vaccination after all modalities of cellular therapy and have particular relevance for immunocompromised patients managed at NCKUH and other transplant centres.

A *Blood Advances* study reveals a previously undescribed interaction between graft leukocyte telomere length (LTL) and clonal haematopoiesis (CH) on ASCT outcomes in 452 myeloma patients.[^33] Longer LTL was associated with increased PFS in patients without CH (multivariate analysis), but this protective association was absent in patients with CH. Among patients with CH, longer LTL was paradoxically associated with increased red cell distribution width before and after transplant. These data suggest that graft LTL and CH interact to influence engraftment and post-transplant haematopoiesis in ways not captured by current risk models.
