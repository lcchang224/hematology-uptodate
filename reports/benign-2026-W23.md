## Summary

The five most clinically actionable signals this week are as follows. First, a large international survey (I-WISh 2.0) published in *AJH* by Cooper et al., encompassing 1,018 patients and 431 physicians across 15 countries, reveals a striking disconnect between patient and physician perceptions of ITP burden: fatigue was ranked common and problematic by 48% of patients yet only 29% of physicians, and 28% of patients would have chosen a different treatment — data that carry direct implications for shared decision-making strategies at NCKUH and other ITP clinics in Taiwan[^1]. Second, a large real-world cohort study from the Spanish GEPTI group published in *BJH* by Pascual-Izquierdo et al. (n = 208) demonstrates that switching ITP patients from eltrombopag or romiplostim to avatrombopag achieves a 92% response rate even among those who switched due to ineffectiveness of a prior TPO-RA, with 81.2% sustaining response at a median of 56 weeks — the largest real-world evidence base for intra-class TPO-RA switching published to date[^2]. Third, a multicentre *BJH* case-control study by Cao et al. (n = 113 ITP-AIS patients, each matched to three controls) provides novel data on ischaemic stroke risk in ITP: age >60 years (OR 3.12; 95% CI 1.82–5.37), smoking (OR 5.02; 95% CI 2.31–10.88), and hypertension (OR 2.75; 95% CI 1.62–4.66) were independent predictors, and ITP-specific therapy was not itself an independent stroke risk factor[^3]. Fourth, a mechanistic *Blood* study by Lee et al. demonstrates that the fibrinogen αC region, rather than hypofibrinogenemia per se, is the critical driver of arterial thrombosis: 0 of 8 patients with αC-truncation mutations developed arterial thrombosis over 286 patient-years, and Fga270/270 mice — but not siRNA-induced hypofibrinogenemic mice — were protected from carotid FeCl₃ challenge, with the protective effect mediated via loss of αC–GPVI interaction[^4]. Fifth, a 10-year follow-up study of the DREPAGREFFE trial published in *AJH* by Bernaudin et al. demonstrates that matched sibling donor haematopoietic cell transplantation (MSD-HCT) in sickle cell anaemia children with cerebral vasculopathy produces significantly greater reductions in stenosis score (p = 0.027) and silent cerebral infarct score (p = 0.041) at Year 10 compared with standard care, with improvements in working memory (p = 0.016) and processing speed (p = 0.011) that were not detectable at shorter follow-up — strongly supporting earlier transplant consideration in eligible children[^5].

## I. Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP)

Three distinct new datasets bearing on ITP management were published this week, each addressing a different clinical domain.

The I-WISh 2.0 survey, reported in *AJH* by Cooper et al., enrolled 1,018 patients with ITP and 431 haematologists or other treating physicians across 15 countries[^1]. Approximately one-third of patients reported that ITP had a high impact on daily activities and family or social life, while 54% reported a high impact on emotional wellbeing. The most pronounced patient-physician discordance was for fatigue, reported as common and problematic by 48% of patients but only 29% of physicians — a gap with direct clinical implications, as fatigue is not routinely assessed in standard platelet-count–focused follow-up. Although 97% of patients had received treatment, 28% stated they would have selected a different therapy, and two-thirds expressed a preference for limiting duration of treatment or avoiding lifelong therapy. These patient-preference data are particularly relevant in the Taiwanese context, where TPO-RA use is increasing under NHI coverage and treatment discontinuation strategies are under discussion.

The GEPTI real-world cohort, reported in *BJH* by Pascual-Izquierdo et al., represents the largest published experience of intra-class TPO-RA switching in ITP[^2]. Of 208 patients who transitioned to avatrombopag from eltrombopag or romiplostim, 55.3% switched due to treatment ineffectiveness, 31.7% due to convenience, and 13.0% due to treatment-emergent adverse events. Among switchers for ineffectiveness with baseline platelet count <50 × 10⁹/L, 92% responded to avatrombopag (defined as platelet count ≥50 × 10⁹/L with doubling of baseline), and 81.2% maintained response over a median of 56.4 weeks. Patients who had switched for convenience or adverse event reasons also achieved durable responses (92% maintenance), and the maintenance dose was reduced from a median of 140 mg/week to 80 mg/week in this subgroup. Fifty-one percent of ineffectiveness-switchers who had been on concomitant ITP medications successfully discontinued these on avatrombopag. Fourteen patients (6.7%) discontinued avatrombopag due to adverse events, and the thromboembolism rate was 3.8%, consistent with historical TPO-RA data. Crucially, avatrombopag does not require food restrictions related to calcium-rich products, an advantage that may improve adherence in the Taiwanese population where such dietary habits are prevalent.

The multicentre retrospective case-control study by Cao et al. published in *BJH* provides the first substantial characterisation of acute ischaemic stroke (AIS) in ITP patients[^3]. Among 113 ITP-AIS patients (median age 65 years; 61 female), the median platelet count at the time of AIS was 44 × 10⁹/L (range 4–355 × 10⁹/L), illustrating that thrombotic events in ITP occur across the full platelet count spectrum. Post-infarction antithrombotic management was conservative: only 31.9% received antiplatelet therapy and 3.5% received anticoagulation. Haemorrhagic transformation occurred in 4.4%, and three patients (2.7%) died. On multivariate analysis, independent risk factors for AIS were age >60 years (OR 3.12; 95% CI 1.82–5.37; p < 0.001), diabetes mellitus (OR 1.99; 95% CI 1.13–3.52; p = 0.018), hypertension (OR 2.75; 95% CI 1.62–4.66; p < 0.001), renal failure (OR 3.00; 95% CI 1.08–8.38; p = 0.036), smoking (OR 5.02; 95% CI 2.31–10.88; p < 0.001), and hyper-LDL-C (OR 2.34; 95% CI 1.14–4.78; p = 0.02). Importantly, ITP-specific treatments were not independently associated with AIS risk, reinforcing that conventional cardiovascular risk factor management should not be neglected in ITP patients. The study highlights the need for close haematologist–vascular neurologist collaboration when AIS occurs in the setting of thrombocytopenia.

## VI. Von Willebrand Disease

A mechanistic study published in *Blood* by Malik et al. characterises the role of histidine-rich glycoprotein (HRG), a 75-kDa plasma protein circulating at approximately 2 µM, in regulating platelet adhesion and aggregation — with direct implications for understanding VWF-mediated haemostasis[^7]. HRG was shown to bind GPIbα on both resting and activated platelets and GPIIb/IIIa on activated platelets, competing directly with VWF for GPIbα occupancy and with fibrinogen for GPIIb/IIIa occupancy. In flow-chamber experiments under high-shear conditions, HRG attenuated VWF-mediated platelet string formation and platelet deposition on collagen-coated surfaces. Critically, plasma HRG levels in patients with sepsis or COVID-19 were approximately half those of healthy controls, and reducing HRG to these levels in vitro promoted a hyperreactive platelet phenotype — suggesting that HRG depletion may amplify thrombotic risk during systemic illness. Although this study focuses on HRG biology rather than VWD per se, its delineation of competitive VWF–GPIbα binding has mechanistic relevance for understanding how GPIbα receptor occupancy shapes VWF-mediated platelet tethering, and may inform future research into VWD phenotypic variability.

## VII. Thalassemia (alpha & beta; Taiwan-relevant)

A mechanistic study published in *BJH* by Thubthed et al. (Mahidol University, Thailand) investigates the molecular basis of platelet-induced neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation in β-thalassaemia/haemoglobin E (HbE) patients — a Southeast Asian disease variant highly prevalent in the region and encountered regularly at NCKUH[^8]. Three-dimensional confocal microscopy demonstrated that activated platelets induce NET formation via P-selectin/PSGL-1 and HMGB1/RAGE axis signalling, activating MEK/ERK, NOX2, MPO, and PAD4 pathways. Crucially, the study identified splenectomy as a modifier of these signalling dynamics: in splenectomised β-thalassaemia/HbE patients, the P-selectin pathway was altered such that MEK — but not ERK — inhibition (using U0126) was the effective block, and was associated with antioxidant effects and reduced neutrophil–platelet aggregate formation. In non-splenectomised patients, conventional MEK/ERK/NOX2/MPO/PAD4 pathways predominated. These findings suggest that the ROS pathway is a key regulator of NET-driven thrombosis specifically in splenectomised thalassaemia patients, and identify MEK/ROS inhibition as a potentially tractable therapeutic strategy in this high-risk subgroup. This mechanistic insight is directly relevant to the management of transfusion-dependent β-thalassaemia patients post-splenectomy at Taiwanese haematology centres, where thrombotic and cardiovascular complications represent an underrecognised late complication.

## VIII. Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia (AIHA)

A case report published in *Haematologica* by Yokoyama et al. documents sustained molecular clearance of the *MYD88* p.L265P-mutant clone with consequent resolution of cold agglutinin syndrome (CAS) following autologous stem cell transplantation[^9]. Although a single-patient observation, this represents a rare documented instance of CAS remission through clonal eradication rather than complement pathway inhibition, and is conceptually important in distinguishing CAS driven by an underlying clonal B-cell disorder (typically IgM MGUS or lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma) from idiopathic CAS. No full abstract was available at publication; the case underscores the potential for treating the underlying *MYD88*-mutant clone as a disease-modifying strategy when complement inhibitors achieve only partial control.

## IX. Red Cell Disorders (G6PD, PK deficiency, hereditary spherocytosis)

A novel metabolic mechanism underpinning thrombocytopoiesis — though not a red cell disorder per se — was reported in *Blood* by Chen et al., identifying glutamine synthetase (GLUL) as an essential regulator of megakaryocyte polyploidization and platelet production[^10]. GLUL expression was found to increase with megakaryocyte polyploidization, and GLUL deficiency impaired platelet production by allowing toxic ammonia accumulation derived from ADAR1-mediated RNA editing during DNA synthesis, leading to lysosomal and mitochondrial damage. The GLUL agonist fulvotomentoside A (FtA) promoted thrombocytopoiesis in mice after radiation and chemotherapy injury. While not yet clinically applicable, this mechanism may ultimately inform novel strategies for thrombocytopenia states arising from ineffective megakaryopoiesis — relevant to G6PD-deficient patients with haemolysis-induced reactive thrombocytosis or those with pyruvate kinase deficiency experiencing bone marrow stress.

## X. Iron Deficiency & Nutritional Anemias

A cross-sectional epidemiological study published in *AJH* by Al Ta'ani et al. examines the distribution of gastrointestinal and dietary risk factors among U.S. adults classified as having iron deficiency anaemia (IDA) across different diagnostic haemoglobin thresholds, though the abstract was not fully available at time of writing[^11]. The study's use of differential diagnostic thresholds for IDA highlights ongoing debates about the optimal ferritin and haemoglobin cut-offs for IDA definition in population surveillance — a methodological question with implications for prevalence estimates in Taiwan, where IDA remains common in reproductive-age women and elderly patients.

## XI. Thrombosis & Coagulation (VTE, APS, inherited thrombophilia)

The most mechanistically important coagulation paper this week is the *Blood* study by Lee, Ferraresso, Couzens, Jameson et al. interrogating the role of the fibrinogen αC region in arterial thrombosis in the context of hypofibrinogenemia[^4]. In a cohort of 264 patients with congenital fibrinogen disorders, 19 (approximately 7%) developed arterial thrombosis; of the 41 patients with hypofibrinogenemia specifically, 4 (approximately 10%) had arterial events. Strikingly, 0 of 8 patients carrying fibrinogen αC-region truncation mutations reported arterial thrombosis across 286 patient-years of follow-up. To dissect mechanism, two murine models were used: siRNA-mediated knockdown of fibrinogen (siFga, inducing hypofibrinogenemia without αC truncation) and Fga270/270 mice expressing fibrinogen with a truncated αC region. Whereas siFga-treated hypofibrinogenemic mice developed occlusive carotid artery thrombi comparably to controls following FeCl₃ challenge, Fga270/270 mice were protected — demonstrating that αC-region loss, rather than hypofibrinogenemia per se, is the protective determinant. Mechanistic experiments using JAQ1 anti-GPVI antibody to deplete platelet GPVI in wild-type mice showed protection from arterial thrombosis at lower FeCl₃ concentrations but not higher, and JAQ1 did not further enhance protection in Fga270/270 mice. This strongly implicates fibrinogen αC–GPVI interaction as a critical amplifying pathway for arterial thrombosis under hypofibrinogenemic conditions. These findings have immediate relevance for the clinical assessment of patients with congenital hypofibrinogenemia: the αC-truncation mutation class may represent a genuinely low-arterial-thrombosis subgroup, whereas standard hypofibrinogenemia without αC deficiency should not be assumed to be similarly protective for arterial events.

A large nationwide cohort study published in *AJH* by Lim, Gangaraju, Jafari, Yang et al. leveraged the Epic Cosmos database (2,455,332 individuals with incident cancer from 215 US health systems, 2018–2024) to characterise clinically relevant bleeding (CRB) as an underappreciated complication in cancer care[^12]. In the 12 months preceding cancer diagnosis, 5.3% of patients had CRB, identifying it as a frequent prodromal presentation. In the 12 months after cancer diagnosis, the cumulative CRB incidence was 6.3% overall and 15.0% among patients on baseline anticoagulants. On multivariable analysis, intracranial haemorrhage was associated with an HR for mortality of 4.17 (95% CI 4.11–4.24), gastrointestinal bleeding with HR 2.52 (95% CI 2.50–2.55), and other bleeding with HR 2.46 (95% CI 2.44–2.49). The study makes a compelling empirical case for integrated bleeding risk models alongside VTE prediction tools — an unmet need acknowledged in current haemostasis guidelines but not yet operationalised in most haematology-oncology programmes, including in Taiwan.

## XIII. Taiwan Clinical Context

Several findings this week carry direct relevance for clinical practice at NCKUH and affiliated centres. The I-WISh 2.0 survey data[^1] should prompt ITP clinics to formally incorporate patient-reported fatigue assessment — for example, using validated tools such as the FACT-Th6 subscale — into routine follow-up, given that fatigue is systematically underestimated by physicians. Given Taiwan's NHI coverage of eltrombopag and romiplostim for chronic ITP, and emerging coverage discussions around avatrombopag, the GEPTI cohort data[^2] provide a compelling real-world evidence base supporting intra-class TPO-RA switching as a viable strategy when ineffectiveness or adverse events complicate current therapy, with a 92% response probability even in the ineffectiveness-switching cohort.

For thalassaemia, the β-thalassaemia/HbE NET study by Thubthed et al.[^8] is directly applicable to the Taiwanese clinical context: Taiwan has an approximately 4–5% alpha-thalassaemia carrier rate and approximately 1–2% beta-thalassaemia carrier rate, with HbE/β-thalassaemia compound heterozygotes regularly managed at NCKUH. Post-splenectomy thrombotic complications are a recognised late risk, and the identification of the MEK/ROS pathway as the operative NET-amplification mechanism in splenectomised patients suggests a potential role for agents targeting this pathway in future clinical evaluation.

The DREPAGREFFE-2 10-year data[^5] — though derived from a French SCA cohort — are relevant to discussions about HSCT timing in paediatric haemoglobinopathy patients in Taiwan. MSD-HSCT remains infrequently pursued early in Taiwan, partly due to limited donor availability; however, the demonstration that neurocognitive benefits (working memory, processing speed) only become statistically apparent at 10-year follow-up, and are not detectable at 3 years, strongly supports considering transplant before irreversible neurological injury accumulates in children with abnormal transcranial Doppler velocities who have an MSD available.

A new long-term gene therapy dataset published in *Blood* by Esrick et al. from the pilot BCL11A shmiR lentiviral vector study in SCD (n = 10 infused patients; median follow-up 58 months, range 35–82 months) reports stable F-cell percentage of 71% and HbF per F-cell of 11.9 pg at ≥48 months in 9 patients, with complete elimination of vaso-occlusive episodes and no gene vector–attributed adverse events[^13]. Transduction efficiency was 93.1%, and the product was successfully manufactured for 11/11 patients in a single mobilisation cycle. One patient had low engraftment with suboptimal HbF induction. These durable long-term data have informed the initiation of a pivotal multisite phase 2 trial (NCT05353647), which will be tracked in future reports.

## XIV. Key Takeaways

The I-WISh 2.0 survey establishes quantitatively that fatigue — underreported to physicians by nearly 20 percentage points — represents the most significant unmet patient-perceived need in ITP, warranting formal incorporation into clinic assessments. The GEPTI real-world switching cohort of 208 ITP patients provides the strongest available evidence that avatrombopag achieves high and durable platelet responses even after failure of eltrombopag or romiplostim, supporting intra-class TPO-RA switching as a strategy before escalating to more immunosuppressive agents. The ITP-AIS multicentre study clarifies that conventional cardiovascular risk factors — not ITP-specific treatments — drive ischaemic stroke risk in ITP, with smoking conferring the highest odds ratio (5.02), and underscores that antiplatelet therapy can be cautiously deployed in the setting of adequate platelet counts without prohibitive haemorrhagic transformation risk. The fibrinogen αC-region study elegantly resolves a longstanding ambiguity: hypofibrinogenemia alone does not protect against arterial thrombosis; only αC-truncation does, via GPVI pathway loss — a finding that should sharpen clinical risk stratification in patients with congenital fibrinogen disorders. Finally, the 10-year DREPAGREFFE follow-up establishes that the neurocognitive and cerebrovascular benefits of early MSD-HCT in SCA children with cerebral vasculopathy are real but delayed, becoming statistically detectable only at decade-scale follow-up, which has profound implications for the timing of transplant discussions in eligible paediatric patients.
