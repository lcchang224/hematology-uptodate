## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

Two high-impact translational and clinical studies reshape the AML landscape this week. The first, published in *Blood*, addresses the clinical significance of low-level FLT3-ITD subclones — defined as "microclones" with an allelic ratio between 0.0004 and 0.05 by next-generation sequencing — in a post-hoc analysis of the BIG-1 trial (NCT02416388), which enrolled 1,733 patients with newly diagnosed AML.[^1] FLT3-ITD microclones were detected in 17.4% of patients who lacked conventional macroclones (AR ≥0.05). After adjustment for age, white blood cell count, co-mutations, midostaurin treatment, and allogeneic HCT, microclones were independently associated with increased relapse risk (sHR 1.50, 95% CI 1.18–1.91), a magnitude comparable to low-allelic-ratio macroclones (sHR 1.98, 95% CI 1.50–2.62) and high-AR macroclones (sHR 2.33, 95% CI 1.69–3.22). At 2 years, the cumulative incidence of relapse reached 45.1% (95% CI 38.3–51.6) in patients with microclones versus 29.4% (95% CI 26.6–32.3) in those without any FLT3-ITD. Notably, 41.8% of relapses in the microclone cohort were associated with macroclone emergence, indicating clonal selection under therapeutic pressure. In NPM1-mutated AML, microclones were associated with higher MRD levels and increased relapse risk but did not independently impact OS after MRD adjustment. These findings provide compelling evidence for integrating NGS-based FLT3-ITD detection — including microclone-level sensitivity — into routine diagnostic workflows and call into question the current ELN practice of risk-stratifying only by macroclone allelic ratio.

A second study in *Blood* employs patient-derived iPSCs to model aneuploid evolution in complex karyotype AML (AML-CK).[^4] Using clonal single-cell chromosome tracking, the investigators demonstrate that TP53-mutant HSPCs with del(5q) — but not TP53 mutation alone — evolve stepwise complex chromosomal abnormalities following transient mitotic inhibition, closely mirroring the karyotypic landscape of primary AML-CK specimens. Aneuploid HSPCs and primary AML-CK patient samples shared a conserved gene expression signature marked by upregulation of PTEN, cohesin subunits, and BCL2. Venetoclax eradicated BCL2-dependent aneuploid clones in this model, though resistant subclones underwent lineage switching with upregulation of alternative BCL2 family members — a mechanism with direct clinical implications for sequencing anti-apoptotic therapies.

A complementary pediatric dataset in *Haematologica* from the Italian AIEOP group retrospectively reports outcomes in 50 children and adolescents (median age 11 years) with relapsed/refractory AML (n = 32), MDS with excess blasts (n = 10), and therapy-related MDS/AML (n = 8) treated with venetoclax plus azacitidine.[^6] Overall, 60% achieved complete remission (24 MRD-negative), 18% partial remission, and 22% no response. CR rates were notably 100% in UBTF-TD myeloid neoplasms and 57–58% in KMT2A-AML and FLT3-ITD–AML (the latter receiving concomitant FLT3 inhibitors). Two-year EFS was 54.5% (95% CI 46.7–62.3) in the overall cohort and 75.8% in the 66% of patients bridged to HCT. These data establish ven/aza as an effective and tolerable bridge-to-transplant strategy in pediatric high-risk myeloid diseases, particularly in specific molecular subgroups. Also appearing in *Haematologica* this week is a drug-repurposing study identifying itraconazole as a potent OXPHOS inhibitor in AML cells through inhibition of CYP51A1 and electron transport chain complex I activity; in patient-derived xenograft models, itraconazole combined with cytarabine targeted therapy-resistant leukemic stem cells, positioning this FDA-approved antifungal as a potentially safe metabolic sensitizer warranting clinical evaluation.[^7]

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

This week brings substantive new data across multiple ALL subsets. A prospective phase 2 study (NCT05660473) published in *Blood* enrolled 167 adolescents and adults aged 14–60 years with newly diagnosed Ph-negative ALL to receive venetoclax combined with a pediatric-inspired chemotherapy backbone.[^2] The primary endpoint — MFC-MRD negativity rate — was achieved in 73.0% of the 91.0% who attained complete remission, meeting the pre-specified threshold. After a median follow-up of 19.3 months, both median OS and median disease-free survival were not reached; estimated 2-year survival rates were 78.5% and 76.7%, respectively. Propensity score-matched analysis against a historical chemotherapy-only cohort confirmed superior survival outcomes, while grade ≥3 adverse events were primarily hematologic and infectious, at rates comparable to the historical cohort. These results position venetoclax as a promising frontline addition in Ph-negative ALL, though longer follow-up and randomized validation remain necessary before practice change.

A mechanistic study in *Blood* characterizes the TCF3::HLF–IL-1β signaling axis in a novel murine model that faithfully recapitulates human TCF3::HLF B-ALL, including osteolytic bone lesions.[^8] Genetic deletion of IL1B or its receptor IL1R1 suppressed leukemic growth, reduced RANKL expression, and ameliorated bone destruction in vivo. Single-cell RNA sequencing of patient samples demonstrated strong IL1B induction at relapse compared with diagnosis, establishing the clinical relevance of this self-reinforcing inflammatory circuit. These data nominate IL-1β blockade as a potential strategy for this otherwise incurable subtype, which is exceptionally rare but uniformly fatal in current treatment paradigms.

In *Blood Advances*, a multicenter retrospective analysis of 308 children and young adults who received CD19-CAR T cells for relapsed/refractory B-ALL stratified outcomes by disease compartment at infusion.[^9] Overall survival at 24 months was 71.5% (95% CI 66.0–77.6) for isolated bone marrow disease, 66.7% (95% CI 49.3–90.2) for active non-CNS extramedullary disease, and 57.0% (95% CI 42.6–76.3) for active CNS disease (p = 0.032). Crucially, concurrent extramedullary or CNS disease with high bone marrow burden was associated with markedly inferior outcomes: for EMD patients, OS was 41.7% (high burden) versus 100% (low burden; p = 0.005), and for CNS patients, 33.3% versus 92.3% (p = 0.001). All five patients with isolated extramedullary disease without bone marrow involvement achieved complete response and did not relapse, suggesting a particularly favorable niche for CAR T therapy in this anatomical configuration. Separately, a meta-analysis in *Blood Advances* of 19 studies (N = 3,607 AYAs) confirms that pediatric-inspired regimens are associated with higher 5-year OS (RR 1.40, 95% CI 1.18–1.65) and lower treatment-related mortality (RR 0.29, 95% CI 0.09–0.90), while consolidative HSCT in first CR was associated with lower OS (RR 0.72, 95% CI 0.61–0.84) and higher nonrelapse mortality (RR 2.70, 95% CI 1.18–6.18) compared with chemotherapy alone.[^10]

A comprehensive real-world reclassification of 1,015 consecutively diagnosed B-ALL patients in *Blood Advances* using WHO-HAEM5 and ICC frameworks reduced the proportion of unclassifiable cases from 41.9% (WHO-HAEM4R) to 15.9% and 11.9%, respectively.[^11] Multivariable analysis confirmed the molecular classification as a robust independent predictor of survival. HLF-rearranged and MEF2D-rearranged B-ALL conferred persistently poor prognosis across all age groups despite allo-HCT, underscoring the clinical urgency of TCF3::HLF-targeted strategies described above. WNK1 inhibition was also identified as a potential strategy in TAL1/2-related T-ALL, with therapeutic WNK1 repression inducing polyploidy and proliferation arrest through mitotic spindle assembly defects in both cell lines and patient-derived xenograft models.[^12]

A Taiwan-relevant study from the Taiwan Pediatric Oncology Group, published in *Blood Advances*, validated a reduced FCM positivity cutoff of ≥3 events (vs the traditional ≥10) for CNS disease detection in pediatric ALL by NGS confirmation.[^13] This lower threshold significantly improved sensitivity over conventional cytomorphology (p = 0.005) and correlated with inferior EFS and higher cumulative incidence of CNS relapse. Patients treated under the TPOG-2013 ALL protocol who were CC-negative but FCM-positive had worse outcomes when assigned to less intensive CNS-directed therapy — directly actionable data for pediatric hematologists at CGMH, NTUH, CMUH, and NCKUH.

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

A narrative review in *Blood Advances* systematically evaluates venetoclax retreatment strategies in CLL, compiling 10 published abstracts or articles.[^14] Overall response rates to rechallenge ranged from 72% to 100%, and undetectable MRD rates in peripheral blood from 32% to 92%. Median PFS upon retreatment, when available, ranged from 23 to 58 months. The review highlights that fixed-duration initial regimens, compared with continuous therapy, are associated with a lower incidence of BCL2 mutations at relapse, rendering the biology more favorable for rechallenge. The depth of the initial venetoclax response and time off venetoclax (median 9 to 90 months across included studies) are identified as potential predictors of rechallenge efficacy, informing patient selection in an era where many patients are completing their first fixed-duration course and approaching progression.

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

_No substantively new clinical trial data this week._ The STAG2-GATA1 mechanistic study in *Blood* provides insight into how STAG2 loss — a recurrent MDS mutation — reprograms the GATA1 cistrome from erythroid to megakaryocytic targets via altered chromatin accessibility and enriched FLI1 binding motifs, partially explaining the dyserythropoiesis characteristic of STAG2-mutant MDS.[^15] Human models and primary MDS patient samples recapitulated these findings. The *BJH* time-driven activity-based costing study from Australia estimated the true per-unit cost of red cell transfusion in MDS at USD $440.47, with annual per-patient cost of USD $6,607.12, and found that alloimmunization increased process costs by 24% — data directly relevant to cost-effectiveness analyses of novel disease-modifying agents.[^16]

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

Two important MPN studies appear this week. In *Blood Advances*, the phase 1 portion of SENTRY/XPORT-MF-034 (NCT04562389) reports the safety and preliminary efficacy of the XPO1 inhibitor selinexor (40 or 60 mg once weekly) plus ruxolitinib in 24 JAK inhibitor–naïve patients with myelofibrosis.[^5] No dose-limiting toxicities were reported, and common adverse events were nausea (predominantly grade 1, transient, manageable with antiemetic prophylaxis), fatigue, anemia, and thrombocytopenia. The selinexor 60 mg cohort demonstrated SVR35 of 79% and TSS50 of 58% at week 24, substantially superior to the 40 mg cohort (SVR35 38%, TSS50 25%). Among patients with inadequate ruxolitinib dosing (≤5 mg twice daily) in the 60 mg cohort, SVR35 was observed in 100% (6/6) and TSS50 in 75% (3/4). These data establish selinexor 60 mg as the recommended phase 3 dose and support advancement to the randomized phase of this trial.

The bomedemstat phase 2 data in essential thrombocythemia, while the article appears in *Blood Advances* this week, substantively recapitulates findings already published and covered in the W20 report.[^17] No new efficacy data beyond those previously documented are presented; the full manuscript confirmation adds granularity on the hemorrhagic event rate (21% of participants, grade 3–4 AEs in 47%) and confirms the 72% durable platelet response and 85% VAF reduction at week 24 in evaluable participants.

A provocative perspective in *Blood Advances* makes the case for statins as potential disease-modifying partners in MPNs, summarizing 20 years of mechanistic and epidemiological evidence.[^18] Danish epidemiological data demonstrate that statins protect against MPN development in the general population; emerging clinical observations suggest they may enhance interferon-alpha efficacy — the only therapy capable of inducing MRD in a subset of MPN patients. The anti-inflammatory, antifibrotic, and antiproliferative pleiotropic effects of statins are proposed to attenuate the chronic inflammatory drive that fuels clonal expansion, fibrogenesis, and premature atherosclerosis in MPNs, with a call for prospective evaluation particularly in combination with IFN-α and ruxolitinib.

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

The full published dataset for the UK bispecific antibody real-world study (Osborne et al.) from *Haematologica* appears in this week's journal feed and was covered in W20 with complete efficacy and safety statistics.[^19] No new primary data beyond those previously documented are present. An editorial in *Haematologica* raises the question of whether universal CAR T-cell referral is absolutely required for transplant-ineligible patients with primary refractory or early relapsed DLBCL, noting that a subset of patients without standard features of high-risk disease may benefit from salvage immunochemotherapy or bispecific antibodies without meeting the full threshold for cellular therapy referral.[^20]

A site-specific molecular subtype analysis of tongue base aggressive B-cell lymphomas (n not fully available from abstract) in *Haematologica* identifies enrichment of certain genetic subtypes at this anatomical site, providing a basis for understanding the unique biology and potential targetable alterations in this rare extranodal presentation.[^21]

An early intervention toxicity management study for axicabtagene ciloleucel in *Blood Advances* reports that an institutional protocol incorporating earlier steroid use and anakinra (modified from ZUMA-1 cohort 4) significantly reduced total CRS duration (adjusted estimate −0.93 days, 95% CI −1.67 to −0.19) and ICANS duration (−2.49 days, 95% CI −4.96 to −0.03) across 195 R/R LBCL patients treated in the third-line setting without compromising overall or complete response rates or hospitalization length.[^22] Day 7 CAR-T expansion was modestly lower in the post-protocol cohort, possibly attributable to earlier steroid exposure, but this did not translate into inferior efficacy outcomes. These findings support proactive toxicity intervention and establish anakinra as a clinically safe alternative to tocilizumab in CRS/ICANS management.

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

The LEO-MER prospective cohort study in *Blood Advances* provides the most comprehensive real-world benchmark to date for systemic PTCL, reporting patterns of first-line care and outcomes in 720 patients enrolled between 2002 and 2020 and followed to 2024.[^23] CHOP-based induction predominated (70%), given as CHOP (36%), CHOP+etoposide (23%), or CHOP-like plus novel agents (11.5%, including 5% BV-CHP). Consolidative autoSCT was performed in 102 patients (14%). Patients failing EFS6 and EFS24 had dismal 5-year subsequent OS of 12% and 17%, respectively, underscoring the profound unmet need in non-ALCL subtypes. Adding etoposide to CHOP was associated with better OS specifically in ALK-negative ALCL within the LEO cohort, and BV-CHP showed a trend toward OS improvement in ALCL, providing real-world corroboration of phase 3 findings. The study strongly supports prioritization of clinical trial enrollment for non-ALCL PTCL patients failing initial therapy.

A multiomics study of 34 CTCL patients, incorporating exome, whole-genome, epigenome, bulk and single-cell RNA-seq, and TCR sequencing of 99 serial samples from skin, blood, and lymph nodes, identifies recurrent progression-associated alterations including CCR4 mutation, PI3K pathway activation, and PD-1 checkpoint evasion in *Blood*.[^24] A gain-of-function STAT3 D661Y mutation was found to enhance binding to and transcription of Rho GTPase pathway genes, previously implicated in HDAC inhibitor resistance. Recurrent EZH2 mutations at progression nominate EZH2 inhibition as a rational therapeutic strategy, and the authors propose genome-guided clinical trial design as a feasible paradigm for this heterogeneous disease.

The m6A reader IGF2BP2 is identified as an oncogenic driver in PTCL in *Blood*, with consistently high expression across common nodal PTCL subtypes confirmed in an independent RNA-seq cohort of 196 newly diagnosed patients.[^25] Mechanistically, IGF2BP2 stabilizes endosome-related transcripts (RAB4, VPS35, RAB9, STAM), enhancing endocytic internalization of membrane proteins, suppressing CD8+ T-cell cytotoxicity, and promoting tumor proliferation. Therapeutic targeting with CWI1-2 suppressed these effects in PDX models, positioning IGF2BP2 as a novel RNA modification–based therapeutic target in PTCL with dual tumor-intrinsic and immune-modulatory activity.

## X. NK/T-cell Lymphoma (Asia Focus)

A long-term outcomes analysis of 155 patients with limited-stage ENKTL at Samsung Medical Center (2000–2023) in *Blood Advances* evaluates the efficacy of a 40 Gy CCRT regimen (concurrent weekly cisplatin 30 mg/m²) followed by systemic chemotherapy.[^26] At a median follow-up of 73 months, 5-year PFS and OS were 68.0% and 82.2%, respectively. Pretreatment EBV-DNA positivity correlated with inferior PFS (p = 0.043), and patients receiving CCRT plus systemic chemotherapy had better PFS than CCRT alone (p = 0.004). The local control rate was high at 86.5%, while relapses occurred predominantly at extranodal distant sites (21.3%), suggesting that the primary failure mode under reduced-dose CCRT is systemic rather than locoregional. The median SNOT-22 sinonasal symptom score of 8 indicates minimal late sinonasal morbidity. These data support the feasibility of 40 Gy as an alternative to the guideline-recommended 50–56 Gy when combined with effective systemic therapy, potentially reducing late toxicity in Asian centers where ENKTL is more prevalent. This dataset is directly relevant to practice at NCKUH, NTUH, and CGMH given the higher ENKTL incidence in the Taiwanese population. An editorial commentary in *Haematologica* this week discusses the epigenetic landscape of ENKTL and whether EZH2 and other epigenetic vulnerabilities represent actionable targets in this disease.[^27]

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

A phase 2 study in *Blood* reports on anti-BCMA/GPRC5D bispecific CAR T cells in 37 RRMM patients with extraosseous extramedullary disease (EMD), which represents one of the highest unmet-need subsets in myeloma.[^3] At a median follow-up of 10.1 months (IQR 6.4–19.1), 36 of 37 patients (97%) achieved an overall response with concurrent MRD negativity, including 16 patients (43%) with stringent complete response. Median PFS was 5.8 months (95% CI 2.2–9.4), with median OS not reached. CRS occurred in 73% of patients, all grade 1–2; ICANS was observed in 2 patients (5%, one grade 1, one grade 3). The duality of BCMA and GPRC5D targeting appears to partially overcome the antigen-escape resistance commonly seen with single-target approaches in extraosseous EMD. The 97% ORR substantially exceeds historical single-agent bispecific antibody data in this subset and positions bispecific CAR T cells as a potential standard salvage in EMD-positive RRMM, pending longer follow-up and randomized evaluation.

A toxicity mitigation study in *Blood Advances* evaluates prophylactic dexamethasone for excessive lymphocyte expansion after ciltacabtagene autoleucel in 53 RRMM patients at a single center.[^28] Peak absolute lymphocyte count >5 × 10³/µL was independently associated with atypical neurologic events (OR 6.8, p = 0.0157) and lower OS (HR 6.2, p = 0.0106). In the pre-intervention cohort, 5 of 9 patients (55.6%) with peak ALC >5 × 10³/µL experienced atypical neurologic events, all of whom died. Implementation of a dexamethasone prophylaxis protocol in patients exceeding this threshold resulted in rapid ALC reduction, with only 1 of 7 subsequent patients experiencing an atypical neurologic event and no cilta-cel–attributable deaths in the intervention cohort. Overall survival was significantly improved in the intervention group (p = 0.0013). These data provide a practical, institution-implementable safety protocol for centers administering cilta-cel.

A comprehensive review in *Blood* examines the evolving prognostic landscape in AL amyloidosis following the ANDROMEDA results, noting that traditional baseline biomarkers (dFLC, bone marrow plasma cell burden) are losing independent prognostic value with effective daratumumab-based regimens that achieve 76% 5-year OS.[^29] The review prioritizes emerging dynamic markers — including MRD assessment by free light chain mass spectrometry and cardiac strain imaging parameters — as the most clinically actionable targets for ongoing prognostic refinement. This is particularly relevant for the significant population of AL amyloidosis patients at NCKUH who proceed to a second assessment after daratumumab induction.

A proteasome-focused study in *Blood* identifies PSMD1, a key 19S regulatory particle subunit, as overexpressed and correlated with poor prognosis in MM.[^30] Lipid nanoparticle–delivered PSMD1 siRNA reduced tumor growth and prolonged survival in MM xenograft models while sparing normal cells, and overcame both proteasome inhibitor resistance and bone marrow stromal protection. FDG-PET/MRI for response assessment after cilta-cel in RRMM is described in a *Haematologica* case series that explores the unique challenges of radiographic evaluation in this context.[^31]

## XII. Transplant & Cellular Therapy (CAR-T / HSCT / GVHD)

A single-cell RNA sequencing and flow cytometric study in *Blood* characterizes immune reconstitution in 51 HLA-matched patients receiving Orca-T (a regulatory T cell–enriched allogeneic graft) versus unmanipulated PBSC grafts.[^32] Orca-T recipients demonstrated increased frequencies of effector memory CD4+ T cells at 3 weeks post-transplant, persisting through 6 months, alongside enrichment of a novel CD4+CD25−FOXP3+Helios+ T conventional cell population. This regulatory-like subset correlated significantly with frequencies of activated CD4+ and CD8+ T cells at 3 months post-transplant, regardless of graft type, and may serve as an early predictor of long-term immune activation trajectory — a potential biomarker relevant to GVHD risk stratification.

A preclinical study in *Blood Advances* demonstrates a paradoxical finding that pharmacological BCL2 inhibition with venetoclax increased GVHD risk in murine models, whereas donor lymphocyte BCL2 overexpression reduced GVHD severity without impairing graft-versus-leukemia activity.[^33] Mechanistically, venetoclax hampered resting T-cell viability but did not affect activated lymphocytes due to reduced BCL2-BIM binding post-activation; overexpression of BCL2 conferred impaired T-cell functionality consistent with a tolerance-promoting phenotype. These unexpected findings warrant prospective investigation, particularly given the increasingly common scenario of patients receiving venetoclax-based regimens prior to transplant, and the potential for residual venetoclax to influence early post-transplant T-cell homeostasis.

A large survivorship study in *Blood Advances* of 6,981 BMT survivors from 1974 to 2014 reports a 40% higher lung cancer incidence compared with the general population (95% CI 1.2–1.7), with a significant TBI-smoking interaction (p < 0.0001).[^34] TBI-exposed patients with ≥10 years of smoking history carried an adjusted subdistribution HR of 3.9 (95% CI 3.0–8.9) compared with non-TBI never-smokers, identifying a high-risk group for whom structured pulmonary surveillance is warranted. TBI exposure in the absence of smoking did not significantly increase lung cancer risk (asHR 0.6, 95% CI 0.2–1.7).

## XIV. Taiwan Clinical Context

Several findings this week carry direct relevance for hematology practice in Taiwan. The TPOG-2013–based CNS detection study by Chou et al. from a Taiwanese center validates a reduced FCM cutoff of ≥3 events for CNS involvement in pediatric ALL, demonstrating that this threshold improves sensitivity and identifies patients needing treatment intensification — a finding immediately applicable at CGMH, NTUH, NCKUH, and CMUH pediatric hematology units.[^13] The NHI currently covers standard prophylactic CNS-directed therapy in ALL but does not separately reimburse NGS-based CSF MRD; implementation of this refined FCM protocol using existing infrastructure is immediately feasible without additional regulatory barriers.
