## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

### CXCR4-Directed Consolidation with Motixafortide: Phase 2 Trial and Single-Cell MRD Biomarker Discovery

A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2 trial (NCT02502968) published in *Blood* enrolled 128 AML patients in first complete remission to receive high-dose cytarabine consolidation with either motixafortide or placebo[^1]. The primary endpoint of relapse-free survival was not met in the overall cohort: median RFS was 10.3 months (95% CI, 8.0–12.0) in the motixafortide arm versus 11.5 months (95% CI, 8.6–24.1) in the placebo arm (log-rank p = 0.98). However, the study's most provocative contribution lies in its paired single-cell MRD (scMRD) and biomarker analyses. In the placebo group, higher CXCR4 expression was associated with significantly increased relapse risk (p = 0.02), whereas in the motixafortide arm, higher CXCR4 expression was paradoxically linked to a reduced relapse rate (p = 0.047), suggesting that CXCR4-high leukemic cells depend on niche retention for survival and are rendered therapeutically vulnerable by motixafortide. Exploratory scMRD analyses further demonstrated that higher MRD burden before consolidation was associated with inferior overall survival. These data collectively argue that patient selection based on CXCR4 expression and scMRD profiling may enable more rational, biomarker-driven use of CXCR4 antagonism in AML consolidation, even though the unselected population did not benefit.

### FLT3-ITD Microclones Confer Independent Relapse Risk: Analysis of 1,733 Patients from the Backbone Intergroup 1 Trial

A post-hoc analysis of 1,733 patients enrolled in the Backbone Intergroup 1 trial (NCT02416388), published in *Blood*, systematically evaluated the prognostic significance of low-level FLT3-ITD subclones (microclones) defined by next-generation sequencing as allelic ratio (AR) between 0.0004 and 0.05[^2]. FLT3-ITD microclones were identified in 17.4% of patients lacking conventional macroclones (AR ≥ 0.05). In multivariable analysis adjusting for age, white blood cell count, comutations, midostaurin treatment, and allogeneic HSCT, microclones were independently associated with increased relapse risk at a cause-specific HR of 1.50 (95% CI, 1.18–1.91). Low-AR macroclones carried a HR of 1.98 (95% CI, 1.50–2.62) and high-AR macroclones a HR of 2.33 (95% CI, 1.69–3.22), establishing a continuum of risk. The 2-year cumulative incidence of relapse was 42.5% for patients with macroclones, 45.1% for microclone patients, and 29.4% for those without FLT3-ITD. Among NPM1-mutated patients, both microclones and macroclones were associated with higher MRD burden and relapse risk without independent impact on OS after MRD adjustment. Critically, 41.8% of relapses in patients with microclones at diagnosis evolved a macroclone by relapse, confirming clonal evolution. These findings challenge current ELN risk stratification models, which do not account for microclone burden, and support integration of NGS-based FLT3-ITD quantification into diagnostic workflows.

### MRD Negativity in AZA/VEN-Treated AML: VENAURA Registry

A retrospective analysis of 220 newly diagnosed AML patients from the French VENAURA real-world registry who achieved composite complete remission under azacitidine/venetoclax (AZA/VEN) and underwent MRD evaluation by multiparametric flow cytometry (LAIP/LSC) and/or NPM1 RT-qPCR was published in *Leukemia*[^4]. Cumulative MRD negativity was achieved in 62–67% of patients by the respective assays. Achieving MRD negativity at any time point was strongly associated with superior OS: 31.3 months versus 15.7 months (HR 0.47; p < 0.001) by LAIP, and not reached versus 10.8 months (HR 0.38; p < 0.001) by NPM1 PCR. Dual LAIP/LSC negativity conferred the best outcomes: dual-negative patients had markedly superior OS compared with the NEG/POS subgroup (HR 0.36; p = 0.02) and the POS/NEG subgroup (HR 0.24; p < 0.001). Notably, MRD response mitigated the adverse prognostic impact of ELN 2024 intermediate- and poor-risk disease, with MRD-negative patients achieving survival outcomes comparable to favorable-risk cases. G-CSF use improved MRD conversion rates and was associated with improved OS. These data firmly establish deep MRD negativity as the dominant prognostic determinant in real-world AZA/VEN-treated AML, superseding baseline risk stratification, and support integration of MRD-adaptive strategies into routine practice.

### AML Consolidation Benchmarks Without Transplant: Haematologica Registry Data

A retrospective analysis of 362 adults with newly diagnosed de novo AML achieving CR/CRi between 2016 and 2023 who did not proceed to allogeneic SCT in first remission, published in *Haematologica*, provides important survival benchmarks for the non-transplanted population[^6]. Patients with core-binding factor AML and APL were excluded. Median relapse-free survival and overall survival for the entire cohort were 11 and 19 months, respectively. Venetoclax-containing regimens were associated with significantly lower 24-month relapse rates compared with non-venetoclax approaches in both low-intensity therapy recipients (45% vs. 68%) and intensive therapy recipients (27% vs. 49%; p-values statistically significant). These real-world benchmarks provide an important reference for designing post-remission studies and evaluating novel maintenance strategies in patients who are not candidates for or decline allogeneic SCT.

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

### Obecabtagene Autoleucel Versus Standard-of-Care Immunotherapy in Adult Relapsed/Refractory B-ALL: FELIX External Control Analysis

A propensity score–matched external control analysis contextualising the single-arm phase Ib/II FELIX study (NCT04404660) was published in *Leukemia*[^3]. The external control arm comprised historical trial patients treated with standard-of-care non-CAR T-cell therapies—blinatumomab, inotuzumab ozogamicin, or conventional chemotherapy—and was matched to the FELIX intent-to-treat population of 107 patients. Obe-cel demonstrated a significantly higher overall remission rate (67.3% vs. 51.4%; odds ratio 1.9; p = 0.026). Median OS was substantially longer with obe-cel when censoring for allogeneic HSCT (15.1 vs. 7.0 months; p = 0.0015) and without transplant censoring (13.9 vs. 7.8 months; p = 0.043). Event-free survival was significantly improved with obe-cel (median 9.8 vs. 2.5 months; p < 0.0001). Safety profiles were comparable between groups, with similar rates of grade ≥3 adverse events. These data provide important contextualisation for obe-cel's single-arm results and support its superiority over current standard-of-care options in adult relapsed/refractory B-ALL, where unmet need remains profound.

### MRD Status and Transplant Benefit in B-ALL Salvage with Blinatumomab/InO: MD Anderson Series

A retrospective analysis of 172 adults receiving blinatumomab and/or inotuzumab ozogamicin (InO)–based regimens in first salvage for relapsed/refractory B-ALL was published in *Leukemia*[^7]. On multivariate analysis, relapse within 12 months of initial diagnosis (HR 2.21; 95% CI, 1.28–4.16) and MRD positivity by multiparametric flow cytometry after cycle 1 of salvage (HR 3.06; 95% CI, 1.75–5.33) were independent predictors of inferior relapse-free survival. Critically, patients achieving early MRD negativity with late relapse (>12 months) or primary refractory disease did not benefit from consolidative allogeneic HSCT (4-year RFS 60% vs. 63% without allo-HSCT; p = 0.82), whereas allo-HSCT significantly improved outcomes in patients with MRD positivity or early relapse (4-year RFS 56% vs. 22% without allo-HSCT; p = 0.03). These findings support a risk-adapted approach to transplant consolidation in adult B-ALL: MRD status after one cycle of immunotherapy, together with timing of first relapse, can identify patients who may safely forego transplant and those for whom transplant remains essential.

### CNS Involvement in ALL: Pathogenesis and Emerging Therapeutic Targets

A comprehensive review in *Leukemia* synthesises recent advances in the mechanisms of CNS infiltration and persistence in ALL[^8]. The authors describe multiple CNS-infiltrating mechanisms including leptomeningeal spread, transchoroidal migration, and perivascular trafficking, and highlight the emerging role of the CNS microenvironment—mediated through adhesion molecules and metabolic reprogramming—in conferring therapy resistance once leukemic cells are established in the sanctuary site. Pathways with therapeutic potential include CXCR4–CXCL12 axis inhibition, BCL-2 family targeting given metabolic adaptation to the cerebrospinal fluid milieu, and CNS-penetrant bispecific antibodies. This review is particularly relevant to centers in Taiwan managing adult and pediatric ALL where CNS relapse remains a significant clinical challenge.

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

### Molecular Profile and Cardiovascular Risk in Triple-Negative Essential Thrombocythemia

A multi-center Spanish study of 241 triple-negative essential thrombocythemia (TN-ET) patients with confirmatory myeloid panel sequencing and bone marrow biopsy, published in *Leukemia*, provides the most detailed prognostic characterization of this diagnostically challenging subset to date[^12]. Pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants were identified in 19.5% of patients. Mutation carriers were significantly older (median age 66 vs. 53 years; p < 0.001) and had a higher frequency of prior thrombosis (19.6% vs. 6.5%; p = 0.013). The presence of such variants was independently associated with leukemic progression (HR 12.608; 95% CI, 2.616–60.775; p = 0.002) and inferior overall survival (HR 3.008; 95% CI, 1.43–6.327; p = 0.004). ASXL1 (p = 0.004), CBL (p < 0.001), EZH2 (p < 0.001), and ZRSR2 (p < 0.001) mutations were specifically associated with inferior leukemia-free survival. Thrombotic risk stratification by revised IPSET-thrombosis performed well in this cohort, with 10-year overall thrombosis probabilities of 30%, 15%, and 6% for high-, intermediate-, and very-low risk patients respectively (p < 0.001). Progression to myelofibrosis was rare (2.5%). These data clarify that TN-ET is biologically heterogeneous, and that molecular profiling substantially refines both leukemic and thrombotic risk in this population.

### JAK2V617F Clonal Hematopoiesis in BRCA1 vs. BRCA2 Carriers

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

### CD163/CD11c Immune Microenvironment Subgroups Define Prognosis and Potential Therapeutic Stratification in DLBCL

A spatially guided transcriptomic and multiplex immunofluorescence imaging study of 561 DLBCL cases published in *Haematologica* identifies two prognostically distinct tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) subgroups based on combined CD163 and CD11c cell density quantification[^14]. A CD163-low/CD11c-high TIME was associated with favorable prognosis, while a CD163-high/CD11c-low TIME conferred poor outcome independent of established clinicopathological risk factors. The high-risk CD163-high/CD11c-low subgroup exhibited abundant M2-like macrophage markers (MRC1, SIGLEC1, MARCO) and evidence of T-cell exhaustion including elevated LAG3 expression, whereas the low-risk TIME expressed the checkpoint inhibitor CTLA4. The authors validated these prognostic subgroups by routine pathology dual IHC staining, conferring practical implementability in clinical laboratories. Multivariate modeling confirmed independent prognostic value in external public datasets of CHOP- and R-CHOP-treated DLBCL. These findings provide a rationale for directing LAG3-targeted therapy to the high-risk CD163-high subgroup and CTLA4 inhibition to the low-risk group, representing a candidate framework for TIME-based therapeutic stratification in DLBCL.

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

### Dose-Reduced Rituximab-ISRT Achieves 100% CR Rate in Non-Gastric MALT Lymphoma: Phase II Results

A prospective phase II trial evaluating rituximab combined with 24 Gy involved-site radiotherapy (ISRT) in 60 patients with early-stage non-gastric MALT lymphoma, published in *Leukemia*, achieved a complete response rate of 100% in the per-protocol efficacy-evaluable cohort (n = 55)[^15]. At a median follow-up of 30.2 months, only one distant recurrence was observed, yielding an estimated 5-year distant recurrence rate of 1.9%. The 2-year and 4-year progression-free survival rates were 98.0% and 92.9% in the full analysis set, improving to 100% and 94.4% in the per-protocol set. Infections occurred in 23.3% of patients, including one grade 4 respiratory infection necessitating treatment discontinuation. Immunophenotypic profiling revealed near-complete B-cell depletion with compensatory NK cell expansion post-treatment, suggesting a potential mechanistic synergy between rituximab and immune effector activation. These results support combined immunoradiotherapy as a durable treatment paradigm for localized non-gastric MALT lymphoma and are especially relevant to centers in Taiwan treating the frequent orbital and salivary gland MALT presentations in this region.

### CD70 as a Biomarker and Dual CD19-CD70 CAR-T Target in Follicular Lymphoma

A translational study published in *Blood* examined the immune microenvironment at diagnosis in uniformly treated follicular lymphoma (FL) patients with extended follow-up (>11 years), comparing relapsed and relapse-free cases[^16]. Transcriptomic profiling identified enrichment of inflammatory response pathways in relapsed patients, with CD70 overexpression identified in tumor cells as well as CD8+ and CD4+ T follicular helper cells correlating with inferior progression-free survival. Functional studies demonstrated that CD70-positive tumor cells were more proliferative and induced CD70 expression in T cells, suggesting a feed-forward inflammatory loop. The investigators then engineered dual CD19-CD70 CAR T cells by co-transducing the ARI-0001 CD19 CAR with a CD27-based anti-CD70 CAR construct. Dual CAR T cells showed enhanced in vitro cytotoxicity against patient-derived FL, DLBCL, and MCL spheroids, and achieved superior disease control in FL xenograft models including complete tumor clearance in spleen and bone marrow, outperforming monotargeted CAR T cells. These preclinical data provide strong rationale for clinical development of dual-targeted CAR T therapy in high-risk FL.

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

### AVD Superior to ABVD in Older Patients with Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma: Nordic Registry Data

A large Nordic registry study of 1,569 patients aged ≥60 years with classical Hodgkin lymphoma (cHL) diagnosed between 2000 and 2021 in Sweden, Denmark, and Norway, published in *Blood Advances*, provides definitive real-world evidence that AVD (doxorubicin, vinblastine, dacarbazine) outperforms both ABVD and CHOP in this age group[^17]. In multivariable analyses, AVD was associated with improved OS compared to ABVD, while CHOP showed a trend toward inferior OS. For PFS, AVD demonstrated significantly improved outcomes relative to ABVD, while CHOP outcomes were comparable to ABVD. By omitting bleomycin, AVD substantially reduces pulmonary toxicity—a critical consideration in patients aged ≥60 years with pre-existing comorbidities and limited pulmonary reserve. These registry data, drawn from 1,569 patients across three Nordic countries, provide robust real-world confirmation that AVD should be the preferred backbone for older cHL patients and the foundational regimen for combinations with novel agents such as nivolumab (Nivo-AVD) in this population.

### PD-1 Inhibition in Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma: Comprehensive Review

A narrative review in *Haematologica* synthesizes the clinical evidence base for anti-PD-1 therapy across all treatment settings in classical Hodgkin lymphoma[^18]. The authors highlight that anti-PD-1 monotherapy achieves a 5-year OS of approximately 70% in the relapsed/refractory setting based on clinical trial data, and landmark findings from SWOG S1826 establishing Nivo-AVD as an emerging standard for advanced-stage cHL. The review emphasizes the biological rationale of near-universal 9p24.1 alterations driving PD-L1/PD-L2 overexpression on Reed-Sternberg cells, the emerging role of anti-PD-1 therapy in chemotherapy-ineligible patients, and the particular relevance for adolescent and young adult populations at risk of long-term chemotherapy and radiation toxicity.

### PLK1 Inhibition Synergizes with Brentuximab Vedotin in CD30-Positive PTCL

A study published in *Leukemia* identified polo-like kinase 1 (PLK1) inhibitors—specifically volasertib—as potent synergistic partners for brentuximab vedotin (BV) in CD30-positive peripheral T-cell lymphoma (PTCL) through a cell cycle–focused chemical screen[^19]. Mechanistically, the BV–volasertib combination induced sustained mitotic arrest through spindle assembly checkpoint activation, followed by mitotic catastrophe and apoptosis. Importantly, the combination significantly suppressed tumor growth and prolonged survival in both cell line–derived and patient-derived xenograft models. These findings highlight SAC activation as a mechanism to overcome BV resistance and underscore the therapeutic potential of combining BV with PLK1 inhibition in a disease area with severely limited treatment options.

### Pembrolizumab plus Radiotherapy in CTCL: Final Phase II PORT Trial Results

Final results of the UK NCRI PORT trial, a single-arm multicenter phase II study of pembrolizumab followed by 12 Gy in 3 fractions radiotherapy to a localized lesion in 46 patients (41 mycosis fungoides, 5 Sézary syndrome) with relapsed/refractory cutaneous T-cell lymphoma, were published in *Haematologica*[^20]. The median age was 63 years (range 24–83), with 24% at stage IV. Median follow-up was 24.8 months, with 24% of patients remaining on treatment for >1 year. Detailed ORR and survival data were not available in the submitted abstract at press time, but the trial provides important efficacy and abscopal response data for this immunotherapy-plus-radiation combination strategy in CTCL, a disease with particularly limited salvage options.

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

### Cytogenetics Override MRD Negativity Post-ASCT: Mayo Clinic Cohort of 351 Patients

A *Leukemia* study from the Mayo Clinic analyzed 351 newly diagnosed myeloma patients who achieved MRD negativity (sensitivity 2.4 × 10⁻⁶) between day 60–100 post-ASCT and had baseline cytogenetic data available[^5]. With a median follow-up of 50.4 months, standard-risk cytogenetics were present in 46% (n = 162), one high-risk cytogenetic abnormality (HRCA) in 34% (n = 118), and ≥2 HRCAs in 20% (n = 71). Despite uniform MRD negativity, several HRCAs remained associated with inferior 4-year PFS relative to the standard-risk group (4-year PFS 84%): del(17p) 56% (HR 2.89; p = 0.0001); 1q21+ 60% (HR 2.46; p = 0.0001); t(4;14) 43% (HR 3.50; p = 0.0001); MAF translocations [t(14;16)/t(14;20)] 58% (HR 2.51; p = 0.01). Patients with one HRCA had a 4-year PFS of 65% (HR 2.31; p = 0.0004) and those with ≥2 HRCAs 55% (HR 2.75; p = 0.0001). Notably, even isolated del(17p) and isolated 1q21+ retained adverse PFS impact in MRD-negative patients. These data carry a critical clinical message: MRD negativity post-ASCT is not a sufficient surrogate for cure in cytogenetically high-risk disease, and MRD-negative patients with HRCAs should remain candidates for maintenance intensification trials.

### Plasma Cell Leukemia: Comprehensive Treatment Review

A "How I Treat" article on plasma cell leukemia (PCL) published in *Blood* synthesizes current evidence for this ultra-high-risk disease defined by ≥5% circulating plasma cells meeting MM diagnostic criteria[^22]. The authors highlight distinctive clinical features including frequent extramedullary involvement, severe cytopenias, and markedly elevated β2-microglobulin and LDH. Novel therapeutic approaches discussed include BCMA-directed bispecifics and CAR T-cell therapy, GPRC5D-targeted therapy, and BCL-2 inhibition, noting promise in both primary and secondary PCL. The review acknowledges that PCL has been largely excluded from MM registration trials and that current treatment approaches are predominantly extrapolated from MM data, underscoring the critical need for dedicated prospective trials.

### Tunneling Nanotube–Mediated Filamin A Transfer Drives Myeloma Osteoclastogenesis

A mechanistic study published in *Blood* demonstrates that tunneling nanotubes (TNTs) directly connect multiple myeloma cells to osteoclast precursor cells, facilitating transport of filamin A (FLNA) as a major cargo protein[^23]. FLNA acts as a molecular clutch linking extracellular matrix–bound MAC1 to the cytoskeleton, activating Rho and MAPK signaling and promoting F-actin polymerization, which enhances osteoclast differentiation by modulating cellular stiffness, traction force, and deformability. FLNA directly binds vinculin and promotes podosome formation, thereby augmenting bone resorption capacity. In the Vk*MYC myeloma mouse model, administration of the TNT inhibitor latrunculin B disrupted FLNA transport and alleviated osteolytic bone disease. These findings identify TNT-mediated intercellular protein trafficking as a novel therapeutic vulnerability in myeloma bone disease, complementing existing anti-resorptive strategies.

### Functional T-Cell Profiling Predicts Response to Bispecific T-Cell Engagers in Myeloma

A *Leukemia* study developed an image-based, high-content ex vivo assay using patient-derived bone marrow mononuclear cells exposed to teclistamab or talquetamab to predict clinical response to bispecific T-cell engagers (TCE)[^24]. Multiplex immunofluorescence enabled single-cell quantification of plasma cell lysis, T-cell expansion, T-cell morphology, and spatial T-cell–plasma cell engagement across patient samples. Three functional response phenotypes were identified: non-responder, cytotoxic, and cytotoxic-expansive. A morphologic T-cell activation trajectory from resting to polarized and effector states was delineated, with progression along this trajectory reflecting ex vivo plasma cell killing efficacy and distinguishing productive from abortive immunological synapses. Crucially, ex vivo response phenotypes stratified clinical response and time-on-therapy in patients receiving TCE monotherapy. Given that approximately 40% of patients are primarily refractory to BCMA- or GPRC5D-directed TCE, this functional imaging platform represents an important advance toward prospective biomarker-guided patient selection for bispecific therapies.

### CMV Reactivation During Bispecific Antibody Therapy in Multiple Myeloma

A study in *Blood Advances* characterized cytomegalovirus (CMV) reactivation in myeloma patients receiving bispecific antibodies[^25], an increasingly recognized infectious complication of prolonged T-cell engagement and B-cell depletion. Although the full abstract was not available at press time, this represents an important clinical safety signal for hematologists initiating BCMA- or GPRC5D-directed bispecific therapy, particularly in CMV-seropositive patients, and adds to emerging guidance on prophylaxis strategies in this immunocompromised population.
