## Summary

Five signals define this week's literature. **(1)** A randomized multicenter noninferiority trial in *Blood* demonstrates that low-dose cytarabine/mitoxantrone/G-CSF induction achieves CR/CRi rates of 95.1% — statistically non-inferior to standard-dose chemotherapy (95.3%) — with significantly shorter neutrophil and platelet recovery times and comparable 4-year OS (81.3% vs. 83.6%, p = 0.611) in pediatric AML, potentially redefining the intensity floor for this population[^1]. **(2)** Full publication of the TRANSFORM-1 phase III trial in *Blood* demonstrates that navitoclax plus ruxolitinib achieves SVR35 at Week 24 in 63.2% of JAK inhibitor–naïve myelofibrosis patients versus 31.5% with ruxolitinib alone (p < 0.0001), establishing a new efficacy benchmark for frontline combination therapy though at the cost of substantial hematologic toxicity[^2]. **(3)** A 644-patient genomic analysis in *Blood* of secondary myelofibrosis introduces the MYSEC-mPM, integrating molecular mutations (UTS variants conferring median OS 4.1 years vs. 8.4 years for ASXL1-only) into a refined prognostic model that significantly outperforms the clinical MYSEC-PM alone[^3]. **(4)** A *Blood* multiomics study identifies a STAT1-dependent glucocorticoid receptor deficit in expanded CD28+CD8+ effector-memory T cells as a key mechanism of steroid-refractory acute GVHD, demonstrating that ruxolitinib reverses this phenotype in vivo, and that pre-treatment CD28+CD8+ Tem cell counts predict steroid refractoriness comparably to established clinical classifiers[^4]. **(5)** Updated consensus guidelines for hairy cell leukemia and HCL variant published in *Blood* reinforce BRAF-V600E testing as a diagnostic cornerstone and incorporate BRAF inhibitor–based therapy as a standard option, while delineating distinct management pathways for HCLv given its lack of BRAF mutation and inferior durability to purine analogue monotherapy[^5].

## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

### Low-Dose vs. Standard-Dose Induction in Pediatric AML: A Randomized Noninferiority Trial

A pivotal multicenter randomized trial from China, now published in *Blood*, addresses a longstanding question in pediatric oncology: whether chemotherapy dose intensity can be reduced in AML induction without sacrificing efficacy[^1]. The trial enrolled patients younger than 18 years with AML and randomized them to a low-dose chemotherapy (LDC) regimen consisting of cytarabine, mitoxantrone or idarubicin, and granulocyte colony-stimulating factor, versus a standard-dose chemotherapy (SDC) regimen of cytarabine, daunomycin, and etoposide. All patients received standard postremission consolidation including hematopoietic stem cell transplantation as appropriate. The primary endpoint — composite CR/CRi rate — was 95.1% in the LDC arm and 95.3% in the SDC arm, meeting the predefined noninferiority margin. Measurable residual disease below 0.1% after induction II was achieved in 87.4% and 87.1% of patients in the LDC and SDC arms respectively, confirming depth of response was also equivalent. Long-term survival outcomes were similarly non-inferior: 4-year OS was 81.3% in the LDC arm versus 83.6% in the SDC arm (p = 0.611), and 4-year EFS was 61.5% versus 63.1% (p = 0.832). Crucially, the LDC regimen was associated with significantly shorter median time to both neutrophil and platelet recovery. The accompanying *Blood* editorial by Kotecha highlights the clinical and quality-of-life implications of a less toxic induction that does not compromise remission depth, positioning this regimen for prospective evaluation in international pediatric cooperative group contexts[^6].

### CK2-DEK Axis as a Leukemia Stem Cell Dependency: Mechanistic and Therapeutic Insights

A mechanistic study in *Blood* identifies DEK phosphorylation — specifically at Ser301/303/306/307 mediated by casein kinase 2 (CK2) — as a previously unrecognized post-translational modification that sustains leukemia stem cell (LSC) maintenance in AML[^7]. Using phosphoproteomic profiling, MLL-AF9 and HOXA9/MEIS1 murine AML models, and patient-derived xenografts, the investigators demonstrate that DEK deficiency impairs LSC maintenance and enhances chemosensitivity to azacitidine plus venetoclax. Conversely, DEK overexpression conferred robust resistance to this combination. Mechanistically, phosphorylated DEK recruits GABPA to upregulate the oncogenic cofactor PBX3, sustaining the leukemogenic transcriptional program. Importantly, the clinical-stage CK2 inhibitor CX-4945 selectively depleted LSCs while sparing normal hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells in vivo, and the combination of CX-4945 with venetoclax produced synergistic anti-AML effects both in vitro and in vivo. These findings open a pathway to a biomarker-selected — DEK-phosphorylation–positive — intervention strategy for AML, particularly in the context of venetoclax resistance.

### p300/CREBBP Catalytic Inhibition: Paradoxical Activation of Interferon Programs in AML

A mechanistically sophisticated study in *Blood* reveals that inhibition of the lysine acetyltransferase (KAT) activity of p300/CREBBP paradoxically increases transcription at a subset of AML regulatory elements[^8]. Using time-resolved nascent transcription profiling combined with genome-wide CRISPR-Cas9 and Perturb-seq screens, the investigators show that KAT inhibition retains p300/CREBBP at interferon regulatory factor (IRF) motif-enriched loci, promotes cooperative transcription factor assembly, and enables STAT1 recruitment to interferon-stimulated gene (ISG) loci. Consequentially, ISG transcription is converted to an active state that induces cell-cycle arrest, differentiation, and apoptosis in AML cells. Therapeutically, combining KAT inhibition with interferon-alpha augmented ISG expression, synergistically drove AML cell death in vitro, and significantly extended survival in both AML xenograft and murine models. These findings suggest that p300/CREBBP inhibitors may be most rationally deployed in combination with type I interferons in AML, a hypothesis with immediate translational implications for early-phase trial design.

### FGF18-Mediated Stromal-Leukemia Crosstalk Drives AML Progression and Immune Evasion

A *Blood* study employing single-cell RNA sequencing of the AML bone marrow niche identifies fibroblast-derived FGF18 as a novel stromal cytokine promoting AML progression through FGFR3 signaling on leukemic cells[^9]. Fibroblast-specific Fgf18 depletion markedly delayed disease development and improved survival in mice. Mechanistically, FGF18/FGFR3 signaling activates AKT-mTOR in AML cells, inducing IL-6 production that both reinforces leukemic signaling autocrinally and activates fibroblast JAK-STAT3 paracrinely, forming a feed-forward loop that suppresses CD8+ T-cell effector function. Elevated FGF18 expression correlated with poor prognosis in clinical AML datasets, and a neutralizing FGF18 antibody disrupted this loop, restored CD8+ T-cell function, and synergized with anti-PD-1 therapy in vivo. This work identifies the FGF18–FGFR3 axis as both a prognostic biomarker and a potential therapeutic target with particular relevance to immunotherapy combinations.

### Menin Inhibitor Resistance via JUND: A Mechanistic Vulnerability

A *Blood* study demonstrates that menin-MLL inhibitors — including the clinically approved revumenib — also disrupt menin-JUND interactions in MLL-rearranged (MLLr) AML and ALL, leading to paradoxical upregulation of JUND chromatin occupancy and induction of resistance-associated target genes[^10]. CRISPR-mediated JUND inactivation or pharmacological inhibition with JNK inhibitors synergistically enhanced the anti-leukemic effects of menin-MLL inhibitors, reducing proliferation, inducing cell-cycle arrest, and promoting apoptosis. Immunocompromised mice engrafted with JUND-deficient leukemia cells showed reduced tumor burden compared to wild-type controls. These findings identify JUND as a functionally relevant resistance mechanism to menin inhibition and nominate JNK inhibitor combination strategies as a hypothesis for clinical testing, particularly relevant given the growing adoption of revumenib in MLLr and NPM1-mutated acute leukemia at NCKUH and nationally.

### Oral Decitabine/Cedazuridine plus Venetoclax in Older/Unfit AML: Extended Follow-Up

Long-term follow-up data from a single-center phase II study, published in *Haematologica*, confirm the durability and safety of oral decitabine/cedazuridine (ASTX727) plus venetoclax in 68 older or unfit patients with newly diagnosed AML (median age 79 years)[^11]. With a median follow-up of 32 months, overall response rate was 75% in de novo AML versus 58% in secondary AML; MRD negativity among responders was 58% and 56% respectively. Median OS was 12.7 months (95% CI 9.1–20.3) in de novo and 7.2 months (95% CI 3.6–29.9) in secondary AML (p = 0.61), with no statistically significant differences in relapse-free survival (9.2 vs. 11.7 months, p = 0.56) or non-relapse mortality between groups. The fully oral regimen requires no intravenous access and is notable for practicality in frail patients, though the survival gap in secondary AML remains an unmet need.

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

### FATP2-Mediated Lipid Metabolism as a CAR-T Resistance Mechanism in TP53-Mutant B-ALL

A *Leukemia* study identifies FATP2 (encoded by SLC27A2) as a leukemia-intrinsic mechanism of CAR-T resistance in TP53-mutant B-ALL, discovered through genome-wide CRISPR/Cas9 screening in matched CAR-sensitive (TP53 wild-type) and CAR-resistant (TP53-mutant) CD19+ cell lines[^12]. FATP2-expressing TP53-mutant B-ALL cells use exogenous lipid uptake to fuel fatty acid oxidation and survive CAR-T attack; pharmacologic inhibition of neutral lipolysis and CPT1 restored CAR-T sensitivity in patient-derived xenografts. High SLC27A2 expression in pediatric B-ALL patients correlated with inferior survival outcomes following conventional chemotherapy. These findings suggest that metabolic reprogramming in the p53-deficient niche represents a tractable vulnerability for overcoming CAR-T resistance in a high-risk ALL subset.

### Lineage Switch in Acute Leukemia: Biology, Detection, and Management

A comprehensive *Leukemia* review addresses lineage switch in the era of antigen-targeted therapies, noting that this phenomenon — most classically KMT2A-rearranged B-ALL relapsing as AML — is increasingly recognized with broader use of CD19-directed CAR-T cells and bispecific T-cell engagers[^13]. The authors provide recommendations for immunophenotypic and molecular monitoring designed to capture lineage switch events early, emphasizing that the underlying genetic driver(s) must be conserved to confirm clonal relationship. Patients with lineage switch have uniformly poor outcomes, and no standard therapeutic approach exists. The review is particularly timely given Taiwan's expanding CAR-T programs, where KMT2A-rearranged cases — a proportion of which are treated at NCKUH and NTUH — carry non-trivial lineage-switch risk following CD19-targeted salvage.

### Inotuzumab Ozogamicin Triple Combination in BCP-ALL: Preclinical Synergy Including t(17;19)

A *Haematologica* preclinical study demonstrates that inotuzumab ozogamicin (INO) combined with the p38 inhibitor BIRB 796 and venetoclax exerts synergistic cytotoxicity in BCP-ALL cell lines and patient-derived xenografts, including the very high-risk t(17;19) subtype[^14]. Mechanistically, p38 inhibition selectively potentiated INO-induced DNA strand cleavage and enhanced γH2AX expression, while INO augmented venetoclax-mediated mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization. The triple combination produced complete remissions and long-term leukemia-free survival in 5 of 7 NSG mice engrafted with t(17;19) PDX cells (71%). These findings provide a mechanistic rationale for clinical exploration of p38 inhibition as a combinatorial partner for ADC-based BCP-ALL therapy.

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

### ASC4FIRST Tolerability Analysis: Asciminib Superiority Confirmed Across Both Strata

An exploratory post-hoc tolerability analysis of the ASC4FIRST phase III trial, published in *Haematologica*, demonstrates that within both the imatinib and the second-generation (2G) TKI strata, patients receiving asciminib experienced fewer difficult-to-tolerate adverse events — including gastrointestinal toxicity, rash, and pleural effusion — and fewer adverse events leading to dose modifications or discontinuations compared with the respective investigator-selected TKI comparator[^15]. The median onset of AEs leading to dose modification occurred later with asciminib versus investigator-selected TKIs in both strata, and the median duration of dose modification was shorter. These tolerability data reinforce asciminib's benefit-risk profile as frontline therapy for newly diagnosed CML-CP, complementing the previously reported efficacy superiority. This analysis is clinically relevant to Taiwan, where asciminib (Scemblix) received NHI reimbursement consideration and tolerability-driven switch decisions are common in routine practice at NCKUH and affiliated centers.

### Immunological Mechanisms and Therapeutic Advances in CML: 2026 Review

A *Leukemia* review series inaugurates a 2026 CML update, examining immunological pathways contributing to CML development, progression, and control, with particular attention to barriers to treatment-free remission (TFR)[^16]. The review discusses the challenge of pediatric CML management, noting that adult TKI guidelines do not account for developmental biology differences in younger patients. The analysis of immunoregulatory pathways — including NK cell dysfunction, regulatory T-cell expansion, and leukemic immune evasion — positions immune restoration as a rational TFR-enabling strategy, setting the stage for upcoming immunotherapy combination trials.

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

### AIHA in CLL: Prognostic Impact Attenuated by Targeted Therapies

A retrospective multicenter analysis of 1,404 CLL patients published in *Haematologica* demonstrates that autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AIHA) complicates approximately 10.4% of CLL cases, with 69.2% classified as warm-antibody AIHA[^17]. CLL-AIHA patients were enriched for male sex, advanced stage, IGHV4-34 usage, and DNMT3A mutations (the latter also associated specifically with AIHA development), while MYD88 mutations were enriched in the cold-antibody AIHA subset. AIHA conferred a significantly adverse prognostic impact on CLL outcomes overall; however, this effect was markedly attenuated in patients who received BTK inhibitor or BCL2 inhibitor–based targeted therapies. Unmutated IGHV status, a strong adverse prognostic factor in the overall cohort, did not predict inferior outcomes among patients with AIHA specifically. These findings argue for routine AIHA screening in high-risk CLL and favor early deployment of targeted therapies to mitigate AIHA-related survival detriment.

### MYD88 Variants in CLL: Distinct Biological Behaviors

A *Leukemia* study characterizes MYD88 variants across a large CLL cohort and demonstrates that different MYD88 mutations exhibit distinct biological behaviors and prognostic implications, unlike the relatively uniform pathogenic role of MYD88 L265P in Waldenström macroglobulinemia[^18]. This study adds molecular granularity to CLL genomics relevant to variant-specific risk stratification and raises the question of whether MYD88 mutation type should influence therapeutic sequencing — particularly given the cold-AIHA enrichment noted in the AIHA analysis above.

### Outcomes After BTK Inhibitor Discontinuation Due to Adverse Events

A *Leukemia* multicenter real-world analysis examines outcomes in CLL patients who discontinued covalent BTK inhibitors specifically due to adverse events, a clinically common scenario given the long treatment durations required[^19]. The analysis characterizes subsequent treatment selection, response rates, and survival after BTK inhibitor cessation for toxicity versus progression, providing data to support clinical decision-making at NCKUH when managing patients who develop cardiac arrhythmias, bleeding, or other TKI-associated complications. Full outcome data are awaited in the published manuscript.

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

### Spatial Bone Marrow Architecture as a Dynamic Biomarker in MDS

A *Leukemia* study employing whole-slide multiplex immunofluorescence imaging with single-cell phenotyping of bone marrow biopsies from 99 MDS, precursor, and normal control samples introduces the Microarchitectural Perturbation Score (MDS-MAPS)[^20]. Derived from 82 cellular and spatial features across diagnostic biopsies, the MDS-MAPS discriminated remission from active MDS more accurately than blast percentage alone (AUC 0.883 vs. 0.660 in leave-one-patient-out cross-validation) and distinguished low-blast MDS from clonal cytopenia of undetermined significance (AUC 0.815). Mixed-effects modeling confirmed that MDS-MAPS decreased during remission independently of blast burden and re-emerged at relapse. These findings validate spatial bone marrow architecture as a tissue-state biomarker that could complement MRD and blast-based disease monitoring in MDS clinical trials and, eventually, routine practice.

### Single-Cell DNA Methylation Uncovers Epigenetic Pathways in MDS-to-AML Transformation

A *Leukemia* single-cell DNA methylation study maps epigenetic heterogeneity across MDS and transformation to AML, identifying discrete epigenetic trajectories that parallel clonal evolution[^21]. The analysis resolves cell-type-specific methylation states not captured by bulk sequencing, identifying candidate epigenetic drivers of transformation. This platform may ultimately inform biomarker-stratified selection for hypomethylating agent versus intensive induction therapy in high-risk MDS.

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

### TRANSFORM-1 Full Phase III Results: Navitoclax plus Ruxolitinib in Frontline Myelofibrosis

The full peer-reviewed publication of the TRANSFORM-1 phase III trial in *Blood* provides definitive data on navitoclax plus ruxolitinib in JAK inhibitor–naïve myelofibrosis[^2]. A total of 252 patients with intermediate-2 or high-risk disease (>80% intermediate-2, ~50% high-molecular risk) were randomized 1:1 to navitoclax (starting at 100–200 mg/day) plus ruxolitinib versus placebo plus ruxolitinib, with a median follow-up of 20.3 months. The primary endpoint — SVR35 at Week 24 — was achieved by 63.2% of patients in the navitoclax arm versus 31.5% in the placebo arm (p < 0.0001). SVR35 at any time was 76.8% versus 44.1% (nominal p < 0.0001). Mean change in Total Symptom Score at Week 24 was −10.2 in the navitoclax arm versus −11.6 in the placebo arm (p = 0.2852), representing the one notable negative finding — symptom burden improvement was not significantly better with the combination. A ≥20% variant allele frequency reduction was achieved in 58.5% (95% CI 49.0–67.5) with navitoclax versus 45.5% (95% CI 36.4–54.8) with placebo. Grade 3/4 thrombocytopenia occurred in 54.0% versus 19.2% and grade 3/4 neutropenia in 40.3% versus 8.8% in the combination versus placebo arms respectively; diarrhea of any grade occurred in 41.9% versus 16.8%. Overall survival data remain immature. These data establish navitoclax plus ruxolitinib as the most efficacious frontline spleen reduction strategy to date, though the hematologic toxicity burden will require careful patient selection and proactive dose management.

### MYSEC-mPM: Genomic Profiling Refines Prognosis in Secondary Myelofibrosis

A 644-patient next-generation sequencing analysis from the MYSEC cohort, published in *Blood*, defines the molecular landscape of post-PV and post-ET secondary myelofibrosis (SMF) and builds upon the established MYSEC-PM prognostic model[^3]. Overall, 66.6% of patients harbored at least one cancer gene variant, with ASXL1, TET2, and DNMT3A most frequently involved. Two molecular profiles significantly influenced OS (p < 0.001): U2AF1, TP53, or SRSF2 variants (UTS; present in 9.3% of patients) carried a median OS of 4.1 years, while ASXL1 mutations without UTS (25.3% of patients) carried a median OS of 8.4 years. Integrating these signatures into a penalized Cox regression model generated the MYSEC-mPM, allocating 582 patients into four risk groups with median OS of 18.0 years (low, score <14), 8.8 years (intermediate-1, score 14–16), 4.6 years (intermediate-2, score 17–18), and 1.9 years (high, score ≥19), all highly significant (p < 0.001). A further karyotype-enhanced MYSEC-kmPM incorporating complex/monosomal karyotype in 381 evaluable patients provided additional stratification. These data make a compelling case for reflex NGS panel testing at SMF diagnosis to guide transplant timing decisions.

### Fibrocytes as Major Collagen Producers in JAK2V617F-Mutated Myelofibrosis: Pitavastatin as an Antifibrotic Strategy

A *Blood* preclinical study challenges the prevailing model of bone marrow fibrosis in JAK2V617F-mutated MPN, demonstrating that fibrocytes — monocyte-lineage hematopoietic-derived collagen-producing cells — account for nearly two-thirds of collagen-producing cells in bone marrow biopsies from patients with JAK2V617F-mutated MF, with myofibroblasts representing a smaller subset[^22]. High-throughput drug screening of bone marrow–derived fibrocytes from Jak2V617F transgenic mice identified statins as inhibitors of fibrocyte proliferation. In vivo, pitavastatin reduced fibrocyte numbers, ameliorated bone marrow fibrosis, and improved anemia in Jak2V617F mice. Pitavastatin also decreased TGF-β1 production by neoplastic fibrocytes, reducing myofibroblast expansion. Peripheral blood fibrocytes from JAK2V617F-mutated MPN patients were similarly sensitive to pitavastatin in vitro. Given that pitavastatin is an inexpensive, widely available, and generally well-tolerated agent with an established safety profile, these findings provide a strong rationale for a clinical proof-of-concept trial of pitavastatin in JAK2V617F-mutated MF, either alone or combined with JAK inhibition.

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

### Immunodeficiency-Associated Primary CNS Lymphoma: International Multicenter Prognostic Model

A 308-patient retrospective international cohort study published in *Blood* defines the clinicobiological features of immunodeficiency-associated primary CNS lymphoma (ID-PCNSL) across 23 sites in 7 countries[^23]. Preexisting immunodeficiency included transplant immunosuppression (41.2%), autoimmune disease treatment (36.7%), and HIV infection (21.7%). All tumors were DLBCL, with EBV detected in 79.2%. Immune reconstitution combined with rituximab plus methotrexate-based chemotherapy was associated with the highest response rates and prolonged PFS regardless of immunodeficiency subtype or EBV status. Median OS was 54 months. Multivariable Cox regression identified three independent prognostic factors: age per year (HR 1.05, 95% CI 1.02–1.07, p < 0.001), Karnofsky performance status <70 (HR 3.10, 95% CI 1.67–5.87, p < 0.001), and EBV positivity (HR 3.26, 95% CI 1.47–7.33, p = 0.004). A three-point prognostic score yielded dramatically different median survival times: 135 months for 0–1 adverse markers, 29 months for 2 markers, and 3 months for 3 markers (p < 0.0001). This score outperformed existing PCNSL models (MSK and IELSG) designed for immunocompetent patients, providing a practical stratification tool for this clinically challenging and growing population, including post-transplant lymphoproliferative disease patients in Taiwan's expanding solid organ transplant programs.

### CD79B Expression Gradient in DLBCL: Implications for Polatuzumab Vedotin Targeting

A *Haematologica* immunohistochemical study of 590 de novo DLBCL cases plus an independent validation cohort of 272 cases demonstrates a significant CD79B protein expression gradient according to cell-of-origin subtype: ABC-DLBCL showed the lowest CD79B expression, followed by GCB-DLBCL and dark-zone signature-positive (DZsigpos) cases in ascending order (p < 0.0001)[^24]. Single-cell proteomic and transcriptomic analyses of 2,447 normal germinal center B-cells confirmed a progressive decrease in CD79B expression during terminal differentiation, explaining the observed COO-dependent gradient. These findings complicate the simple assumption that polatuzumab vedotin — a CD79B-targeted ADC — preferentially benefits ABC-DLBCL due to higher target expression. Rather, they suggest that ABC-DLBCL derives its clinical benefit from pola through orthogonal mechanisms, and that CD79B expression-based patient selection for ADC therapy may require nuanced re-evaluation.

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

### Diagnosis-to-Treatment Interval as an Independent Prognostic Factor in Follicular Lymphoma

A *Blood* multicenter study using international discovery and validation cohorts demonstrates that a short diagnosis-to-treatment interval independently predicts inferior outcomes in patients with follicular lymphoma treated with immunochemotherapy, with prognostic value that exceeds established indices including FLIPI[^25]. The analysis has immediate implications for clinical trial design — short diagnosis-to-treatment interval may be a confounding variable that should be incorporated as a stratification factor — and for clinical practice, where watchful waiting remains standard in low-grade FL but the speed of progression to treatment eligibility carries prognostic meaning.

### Updated Consensus Guidelines for Hairy Cell Leukemia and HCL Variant

Comprehensive updated consensus guidelines for HCL and HCL variant, convened by the Hairy Cell Leukemia Foundation and published in *Blood*, incorporate the diagnostic and therapeutic advances since the 2017 edition[^5]. HCL, caused by BRAF-V600E in >95% of patients, is characterized by excellent responses to purine analogues and is now also amenable to BRAF inhibitor–based targeted therapy (vemurafenib, dabrafenib). HCLv, which lacks BRAF V600E, requires combined purine analogue plus rituximab therapy and generally shows less durable responses. The guidelines address emerging options including anti-CD22 immunotoxins (moxetumomab pasudotox) and outline criteria for treatment initiation, relapse management, and MRD assessment. Clinicians at NCKUH encountering the rare HCLv — which may be phenotypically confused with splenic marginal zone lymphoma — should note the distinct management pathway mandated by the guidelines.

### Rapid Glofitamab Ramp-Up: Feasibility and Safety

A *Leukemia* study evaluates a condensed step-up dosing schedule for glofitamab in patients with relapsed/refractory B-cell NHL, demonstrating the feasibility and safety of rapid ramp-up as a strategy to reduce tocilizumab administration and hospitalization burden associated with cytokine release syndrome prophylaxis[^26]. These data are operationally relevant to centers initiating glofitamab programs.

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

### Anti-CD47/Pembrolizumab Combination in Relapsed/Refractory Classic Hodgkin Lymphoma

A *Haematologica* study reports early data from the combination of the anti-CD47 antibody magrolimab with pembrolizumab in relapsed/refractory classic Hodgkin lymphoma (cHL)[^27]. The rationale is mechanistically compelling: CD47 blockade enables macrophage-mediated phagocytosis of tumor cells while PD-1 blockade restores T-cell cytotoxicity, with preclinical synergy in lymphoma models. Full response and safety data from this study are anticipated and will be important given the established efficacy of pembrolizumab monotherapy in relapsed cHL and the need for additional options in brentuximab vedotin/PD-1 inhibitor–refractory disease.

### Single-Cell Atlas of Cutaneous T-Cell Lymphoma Identifies Therapeutic Vulnerabilities

A *Blood* single-cell RNA sequencing atlas of CTCL incorporating >2 million cells from 116 patients — the largest CTCL single-cell resource to date — reveals recurrent transcriptional programs in malignant T cells, a subset driven by GATA-3–dependent pathways enriched in large cell transformation and advanced disease[^28]. The tumor microenvironment is characterized by exhausted cytotoxic T cells constrained by robust regulatory T-cell infiltration, transcriptionally polarized monocytes/macrophages, and cancer-associated fibroblasts. Therapeutically actionable targets identified include HDAC, XPO1, CDK9, JAK/CSF1R, and IKZF1/IKZF3 — all of which have approved or investigational agents — and PD-1 checkpoint pathways. The construction of this atlas provides a community resource for rational combinatorial strategy design in advanced-stage CTCL, where complete durable responses remain rare.
