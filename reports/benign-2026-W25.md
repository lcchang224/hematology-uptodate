## Summary

The five most clinically actionable signals this week are as follows. First, a multicentre Chinese retrospective study in *BJH* by Xu et al. (n = 132) reports the largest Asian real-world cohort of long-term emicizumab prophylaxis for haemophilia A, with a model-based annualized bleeding rate (ABR) of 0.81 (95% CI 0.62–1.07) over a median 26 months, 95.9% target joint resolution, and no thromboembolic or fatal events — data that are directly applicable to NHI-covered emicizumab programmes in Taiwan[^1]. Second, a *BJH* commentary by Masias and an accompanying cohort study by Javed et al. highlight that silent cerebral infarctions (SCIs) occur in iTTP survivors at a higher prevalence and younger age than the general population, with prior stroke, cardiovascular disease, and diabetes as independent risk factors — establishing a surveillance framework with direct implications for long-term iTTP follow-up[^2]. Third, a *Haematologica* report from the CLIMB THAL-111 and CLIMB-131 studies by Sheth et al. demonstrates that exagamglogene autotemcel (exa-cel) in transfusion-dependent β-thalassemia not only restores transfusion independence (91% at data cut) but also corrects ineffective erythropoiesis and normalises iron homeostasis: hepcidin levels normalised in all transfusion-independent participants, 68% discontinued iron removal therapy, and serum ferritin, liver iron concentration, and cardiac T2* all improved — confirming the downstream metabolic benefits of CRISPR-based haemoglobin reactivation[^3]. Fourth, joint ASH/ISTH 2026 guidelines for anticoagulant prophylaxis in paediatric VTE, published in *Blood Advances* by Betensky et al., issue 12 GRADE-based recommendations covering eight paediatric subgroups and provide the first unified international guidance for this difficult clinical area, with conditional recommendations against prophylaxis in solid cancer, trauma, and critical illness, and conditional recommendations in favour of prophylaxis in antiphospholipid antibody syndrome and long-term parenteral nutrition[^4]. Fifth, a large meta-analysis in *Blood Advances* by Boccatonda et al. (21 studies, 21,506 patients) establishes that direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) in cirrhotic portal vein thrombosis achieve a complete recanalization rate ratio of 3.49 (95% CI 2.06–5.93) compared with vitamin K antagonists, reduce PVT progression (RR 0.19; 95% CI 0.04–0.78), and confer lower intracranial haemorrhage risk (RR 0.49; 95% CI 0.30–0.80) without increasing major bleeding — a finding relevant to haematologists managing thrombosis in portal hypertension[^5].

## II. TTP / HUS / Thrombotic Microangiopathy

Two publications this week advance our understanding of long-term neurological sequelae and cardiovascular risk in immune-mediated TTP (iTTP).

A *BJH* commentary by Masias, published on 16 June 2026, contextualises a companion cohort study by Javed et al. examining risk factors for silent cerebral infarctions in iTTP survivors[^2]. SCIs are present in iTTP patients at a younger age and with higher prevalence than in the general population. Prior history of stroke, established cardiovascular disease, and diabetes mellitus were identified as independent risk factors for SCI in this cohort. The commentary highlights that conventional iTTP follow-up, which focuses on ADAMTS13 activity monitoring and relapse surveillance, does not systematically incorporate neuroimaging for SCI detection. This observation reinforces the emerging paradigm that iTTP should be considered a chronic neurological disease, and it raises the question of whether structured MRI-based surveillance protocols should be incorporated into long-term follow-up pathways for iTTP patients in clinical remission — particularly those with concurrent cardiovascular risk factors.

Complementing this, a separate *Haematologica* article by Agosti et al. on major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) during clinical remission of iTTP was published on 11 June 2026[^6]. Although the full abstract was unavailable at the time of writing, the article addresses the cardiovascular event burden in remission-phase iTTP patients, a topic of increasing clinical relevance given that cumulative endothelial injury and acquired ADAMTS13 deficiency may contribute to a pro-thrombotic milieu even during apparent disease quiescence. The co-occurrence of these two publications underscores the need for a structured, multidisciplinary approach to long-term iTTP survivorship care.

## III. Aplastic Anemia

A *Haematologica* perspective piece by On and Ram, published on 18 June 2026, provides a conceptual reassessment of the role of bone marrow transplantation in aplastic anaemia in the contemporary era of eltrombopag-containing immunosuppressive regimens[^7]. The piece, while editorial in nature, addresses a critical unresolved question: given that horse antithymocyte globulin plus cyclosporine plus eltrombopag achieves complete response rates in approximately 60–70% of patients, and given the expanding availability of matched unrelated donor and haploidentical transplants, is allogeneic HCT still the preferred front-line therapy for patients without a matched sibling donor? The perspective frames the remaining clinical evidence base and highlights the absence of randomised head-to-head data comparing first-line IST with eltrombopag versus upfront MUD-HCT in younger patients — an ongoing unmet need in the field.

Additionally, a *AJH* multicentre comparative cohort study by Fathima et al. on VEXAS syndrome, while primarily relevant to the malignant overlap (UBA1-mutated clonal haematopoiesis), has implications for non-malignant haematology practice given the frequent diagnostic overlap of VEXAS with aplastic anaemia and MDS[^8]. Among 66 patients treated with either hypomethylating agents (HMA, n = 35) or allogeneic HCT (alloHSCT, n = 31), alloHSCT achieved molecular remission in all evaluable patients and glucocorticoid discontinuation in 58%, versus molecular remission in only 22% and glucocorticoid discontinuation in 6% with HMA. On multivariable analysis adjusted for age and Charlson Comorbidity Index, alloHSCT was associated with significantly improved overall survival (HR 0.20; 95% CI 0.05–0.81; p = 0.024). The median follow-up was 18 months (95% CI 11–26), and HMA therapy was associated with high rates of discontinuation due to toxicity or lack of response. These findings are hypothesis-generating given the retrospective design, but they support early transplant referral in eligible VEXAS patients at NCKUH and comparable centres.

## IV. PNH — Paroxysmal Nocturnal Hemoglobinuria

An EHA News item from 13 June 2025 (republished in feed this cycle) references a presentation by Risitano et al. reporting 2-year safety and efficacy data for iptacopan monotherapy in PNH[^9]. While the full dataset from this presentation is not yet published in peer-reviewed form, the data described — which reflect the APPLY-PNH 2-year follow-up — demonstrate sustained haematological response with iptacopan as oral factor B inhibitor monotherapy in patients previously on anti-C5 therapy. A separate EHA item confirms that a head-to-head indirect treatment comparison of iptacopan versus pegcetacoplan was also presented, with iptacopan demonstrating favourable indirect estimates for haemoglobin response. Additionally, the APPULSE-PNH presentation by Kulasekararaj et al. (EHA 2025) reported that iptacopan oral monotherapy demonstrates clinically meaningful haematological responses in treatment-naïve PNH, providing a potential pathway for oral monotherapy as initial treatment[^10]. These are conference-level signals; clinicians should await peer-reviewed publication before modifying practice.

## V. Hemophilia A & B (including gene therapy)

The most substantive new data this week come from a multicentre retrospective study published in *BJH* by Xu et al. on behalf of 25 Chinese haemophilia centres[^1]. The study enrolled 132 patients with haemophilia A (131 male, 1 female) who received emicizumab prophylaxis for at least 12 months, with a median age at treatment initiation of 2.0 years (IQR 1.0–4.8) and a median follow-up of 26 months (IQR 16.0–40.0). The negative binomial regression model-based ABR for all bleeds was 0.81 (95% CI 0.62–1.07) over the entire follow-up period. Across sequential 12-month intervals, the calculated mean and model-based annualized joint bleeding rate (AJBR) remained consistently between 0.1 and 0.3, indicating sustained and durable joint protection. Among the 74 baseline target joints, 95.9% (n = 71) resolved over the course of emicizumab prophylaxis, and no new target joints developed during the observation period. Forty-two patients (31.8%) required supplemental factor concentrate during emicizumab treatment, predominantly for surgery or breakthrough bleeding events. Injection-site reactions occurred in 7.6% of patients and were the most common adverse event; no thromboembolic events, thrombotic microangiopathy, or fatal adverse events were observed.

These data are the largest published Asian real-world cohort of long-term emicizumab prophylaxis and carry particular relevance for NCKUH haemophilia clinicians. The median age of 2.0 years at switch reflects a predominantly paediatric population — consistent with current practice in Taiwan of initiating emicizumab early in severe haemophilia A without inhibitors. The absence of thromboembolic events and the high target joint resolution rate reinforce the long-term safety and efficacy profile established in the HAVEN trial programme, with 95.9% target joint resolution exceeding the approximately 80% rate reported in earlier HAVEN data. The 31.8% rate of concomitant factor use reflects surgical and breakthrough bleed management rather than prophylaxis failure.

Separately, an *AJH* review by Young et al. published on 7 June 2026 addresses the evolving landscape of haemophilia management, including FVIII target levels, subclinical bleeding and joint monitoring strategies, and the clinical integration of rebalancing agents (fitusiran, concizumab, marstacimab)[^11]. While primarily a CME-oriented synthesis rather than original data, it provides a useful framework for the integration of non-factor replacement strategies into clinical practice, particularly for inhibitor patients or those with difficult venous access.

A *BJH* article by Ciavarella et al., published on 10 June 2026, characterises acquired von Willebrand syndrome (AVWS) across 140 patients, identifying lymphoproliferative disorders (LPDs, 19%) and myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPNs, 76%) as the dominant underlying conditions[^12]. This is covered more fully under Section VI below, but it has indirect relevance to haemophilia practice in that AVWS in MPN patients undergoing cytoreductive therapy may mimic factor deficiency bleeding patterns and requires VWF-specific diagnostic workup rather than factor replacement.

## VI. Von Willebrand Disease

Two important publications this week advance understanding of both inherited VWD and acquired VWF defects.

The clinical characterisation study from the Angelo Bianchi Bonomi Centre, published in *AJH* by Ciavarella et al. (n = 140), provides the most granular phenotypic and laboratory distinction between LPD-associated and MPN-associated AVWS to date[^12]. Among 140 patients, bleeding symptoms were present in 70%, with clinically significant bleeding occurring in 24% of the cohort. Clinically relevant bleeding was substantially more frequent and more severe in LPD-associated AVWS (58%) than in MPN-associated AVWS (13%). The laboratory profiles diverged sharply: LPD patients showed severe VWF functional deficits, pronounced high-molecular-weight multimer (HMWM) loss, and elevated VWFpp/VWF:Ag ratios (median 6.7), consistent with accelerated VWF clearance as the dominant mechanism. MPN patients demonstrated mild HMWM reduction with normal clearance (median VWFpp/VWF:Ag ratio 1.0) and a significant inverse correlation between platelet count and HMWM depletion (ρ = −0.48; p < 0.001), consistent with shear-mediated proteolysis proportional to thrombocytosis. In LPD patients, bleeding severity correlated inversely with VWF:GPIbR (ρ = −0.50; p = 0.02), while in MPN patients the correlation was with VWF:RCo. Anti-VWF antibodies were detected in 30% of tested LPD or autoimmune cases. These data firmly establish the mechanistic dichotomy in AVWS and support algorithm-based diagnostic workup with VWFpp/VWF:Ag ratios and multimer analysis to guide therapy — desmopressin and VWF concentrates for LPD-immune clearance phenotypes, and cytoreduction for platelet-driven shear in MPN.

A mechanistic *Blood Advances* study by Mobayen et al. published on 23 June 2026 identifies missense variants in the C1 and C2 domains of VWF that confer gain-of-function–like activity by altering C-terminal stem dynamics[^13]. Using site-directed mutagenesis, single-molecule optical tweezers (OT), and atomic force microscopy (AFM) imaging, the authors show that Arg2287Trp (C1 domain) and Arg2384Trp (C2 domain) variants demonstrate enhanced platelet capture under high-shear conditions (5,000/second), more frequent unfolding events on OT analysis, and a preference for the open stem conformation on AFM — all consistent with increased VWF responsiveness to shear stress. These findings extend beyond three previously described gain-of-function C-domain variants (Phe2561Tyr, Pro2555Arg, Gly2705Arg) and implicate the broader C-terminal stem, comprised of D4-C6 domains, as a critical regulator of VWF mechanosensing. Clinically, these data are relevant to the classification of VWD variants of uncertain significance detected on next-generation sequencing panels and may ultimately inform the diagnosis of platelet-type or type 2B–like VWD phenotypes driven by C-domain rather than A1-domain mutations.

## VII. Thalassemia (alpha & beta; Taiwan-relevant)

A *AJH* study from the CLIMB THAL-111 Phase 3 trial and CLIMB-131 long-term follow-up study, published by Sheth et al. on 7 June 2026, provides important secondary-endpoint data on the impact of exagamglogene autotemcel (exa-cel) on ineffective erythropoiesis and iron homeostasis in transfusion-dependent β-thalassemia (TDT)[^3]. This is the first detailed report on erythropoietic and iron biomarker outcomes following exa-cel-mediated fetal haemoglobin reactivation.

At data cut (April 2025), 98% of participants (55 of 56) had been transfusion independent for ≥12 months. Among those who achieved transfusion independence, 38 patients (68%) had discontinued iron removal therapy, with a mean duration off iron chelation of 19.4 months. Following transfusion independence and cessation of iron removal, erythroferrone concentrations decreased and hepcidin levels normalised in all participants — providing biochemical proof that ineffective erythropoiesis, the driver of hepcidin suppression and iron overload in TDT, was corrected. Supportive erythropoiesis biomarkers including erythropoietin levels, reticulocyte counts, and soluble transferrin receptor concentrations all trended toward normalisation. Iron overload markers, including serum ferritin, liver iron concentration by MRI, and cardiac T2*, decreased and remained stable throughout follow-up even after cessation of iron chelation therapy.

These findings are highly relevant for Taiwan, where β-thalassemia carrier rates are approximately 1–2% and TDT is a priority indication under NHI rare disease coverage. While exa-cel is not yet approved or reimbursed in Taiwan, the demonstration that a single gene-editing intervention effectively reverses decades of iron-loading and erythropoietic dysregulation — without ongoing chelation therapy — provides a compelling long-term cost-effectiveness argument. Taiwanese haematologists should be aware that the iron homeostasis data are now sufficiently mature to begin discussing exa-cel with families of TDT patients, particularly those with suboptimal chelation compliance or cardiac iron overload (low T2*).

## XI. Thrombosis & Coagulation (VTE, APS, inherited thrombophilia)

Three clinically important publications were identified this week across VTE, portal thrombosis, and vaccine-induced immune thrombocytopenia and thrombosis (VITT).

The most immediately practice-relevant publication is the joint ASH/ISTH 2026 guideline on anticoagulant prophylaxis in paediatric VTE, published in *Blood Advances* by Betensky et al. on 23 June 2026[^4]. The guideline panel of 12 recommendations was developed using the GRADE approach and addressed eight paediatric subgroups. For paediatric patients with solid cancer, trauma, or critical illness, the panel issued conditional recommendations suggesting no anticoagulant prophylaxis, reflecting the low certainty of evidence and the absence of demonstrated net benefit in these groups. For paediatric patients with antiphospholipid antibody syndrome or those receiving long-term total parenteral nutrition, conditional recommendations favouring anticoagulant prophylaxis were issued. Subgroups also addressed include patients with acute lymphoblastic leukemia or lymphoma receiving asparaginase, surgical and general inpatient patients, and those with central venous access devices (CVADs). The panel highlighted that high-quality data in paediatric VTE prophylaxis remain scarce, and identified the development of subgroup-specific risk assessment models and evaluation of risk-stratified prophylaxis strategies as priority research needs. For Taiwanese haematologists and paediatric haematologists at NCKUH, this guideline provides the first joint international framework for CVAD-associated and condition-specific prophylaxis decisions and supersedes previous institution-based protocols.

A *Blood Advances* meta-analysis by Boccatonda et al. on DOACs in cirrhosis-associated portal vein thrombosis (PVT) pooled data from 21 studies encompassing 21,506 patients, of whom 2,099 received DOACs[^5]. Compared with vitamin K antagonists (VKAs), DOAC therapy was associated with a substantially higher rate of complete PVT recanalization (RR 3.49; 95% CI 2.06–5.93), a lower risk of PVT progression (RR 0.19; 95% CI 0.04–0.78), and reduced intracranial haemorrhage (RR 0.49; 95% CI 0.30–0.80), with no statistically significant increase in major bleeding (RR 1.09; 95% CI 0.79–1.51). Compared with no anticoagulation at all, DOAC therapy was associated with a lower mortality (RR 0.77; 95% CI 0.71–0.84) without increased bleeding. The recanalization benefit was most pronounced in studies restricted to Child-Pugh class A or B patients, whereas safety outcomes were consistent across cirrhosis severity strata. These data provide robust observational evidence supporting DOAC use in carefully selected cirrhotic PVT patients, acknowledging that prospective randomised trials remain needed. The finding is clinically relevant in Taiwan where hepatitis B– and C–related cirrhosis with PVT is relatively common.

A *BJH* article by Petito et al. on the association between VITT (vaccine-induced immune thrombocytopenia and thrombosis) and HLA loci was published on 10 June 2026[^14]. The abstract was not available at the time of writing; however, the paper addresses whether specific HLA alleles serve as genetic determinants of anti-PF4 antibody generation following adenoviral vector vaccination. This is an important unresolved question for future vaccine safety strategies, and the data — when published in full — will be of interest for populations in Asia where adenoviral vector COVID-19 vaccines were widely deployed.

Two NEJM studies on prehospital whole blood transfusion in traumatic haemorrhage (Smith et al. and Sperry et al., both published 18 June 2026) provide high-quality evidence pertinent to transfusion medicine and emergency haematology[^15][^16]. In the UK pragmatic trial (Smith et al., n = 616 analysed), prehospital transfusion with up to 2 units of whole blood was not superior to standard component therapy in reducing the composite of death or massive transfusion within 24 hours (48.7% vs 47.7%; RR 1.02; 95% CI 0.80–1.31; p = 0.84). Similarly, in the US cluster-randomised TOWAR trial (Sperry et al., n = 993 analysed), whole blood did not reduce 30-day mortality compared with components (25.9% vs 20.5%; adjusted OR 1.24; 95% CI 0.87–1.76; p = 0.24). Notably, the observational substudy in TOWAR found no significant difference in mortality based on whole blood storage age (15–21 days vs 1–14 days; adjusted OR 0.99; 95% CI 0.74–1.32). Together these two concurrent randomised trials — the first adequately powered phase 3 data for prehospital whole blood — cast substantial doubt on the growing clinical enthusiasm for prehospital whole blood as superior to component therapy and have immediate implications for trauma transfusion protocols.

## XIII. Taiwan Clinical Context

Several findings this week have specific relevance for clinical practice at NCKUH and other Taiwan centres.

The Chinese multicentre emicizumab cohort (Xu et al.)[^1] is the most directly applicable dataset for Taiwan haematologists managing haemophilia A under the current NHI emicizumab reimbursement criteria. Taiwan's NHI covers emicizumab for severe haemophilia A patients with or without inhibitors; the reported target joint resolution rate of 95.9% and ABR of 0.81 over 26 months provide real-world Asian benchmarks that clinicians can use when counselling patients and families, particularly given that clinical trial populations may not fully reflect the resource-constrained or late-presenting Asian patients included in the Chinese cohort. The 31.8% concomitant factor concentrate use rate is also useful for NHI budget planning.

The exa-cel (Casgevy) iron homeostasis data[^3] are relevant to Taiwan because β-thalassemia TDT is a recognised priority rare disease under the NHI rare disease programme. The finding that 68% of exa-cel-treated patients could safely discontinue iron chelation therapy (mean 19.4 months off chelation) is of particular practical importance given that iron chelation is a lifelong financial and quality-of-life burden in Taiwan. Although exa-cel is not yet NHI-reimbursed in Taiwan, this report strengthens the case for health technology assessment submission.

The ASH/ISTH 2026 paediatric VTE prophylaxis guideline[^4] covers CVAD-related thrombosis, which is highly relevant to NCKUH's paediatric haemato-oncology practice. The conditional recommendation against universal prophylaxis in hospitalised surgical or critically ill children should prompt review of current prophylaxis protocols in the NCKUH PICU, where practice has been variable. The guideline's explicit recommendation in favour of prophylaxis for children with antiphospholipid antibody syndrome should also be noted given the relatively high seroprevalence of antiphospholipid antibodies in patients with lupus in Taiwanese paediatric rheumatology-haematology overlap clinics.

Taiwan's approximately 4–5% alpha-thalassemia carrier rate and 1–2% beta-thalassemia carrier rate[^3] remain relevant background context for thalassemia genetics counselling at NCKUH. The G6PD deficiency rate of approximately 3% in Taiwan (higher in Indigenous populations) continues to drive referrals to the NCKUH red cell disorders clinic, although no new G6PD-specific data were published this week.

## XIV. Key Takeaways

Four key actionable conclusions emerge from this week's literature. First, long-term emicizumab prophylaxis data from 25 Chinese haemophilia centres confirm a model-based ABR of 0.81 and 95.9% target joint resolution over 26 months, with no thromboembolic events — providing directly applicable Asian real-world benchmarks for NHI-reimbursed emicizumab programmes in Taiwan[^1]. Second, exa-cel in TDT restores iron homeostasis as well as transfusion independence: 68% of patients stopped chelation therapy, hepcidin normalised in all transfusion-independent participants, and cardiac and hepatic iron biomarkers stabilised even after chelation cessation — data that will be pivotal for health technology assessment of gene editing in Taiwan[^3]. Third, the first joint ASH/ISTH paediatric VTE prophylaxis guidelines (12 recommendations, GRADE-based) provide clinicians with a unified international framework for decisions in solid cancer, trauma, critically ill, APS, and CVAD paediatric subgroups — superseding institutional protocols[^4]. Fourth, two concurrent NEJM phase 3 trials (UK and US) find that prehospital whole blood is not superior to standard component therapy for traumatic haemorrhage in terms of 30-day mortality or early massive transfusion — a major negative result that should temper enthusiasm for whole-blood–centric trauma transfusion protocols[^15][^16].
