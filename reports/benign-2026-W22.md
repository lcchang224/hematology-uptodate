## Summary

The five most clinically actionable signals this week are as follows. First, a *Blood Advances* report describes the first-in-human application of lentiviral vector-mediated α-globin gene therapy (Lenti-HBA) in a 14-year-old girl with transfusion-dependent Hemoglobin H disease (--SEA/αCSα), achieving transfusion independence from week 4 onward and a stable mean hemoglobin of approximately 90 g/L with HbA constituting greater than 85% of total hemoglobin at 12–24 months of follow-up — the first published human proof-of-concept for curative gene therapy in transfusion-dependent α-thalassemia[^1]. Second, a multicenter randomised phase 2 trial published in the *British Journal of Haematology* demonstrates that adding glycyrrhizinate to dexamethasone as first-line therapy for newly diagnosed ITP significantly improves the 6-month overall response rate compared to dexamethasone monotherapy (56.25% vs. 31.25%; p = 0.0231), with a longer duration of response and no grade 3–5 adverse events[^2]. Third, a landmark 25-year anniversary review of ADAMTS13 in TTP, published in *Blood*, synthesises the trajectory from enzyme discovery to the current therapeutic standard — caplacizumab in acute immune TTP and recombinant ADAMTS13 (rADAMTS13) in congenital TTP — and previews the pipeline for ADAMTS13-directed therapy in iTTP[^3]. Fourth, a *Blood Advances* European multicenter retrospective study of 121 acquired pure red cell aplasia cases across 14 centres establishes that cyclosporine A achieves a 61% overall response rate (47% complete response) as first-line immunosuppression, and that mTOR inhibitors yield responses in 71% of cyclosporine-refractory cases — the largest adult PRCA cohort published to date, with 30% mortality predominantly from infectious complications[^4]. Fifth, a *Blood* study uncovers a novel functional role for clusterin as a Weibel-Palade body component that stabilises high-molecular-weight VWF multimers by competitively inhibiting ADAMTS13-mediated proteolysis at the D4N domain under shear stress, with plasma clusterin significantly reduced in type 2A von Willebrand disease patients and clusterin-deficient mice exhibiting hemorrhagic diathesis and impaired thrombosis[^5].

## I. Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP)

A multicentre randomised open-label phase 2 trial conducted in China (NCT05023915) and published this week in the *British Journal of Haematology* addresses the persistent challenge of optimising first-line therapy for newly diagnosed primary ITP[^2]. Eligible adult patients with newly diagnosed, treatment-naïve ITP and a baseline platelet count below 30 × 10⁹/L were randomised 1:1 to dexamethasone plus glycyrrhizinate (n = 55) or dexamethasone alone (n = 56); the modified intention-to-treat population comprised 96 patients (48 per arm) who received at least one dose. Glycyrrhizinate is a compound derived from licorice root with previously demonstrated activity in ITP murine models, postulated to modulate regulatory T-cell function and suppress phagocytic clearance of opsonised platelets. The primary endpoint — overall response (platelet count ≥30 × 10⁹/L, at least doubling of baseline count, and no bleeding) at month 6 — was achieved in 56.25% (27/48) of the combination arm versus 31.25% (15/48) of the monotherapy arm (p = 0.0231), with a statistically longer duration of response in the combination group. Importantly, no significant difference was observed at day 14, suggesting that glycyrrhizinate's principal benefit is in promoting sustained rather than acute platelet recovery. The most common treatment-emergent adverse events were gastrointestinal reactions and anxiety; no grade 3–5 adverse events or deaths occurred. Whilst the sample size precludes definitive conclusions, these data suggest that glycyrrhizinate addition to standard high-dose dexamethasone may represent a feasible strategy to improve long-term remission rates in newly diagnosed ITP — an endpoint that has historically been difficult to achieve with corticosteroids alone.

Separately, a *Haematologica* study this week provides a retrospective single-centre analysis of autoimmune cytopenias occurring after alemtuzumab-based induction immunosuppression for renal transplantation over an 8-year period[^6]. Forty patients developed immune thrombocytopenia (n = 28), autoimmune haemolytic anaemia (AIHA; n = 7), or Evans syndrome (n = 5), with two incidence peaks at 18 and 36 months post-transplant. Response and relapse rates to standard first-line therapy were comparable to primary ITP and AIHA, but — notably — rituximab achieved an overall response rate of 91.6% with a median duration of response of 56 months (higher than typically reported in primary ITP), and 50% of patients on thrombopoietin receptor agonists (TPO-RAs) were able to discontinue therapy in sustained remission. Infection occurred in 62.5% of patients, deep vein thrombosis in 25%, and cardiovascular events in 27.5%, underscoring the complexity of management in the post-transplant immunosuppressed setting.

## II. TTP / HUS / Thrombotic Microangiopathy

The 25th anniversary of the identification of ADAMTS13 as the enzymatic defect underlying TTP is marked this week by a comprehensive review by Scully, Carter, and Subhan published in *Blood*[^3]. The authors trace the landmark 2001 discovery — which explained both immune TTP (iTTP, antibody-mediated ADAMTS13 deficiency) and congenital TTP (cTTP, biallelic *ADAMTS13* gene mutations) — through to its translational consequences: the development of validated ADAMTS13 activity assays enabling rapid diagnosis, the incorporation of rituximab into both acute and elective iTTP therapy, the approval of caplacizumab (an anti-VWF nanobody) for acute iTTP, and the establishment of recombinant ADAMTS13 (rADAMTS13) as the current gold standard for cTTP treatment. The review identifies the expanding pipeline: rADAMTS13 is under investigation for use in iTTP (where it would bypass the need for immunosuppression to restore enzyme activity), novel rapid-turnaround ADAMTS13 assay formats are in development for point-of-care deployment, and the potential role of ADAMTS13 augmentation in other thrombotic indications — including stroke, coronary disease, and pre-eclampsia — is explored. For clinicians, the review reinforces that ADAMTS13 activity monitoring is central not only to diagnosis but to treatment tailoring: residual ADAMTS13 activity below 10% at remission identifies iTTP patients requiring pre-emptive rituximab, while ADAMTS13 pharmacokinetics guide rADAMTS13 dosing intervals in cTTP.

A closely related mechanistic study published simultaneously in *Blood* by Qiao and colleagues identifies clusterin as a previously unrecognised Weibel-Palade body (WPB) component with a critical hemostatic regulatory function[^5]. Using proteomic analysis of the endothelial secretome and validated by immunostaining and co-transfection assays, the authors demonstrate that clusterin is secreted from WPBs and binds the D4N domain of VWF, competitively inhibiting ADAMTS13-mediated proteolysis under shear stress to preserve high-molecular-weight (HMW) VWF multimers. In clusterin-deficient mice, HMW VWF levels were reduced, resulting in hemorrhagic diathesis and impaired thrombosis that was fully rescued by exogenous clusterin administration. Critically, plasma clusterin was significantly reduced in patients with type 2A von Willebrand disease — a disorder defined by selective loss of HMW multimers — establishing translational relevance. The companion editorial in *Blood* by Ng contextualises clusterin as part of a broader emerging paradigm in which WPBs serve as regulatory protein hubs rather than simple VWF storage organelles[^7]. For clinical hematologists, this raises the conceptual possibility of multimer stabilisation-based therapeutic strategies as an alternative to factor replacement in selected bleeding disorders.

## III. Aplastic Anemia

A *Blood* article this week by Duan et al. provides the most detailed metabolic characterisation of human hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) yet published, using a low-input mass spectrometry metabolomics platform capable of profiling as few as ~10,000 cells[^8]. Across 13 immunophenotypically defined hematopoietic cell types from adult human bone marrow, L-carnitine-driven fatty acid oxidation (FAO) was identified as a key metabolic feature of primitive HSPCs. Mechanistically, L-carnitine activates the PPARA–TFEB signalling axis, promoting mitochondrial metabolism and autophagy to preserve HSPC regenerative capacity. Importantly, functional assays in primary CD34⁺ HSPCs from patients with aplastic anemia confirmed that L-carnitine supplementation improves stem cell function both ex vivo and in vivo, establishing aplastic anemia-context relevance. Although this is a mechanistic study rather than a clinical trial, the identification of a targetable, commercially available metabolic substrate as a potential adjunct to HSPC regeneration is of direct translational interest, particularly in the context of immunosuppression-refractory aplastic anemia or settings where allogeneic transplantation is unavailable.

In the context of secondary aplastic anemia, a brief case report in *Blood* this week documents the co-occurrence of thymoma-associated aplastic anemia with Good syndrome (thymoma-associated immunodeficiency), a rare but instructive combination highlighting the importance of screening for both immune cytopenia and humoral immunodeficiency in thymoma patients[^9].

## VI. Von Willebrand Disease

The clusterin-VWF study by Qiao et al. (see Section II above) provides new mechanistic insight into the regulation of VWF multimer quality that is directly relevant to von Willebrand disease pathophysiology[^5]. Specifically, the finding that plasma clusterin is significantly reduced in type 2A VWD patients and that clusterin loss phenocopies selected features of HMW multimer deficiency in mice reframes VWD type 2A as potentially involving not only intrinsic VWF cleavage hypersensitivity but also deficiency of an endogenous multimer-stabilising factor. The structural study by Zhang et al. published in *Blood* provides crystal structures of the HPA-1a antigen bound to Fab fragments from four anti-HPA-1a monoclonal antibodies (including three clinically relevant maternal alloantibodies: 26.4, D-204, and M-204)[^12]. While primarily relevant to FNAIT (see below), the structural data demonstrate that two of these antibodies stabilise the bent, inactive β3 integrin conformation, thereby inhibiting αIIbβ3-mediated platelet aggregation and αVβ3-mediated endothelial function — findings with broader implications for understanding integrin–VWF interactions at the platelet surface.

## VII. Thalassemia (alpha & beta; Taiwan-relevant)

The most significant publication this week in thalassemia is a first-in-human case report of lentiviral vector-mediated α-globin gene therapy (Lenti-HBA) in a 14-year-old girl with transfusion-dependent Hemoglobin H disease (genotype --SEA/αCSα), published in *Blood Advances* by Yin et al.[^1]. The patient underwent mobilisation and collection of autologous CD34⁺ HSPCs, ex vivo transduction with the Lenti-HBA vector engineered to restore α-globin expression, and reinfusion following busulfan-based myeloablative conditioning. Engraftment was confirmed within three weeks and transfusion independence was achieved from week 4 onward. Over the 12–24 month follow-up period, the mean haemoglobin level was sustained at approximately 90 g/L without transfusion support, with HbA constituting greater than 85% of total haemoglobin. Vector copy numbers were stable throughout, with no evidence of clonal dominance, insertional mutagenesis, or replication-competent lentivirus. The only serious adverse event was grade 2 busulfan-related menstrual irregularity and ovarian failure. Iron overload progressed minimally despite cessation of chelation therapy, and no cardiac iron deposition was detected. Immune recovery and growth were preserved. This case is of particular significance for Taiwan and the Asia-Pacific region, where --SEA deletion is the predominant severe α-thalassemia genotype, accounting for virtually all cases of HbH disease and Hb Bart's hydrops fetalis among the ~4–5% of the Taiwanese population who are α-thalassemia carriers. The NCT05851105 trial is ongoing; further patients and longer follow-up will be critical to establish durability and safety.

A mechanistic study in the *British Journal of Haematology* by Thubthed et al. (a Thai group) investigates the role of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) in the thrombotic pathophysiology of β-thalassemia/HbE — the compound heterozygous genotype that accounts for the majority of symptomatic β-thalassemia in Southeast Asia, including Taiwan[^13]. Using three-dimensional confocal microscopy, the authors demonstrate that activated platelets from β-thalassemia/HbE patients induce NET formation through P-selectin–PSGL-1 and HMGB1–RAGE binding on neutrophils. In splenectomised patients, the MEK/ERK pathway (inhibited by U0126 but not by ERK inhibition alone) drove web-like NET formation and neutrophil-platelet aggregation through an antioxidant-linked mechanism distinct from that operating in non-splenectomised patients. HMGB1 activated NETs via MEK/ERK, NOX2, MPO, and PAD4 pathways across all patient groups. The data identify the ROS pathway as a key regulator of NET-driven immunothrombosis in splenectomised β-thalassemia/HbE and suggest it as a potential therapeutic target to reduce the substantially elevated thromboembolic risk in this population — a risk that is particularly clinically relevant in Taiwan, where splenectomy was historically more commonly performed in thalassemia major patients.

## VIII. Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia (AIHA)

The alemtuzumab-associated autoimmune cytopenia study reported in *Haematologica* (Lucchini et al.) includes seven patients with AIHA and five with Evans syndrome, in addition to 28 with ITP (see Section I above)[^6]. The temporal clustering of AIHA onset at 18 and 36 months post-alemtuzumab mirrors the ITP pattern and is consistent with the known kinetics of CD52⁺ immune cell reconstitution. Among AIHA patients, standard first-line therapy with corticosteroids ± intravenous immunoglobulin achieved initial responses comparable to primary AIHA, but relapse rates were substantial, requiring second-line therapy in most patients. Rituximab and TPO-RAs were the principal second-line agents used. The study's key clinical message is that all patients should receive antimicrobial prophylaxis and that anticoagulation — interrupted during the thrombocytopenic phase of ITP or AIHA-associated anaemia — should be re-introduced promptly upon platelet recovery, given the high rate of thrombotic complications in this cohort (DVT in 25%).

## IX. Red Cell Disorders (G6PD, PK deficiency, hereditary spherocytosis)

A mechanistic study published in *Blood* this week by Ling et al. demonstrates that GATA1 controls metabolic reprogramming in erythropoiesis by directly regulating pyruvate kinase M (*PKM*) expression[^14]. Using single-cell RNA sequencing on fetal liver cells from Gata1-mutant embryos expressing only the short isoform GATA1s (which lacks the N-terminal domain encoded by exon 2 — the domain deleted in Diamond-Blackfan anaemia and the myeloid leukemia of Down syndrome), the authors found aberrant upregulation of glycolytic genes including *PKM*. CUT&RUN and precision nuclear run-on sequencing confirmed PKM as a direct GATA1 target. Substitution of GATA1FL with GATA1s induced histone lactylation at the PKM promoter, increased pyruvate kinase M (PKM) expression and activity, and enhanced glycolytic flux in erythroid progenitors without affecting mitochondrial respiration. Critically, PKM expression was also significantly elevated in DBA patients carrying *RPS19* mutations (which reduce GATA1 protein levels), directly linking GATA1-driven erythropoietic defects to dysregulated glycolysis. This has implications for understanding pyruvate kinase deficiency: whilst classical PK deficiency arises from loss-of-function *PKLR* mutations, this study demonstrates that GATA1-mediated PKM isoform dysregulation can create a functionally distinct glycolytic imbalance in erythroid progenitors — a finding relevant to the mechanistic heterogeneity underlying erythroid PKM2/PKLR ratios in clinical disease.

A separate *Blood Journal* report by Zeng et al. identifies biallelic loss-of-function mutations in *BPNT1* as a novel genetic cause of recurrent vitamin B12-dependent megaloblastic anaemia in three patients[^15]. Mechanistically, *BPNT1* deficiency causes accumulation of 3′-phosphoadenosine 5′-phosphate (PAP), impairs ribosome biogenesis, and reduces ileal expression of the cubam receptor complex (responsible for vitamin B12 absorption) in Bpnt1-null mice. This establishes *BPNT1* as a new gene in the differential diagnosis of congenital megaloblastic anaemia, potentially actionable with pharmacological doses of parenteral B12.

## X. Iron Deficiency & Nutritional Anemias

A large retrospective cohort study from the TriNetX Research Network (2000 to June 2025) published in *Blood* this week by Sohail et al. addresses the longstanding clinical controversy surrounding intravenous iron administration during acute infection[^16]. The study included propensity-matched adult patients with iron deficiency anaemia who received concurrent IV iron and antibiotic therapy for MRSA bacteraemia, pneumonia, urinary tract infection, colitis, or cellulitis. Across all five infection types, 14-day and 90-day survival was significantly higher in patients who received IV iron (all p < 0.001). In MRSA bacteraemia — the highest-stakes scenario — 14-day survival was 97.6% vs. 95.0% and 90-day survival was 88.6% vs. 83.8% in the IV iron versus no IV iron groups, respectively. Haemoglobin recovery at 60–90 days was significantly greater in the IV iron group across all infection subtypes (e.g., MRSA bacteraemia: +1.3 vs. +1.0 g/dL, p < 0.001; colitis: +1.5 vs. +0.7 g/dL, p < 0.001). These findings challenge the conventional teaching that IV iron should be withheld during acute infection due to concerns about providing substrate for bacterial growth, and instead suggest that IV iron administered concomitantly with appropriate antibiotic therapy may be safe and beneficial. The authors appropriately note that prospective randomised data are needed before this practice can be recommended definitively, and that the TriNetX database may reflect confounding by indication despite propensity matching.

## XI. Thrombosis & Coagulation (VTE, APS, inherited thrombophilia)

A *Blood* mechanistic study by Maroney et al. examines the role of tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI) in embryonic cerebrovascular development using a mouse model of complete TFPI deficiency (Tfpi⁻/⁻), which causes embryonic lethality from excess thrombin generation and cerebrovascular glomeruloid body formation[^17]. Breeding a transgene producing hyperactivatable mouse protein C (hMPC) into Tfpi⁺/⁻ mice rescued embryonic lethality in Tfpi⁻/⁻/hMPC⁺ offspring, but hMPC reduced glomeruloid body numbers by only 36% and did not prevent fibrin deposition or blood-brain barrier disruption within these lesions. Bulk RNA sequencing of embryonic day 15.5 brain tissue revealed that increased angiogenesis — through apelin, adrenomedulin, and UNC5b — was the dominant biological process altered in the Tfpi⁻/⁻ brain, and was fully reversed by hMPC. Glomeruloid bodies were completely resolved by postnatal day 10 in Tfpi⁻/⁻/hMPC⁺ pups, revealing a temporally restricted window of TFPI dependence during embryonic angiogenesis. These findings define TFPI as an essential, non-redundant inhibitor of thrombin generation during cerebrovascular development and suggest that its cytoprotective — rather than purely anticoagulant — function underpins the survival rescue by activated protein C.

The *British Journal of Haematology* this week carries a review from an Irish group (Lê, Dillon, Smith, and McMahon) summarising the pathophysiological role of thrombocytosis and platelet-derived microparticles (PMPs) in sickle cell disease[^18]. The authors describe how thrombocytosis — common in SCD, particularly in response to acute chest syndrome, vaso-occlusive crises, and chronic haemolysis — contributes to the proinflammatory and prothrombotic milieu. Activated platelets release PMPs containing phospholipids and inflammatory mediators, which enhance thrombin generation, entrap leucocytes, and promote cellular adhesions leading to endothelial dysfunction and vaso-occlusion within the microvasculature. The review discusses emerging therapeutic strategies targeting platelet activation and PMP formation, including P2Y12 inhibitors, aspirin, and statins, as adjuncts to standard SCD therapy.

A *British Journal of Haematology* survey of UK regional fetal medicine centres characterises current antenatal management of FNAIT[^19]. Sixteen of 22 centres responded (73% response rate). Strong consensus (≥75% concordance) was achieved for treating standard- and high-risk pregnancies associated with anti-HPA-1a or anti-HPA-5b antibodies with weekly intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIg) at 1 g/kg/week. Earlier initiation was favoured for high-risk pregnancies. Fetal blood sampling and intrauterine platelet transfusion were generally avoided, and corticosteroids were not routinely used in standard-risk pregnancies. Significant variation was observed in delivery planning and in management of pregnancies at risk without a prior confirmed diagnosis — highlighting persistent evidence gaps. The accompanying structural study by Zhang et al. in *Blood* provides mechanistic grounding for understanding why anti-HPA-1a antibody heterogeneity (in terms of affinity to αIIbβ3 vs. αVβ3 and in terms of conformational selectivity) may underlie the unpredictable clinical severity — including intracranial haemorrhage — that makes FNAIT management so challenging[^12].

## XIII. Taiwan Clinical Context

Several findings this week carry direct relevance to clinical practice in Taiwan. The first-in-human α-thalassemia gene therapy case (Lenti-HBA)[^1] is of immediate priority concern for NCKUH, NTUH, CGMH, and CMUH haematology divisions. Taiwan has a reported α-thalassemia carrier frequency of approximately 4–5%, with --SEA deletion predominant; transfusion-dependent HbH disease (--SEA/αCS or --SEA/α²^3.7^) affects hundreds of Taiwanese patients who are currently managed lifelong with transfusions and iron chelation. This report establishes that ex vivo lentiviral α-globin gene addition is feasible and durable in humans, though regulatory and manufacturing pathways for this approach in Taiwan remain early-stage. Clinicians should initiate consultation regarding patient eligibility for ongoing international trials and consider protocol adoption through appropriate investigational new drug (IND) mechanisms.

The NET-driven thrombosis study in β-thalassemia/HbE[^13] is directly relevant to the management of splenectomised thalassemia patients in Taiwan, where historical rates of splenectomy in transfusion-dependent β-thalassemia major were relatively high and thromboembolic events remain a major cause of morbidity and mortality. The identification of the MEK/ERK–ROS axis as a potential therapeutic target in splenectomised patients suggests that antioxidant or MEK inhibitor strategies may warrant prospective evaluation in this population. Standard thromboprophylaxis with aspirin or anticoagulation should be considered in all splenectomised thalassemia patients per current guideline recommendations.

Regarding immunosuppression for aplastic anemia: the L-carnitine HSPC study[^8] raises the question of whether L-carnitine supplementation could serve as a low-risk adjunct during immunosuppressive therapy, particularly in patients who are not transplant candidates. NHI coverage for novel agents including eltrombopag (for aplastic anemia), eculizumab and ravulizumab (for PNH), and emicizumab (for hemophilia A with inhibitors) is established in Taiwan, though access to rADAMTS13 for cTTP remains limited and should be pursued via compassionate use pathways.

## XIV. Key Takeaways

The single most important development this week for non-malignant hematology is the publication of first-in-human α-globin gene therapy data for transfusion-dependent HbH disease, demonstrating transfusion independence and sustained HbA expression at 24 months — a critical proof-of-concept for what may become a curative therapeutic platform for the most common severe thalassemia genotype in Taiwan and Southeast Asia[^1]. Clinicians should note that this was a single patient on an ongoing trial and longer follow-up in additional patients is needed before conclusions about efficacy and safety can be generalised.

In ITP, the glycyrrhizinate addition to dexamethasone trial[^2] provides the first randomised evidence for a dexamethasone-based combination strategy achieving significantly improved 6-month remission rates, though the small sample size and open-label design limit immediate practice change. The PRCA multicenter data[^4] represent the most robust evidence yet for cyclosporine A as first-line therapy and mTOR inhibitors as second-line rescue in acquired PRCA, now supported by a European cohort of 121 patients.

The TTP/ADAMTS13 review[^3] and the clusterin-VWF discovery[^5] together advance both clinical and mechanistic understanding of how VWF multimer quality is regulated and how it may be therapeutically manipulated — a paradigm shift with potential long-term implications for management of both TTP and VWD type 2A. Finally, the TriNetX IV iron safety data during acute infection[^16] — while retrospective — provide the strongest real-world evidence yet that IV iron can be safely administered concurrently with antibiotics in selected hospitalised patients with IDA, potentially changing the threshold for withholding iron during acute illness.
