## Summary

The five most clinically actionable signals this week are as follows. First, a landmark 25-year anniversary review in *Blood* by Scully et al. synthesises the therapeutic advances enabled by ADAMTS13 discovery — including caplacizumab and recombinant ADAMTS13 (rADAMTS13) for congenital TTP — and identifies future directions including rADAMTS13 expansion to immune TTP and novel immunomodulatory strategies[^1]. Second, a mechanistic *Blood* study by Qiao et al. identifies clusterin as a novel endothelial Weibel-Palade body (WPB) component that stabilises high-molecular-weight VWF multimers by competitively blocking ADAMTS13-mediated proteolysis; clusterin deficiency in mice produces a haemorrhagic diathesis closely resembling type 2A VWD, suggesting a new therapeutic paradigm for VWF multimer disorders[^2]. Third, a randomised phase 2 trial in *BJH* (Wang et al.) demonstrates that dexamethasone plus glycyrrhizinate achieves significantly higher 6-month overall response rates than dexamethasone monotherapy in newly diagnosed ITP (56.25% vs. 31.25%; p = 0.0231), with a longer duration of response and no grade ≥3 adverse events[^3]. Fourth, a first-in-human case report in *Blood Advances* by Yin et al. documents successful lentiviral α-globin gene therapy in a 14-year-old girl with transfusion-dependent HbH disease (--SEA/αCSα), achieving transfusion independence from week 4 with a stable haemoglobin of approximately 90 g/L and HbA >85% of total haemoglobin at 12–24 months, representing the first curative gene therapy application for alpha-thalassemia[^4]. Fifth, a large retrospective TriNetX study in *Blood* by Sohail et al. (n = propensity-matched cohorts across five infection types) provides the first real-world evidence that intravenous iron administered during acute infection is associated with significantly higher 14-day and 90-day survival rates (all p < 0.001) and greater haemoglobin recovery (+1.3 to +1.5 g/dL vs. +0.7 to +1.0 g/dL) compared with no IV iron, directly challenging the longstanding clinical reluctance to administer IV iron during active infection[^5].

## I. Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP)

A multicentre randomised open-label phase 2 trial published in *BJH* by Wang et al. enrolled 111 patients with newly diagnosed, treatment-naïve primary ITP (baseline platelet count <30 × 10⁹/L) and randomly assigned 96 evaluable patients 1:1 to dexamethasone plus glycyrrhizinate (n = 48) or dexamethasone monotherapy (n = 48)[^3]. The primary endpoint of overall response at month 6 — defined as platelet count ≥30 × 10⁹/L, at least doubling of baseline count, and no bleeding — was significantly higher in the combination group (56.25% [27/48] vs. 31.25% [15/48]; p = 0.0231). Notably, no difference was observed at day 14, suggesting that glycyrrhizinate's contribution emerges during sustained rather than immediate immunomodulation. Duration of response was longer in the combination arm, and no grade 3–5 adverse events or deaths were recorded; the most common treatment-emergent adverse events were gastrointestinal reactions and anxiety. Glycyrrhizinate's mechanism in ITP murine models involves platelet response induction, though the precise human immunological pathway remains incompletely characterised. This trial (NCT05023915) provides the first prospective evidence for glycyrrhizinate as an optimised first-line adjunct to dexamethasone, though the study size (n = 96 evaluable) warrants cautious interpretation pending larger replication.

A separate *Haematologica* study by Lucchini et al. characterised autoimmune cytopenias arising after alemtuzumab induction for renal transplantation across 40 patients over eight years[^6]. Of these, 28 developed ITP, 7 developed AIHA, and 5 developed Evans syndrome, with two incidence peaks at 18 and 36 months post-alemtuzumab. Response to first-line ITP therapy was comparable to primary ITP, with two-thirds requiring second-line agents. Rituximab achieved a 91.6% response rate with median duration of 56 months — numerically superior to primary ITP benchmarks — while 50% of TPO-RA–treated patients were able to discontinue and maintain remission. Infection (62.5%), cardiovascular events (27.5%), and deep vein thrombosis (25%) were the most common serious adverse events, underscoring the need for close antimicrobial prophylaxis and prompt anticoagulation management in this immunocompromised population.

## II. TTP / HUS / Thrombotic Microangiopathy

A landmark 25-year anniversary review in *Blood* by Scully, Carter, and Subhan surveys the full arc of therapeutic progress enabled by the 2001 identification of ADAMTS13 deficiency as the mechanistic basis of both immune TTP (iTTP) and congenital TTP (cTTP)[^1]. The authors trace how quantitative ADAMTS13 activity assays enabled faster and more accurate diagnosis, facilitated individualised immunosuppressive titration in iTTP, and guided recombinant ADAMTS13 (rADAMTS13) dosing in cTTP — where rADAMTS13 is now established as the gold standard, reducing end-organ damage and long-term morbidity. Landmark therapeutic advances reviewed include rituximab in both acute and elective iTTP settings (reducing relapse through anti-B-cell–mediated ADAMTS13 inhibitor clearance) and caplacizumab, the anti-VWF nanobody that dramatically curtails platelet microvascular occlusion during acute episodes. Looking forward, the review identifies ADAMTS13-centric future directions including rADAMTS13 expansion to acute iTTP — theoretically bypassing the need to replete ADAMTS13 activity while reducing inhibitor burden — new rapid diagnostic assays, and investigation of ADAMTS13 in non-TTP thrombotic indications such as sickle cell disease vaso-occlusion and COVID-19–associated coagulopathy. This review provides the definitive framework for understanding how a single molecular discovery restructured the clinical management of a previously near-universally fatal condition.

A mechanistically related study by Qiao et al. in *Blood* identifies clusterin as a WPB component that stabilises VWF multimers by binding the D4N domain of VWF and competitively inhibiting ADAMTS13-mediated proteolysis under shear stress[^2]. Clusterin knockout mice exhibited haemorrhagic diathesis, impaired thrombosis, and reduced high-molecular-weight VWF levels; all defects were rescued by exogenous clusterin administration. Plasma clusterin was significantly reduced in patients with type 2A VWD, establishing clinical relevance. A companion commentary by Ng in *Blood* frames these findings as redefining WPBs as hubs for regulatory proteins beyond VWF itself[^7]. The therapeutic implication — multimer quality stabilisation as a strategy complementary to VWF replacement — is particularly relevant to type 2A VWD management.

## III. Aplastic Anemia

A *Blood* study by Duan et al. applies a low-input mass spectrometry metabolomics platform (as few as ~10,000 cells per sample) to profile 13 immunophenotypically defined hematopoietic cell types from adult human bone marrow, identifying L-carnitine–driven fatty acid oxidation (FAO) as a key metabolic feature of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs)[^8]. Mechanistically, L-carnitine activates the PPARA-TFEB signalling axis, promoting mitochondrial metabolism and autophagy to preserve HSPC regenerative capacity. In functional validation assays, L-carnitine supplementation improved ex vivo and in vivo stem cell function in primary CD34+ HSPCs from both healthy donors and, critically, patients with aplastic anemia. While these remain preclinical data, the identification of a targetable, dietary-supplement-accessible metabolic circuit governing HSPC fitness has direct translational implications for aplastic anemia, particularly in patients awaiting transplant or undergoing immunosuppressive therapy. This study also provides the most comprehensive metabolic atlas of human HSPC subtypes published to date.

A *BJH* study by Dellal et al. reports whole-genome sequencing findings in a 6-month-old girl with a novel heterozygous *SAMD9L* gain-of-function variant presenting with pancytopenia and severe infections[^9]. Trio-based WGS identified maternal transmission and revealed distinct somatic genetic rescue (SGR) mechanisms in both proband and asymptomatic mother, including uniparental disomy of 7q (UPD7q) and additional somatic loss-of-function variants. Longitudinal molecular follow-up showed dynamic clonal evolution associated with haematological improvement. These findings support systematic screening for SGR, including mosaic UPD7q, even in cases with apparently normal variant allele frequency — a critical diagnostic consideration when evaluating unexplained paediatric cytopenias, an area relevant to the inherited bone marrow failure work-up carried out at NCKUH's paediatric haematology service.

## VI. Von Willebrand Disease

The clusterin-VWF interaction study by Qiao et al. described in Section II has direct relevance to Von Willebrand Disease pathobiology[^2]. The finding that clusterin plasma levels are significantly reduced in type 2A VWD patients, and that exogenous clusterin rescues high-molecular-weight VWF multimer levels in knockout mice, raises the possibility that clusterin deficiency may contribute to type 2A VWD pathogenesis beyond the canonical *VWF* gene mutations. This is the first identification of an endogenous multimer-stabilising factor that operates through competitive inhibition of ADAMTS13-mediated proteolysis — a mechanistic node distinct from, and potentially complementary to, existing VWF concentrate replacement strategies. The structural basis for HPA-1a alloimmunisation published in *Blood* by Zhang et al.[^15] also touches on αVβ3 integrin biology at the endothelial surface, intersecting mechanistically with VWF-endothelial axis regulation, though its primary relevance is to FNAIT (see Section XII).

## VII. Thalassemia (alpha & beta; Taiwan-relevant)

A first-in-human case report in *Blood Advances* by Yin et al. describes lentiviral vector–mediated α-globin gene therapy (Lenti-HBA) in a 14-year-old girl with transfusion-dependent Hemoglobin H (HbH) disease (--SEA/αCSα genotype), the most clinically severe form of alpha-thalassemia and a phenotype with high carrier prevalence in Taiwan and Southeast Asia[^4]. After autologous CD34+ HSPC collection, ex vivo transduction with the Lenti-HBA vector, busulfan conditioning, and reinfusion, the patient achieved engraftment within 3 weeks and became transfusion-independent from week 4 onwards. During months 12–24 of follow-up, the average haemoglobin level remained approximately 90 g/L without transfusion support, with HbA constituting >85% of total haemoglobin. Vector copy numbers were stable throughout, and no clonal dominance, insertional mutagenesis, or replication-competent lentivirus was detected. Iron overload progressed minimally after chelation therapy was stopped, with no cardiac iron deposition. The only serious adverse event was Grade 2 menstrual irregularity and ovarian failure attributable to busulfan conditioning. This case (NCT05851105) represents the first proof-of-concept for curative α-globin gene addition in human alpha-thalassemia, with direct implications for the approximately 4–5% of Taiwan's population carrying alpha-thalassemia traits and the subset of --SEA homozygotes and compound heterozygotes who are transfusion-dependent.

A *BJH* mechanistic study by Thubthed et al. examined NETs (neutrophil extracellular traps) induced by activated platelets in β-thalassaemia/HbE patients — a genotype highly prevalent in Thailand and also encountered in Taiwan's immigrant population[^16]. Three-dimensional confocal microscopy demonstrated that platelets induced NET formation through P-selectin–PSGL-1 and HMGB1-RAGE binding pathways on neutrophils. In splenectomised β-thalassaemia/HbE patients specifically, U0126 (MEK inhibitor) but not ERK inhibitor treatment reduced web-like NET formation and cell aggregation through antioxidant mechanisms, implying that splenectomy alters P-selectin and HMGB1 expression on platelets in a way that reconfigures signalling dynamics toward a more prothrombotic NET morphology. The ROS/MEK/ERK pathway is identified as a potential therapeutic target for reducing NET-driven immunothrombosis in splenectomised thalassaemia patients — a population at elevated thrombotic risk and for whom anti-thrombotic strategies remain incompletely standardised.

## VIII. Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia (AIHA)

The alemtuzumab-associated autoimmune cytopenia study by Lucchini et al. (see also Section I) characterised seven cases of AIHA and five cases of Evans syndrome emerging after renal transplant alemtuzumab induction over an 8-year observation period[^6]. Response rates and relapse dynamics in AIHA were similar to primary forms, but the infection and thrombosis burden — 62.5% and 25% respectively — was substantially higher than in primary AIHA, mandating early antimicrobial prophylaxis and rapid reintroduction of anticoagulation after platelet count recovery. This cohort provides an important real-world dataset for the uncommon but clinically challenging scenario of post-transplant AIHA where decisions about steroid dosing must be balanced against immunosuppression-related infection risk.

## IX. Red Cell Disorders (G6PD, PK deficiency, hereditary spherocytosis)

A *Blood* study by Ling et al. demonstrates that the GATA1 N-terminal domain — whose skipping (producing the GATA1s isoform) is associated with myeloid leukaemia of Down syndrome and Diamond-Blackfan anaemia (DBA) — controls glycolytic reprogramming in erythropoiesis[^17]. Single-cell RNA sequencing of fetal liver cells from Gata1s-mutant embryos revealed aberrant upregulation of glycolytic genes including *PKM* (pyruvate kinase M). GATA1s substitution induced histone lactylation at the PKM promoter, increased PKM expression and activity, and enhanced glycolytic flux in erythroid progenitors without affecting mitochondrial respiration. Importantly, PKM expression was also elevated in DBA patients with *RPS19* mutations associated with reduced GATA1 levels. This establishes a molecular link between GATA1s-driven defective erythropoiesis and dysregulated glycolysis, with mechanistic relevance to pyruvate kinase activators (mitapivat) currently in clinical trials for DBA and inherited red cell disorders — the finding that GATA1s shifts erythroid progenitors toward heightened glycolytic dependence suggests this subpopulation might have differential pharmacodynamic responses to PK activators compared with PK-deficiency–driven haemolytic anaemia.

## X. Iron Deficiency & Nutritional Anemias

A large retrospective real-world study by Sohail et al. published in *Blood* used the TriNetX Research Network (2000–2025) to evaluate IV iron safety and efficacy in adults with iron deficiency anaemia and concurrent active infection (MRSA bacteraemia, pneumonia, UTI, colitis, or cellulitis), requiring antibiotic agents within 2 days of infection for inclusion[^5]. After 1:1 propensity matching, 14-day and 90-day survival was significantly higher in patients who received IV iron across all five infection types (all p < 0.001): for MRSA bacteraemia, 97.6% vs. 95.0% at 14 days and 88.6% vs. 83.8% at 90 days; for pneumonia, 95.7% vs. 91.5% and 84.7% vs. 78.1%; for UTI, 97.6% vs. 95.7% and 89.1% vs. 85.6%; for colitis, 97.6% vs. 95.5% and 89.7% vs. 83.8%; and for cellulitis, 98.5% vs. 97.4% and 92.2% vs. 89.2%. Haemoglobin recovery at 60–90 days post-infection was significantly greater (all p < 0.001) with IV iron across all subgroups, ranging from +1.3 to +1.5 g/dL vs. +0.7 to +1.0 g/dL without IV iron. These findings are directly counter to the prevailing clinical concern that parenteral iron supplementation during acute bacterial infection may exacerbate pathogen growth through increased serum iron availability. While retrospective confounding cannot be entirely excluded despite propensity matching, the consistency of the survival and haemoglobin recovery signal across five distinct infectious pathologies is clinically compelling and should inform shared decision-making regarding iron repletion timing in hospitalised patients with IDA.

A *Blood* study by Zeng et al. identifies biallelic loss-of-function mutations in *BPNT1* as the cause of vitamin B12–dependent megaloblastic anaemia in three patients, mediated by accumulation of 3'-phosphoadenosine 5'-phosphate (PAP), impaired ribosome biogenesis, and reduced ileal expression of the cubam receptor complex in *Bpnt1*-null mice[^18]. This defines a novel monogenic cause of recurrent megaloblastic anaemia that mimics dietary B12 deficiency clinically but requires genetic confirmation for definitive diagnosis — with implications for diagnostic workup in patients with unexplained recurrent B12-responsive macrocytic anaemia at NCKUH.

## XI. Thrombosis & Coagulation (VTE, APS, inherited thrombophilia)

A *Blood* study by Maroney et al. demonstrates that mice lacking tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI) develop lethal embryonic cerebrovascular defects (glomeruloid bodies) through excess thrombin generation, and that a hyperactivatable protein C (hMPC) transgene rescues embryonic lethality through cytoprotective mechanisms rather than full correction of glomeruloid body formation[^19]. Advanced CML exhibited vascular disorganisation in a companion spatial atlas study, but the TFPI data specifically contextualise the anticoagulant-angiogenic interface in human cerebrovascular development. Specifically, hMPC reduced glomeruloid body numbers by only 36% and did not prevent fibrin deposition or blood-brain barrier disruption within the lesions, yet decreased hypoxia and cellular death sufficed to rescue survival. RNA sequencing of embryonic day 15.5 brain tissue identified apelin, adrenomedulin, and UNC5b as key angiogenic gene regulators disrupted by TFPI deficiency and restored by hMPC — establishing TFPI as an irreplaceable temporal regulator of thrombin during cerebral angiogenesis that cannot be compensated by other anticoagulant proteins alone. This has mechanistic relevance to congenital TFPI deficiency and pro-thrombotic vascular malformations in paediatric patients.

A *BJH* review by Lê et al. provides a comprehensive synthesis of thrombocytosis and platelet-derived microparticle (PMP) formation in sickle cell disease (SCD), characterising how activated platelets release PMPs that enhance thrombin generation, entrap leucocytes, and promote endothelial dysfunction driving vaso-occlusion[^20]. The review discusses therapeutic strategies targeting platelet activation as adjuncts to hydroxyurea and anti-sickling agents. While no new clinical data are presented, this mechanistic synthesis is relevant to the rationale for antiplatelet strategies in SCD patients with elevated platelet counts or recurrent acute chest syndrome.

## XIII. Taiwan Clinical Context

Several findings this week carry specific relevance for clinical practice at NCKUH and affiliated centres in Taiwan. The first human gene therapy cure for alpha-thalassemia HbH disease[^4] is directly pertinent to Taiwan's genetic landscape: the --SEA deletion (Southeast Asian deletion) is the predominant alpha-thalassaemia major allele in Taiwan, with an overall alpha-thalassaemia carrier rate of approximately 4–5%. Taiwan's NHI currently does not cover gene therapy for thalassaemia; the NCT05851105 dataset from China will inform regulatory and reimbursement discussions. The NET-driven immunothrombosis data in β-thalassaemia/HbE[^16] reinforce the clinical importance of thromboprophylaxis protocols in splenectomised thalassaemia patients managed at NCKUH's haematology service, where this genotype is encountered in patients of Southeast Asian origin. The IV iron safety data in acute infection[^5] have practical implications for inpatient haematology at NCKUH, where IDA is frequently encountered in hospitalised patients with concurrent infections and decisions regarding IV iron timing are made daily. The glycyrrhizinate-dexamethasone ITP trial[^3] is of particular regional interest as glycyrrhizinate (甘草酸製劑) is widely available and used in clinical practice in Taiwan and China; however, NHI coverage for this indication and replication in non-Asian populations will be needed before adoption. The BPNT1 megaloblastic anaemia findings[^18] should prompt consideration of genetic testing in NCKUH patients with recurrent unexplained B12-responsive macrocytic anaemia, particularly in the paediatric setting.

## XIV. Key Takeaways

The most clinically actionable messages from this week's literature are as follows. Clusterin emerges as a novel endogenous regulator of VWF multimer quality, with direct relevance to type 2A VWD pathogenesis and a potential therapeutic target for multimer stabilisation beyond protein replacement. The first human alpha-thalassemia gene therapy cure, achieved with lentiviral α-globin gene addition in a 14-year-old girl with HbH disease, represents a transformative proof-of-concept directly applicable to the southeast Asian and Taiwanese patient population. Real-world evidence now supports IV iron administration during active bacterial infection as safe and associated with meaningfully improved survival and haemoglobin recovery, directly challenging long-standing clinical conservatism. The 25-year ADAMTS13 anniversary review in *Blood* consolidates the evidence supporting ADAMTS13 activity monitoring as the backbone of TTP treatment individualisation and identifies rADAMTS13 expansion to iTTP as the next major therapeutic frontier. Finally, the dexamethasone–glycyrrhizinate randomised trial provides the first prospective evidence for this combination as an optimised first-line ITP regimen, though its modest sample size and single-region origin warrant confirmation in broader populations before routine adoption.
