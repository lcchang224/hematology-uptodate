## Summary

This cycle brings genuinely new data across PNH, hemophilia, TTP epidemiology, inherited bone marrow failure diagnostics, and a notable AIHA genomics signal, while the ITP CAR T and SRSF1-LCK stories from last week remain the dominant narrative without further follow-up this cycle. First, a randomized Phase 2 study of NTQ5082, a novel oral selective complement factor B inhibitor, in 25 complement-inhibitor-naïve PNH patients reports rapid LDH normalization (239.4 ± 63.5 U/L at 100 mg and 245.8 ± 72.6 U/L at 200 mg by week 12) with 100% of patients achieving a hemoglobin rise ≥20 g/L from baseline and no grade ≥3 adverse events, positioning it as a potential oral alternative in the increasingly crowded complement-inhibitor landscape[^1]. Second, a head-to-head biochemical and in vivo study identifies Factor VIII Aurora (FVIII-R571S), the first naturally occurring gain-of-function FVIII variant, showing 10- to 20-fold increased FIXa affinity that confers functional APC resistance and a four- to fivefold increase in hemostatic potency in murine models, offering mechanistic insight relevant to both hemophilia correction strategies and thrombophilia biology[^2]. Third, a comparative review of licensed AAV gene therapies for hemophilia B standardizes FIX-Padua activity across etranacogene dezaparvovec, (discontinued) fidanacogene elaparvovec, and dalnacogene ponparvovec, reporting mean FIX activity of 27–55 IU/dL and bleeding-rate reductions sustained to five and six years respectively, with over 90% reduction in FIX consumption across all three products[^3]. Fourth, the first Spanish national registry characterization of congenital TTP (cTTP) reports a prevalence of 1.14 cases per million and annual incidence of 0.028 cases per million, with a median diagnosis age of 26.5 years showing a bimodal distribution (ages 1 and 36) and nearly one-third of cases initially misdiagnosed[^4]. Fifth, upfront whole genome and transcriptome sequencing (WGTS) in 237 patients with suspected inherited bone marrow failure syndrome achieved a 36.7% molecular diagnostic yield, identifying novel retrotransposon-mediated gene disruption mechanisms and new gene-disease associations (THRA, MEIS1, TUBB), substantially outperforming conventional panel or exome-based approaches[^5].

## I. Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP)

No new primary data on ITP biology or therapeutics were indexed this cycle beyond what was reported last week; the BCMA CAR T-cell series in refractory primary ITP[^6] and the SRSF1-LCK splicing axis mechanistic paper[^7], along with their accompanying editorials[^8], remain the most recent substantive findings and are not duplicated here. One smaller but clinically relevant item appeared: a *Haematologica* correspondence pushes back on a prior report linking overuse of thrombopoietin receptor agonists (TPO-RAs) in suboptimal responders to myelofibrosis development in pediatric ITP, arguing that the observed association does not establish causation and cautioning against overinterpreting registry-level correlations when guiding TPO-RA dosing decisions in children[^9]. This is a useful cautionary note for clinicians managing pediatric ITP with second-line thrombopoietic agents, reinforcing that marrow fibrosis surveillance should be individualized rather than driven by a presumed dose-response causal relationship.

## II. TTP / HUS / Thrombotic Microangiopathy

The Spanish REPTT registry provides the first national epidemiological characterization of congenital thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura, surveying 57 centers covering 16.6 million inhabitants and identifying 19 patients for a prevalence of 1.14 cases per million and an annual incidence of 0.028 cases per million[^4]. In the expanded registry cohort of 28 patients, median age at diagnosis was 26.5 years with a bimodal distribution peaking at age 1 and age 36, and approximately one-third of patients were initially misdiagnosed, underscoring persistent diagnostic delay in adult-onset congenital TTP. At initial presentation, microangiopathic hemolytic anemia was near-universal, with neurological involvement in 56% and renal involvement in 44%; acute management relied on fresh frozen plasma (71%) and plasma exchange (58%), and during follow-up 48% of patients received prophylaxis, yet successive thrombotic microangiopathy crises remained frequent, with neurological sequelae in 26% and chronic kidney disease in 11% of survivors. These findings confirm that congenital TTP, although ultrarare, carries substantial long-term morbidity even with contemporary supportive care and argue for more systematic prophylactic strategies and registry-based follow-up.

## III. Aplastic Anemia

The most significant development this week is a large *Blood* diagnostic-yield study demonstrating that upfront whole genome and transcriptome sequencing (WGTS), performed on non-hematopoietic DNA in 237 patients with suspected inherited bone marrow failure syndrome (IBMFS), achieved a molecular diagnosis in 87 patients (36.7%), spanning hereditary thrombocytopenia, telomere biology disorders, Diamond-Blackfan anemia, severe congenital neutropenia, Shwachman-Diamond syndrome, and Fanconi anemia[^5]. Median patient age was 29 years, with 34.6% under 18, indicating this diagnostic yield applies across both pediatric and adult suspected-IBMFS populations rather than being confined to classic pediatric presentations. Beyond the headline diagnostic rate, WGTS identified novel pathogenic mechanisms including retrotransposon-mediated gene disruption not detectable by conventional panel or exome sequencing, established new gene-disease associations (MEIS1, TUBB), and uncovered a candidate novel IBMFS-like gene (THRA), while seven patients received secondary actionable findings and four were diagnosed with unrelated non-hematological genetic conditions. Acceptability of WGTS, assessed via the Theoretical Framework of Acceptability, was high among both patients and caregivers. This complements last week's pediatric severe aplastic anemia genetic-screening report showing low diagnostic yield in low-suspicion cases[^10], together suggesting a nuanced approach: broad genomic sequencing adds substantial value in patients with clinical features suggestive of IBMFS, whereas routine testing in unselected low-suspicion severe aplastic anemia may not be warranted before treatment initiation.

## IV. PNH — Paroxysmal Nocturnal Hemoglobinuria

A randomized, open-label Phase 2 study of NTQ5082, a novel oral selective complement factor B inhibitor, evaluated 25 complement-inhibitor-naïve PNH patients across two dose groups (100 mg and 200 mg once daily) over 12 weeks[^1]. Both doses produced rapid reductions in LDH and increases in hemoglobin within the first week of treatment, with no further transfusions required during the study; at week 12, mean LDH was 239.4 ± 63.5 U/L in the 100 mg group and 245.8 ± 72.6 U/L in the 200 mg group. All patients achieved a hemoglobin increase of ≥20 g/L from baseline, with 50% (100 mg) and 61.5% (200 mg) reaching hemoglobin concentrations ≥120 g/L. The safety profile was favorable, with the most common treatment-related adverse events being headache (12%), upper respiratory tract infection (8%), and decreased neutrophil count (8%), and no grade ≥3 adverse events reported, suggesting NTQ5082 may represent a viable oral factor B inhibitor option alongside iptacopan for complement-inhibitor-naïve patients. This is distinct from and complements last week's 151-patient UK/France real-world iptacopan dataset[^11], which this week is further corroborated by a related *AJH* analysis of the same cohort detailing identical headline figures (hemoglobin rising from 104 g/L to 127 g/L at 3 months, 76% achieving hemoglobin ≥120 g/L by 12 months versus 21.9% at baseline, transfusion independence in 88%, breakthrough hemolysis in 6.6% with a mean hemoglobin fall of 24 g/L during breakthrough events, and a suspected bacterial infection rate of 0.18 per patient-year with no meningococcal or pneumococcal infections)[^12]. Together, these two independent oral factor B/complement-pathway inhibitor datasets reinforce that modern complement blockade achieves durable transfusion independence with a favorable infection profile, broadening first-line and switch options for PNH management.

## V. Hemophilia A & B (including gene therapy)

Several complementary hemophilia papers appeared this cycle. Mechanistically, the discovery and biochemical characterization of Factor VIII Aurora (FVIII-R571S) — identified in a patient with recurrent thrombosis and early mortality — demonstrates a sixfold increase in one-stage clotting assay activity driven by a 10- to 20-fold increase in affinity for activated factor IX, which confers reduced A2-domain dissociation and functional resistance to activated protein C-mediated inactivation despite retained intrinsic APC cleavage sensitivity[^2]. In vivo, FVIII-R571S produced a four- to fivefold increase in procoagulant potency in a hemophilia A mouse tail-clip model and significantly increased platelet and fibrin accumulation in a thrombosis model relative to wild-type FVIII, establishing increased FVIIIa-FIXa complex affinity as a unifying mechanism for this naturally occurring prothrombotic phenotype, as discussed in an accompanying *Blood* editorial[^13]. On the replacement/gene therapy front, a comprehensive *Haematologica* comparative review of licensed AAV products for hemophilia B standardizes FIX-Padua activity to a single one-stage assay scale (HemosIL SynthASil) across etranacogene dezaparvovec (US/Canada/Europe-licensed), fidanacogene elaparvovec (discontinued worldwide), and dalnacogene ponparvovec/BBM-H901 (China-licensed), reporting mean FIX activity of 27–55 IU/dL, annualized bleeding rates of 0.6–1.5, and reductions in FIX consumption exceeding 90% for all three, with durability sustained to five years for etranacogene dezaparvovec and six years for the fidanacogene elaparvovec phase 1/2a cohort[^3]. Separately, a real-world French compassionate-access study of efanesoctocog alfa in 50 hemophilia A patients (84% severe, including 9 with inhibitor history) found that switching to weekly efanesoctocog prophylaxis — with 46% transitioning from emicizumab due to persistent breakthrough bleeding — produced an 89% reduction in treated bleeds (P<0.001) and an 85% reduction in joint bleeds (P<0.001) over a median 9-month follow-up, with no new-onset inhibitors, though a clinically important discrepancy emerged between local one-stage assay trough levels (median 14 IU/dL) and centralized drug-calibrated chromogenic substrate assay levels (median 7 IU/dL, P<0.001), highlighting ongoing laboratory monitoring challenges for this ultralong-acting FVIII concentrate[^14]. Finally, a biochemical study of acquired hemophilia A patients receiving emicizumab prophylaxis in the GTH-AHA-Emi study found non-neutralizing anti-emicizumab antibodies in only 1 of 47 patients (2.1%) with no effect on bleeding phenotype, supporting low immunogenicity of emicizumab in this population comparable to or better than the ~5% rate reported in congenital hemophilia A[^15]; and a mechanistic RPTH study demonstrated that low antithrombin activity (as achieved pharmacologically by fitusiran) is associated with reduced activated protein C-mediated suppression of thrombin generation in hemophilic plasma models, with 10–30% of initial peak thrombin generation persisting despite APC addition under 10–25% antithrombin activity conditions, providing mechanistic context for fitusiran's hemostatic rebalancing effect[^16].

## VII. Thalassemia (alpha & beta; Taiwan-relevant)

A *Blood Advances* study from multiple centers across Asia and the Middle East describes a simplified, low-cost matched sibling bone marrow transplant protocol for transfusion-dependent thalassemia using medicines from the WHO Model List of Essential Medicines (fludarabine, dexamethasone, busulfan, cyclophosphamide, with G-CSF-primed bone marrow graft and cyclosporine/methotrexate GVHD prophylaxis) in 176 pediatric patients treated between February 2022 and November 2025[^17]. Engraftment was achieved in 97.3% of patients, with thalassemia-free survival of 93.4% and overall survival of 96% at a median follow-up of 11.3 months; moderate-to-severe acute GVHD occurred in only 3.9% of patients, yielding a GVHD-free and thalassemia-free composite actuarial outcome of 93.4%. Cytomegalovirus reactivation was observed in 19.2% of patients, with only 0.6% progressing to CMV disease, and outcomes were comparable between experienced and newly established transplant centers, supporting the feasibility of exporting this protocol to additional low-resource settings. An accompanying *Blood Advances* commentary frames this as evidence that an "affordable cure" for thalassemia is within reach for broader global populations when transplant protocols are deliberately simplified around essential medicines rather than requiring high-cost novel agents[^18]. This is directly relevant to Taiwan's thalassemia carrier population and reinforces that matched sibling transplant, where a donor is available, remains a highly effective and increasingly accessible curative option.

## VIII. Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia (AIHA)

A new *Blood Advances* report examines clonal hematopoiesis in autoimmune hemolytic anemia, representing an emerging area of interest linking somatic clonal evolution in hematopoietic stem cells to autoimmune cytopenia pathogenesis[^19]. While the abstract text was not available in the indexed record, the appearance of this paper alongside the broader clonal hematopoiesis literature in other autoimmune cytopenias (echoing the ITP splicing-axis mechanistic work covered previously) suggests growing interest in somatic mosaicism as a contributor to AIHA susceptibility or severity, an area warranting close follow-up as full data become available in subsequent indexing cycles.

## IX. Red Cell Disorders (G6PD, PK deficiency, hereditary spherocytosis)

A comprehensive multi-omic characterization of hexokinase-1 (HK1) deficiency — a rare cause of hereditary nonspherocytic hemolytic anemia — integrates population-scale genetics from the REDS RBC-Omics cohorts with detailed metabolomic, lipidomic, oxylipin, and proteomic profiling of erythrocytes from a patient carrying the HK1 p.Arg12Ter variant, which causes complete loss-of-function of the erythroid-specific HK1-R isoform[^20]. The analysis reveals that HK1 deficiency extends well beyond impaired glycolysis to encompass disrupted pentose phosphate pathway flux, glutathione metabolism depletion, ATP depletion, impaired antioxidant defenses, and extensive membrane remodeling with accumulation of oxidized phospholipids, lysophospholipids, and oxylipins. Reticulocytosis, increased mitochondria-retaining erythrocytes, and elevated acylcarnitines and tricarboxylic acid cycle intermediates indicate significant erythropoietic stress and incomplete reticulocyte maturation as compensatory mechanisms. This represents the first integrated multi-omic framework linking both common and rare HK1 variants to molecular red cell phenotypes, with direct relevance to diagnostic evaluation of unexplained hereditary nonspherocytic hemolytic anemia in Taiwan, where G6PD deficiency is already a recognized but distinct cause of hemolysis.

## XI. Thrombosis & Coagulation (VTE, APS, inherited thrombophilia)

Several coagulation-focused papers merit attention this cycle. A population-based Ontario cohort study of 226,374 pregnant women examined the association between first-trimester serum placental growth factor (PLGF) and subsequent venous thromboembolism, finding that higher PLGF multiples of the median were associated with increased risk of pulmonary embolism specifically (adjusted hazard ratio 1.19, 95% CI 1.03–1.38 at PLGF ≥1.6 MoM), with less pronounced associations for deep vein thrombosis and overall VTE[^21]. A French pharmacovigilance analysis of 57 pregnancies exposed to direct oral anticoagulants (57.9% rivaroxaban, mainly for VTE prevention/treatment) found that only 24.6% of pregnancies had no adverse outcome, with 6 early pregnancy losses, 4 medical terminations, 2 stillbirths, 10 cases of fetal growth restriction, and 10 fetal morphological abnormalities among 50 pregnancies with available outcome data, reinforcing the need for systematic reporting of inadvertent DOAC exposure during pregnancy and avoidance of DOACs when anticoagulation is required in pregnant patients[^22]. An Italian multicenter family-based registry study characterizing Bleeding Disorder of Unknown Cause (BDUC) in 115 individuals from 39 families found mucocutaneous bleeding as the predominant phenotype, with cutaneous bleeding frequent in males (81%) and heavy menstrual bleeding the dominant manifestation in females (78%), and bleeding after tooth extraction and oral cavity bleeding showing the strongest associations with BDUC status (odds ratios 8.32 and 8.29 respectively, P<0.05), alongside reduced platelet aggregation and secretion responses to epinephrine and ADP in a subset of patients, supporting a multifactorial platelet-function contribution to this heterogeneous bleeding phenotype[^23]. Finally, a retrospective genotype-phenotype study across the PRO-RBDD and EN-RBD international registries in 422 patients with Factor VII deficiency found that FVII coagulant activity below 11 IU/dL strongly discriminated grade III (severe) bleeding (AUC 0.84), while levels of 19 IU/dL or higher discriminated asymptomatic status (AUC 0.72), with homozygous or compound heterozygous genotypes associated with lower FVII activity and more severe bleeding compared to predominantly asymptomatic heterozygous carriers (bleeding in 71% of heterozygotes being absent)[^24].

## XIII. Taiwan Clinical Context

Taiwan's alpha-thalassemia carrier prevalence (~4-5%) and beta-thalassemia carrier prevalence (~1-2%) make the simplified, essential-medicines-based matched sibling transplant protocol reported this week particularly relevant, as it demonstrates that excellent outcomes (93.4% thalassemia-free survival, 96% overall survival) can be achieved using widely available agents such as fludarabine, busulfan, cyclophosphamide, and cyclosporine rather than costly novel conditioning regimens, a model directly transferable to NHI-funded transplant programs at major centers such as NTUH, CGMH, and VGH[^17]. For PNH, both iptacopan (already NHI-reimbursed in Taiwan for eculizumab-refractory or treatment-naïve patients under specific criteria) and the emerging oral factor B inhibitor class exemplified by NTQ5082 offer complementary oral options that could reduce infusion burden for Taiwanese patients currently reliant on intravenous C5 inhibitors; the real-world iptacopan safety data showing no meningococcal or pneumococcal infections over a median 238-day exposure is reassuring for local vaccination and monitoring protocols already mandated under NHI complement-inhibitor initiation guidelines[^12]. For hemophilia, the efanesoctocog alfa real-world data and emicizumab immunogenicity findings are relevant as Taiwan's NHI continues to expand access to extended half-life factor concentrates and non-factor replacement therapies; the local-versus-central assay discrepancy highlighted in the French efanesoctocog cohort[^14] underscores a practical laboratory consideration for hemophilia treatment centers in Taiwan transitioning to this ultralong-acting product, as one-stage assay results may significantly overestimate true factor activity relative to chromogenic substrate assays calibrated to the drug.

## XIV. Key Takeaways

The PNH complement-inhibitor field continues to expand with genuinely novel oral options, as NTQ5082 joins iptacopan with comparably rapid hemolysis control and hemoglobin normalization in a complement-inhibitor-naïve population, while the independently reported real-world iptacopan dataset corroborates durable transfusion independence without thrombotic or infectious safety signals over extended follow-up. In hemophilia, this week's literature spans the full translational spectrum from basic mechanism (FVIII Aurora's FIXa-affinity-driven APC resistance) through gene therapy comparative effectiveness (standardized FIX-Padua activity across three licensed AAV products) to real-world clinical practice (efanesoctocog alfa bleed reduction and assay discrepancies, emicizumab immunogenicity in acquired hemophilia A), collectively reinforcing a maturing and increasingly nuanced non-factor and gene-therapy treatment landscape. The IBMFS diagnostic yield data provide an important counterpoint to last week's low-yield pediatric aplastic anemia screening report, together suggesting that genomic testing strategy should be tailored to pretest clinical suspicion rather than applied uniformly. Finally, the Spanish congenital TTP registry and the simplified thalassemia transplant protocol both illustrate the value of coordinated national or multinational registries and pragmatic treatment protocols in characterizing and improving outcomes for rare, resource-intensive non-malignant hematologic diseases.
