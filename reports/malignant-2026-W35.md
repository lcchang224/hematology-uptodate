## Summary

This week's literature yields five practice-relevant signals. **(1)** The phase III POLARGO trial establishes polatuzumab vedotin plus rituximab-gemcitabine-oxaliplatin (Pola-R-GemOx) as a new option for transplant-ineligible relapsed/refractory DLBCL, with a significantly lower risk of death versus R-GemOx alone (HR 0.60; 95% CI, 0.43–0.83; p=0.0017) and median OS of 19.5 versus 12.5 months[^1]. **(2)** MAIA's final survival analysis in transplant-ineligible newly diagnosed multiple myeloma reports a new benchmark median OS of 90.3 months with daratumumab-Rd versus 64.1 months with Rd (HR 0.67; 95% CI, 0.55–0.82) at 89.3 months median follow-up, with 7-year OS rates of 53.1% versus 39.3%[^2]. **(3)** A newly published PRISM risk model for AML treated with hypomethylating agent plus venetoclax, derived from 2,092 patients, improves survival discrimination over the 4-gene classifier (C-index 0.63–0.65 vs 0.59–0.61; p<0.05) and stratifies median OS from 25.1–28.8 months (low risk) to 5.8–6.7 months (high risk; p<0.001)[^3]. **(4)** Long-term follow-up of azacitidine-venetoclax-gilteritinib triplet therapy in newly diagnosed FLT3-mutated AML unfit for intensive chemotherapy shows a 96% CR/CRi rate and 3-year OS of 46%, with 67% of relapses occurring without detectable FLT3 mutation, supporting a shift toward front-line triplet regimens[^4]. **(5)** A real-world 19-center US analysis demonstrates that TKI maintenance after brexucabtagene autoleucel in MRD-negative Ph+ ALL significantly reduces 1-year relapse incidence (HR 0.12; 95% CI, 0.02–0.88; p=0.037) and improves PFS (HR 0.19; 95% CI, 0.04–0.85; p=0.029)[^5].

## I. AML — Acute Myeloid Leukemia

Risk stratification for lower-intensity venetoclax-based AML therapy advances substantially this week with publication of the PRISM model in *JCO*, derived from a multinational cohort of 2,092 adults treated with hypomethylating agent plus venetoclax (HMA+VEN)[^3]. Using Elastic Net variable selection across 1,000 bootstrap samples and Ridge regression on 17 clinical and genomic variables, the model generated a continuous score validated in two independent external cohorts (n=500 and n=222). PRISM-3 tertile risk groups showed consistent separation of median OS across all cohorts (25.1–28.8 months low risk, 12.5–14.7 months moderate risk, 5.8–6.7 months high risk; p<0.001), reclassifying approximately 40% of patients relative to the standard 4-gene classifier (FLT3-ITD, N/KRAS, TP53) and improving discrimination (C-index 0.63–0.65 vs 0.59–0.61; p<0.05). The PRISM-AML Risk Calculator is now publicly available, offering a practical tool for individualized treatment planning in this rapidly growing patient population.

Long-term outcomes of the azacitidine-venetoclax-gilteritinib triplet in newly diagnosed FLT3-mutated AML unfit for intensive chemotherapy are reported in *Blood Advances*, extending prior interim data with 41.5 months of median follow-up in 30 treated patients (73% FLT3-ITD)[^4]. The CR/CRi rate was 96%, with 47% proceeding to allogeneic HSCT in first remission; 3-year RFS and OS rates were 43% and 46% overall, and 32% and 36% among FLT3-ITD-mutated patients specifically. Critically, of the 11 patients who relapsed, 67% of evaluable cases showed no detectable FLT3 mutation at relapse, indicating that triplet-based FLT3 inhibition may suppress the FLT3-mutant clone durably enough to shift the dominant relapse mechanism toward FLT3-independent pathways; baseline RAS pathway mutations predicted inferior outcomes, and survival was similar regardless of HSCT consolidation, an important consideration for transplant-ineligible patients. An accompanying editorial frames this as a rationale for randomized comparison against standard-of-care doublets[^6].

Mechanistic and biomarker work this week includes a cheminformatic screen identifying PS127-family small molecules with combined apoptotic, autophagic, and thioredoxin/glutathione reductase-inhibitory activity, showing strong synergy with midostaurin and venetoclax and validated toxicity profiles in AML patient-derived primary cells[^7]. A separate study in *Leukemia* identifies the R222G hotspot mutation in the RNA helicase DHX15 as a cooperating lesion in RUNX1::RUNX1T1 (AML1-ETO)-positive pediatric AML, where DHX15R222G stabilizes TFAM and drives oxidative phosphorylation-dependent leukemic stem cell activity and chemoresistance, with the Complex V inhibitor S-Gboxin restoring chemosensitivity in preclinical models — nominating DHX15R222G as both a prognostic biomarker and an OXPHOS-targetable vulnerability[^8]. In IDH1-mutated myelodysplastic neoplasm/syndrome, a multicenter phase 2 trial (IDIOME, NCT03503409) of ivosidenib monotherapy across three cohorts reports ORR of 63.6% in relapsed/refractory HR-MDS post-azacitidine and 78.3% in treatment-naïve HR-MDS, with 12-month OS of 18.2% and 91.3% respectively, positioning ivosidenib monotherapy as a potential first-line option in frail treatment-naïve high-risk MDS patients, particularly those with larger baseline IDH1 mutant clones[^9].

## II. ALL — Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia

A real-world 19-center US analysis addresses a key unmet question in Ph+ ALL management following CD19-directed CAR-T therapy: whether TKI maintenance improves remission durability after brexucabtagene autoleucel-induced MRD-negative CR[^5]. Among 51 evaluable patients (20 receiving TKI maintenance, 31 without, excluding those proceeding to consolidative transplant), TKI maintenance was associated with a markedly lower 1-year cumulative incidence of relapse (HR 0.12; 95% CI, 0.02–0.88; p=0.037), translating into improved PFS (HR 0.19; 95% CI, 0.04–0.85; p=0.029) and a trend toward improved OS (HR 0.34; 95% CI, 0.07–1.62; p=0.18), with no difference in non-relapse mortality (HR 0.83; 95% CI, 0.12–5.87; p=0.85). These data support incorporating TKI maintenance as standard practice after brexu-cel in Ph+ ALL, pending prospective confirmation. Complementary mechanistic work demonstrates that mTORC1 inhibition upregulates CD20 in B-cell precursor ALL via the AKT-FOXO1 axis, including in IKZF1-deleted high-risk disease, enhancing anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody efficacy and providing rationale for combination strategies in CD20-low BCP-ALL[^10].

## III. CML — Chronic Myeloid Leukemia

A cross-sectional survivorship study compares health status outcomes among 62 TKI-treated CML patients without transplant (median follow-up 12.5 years), 220 CML patients treated with BMT without TKI exposure (median follow-up 19.4 years), and 303 non-CML controls[^11]. TKI-treated CML survivors showed substantially higher rates of adversely-affected health domains than non-CML controls (71.0% vs 42.6%), with significantly elevated odds of poor general health (aOR 4.43; 95% CI, 1.8–10.7), functional impairment (aOR 2.84; 95% CI, 1.4–6.0), activity limitation (aOR 3.37; 95% CI, 1.7–6.7), and frailty/pre-frailty (aOR 3.71; 95% CI, 1.7–8.1). Notably, health status in TKI-treated CML survivors was statistically comparable to that of BMT-treated CML survivors across most domains, though TKI patients reported greater activity limitation (aOR 2.2; 95% CI, 1.1–4.5) — findings that challenge the assumption that chronic TKI therapy confers a substantially milder long-term burden than historical transplant-based cure and argue for systematic long-term survivorship monitoring in this now-large population.

## IV. CLL / SLL — Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia

## V. MDS — Myelodysplastic Syndromes

Beyond the ivosidenib IDIOME data summarized in Section I, a Korean single-center retrospective analysis of 1,437 MDS patients delineates a biologically distinct younger-onset subset (≤50 years)[^12]. Younger patients showed significantly improved OS and leukemia-free survival (both p<0.001), lower mutation burden, and lower IPSS scores relative to older patients, while SAMD9 mutations predominated in the younger cohort versus enrichment of ASXL1, DDX41, DNMT3A, RUNX1, SF3B1, SRSF2, TET2, TP53, and ZRSR2 mutations in older patients. Response rates to hypomethylating agents did not differ by age (p=0.543), though SF3B1 mutation predicted favorable HMA response across ages. Among transplanted patients, only TP53 mutation reliably predicted inferior post-HSCT OS (HR 3.10; p=0.003), while DNMT3A, TP53, and U2AF1 mutations independently predicted worse OS specifically within the younger subgroup — reinforcing that molecular rather than chronological risk stratification should guide transplant decision-making in younger MDS patients.

## VI. MPN — Myeloproliferative Neoplasms (PV / ET / MF)

Final results of a phase II trial combining azacitidine and ruxolitinib in myelofibrosis, updating the previously reported 72% interim response rate (46 patients, 28-month follow-up), are now available with the full 61-patient cohort (median age 66 years, treated 2013–2021)[^13]; response and survival details in the abstract were truncated in the available data but the trial confirms durable IWG-MRT-defined objective responses in both chronic- and advanced-phase disease, supporting continued investigation of HMA-JAK inhibitor combinations in higher-risk myelofibrosis. Separately, a *Leukemia* review consolidates the growing evidence base for CALR-directed immunotherapy in CALR-mutant essential thrombocythemia and myelofibrosis, summarizing early-phase data on Fc-silent mutant CALR-MPL antagonist antibodies achieving rapid, durable hematologic remissions with minimal toxicity, alongside preclinical antibody-drug conjugates, bispecific T-cell engagers, and CAR-T constructs targeting the shared CALR neo-C-terminus — signaling a potential shift from symptom-directed to disease-modifying therapy in this molecularly defined MPN subset[^14].

## VII. DLBCL & Aggressive B-cell Lymphoma

The phase III POLARGO trial provides definitive randomized evidence for polatuzumab vedotin plus R-GemOx in transplant-ineligible relapsed/refractory DLBCL[^1]. Among 255 randomized patients (129 Pola-R-GemOx, 126 R-GemOx), median follow-up of 24.6 months showed a significantly reduced risk of death with the polatuzumab-containing arm (HR 0.60; 95% CI, 0.43–0.83; p=0.0017), with median OS of 19.5 months (95% CI, 13.3–NE) versus 12.5 months (95% CI, 8.9–15.8). Peripheral neuropathy, predominantly grade 1, was more frequent with Pola-R-GemOx (57% vs 29%), and fatal adverse events occurred in 12% versus 4%, largely infection-related. This establishes Pola-R-GemOx as a validated option for the substantial population of R/R DLBCL patients ineligible for autologous transplant or CAR-T.

Complementing this, a Czech real-world registry study (NiHiL project, NCT03199066) of 312 consecutive third-line-treated R/R DLBCL patients demonstrates population-level survival gains coinciding with CAR-T availability: comparing Era 1 (2018–2020) to Era 2 (2021–2023), OS improved significantly (HR 0.70; p=0.01; 2-year OS 43% vs 29%), 2-year EFS rose from 13% to 24% (HR 0.70; p=0.01), and complete remission rates improved from 18% to 30% (p=0.02), with CAR-T utilization increasing from 6% to 32% of the CAR-T-eligible cohort[^15]. Molecular monitoring innovation is also reported for post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder, where cell-free DNA profiling in a 27-patient prospective feasibility study (NTR7402) outperformed PET/CT for interim response assessment in 8 of 27 cases, with undetectable ctDNA correlating with superior outcomes after rituximab monotherapy[^16].

## VIII. Indolent Lymphoma (FL, MCL, MZL)

Real-world Spanish data on zanubrutinib in 118 patients with relapsed/refractory marginal zone lymphoma (median age 75 years) confirm activity outside the pivotal MAGNOLIA trial population, including in the 26.3% of patients who would not have met MAGNOLIA eligibility criteria[^17]. Overall and complete response rates were 76% and 22.2% respectively, with 1-year PFS and OS of 79.4% and 86.8% at 14 months median follow-up; responses were better with fewer prior lines of therapy, and MAGNOLIA-ineligible patients experienced higher adverse event rates, with bleeding events more frequent among those on concurrent anticoagulation (p=0.04). Separately, the newly developed and externally validated LTB-TREAT prognostic model for low tumor burden follicular lymphoma under watchful waiting, derived from 229 patients across the Australasian Lymphoma Alliance, identifies elevated LDH and >4 nodal areas as independent predictors of shorter time to treatment, stratifying patients into low (median TTT 6.34 years), intermediate (4.08 years), and high-risk (1.49 years) groups (p<0.001), offering a practical tool to individualize watch-and-wait counseling[^18].

## IX. Hodgkin Lymphoma & T-cell Lymphoma

Long-term data from the HALO trial evaluating brentuximab vedotin plus bendamustine (Be-BV) as frontline therapy in elderly Hodgkin lymphoma (mean age 70.75 years, 68% with ≥5 comorbidities) show a complete metabolic response in 44/57 patients, with 6-year OS and PFS of 67% (95% CI, 53–84) and 45% (95% CI, 32–65) respectively, establishing Be-BV as an effective option for this comorbidity-laden, poor-risk population previously underrepresented in trials[^19]. Separately, a German single-center implementation study of a condensed 3-day BrECADD protocol (reducing standard 4-day infusion scheduling by 25%) in 30 advanced-stage classic Hodgkin lymphoma patients reports grade ≥3 adverse events in 77% (predominantly hematologic) but 100% 1-year PFS and OS at 18-month median follow-up, suggesting the abbreviated schedule is feasible without compromising early efficacy, pending longer follow-up[^20].

## X. NK/T-cell Lymphoma (Asia focus)

A multimodal spatial profiling study of extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma (ENKTL) identifies two tumor-microenvironment subgroups defined by distinct macrophage programs — an inflammatory subgroup enriched for IFN-α/γ-responsive macrophages co-expressing IDO1 and CD274, and an immune-quiescent, macrophage-sparse subgroup dominated by STAB1-scavenging macrophages[^21]. Spatial neighborhood analysis reveals an NF-κB-tumor/inflammatory-macrophage niche sustained by CCL- and IL1-mediated myeloid self-reinforcement, and higher abundance of this niche correlates with improved survival across independent cohorts — providing a spatially resolved prognostic framework and rationale for myeloid-directed immunotherapeutic strategies in this EBV-associated lymphoma with particular relevance to Asian populations, including Taiwan.

## XI. Multiple Myeloma

MAIA's final survival analysis sets a new benchmark for frontline therapy in transplant-ineligible newly diagnosed multiple myeloma[^2]. At a median follow-up of 89.3 months among 737 randomized patients, daratumumab-lenalidomide-dexamethasone (D-Rd, n=368) achieved median OS of 90.3 months (95% CI, 80.8–NE) versus 64.1 months (95% CI, 56.0–70.8) with Rd alone (n=369; HR 0.67; 95% CI, 0.55–0.82), with estimated 7-year OS rates of 53.1% versus 39.3%. Median time to subsequent antimyeloma therapy was not reached with D-Rd versus 42.4 months with Rd (HR 0.51; 95% CI, 0.41–0.63; p<0.0001), and death due to adverse events was balanced between arms (12% vs 11%). This >7.5-year median OS confirms frontline daratumumab-Rd as the durable standard for transplant-ineligible NDMM, complementing last week's CEPHEUS MRD data in the transplant-deferred setting[^22] (frailty subgroup analysis of CEPHEUS was also published this week but detailed numeric results were not available in the current data cache[^23]).

Real-world comparative data on anti-BCMA cellular therapies continue to mature. A nationwide Austrian retrospective analysis of 90 patients treated with idecabtagene vicleucel or ciltacabtagene autoleucel (January 2024–July 2025) found no early separation of PFS curves between products at 17.6 months median follow-up, contrasting with prior real-world reports of early cilta-cel/ide-cel divergence, an effect the authors attribute to highly effective bridging therapy administered in 94.4% of patients; true extramedullary disease and prior bispecific antibody exposure before apheresis were independently associated with inferior PFS[^24]. A separate single-center case series raises a safety signal of Parkinsonism-like delayed neurotoxicity following idecabtagene vicleucel, characterized clinicopathologically with CSF and autopsy correlates, underscoring the need for long-term neurologic surveillance after BCMA-directed CAR-T[^25]. In the CNS-involvement setting, a retrospective analysis of 304 myeloma patients with extramedullary disease identifies 20 (6.5%) with CNS-MM, associated with high-risk cytogenetics (OR 3.7) and additional visceral EMD (OR 4.6); median OS from CNS-MM diagnosis was only 4.2 months, but patients receiving CAR-T or bispecific antibodies achieved markedly better outcomes (median OS 19.2 vs 1.2 months with non-immune-effector therapy), while intrathecal chemotherapy showed limited benefit[^26]. The International Myeloma Working Group has also published updated consensus recommendations for diagnosis and management of solitary plasmacytomas, emphasizing rigorous exclusion of systemic disease via advanced imaging and sensitive marrow assessment given that approximately 50% of solitary plasmacytomas progress to symptomatic myeloma within 5 years of definitive local radiotherapy[^27].

## XII. Transplant & Cellular Therapy (CAR-T / HSCT / GVHD)

BMT CTN 1702 donor-selection data (covered last week) are complemented this week by a large EBMT registry analysis of HLA evolutionary divergence (HED) in 4,695 patients undergoing 9/10 mismatched unrelated donor allo-HCT[^28]. Beyond conventional allele-level mismatch classification, HED metrics quantifying peptide-binding-groove sequence divergence revealed substantial within-mismatch heterogeneity: in DRB1-mismatched pairs, locus-specific HED-mismatch independently predicted inferior relapse-free survival, while in HLA-B-mismatched pairs, higher DRB1 recipient HED was associated with worse OS and RFS and higher relapse risk, and in HLA-A-mismatched pairs, higher HLA-A recipient HED increased chronic GVHD risk — suggesting HED-based refinement could improve donor selection algorithms beyond simple mismatch counting.

In chronic GVHD, a single-center phase 1/2 study of the JAK1/2 inhibitor baricitinib in 24 patients with severe disease refractory to ≥2 prior lines reports an overall response rate at 6 months of 76.2% (95% CI, 52.8–91.8) with no dose-limiting toxicities across two dose levels, median failure-free survival of 19 months (95% CI, 11–26), and correlative evidence of reduced STAT phosphorylation and proinflammatory cytokine secretion in CD4+ and CD8+ T cells[^29]. A separate 552-patient retrospective cytokine analysis identifies divergent roles for IL-12p40 and IL-18 in severe fibrosing chronic GVHD, with IL-12p40 associated with reduced relapse (cause-specific HR 0.88; 95% CI, 0.84–0.93)[^30]. On the cellular therapy safety front, a 104-patient exploratory study finds that TP53-mutated clonal hematopoiesis (CHIP) — but not overall CHIP status — is linked to lower baseline platelet and hemoglobin counts, higher CAR-HEMATOTOX scores, inferior clinical outcomes, and a distinct inflammatory signature after CAR-T infusion, nominating TP53-mutated CHIP as a high-risk genotype warranting longitudinal monitoring[^31]. Finally, a phase 1/2 study of allogeneic CD19-directed CAR-T CTX110 in 63 heavily pretreated R/R B-cell NHL patients reports objective and complete response rates of 65% and 39% among those receiving dose level ≥3, with a second infusion regimen prolonging response duration relative to single infusion (HR 0.65), and manageable CRS (54%, grade ≥3 in 2 patients) and ICANS (13%, grade ≥3 in 2 patients)[^32], representing a maturing off-the-shelf alternative to autologous CAR-T constructs.

## XIV. Taiwan Clinical Context

The POLARGO and MAIA readouts are directly relevant to NHI-covered practice in Taiwan: daratumumab-Rd is already reimbursed for transplant-ineligible NDMM at CGMH, NTUH, and VGH sites, and the new 90.3-month median OS benchmark reinforces continued frontline use; polatuzumab vedotin, meanwhile, carries more restrictive NHI reimbursement criteria for R/R DLBCL, and the POLARGO data supporting Pola-R-GemOx in the transplant-ineligible population may inform future reimbursement expansion discussions, particularly for elderly Taiwanese DLBCL patients who are frequently transplant-ineligible at relapse. The ENKTL spatial biology findings are of particular regional interest given the markedly higher incidence of extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma in Taiwan and East Asia compared with Western populations; CGMH and NTUH lymphoma programs, both with substantial ENKTL case volumes, are well positioned to validate the myeloid-niche prognostic framework in local cohorts. The CML survivorship data on long-term TKI-related health impairment also merit local attention, as Taiwan's CML population under first- and second-generation TKI therapy (largely NHI-reimbursed) is aging, and structured survivorship clinics analogous to those recommended in the US cohort study could be considered at major transplant centers.

## XV. Key Takeaways

The week's strongest signal is the maturation of large randomized trials into long-term, practice-defining survival benchmarks — POLARGO for R/R DLBCL and MAIA's final analysis for transplant-ineligible NDMM — both reinforcing antibody-based combination backbones as durable standards of care. Molecular risk stratification tools continue to proliferate and improve, exemplified by the PRISM model for HMA-venetoclax AML and the LTB-TREAT model for indolent follicular lymphoma, both offering clinician-usable calculators for individualized decision-making. Cellular therapy data this week emphasize both opportunity and caution: TKI maintenance after CAR-T meaningfully reduces relapse in Ph+ ALL, allogeneic "off-the-shelf" CD19 CAR-T (CTX110) shows durable activity in NHL, yet emerging safety signals — TP53-mutated CHIP as an adverse prognostic marker and rare delayed Parkinsonism-like neurotoxicity after ide-cel — argue for sustained long-term surveillance infrastructure as cellular therapies move into broader and older populations.
